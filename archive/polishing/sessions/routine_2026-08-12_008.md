### Furigana (screening pass)

were re-screened; the runner skipped 82 of them as unchanged since their last
result and screened 21. One flag: entry 09273, where the reviewer objected to
`{砲丸投|ほうがんな}げ` and `{円盤投|えんばんな}げ`, arguing the readings should be
ほうがんなげ / えんばんなげ. That is the okurigana false-positive family documented in
`reviews/calibration_report.md` — this dictionary's furigana wrappers cover only
the kanji, with げ carried outside as okurigana, exactly as here. Rejected, and
per §A's known-noise shortcut the expensive deep pass was skipped, which left the
budget for the accuracy dimension.

Three older flags from the June screening that did not fit an obvious noise family

### Glosses, translations, and semantic tags

458 of the 500 entries were reviewed before upstream throughput collapsed from
roughly nine entries a minute to about two; the pass was stopped at 09308 rather
than left to run, and the cursor now carries the remaining 42 entries to the next
run. Results:

- **229 entries were carrying semantic tags that are not in `VALID_SEMANTIC`** —
  46% of the range. Under the 2026-06-11 semantic-tag policy a "not in the list"
  flag is correct by definition, so all 229 were migrated to in-vocabulary tags.
  Each entry's headword, gloss and part of speech were read before choosing its
  replacement; the mapping was not mechanical, because the right destination
  depends on the word (`body` becomes `body-part` for おでこ but `health` for
  よだれ, and so on). Dictionary-wide, entries carrying off-vocabulary semantic
  tags fell from 1,364 to 1,135.
- **Three further entries had a tag in the vocabulary but in the wrong category**,
  which the policy treats as a real error rather than a nit: 08869 上弦 (first
  quarter moon) was tagged `weather` → `nature`; 08884 EC (e-commerce) was tagged
  `action` → `business`, `technology`; 08853 NGO was tagged `work` →
  `organization-name`, `society`.
- **One gloss was tightened**: 08985 日にち read "date; the day", which is vague in
  a way that would mislead; it now reads "date; day (of a scheduled event)".
- **29 tag flags were rejected as a family** — all of them "too narrow" or "too
  broad" substitutions between two tags that are both in the vocabulary, which
  §A's policy rejects by design to avoid churn.
- **Two gloss flags were rejected**: 08924 一刻 (the model wanted the historical
  "two hours" sense added; the modern gloss is correct) and 08955 一眼 (the model's
  point that 一眼 literally means "single lens" is already explained in the entry's
  own notes).

### Flagged for the curator

flagged this headword five separate times as not a real Japanese word, and the
entry undermines itself: its notes list 助手席 and 後部座席 as the ordinary terms
while its examples invent 助手車席 and 後部車席. The ordinary words are 座席, 席 and
シート. This looks like a fabricated headword from an early batch. Deleting a
published entry changes a live URL on the site, so the decision belongs to the
curator rather than an unattended run. Written up in `reviews/needs_curator.txt`.

## Self-verification (§4)

The 233 entries changed this run were sent back to an independent model with the
`tags` dimension — the dimension this run actually edited. Upstream throttling
made a full pass impractical, so the check covers 127 entries (54%) rather than
all 233; those were the first 127 in ID order, not a filtered selection.

The result is clean on the class that matters: **the model did not report a single
off-vocabulary tag among the entries this run retagged**, and it found no factual
error in any gloss or tag. The 25 issues it did raise were all in-list narrowness
opinions of the same kind rejected above — for example that `media` is too broad
for SE (sound effect), or that `appearance` should come off 蓄える even though the
entry documents "to grow a beard" as its second sense. All 25 rejected as a
family; none indicated a mistake introduced by this run.

One caveat worth recording: `review_accuracy.py` stamps `reviewed_at` with a time
that does not match when the entry was actually reviewed (self-check results
written after 22:45 carry 21:32 timestamps). That made it briefly look as though
two off-vocabulary flags had survived the migration, when in fact those came from
the earlier range pass whose result file the self-check had not yet overwritten.
Logged as a `[tooling]` observation.
