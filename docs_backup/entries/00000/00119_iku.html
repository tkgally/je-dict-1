行(い)く

いく
verb (godan)
to go
1. to go, to move (toward a destination)
Expresses movement away from the speaker's current location toward a destination. The opposite of 来(く)る (to come). Used when the speaker is not at the destination.
学校(がっこう)に行(い)きます。
I go to school.
明日(あした)、東京(とうきょう)に行(い)く予定(よてい)です。
I plan to go to Tokyo tomorrow.
2. to proceed, to continue
When attached to the te-form of another verb (〜ていく), indicates an action continuing into the future or moving away from the speaker's perspective.
これから暑(あつ)くなっていく。
It will get hotter from now on.
〜ていく expressing change continuing into the future

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). Counterpart: 来(く)る (to come). Pattern: Xに/へ行(い)く (go to X).

ASPECT: ている indicates ongoing action or resulting state. 行(い)っている can mean 'is going' or 'has gone (and is there now).'

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 学校(がっこう)に行(い)く (go to school)
  • 買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)く (go shopping)
  • 遊(あそ)びに行(い)く (go to hang out)
  • 〜て行(い)く (go and do ~, or do ~ as one goes)

IRREGULAR FORMS:

  • Te-form: 行(い)って (not 行(い)いて)
  • Also read as ゆく in literary contexts

The auxiliary 〜ていく indicates continuing into the future or moving away.
