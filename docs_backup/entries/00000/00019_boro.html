ぼろ

ぼろ
noun
rag, tattered clothes
1. rag, tattered cloth
Old, worn-out cloth or clothing.
ぼろを着(き)ている。
I'm wearing rags.
ぼろで床(ゆか)を拭(ふ)く。
I wipe the floor with a rag.
ぼろ切(き)れで窓(まど)を拭(ふ)いた。
I wiped the window with a rag.
Sense 1: rag
ぼろぼろの服(ふく)を着(き)ていた。
He was wearing tattered clothes.
Sense 1: tattered cloth
古(ふる)いぼろを捨(す)てた。
I threw away the old rags.
Sense 1: rag
ぼろ同然(どうぜん)の家(いえ)に住(す)んでいた。
They lived in a house that was practically in ruins.
Sense 1: tattered/worn out
2. flaw, fault
A defect or weakness that becomes apparent.
ぼろが出(で)ないように気(き)をつける。
I'm careful not to let my faults show.
質問(しつもん)されてぼろが出(で)た。
When questioned, my faults became apparent.
Sense 2: flaw
嘘(うそ)をつくとぼろが出(で)る。
If you lie, your flaws will show.
Sense 2: fault
長(なが)く話(はな)すとぼろを出(だ)す。
If I talk too long, I'll reveal my weaknesses.
Sense 2: flaw

ぼろ refers to rags or tattered items, and figuratively to flaws.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • ぼろを着(き)る: to wear rags
  • ぼろが出(で)る: one's faults/weaknesses become apparent
  • ぼろを出(だ)す: to reveal one's flaws
  • ぼろ切(き)れ: a rag, a scrap of cloth

ADJECTIVE FORM:

  • ぼろぼろ: worn out, tattered, falling apart

例(れい): 服(ふく)がぼろぼろだ (The clothes are in tatters)

IDIOM:
ぼろが出(で)る is commonly used to mean that someone's true (negative) nature or incompetence becomes visible.
