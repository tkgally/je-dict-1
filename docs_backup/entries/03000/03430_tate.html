盾(たて)

たて
noun
shield
1. shield
A piece of armor carried to protect the body in combat. In modern Japanese, commonly used figuratively to mean something that protects or serves as a defense.
武士(ぶし)は盾(たて)と刀(かたな)を持(も)って戦(たたか)った。
The samurai fought with shields and swords.
子供(こども)を盾(たて)にして逃(に)げるのは卑怯(ひきょう)だ。
It's cowardly to escape using a child as a shield.
2. pretext, excuse
Something used as a protective cover for one's true intentions or actions. Often appears in the phrase 〜を盾(たて)にする (to use ~ as a shield/excuse).
彼(かれ)は病気(びょうき)を盾(たて)にして仕事(しごと)を休(やす)んだ。
He used his illness as an excuse to take time off work.
〜を盾にする = to use ~ as an excuse/shield

USAGE: In modern Japanese, the figurative meaning (excuse, pretext) is more common than the literal meaning (physical shield). Japanese samurai historically did not use shields as commonly as Western soldiers since they needed both hands for swords and bows.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 盾(たて)にする (to use as a shield/excuse)
  • 盾(たて)になる (to serve as protection)
  • 法(ほう)の盾(たて) (shield of the law)
  • 人権(じんけん)を盾(たて)にする (to hide behind human rights)

RELATED: 矛(ほこ) (spear) - these two words appear together in the famous Chinese story about contradiction (矛盾(むじゅん)).
