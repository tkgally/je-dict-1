# Multi-Model Proofreading and Accuracy Verification

**Last updated**: 2026-04-06

## Overview

All dictionary content has been written and checked by Claude. While quality is generally high, systematic verification by other frontier models (GPT, Gemini, etc.) would catch errors that a single model's training data and biases might miss. This page outlines a framework for cross-model proofreading, likely via OpenRouter for unified API access.

## Motivation

### Why cross-model review matters

- **Training data diversity**: Different models were trained on different corpora. A kanji reading that Claude learned from one source may be corrected by a model trained on different Japanese-language data.
- **Methodological diversity**: Models differ in how they handle Japanese text — tokenization, character-level reasoning, cultural knowledge weighting. These differences make disagreements between models genuinely informative.
- **Blind spots are model-specific**: Each model has characteristic weaknesses. Claude may over-rely on certain patterns; GPT may have different Japanese frequency biases; Gemini may handle certain kanji compounds differently. Cross-checking surfaces these blind spots.
- **Curator observation**: The project curator noted in the blog (February 21, 2026) plans for "a comprehensive proofreading and evaluation agent" using GPT or Gemini "to get a second opinion." This page develops that idea into a concrete system.

### What the blog tells us

The about.html blog contains several relevant observations:
- Audio readings by OpenAI TTS had occasional errors (は read as *ha* instead of *wa*), suggesting that even high-quality models make Japanese-specific mistakes that cross-checking can catch.
- Illustrations generated by image models had subtle errors that were "too subtle for me to identify" — the same principle applies to text content.
- The curator explicitly wants to "create a comprehensive proofreading and evaluation agent" that reads entries one at a time and examines every element.

## Areas for verification

### 1. Furigana correctness

**Risk level**: High — incorrect readings directly mislead learners.

**What to check**:
- Kanji with multiple readings: is the correct reading selected for the context? (e.g., 生 can be なま, せい, しょう, い, etc.)
- Rare or literary readings: are they correctly identified as such?
- Compound-specific readings: jukujikun (熟字訓) like 大人(おとな), rendaku patterns (濁音化)
- Okurigana boundaries: is the furigana split correctly between kanji and trailing kana?

**Verification approach**: Present the full entry to a reviewer model and ask it to independently generate the reading for each kanji occurrence, then compare with the entry's furigana. Flag disagreements for human or Claude review.

### 2. Kanji and kana usage

**Risk level**: Medium — incorrect usage looks unnatural to native speakers.

**What to check**:
- Words conventionally written in kana despite having kanji (e.g., できる, ある, いる)
- Words where kanji is standard and kana would look odd
- Okurigana correctness (e.g., 行う vs. 行なう)
- Katakana vs. hiragana for loanwords and onomatopoeia
- Consistency within the entry (same word written differently in different examples)

**Verification approach**: Ask the reviewer model to flag any headword or example where the kanji/kana choice deviates from standard modern usage.

### 3. Glosses, definitions, and notes

**Risk level**: Medium — subtle inaccuracies are hard to detect but can mislead learners.

**What to check**:
- **Completeness**: Are important senses missing? Compare with the model's own knowledge of the word.
- **Accuracy**: Do the English glosses capture the right meaning? Are there false friends or misleading translations?
- **Register accuracy**: Is the register label correct? Is a word marked "neutral" actually quite formal, or vice versa?
- **Note accuracy**: Do usage notes make correct claims about grammar, usage, or cultural context?
- **Contrastive accuracy**: When notes distinguish near-synonyms, are the distinctions correct and well-explained?

**Verification approach**: Present the entry and ask the reviewer to (a) list any senses it thinks are missing, (b) flag any gloss or note claim it disagrees with, and (c) suggest improvements. Weight disagreements by confidence.

### 4. Example sentences

**Risk level**: Medium-high — unnatural examples undermine trust and teach bad patterns.

**What to check**:
- **Naturalness**: Would a native speaker actually say this? Are there awkward constructions or unidiomatic phrasing?
- **Translation accuracy**: Does the English translation correctly convey the Japanese? Are nuances preserved?
- **Sense-headword match**: Does each example actually demonstrate the sense it's listed under? Are there POS mismatches (e.g., a noun example under a verb sense)?
- **Register consistency**: Does the example match the register described in the entry?
- **Vocabulary tier compliance**: Do basic/core tier examples avoid general-tier vocabulary?
- **Progressive difficulty**: Do examples go from simple to complex within each sense?

**Verification approach**: For each example, ask the reviewer to rate naturalness (1-5), check the translation, and verify it demonstrates the intended sense. Flag anything below 4.

### 5. Cross-references and relationships

**What to check**:
- Are prominent_see_also links pointing to the right entries?
- Are relationship labels correct (synonym vs. antonym vs. related)?
- Are obvious connections missing?
- Are cross-references bidirectional?

## Proposed architecture

### OpenRouter as unified gateway

Using OpenRouter provides access to multiple models through a single API:
- **Primary reviewers**: GPT-4o/GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, possibly Llama or Mistral for additional diversity
- **Cost management**: Use cheaper models (Flash, Haiku) for bulk screening; reserve expensive models for entries where screening found disagreements
- **Consistency**: Same prompt format across all models, making it easy to compare outputs

### Two-pass review system

**Pass 1 — Bulk screening** (cheap, fast):
- Run all entries through a fast model (e.g., Gemini Flash, Claude Haiku)
- Structured output: JSON with per-field confidence scores and flags
- Goal: identify the ~10-20% of entries that need closer attention
- Fields checked: furigana readings, basic gloss accuracy, example naturalness

**Pass 2 — Deep review** (expensive, thorough):
- Flagged entries from Pass 1 go to multiple strong models (GPT-5, Gemini Pro, Claude Opus)
- Each model independently reviews and suggests corrections
- Corrections where 2+ models agree are high-confidence
- Single-model flags go to a review queue for Claude or human judgment

### Output format

Each review produces a structured report per entry:

```json
{
  "entry_id": "01234",
  "reviewer_model": "gpt-5",
  "timestamp": "2026-04-06T00:00:00Z",
  "furigana_issues": [
    {"location": "example_3", "current": "せい", "suggested": "しょう", "confidence": "high", "reason": "..."}
  ],
  "gloss_issues": [],
  "example_issues": [
    {"example_index": 2, "issue_type": "naturalness", "severity": "minor", "suggestion": "...", "confidence": "medium"}
  ],
  "note_issues": [],
  "missing_senses": [],
  "overall_quality": 4
}
```

### Integration with existing pipeline

- Review reports stored in a new `reviews/` directory (or database)
- A new polishing task (`polish_cross_model_review.md`) processes flagged entries
- Claude reads the review report, evaluates each suggestion, and applies or rejects it
- Rejected suggestions logged with reasoning (useful for understanding model disagreements)
- Dashboard integration: `report.py` could show review coverage and acceptance rates

## Implementation roadmap

### Phase 1: Proof of concept (near-term)
- Write a review prompt template for a single area (furigana correctness)
- Run 50-100 entries through 2-3 models via OpenRouter
- Manually evaluate the quality of suggestions
- Estimate false positive rate and cost per entry

### Phase 2: Structured pipeline (medium-term)
- Build a review runner script (similar to existing batch prompts)
- Implement structured JSON output parsing
- Create the two-pass screening system
- Add a polishing task for processing review results

### Phase 3: Continuous review (long-term)
- Integrate with the automated pipeline
- Every new entry gets cross-model review before or shortly after publication
- Periodic re-review of older entries as models improve
- Track acceptance rates to calibrate trust in each model's suggestions

## Cost considerations

- At current OpenRouter prices, reviewing all ~19,000+ entries through even a cheap model would cost several dollars
- The two-pass system keeps costs manageable: bulk screening at ~$0.001/entry, deep review at ~$0.01-0.05/entry
- Budget roughly $5-20 for a full dictionary review cycle
- As the dictionary grows, prioritize reviewing new entries first

## Open questions

- **Prompt design**: How much context should each review prompt include? Full entry? Entry + related entries?
- **Disagreement resolution**: When models disagree, what's the tiebreaker? Claude's judgment? Majority vote? Human curator?
- **Hallucination risk**: Reviewer models may confidently suggest incorrect "corrections." How to guard against this?
- **Scope per pass**: Review one area at a time (furigana only, then glosses only) or all areas at once?
- **Version tracking**: How to track which entries have been reviewed, by which models, and when?
