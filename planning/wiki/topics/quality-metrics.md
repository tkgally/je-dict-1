# Quality Metrics Trend

**Last updated**: 2026-06-10 (page created; first snapshot of the Routine v2 metrics era)

## Overview

The Unified Improvement Routine v2 (`prompts/routine2.md`) instruments every run.
Two append-only ledgers accumulate a real time series instead of impressions:

- **`pipeline/metrics-history.jsonl`** — one line per Routine run (§5): mode,
  entries changed, flags applied/rejected/escalated, plus dictionary-wide
  counters (entry total, candidates, review-queue depth, observation lines,
  comprehensive-polish frontier, OpenRouter spend).
- **`reviews/decisions.jsonl`** — one line per *adjudicated external-model flag*
  (§C): the entry, the review source, the dimension, the severity, and the
  APPLY / REJECT / FLAG decision with a telegraphic note.

This page summarises both so the curator (and the weekly wiki review) can watch
two things that used to be anecdotal: **whether dictionary quality is improving**
and **how precise each external reviewer dimension is**. It is regenerated by the
`wiki` mode's metrics-trend activity whenever ≥10 new metrics lines have
accumulated since the last update.

> **Sample caveat.** This first snapshot covers a single day — **4 Routine runs
> (2026-06-10) and 485 adjudicated flags**. Treat every number below as a
> baseline, not a trend. Per-dimension *precision* is the most durable signal in
> this small a sample; run-to-run deltas in the dictionary-wide counters are
> noise until there are weeks of data.

## What the first snapshot shows

1. **`tags` is the highest-yield dimension (6.8%), ~3–4× the gloss/translation
   rate.** This validates the routine2 design bet (§2, §A): the `tags` pass of
   `review_accuracy.py` judges each semantic tag against the *headword* rather
   than the example topics, so it catches the pervasive batch tag-drift
   ([Schema Tag Reliability](schema-tag-reliability.md) → P11) that the
   example-topic-contaminated original tagging produced. The session-level
   observations corroborate the kind of win it lands: clothing→tool on 傘/かばん,
   education→grammatical on 修飾, redundant `health` on anatomy entries that
   should be `body-internal`.

2. **Self-checking your own changes is ~8× more precise than sweeping the
   dictionary (12.7% vs 1.5%).** Reviewing exactly the entries a run just
   touched (§4) concentrates the model on the small, coherent set where fresh
   errors actually live. The whole-dictionary accuracy sweep has a far lower hit
   rate but is the *only* instrument that finds dormant pre-existing errors — it
   is surveillance, not a high-yield fix queue. Both roles are deliberate; the
   numbers just quantify the difference.

3. **Furigana screening is near-zero precision so far (0 of 9 applied).**
   Consistent with the standing pattern (`reviews/calibration_report.md`, and
   repeated polish observations): the screening model over-flags *correct*
   partial-okurigana readings (`果|は` in 果てる, `跳|は`/`上|あ`) and occasionally
   hallucinates a kanji. On format-only `systemic-fix` batches the screener is
   still worth running as a **regression check** — a clean screen confirms the
   edit introduced nothing — but its flags on untouched furigana are noise.

4. **The aggregate apply rate is ~3%: external models raise ~33 issues per
   accepted fix.** Most of the volume is gloss/translation "incompleteness" nits
   on deliberately concise basic/core glosses, plus headword-mismatch misreads
   of compound examples (何か/何も/どうして). This is the cost side of cross-model
   review; it is acceptable *because* the few real catches are ones no
   single-model pass would find, but it argues for weighting dimensions by
   demonstrated precision rather than treating every flag equally.

5. **The review queue is not converging.** `reviews/queue.txt` holds ~19,450
   entries — roughly two-thirds of the dictionary. Only `accuracy-review` drains
   it (−163 on its run), while `new-entries` (+5) and `systemic-fix` (+6) re-add
   their own changed entries by design, and CI re-adds anything that changes
   later. Net movement on 2026-06-10 was about −152 across the whole day. At
   ~150/run and roughly one run in five being accuracy-review, the queue behaves
   as a slow-burn surveillance backlog, not something that reaches zero. (Logged
   as a `[pattern]` observation this run.)

6. **Spend is concentrated in `accuracy-review`.** $0.5525 of the $5.00 daily
   cap was used on 2026-06-10, and ~$0.54 of that was the single accuracy-review
   run (~200 entries at the documented ~$2/1,000 rate). The polish, new-entries,
   and systemic-fix self-checks cost pennies combined — the §4 quality gate is
   nearly free.

## Implications for je-dict-1

- **Weight reviewer dimensions by precision.** On this evidence, `tags` flags
  deserve the most trust; `gloss`/`translation` flags should carry a low prior
  (apply only on a clear error, not on "could be fuller"); the `furigana`
  screener's flags are near-noise except as a regression check on format edits.
  The decision ledger makes this tunable per dimension as the sample grows.
- **Keep §4 self-check as the primary quality instrument.** It is cheap and
  ~8× more precise than the sweep. The whole-dictionary accuracy sweep stays
  valuable for *coverage* (dormant errors, queue drainage), not for hit rate.
- **Decide what to do about a 0%-precision furigana dimension.** Options the
  curator can weigh once there are more data: a consensus gate (require two
  models to agree before surfacing), tightening the screener prompt again, or
  retiring the screening dimension in favour of the deep furigana review only.
- **Treat the review queue as surveillance, not a convergence target** — or fund
  a cheaper bulk screening tier if true convergence is wanted. Its size is
  structural (every change re-enters it), not a backlog that polishing will burn
  down.

## How this page is maintained

`wiki`-mode Routine runs regenerate this page from the two ledgers when ≥10 new
`metrics-history.jsonl` lines have accumulated since the "Last updated" date
above (`prompts/routine2.md` §2, wiki row). Each refresh should: re-tabulate the
latest runs, recompute precision-by-dimension and by-source, and append a
`[pattern]` observation for any metric trending the wrong way (here: the
non-converging review queue). The per-dimension precision table is the headline
the curator uses to tune which reviewer dimensions to trust, gate, or retire.
