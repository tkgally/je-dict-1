# Quality Metrics Trend

**Last updated**: 2026-06-12 (second refresh: 34 runs spanning 3 days, 2,038 adjudicated flags)

## Overview

The Unified Improvement Routine v2 (`prompts/routine2.md`) instruments every run.
Two append-only ledgers accumulate a real time series instead of impressions:

- **`pipeline/metrics-history.jsonl`** — one line per Routine run (§5): mode,
  entries changed, flags applied/rejected/escalated, plus dictionary-wide
  counters (entry total, candidates, review-queue depth, observation lines,
  comprehensive-polish frontier, OpenRouter spend).
- **`reviews/decisions.jsonl`** — one line per *adjudicated external-model flag*
  (§C): the entry, the review source, the dimension, the severity, and the
  APPLY / REJECT / FLAG decision with a telegraphic note.

This page summarises both so the curator (and the weekly wiki review) can watch
two things that used to be anecdotal: **whether dictionary quality is improving**
and **how precise each external reviewer dimension is**. It is regenerated by the
`wiki` mode's metrics-trend activity whenever ≥10 new metrics lines have
accumulated since the last update.

> **Sample size.** This refresh covers **34 Routine runs (2026-06-10 through
> 2026-06-12) and 2,038 adjudicated flags**. A major policy change occurred on
> 2026-06-11 (curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 tags; out-of-taxonomy flags
> are now always applied). This makes the `tags` dimension pre-policy and
> post-policy numbers incomparable — see Findings §1. Per-dimension precision is
> more durable than daily counts as the sample grows. A second notable event on
> 2026-06-12: one accuracy-review run covering entries 03301–03800 hit a 54.6%
> flag rate (well above the 20% noise threshold), driven by the reviewer flagging
> `general` tags as "too vague" despite the standing reject-narrow/broad rule —
> see Findings §8 and Tooling Backlog item 17.

## Findings

### 1. Tags dimension: policy change split pre-/post-2026-06-11

The **51.2%** all-time tags apply rate is not comparable across time. On 2026-06-10
(pre-policy), the tags apply rate was **~6.8%** (6 applied of ~88 total, driven by
the accuracy-review sweep over entries 00001–00650). On 2026-06-11, after the
curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 established tags and mandated that
out-of-taxonomy flags are always applied, the apply rate jumped to
**~45–100% depending on source**: the new-entries §4 self-check applied all 16 tag
flags; the accuracy-review run (entries 1651–2086) applied 70 of 95 tag flags.

This is the policy working correctly, not noise. Every out-of-taxonomy tag is now
a migration candidate that must be corrected — the question is no longer "does this
tag exist in the dictionary somewhere?" (it may) but "is it in VALID_SEMANTIC?".
See [Schema Tag Reliability](schema-tag-reliability.md) → "The tag-vocabulary
contradiction and its resolution."

**Implication**: Watch for tags apply rate *falling* as the P20 migration backlog
is drained. Once most entries carry in-taxonomy tags, the tags dimension will
revert to a lower apply rate (genuine semantic mismatches only, as in the
pre-expansion era). A sustained high apply rate after the P20 backlog is exhausted
would suggest either systematic new-entry creation with wrong tags or the reviewer
is over-flagging valid tags.

**Update (2026-06-12)**: The 54.6% flag rate on range 03301–03800 (Finding §8)
suggests a new noise source is emerging as the P20 backlog is drained: the `general`
tag false-positive family. Watch for the tags apply rate declining while the
total-flags-per-run stays high — that would confirm the noise is swamping the signal.

### 2. Self-check (§4) is the most precise instrument at 45.1% all-time

Reviewing exactly the entries a run just created or modified concentrates the model
on the small, coherent set where fresh errors actually live. The all-time apply rate
(45.1%) is now higher than the accuracy sweep (36.9%), confirming §4 as the primary
quality gate. In the first 18 runs: 34.3%; in runs 19–34: 74.0% — the improvement
likely reflects both more accurate content edits and better reviewer calibration.

Both roles remain deliberate: §4 catches errors at birth; the sweep provides
whole-dictionary surveillance for dormant errors.

### 3. Accuracy sweep apply rate improved from 17.6% to 36.9% all-time

The day-1 measurement (1.5%) was driven by pre-policy runs where out-of-taxonomy
tag flags were routinely rejected. Post-policy, the sweep's all-time apply rate is
36.9%. In runs 19–34 alone it reached 60.0%, driven by the tag-migration pipeline
(P20 backlog drain) producing high-confidence applies on entire entry cohorts.
The gloss and translation dimensions also improved: prompt v3 (2026-06-11)
suppressed the "too narrow/too broad" and ambiguous-formality noise families,
leaving a higher-precision residual (gloss: 7.6%→53.7%; translation: 6.8%→63.6%).

### 4. Furigana screening precision is effectively noise (0.5% all-time)

3 of 573 furigana/screening flags were applied across 34 runs. The screening model
over-flags correct partial-okurigana readings, cross-reference index strings,
and rendaku in compounds — none of these are errors. On format-only systemic-fix
batches the screener still serves as a **regression check** (a clean screen confirms
the edit introduced nothing), but its flags on untouched furigana should be bulk-rejected
without detailed review.

### 5. Review queue convergence is accelerating

Review queue trajectory: 19,607 peak (2026-06-10) → 18,684 (end of runs 1–18) →
17,347 (end of run 34). **Net −2,260 over 3 days**, with 2026-06-12 showing the
largest single-day drop (−1,004). Three accuracy-review runs on 2026-06-12 drained
238 entries from the queue between them.

At the current rate (~−850 per accuracy-review-heavy day), the queue will reach the
structural floor (inflow ≈ outflow) in ~20 days of sustained operation. The
structural dynamic: every new or changed entry re-enters the queue, so the floor is
~(new-entries-per-day + polished-per-day) ≈ 40–60 entries/day; net −1,000/day
comfortably outpaces that inflow.

### 6. OpenRouter daily spend remains within cap; 2026-06-11 was the peak day

Spend history: $0.831 (2026-06-10), $1.038 (2026-06-11, peak), $0.686 (2026-06-12
so far). The 2026-06-11 peak was driven by a high-volume accuracy-review run (entries
2100–2800, 675 flags). No day has approached the $5/day cap. If three or more
accuracy-review runs land in a single day, spend could exceed $1.50 — monitor then.

### 7. Ledger source-value inconsistency in early entries

62 flags (7% of the first 874) in the decisions ledger use `src: "accuracy-review"`
rather than the §C-mandated `src: "accuracy"`. These are all from the 2026-06-10
accuracy-review run (session 001) before the exact-value rule was enforced. The file
is append-only and cannot be retroactively corrected. Precision statistics above
combine both spellings; future entries should use `accuracy` consistently.

Additionally, some screening-pass entries in runs 19–34 use `src: "screening"` or
`src: "furigana-screening"` instead of `src: "furigana"`. These are combined under
"furigana/screening" in the tables above. The `§C` standard is `src: "furigana"`.

### 8. New finding: `general`-tag false-positive noise emerging in accuracy sweep

The 2026-06-12 18:31 accuracy-review run (entries 03301–03800) generated a 54.6%
flag rate (273 flags on 500 entries) — far above the 20% noise threshold. The primary
noise family: the reviewer model flags any entry tagged `general` as needing a more
specific tag, even though `general` is a valid `VALID_SEMANTIC` tag and the standing
adjudication rule (§A) is to reject "too narrow/too broad" substitutions between
in-list tags. One bulk-reject line covered 180 flags from this family alone.

A secondary noise family: `formal` tags on words without explicit contradictions in
the entry's notes — many are genuine applies, but the reviewer infers neutrality from
frequency rather than reading the entry.

This is a **growing problem**: as the P20 migration backlog is drained, the remaining
tag flags will increasingly be `general`-noise, reducing the effective apply rate and
raising the per-run adjudication burden. Tooling Backlog item 17 proposes adding
explicit exceptions to the reviewer prompt for `general`, `descriptive`, `action`,
and `expression` tags. Until that fix ships, bulk-reject the `general`-noise family
in one aggregated §C line per run rather than reading each flag individually.

## Implications for je-dict-1

- **Weight reviewer dimensions by precision, with a policy-change awareness.** The
  `tags` dimension is highest-yield while P20 migration is ongoing. Post-P20,
  expect apply rate to fall; if it stays high, the `general`-noise problem (Finding
  §8 / Tooling Backlog item 17) will be the dominant cause. `gloss`/`translation`
  precision has improved sharply (14–15% apply rate all-time, 54–64% in the post-v3
  period); apply on clear errors. `furigana` screening is near-noise (0.5%).
- **§4 self-check is the primary quality gate.** At 45.1% all-time (74% post-v3) it
  is the most precise instrument and nearly free. The accuracy sweep provides
  whole-dictionary surveillance (coverage), not hit rate.
- **Review queue is converging reliably.** At −2,260 net over 3 days (and −1,004
  on 2026-06-12 alone), the queue is heading toward a structural floor. No action
  needed unless a day shows net growth.
- **Fix the `general`-tag noise before the P20 backlog is exhausted.** Once out-of-
  taxonomy migrations are done, the `general`-noise family will dominate the tags
  dimension and inflate per-run adjudication effort. Ship Tooling Backlog item 17
  (reviewer prompt exception for fallback tags) before that transition.
- **Ledger consistency**: combine `accuracy` and `accuracy-review` source values
  when computing precision statistics (62 early non-standard entries). Similarly
  combine `furigana`, `screening`, and `furigana-screening`.

## How this page is maintained

`wiki`-mode Routine runs regenerate this page from the two ledgers when ≥10 new
`metrics-history.jsonl` lines have accumulated since the "Last updated" date
above (`prompts/routine2.md` §2, wiki row). Each refresh should: re-tabulate the
latest runs, recompute precision-by-dimension and by-source, and append a
`[pattern]` observation for any metric trending the wrong way. The per-dimension
precision table is the headline the curator uses to tune which reviewer dimensions
to trust, gate, or retire.
