# Quality Metrics Trend

**Last updated**: 2026-06-11 (first refresh: 18 runs spanning 2 days, 874 adjudicated flags)

## Overview

The Unified Improvement Routine v2 (`prompts/routine2.md`) instruments every run.
Two append-only ledgers accumulate a real time series instead of impressions:

- **`pipeline/metrics-history.jsonl`** — one line per Routine run (§5): mode,
  entries changed, flags applied/rejected/escalated, plus dictionary-wide
  counters (entry total, candidates, review-queue depth, observation lines,
  comprehensive-polish frontier, OpenRouter spend).
- **`reviews/decisions.jsonl`** — one line per *adjudicated external-model flag*
  (§C): the entry, the review source, the dimension, the severity, and the
  APPLY / REJECT / FLAG decision with a telegraphic note.

This page summarises both so the curator (and the weekly wiki review) can watch
two things that used to be anecdotal: **whether dictionary quality is improving**
and **how precise each external reviewer dimension is**. It is regenerated by the
`wiki` mode's metrics-trend activity whenever ≥10 new metrics lines have
accumulated since the last update.

> **Sample size.** This refresh covers **18 Routine runs (2026-06-10 through
> 2026-06-11) and 874 adjudicated flags**. A major policy change occurred on
> 2026-06-11 (curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 tags; out-of-taxonomy flags
> are now always applied). This makes the `tags` dimension pre-policy and
> post-policy numbers incomparable — see Findings §1. Per-dimension precision is
> more durable than daily counts as the sample grows.

## Findings

### 1. Tags dimension: policy change split pre-/post-2026-06-11

The **35.8%** all-time tags apply rate is not comparable across time. On 2026-06-10
(pre-policy), the tags apply rate was **~6.8%** (6 applied of ~88 total, driven by
the accuracy-review sweep over entries 00001–00650). On 2026-06-11, after the
curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 established tags and mandated that
out-of-taxonomy flags are always applied, the apply rate jumped to
**~45–100% depending on source**: the new-entries §4 self-check applied all 16 tag
flags; the accuracy-review run (entries 1651–2086) applied 70 of 95 tag flags.

This is the policy working correctly, not noise. Every out-of-taxonomy tag is now
a migration candidate that must be corrected — the question is no longer "does this
tag exist in the dictionary somewhere?" (it may) but "is it in VALID_SEMANTIC?".
See [Schema Tag Reliability](schema-tag-reliability.md) → "The tag-vocabulary
contradiction and its resolution."

**Implication**: Watch for tags apply rate *falling* as the P20 migration backlog
is drained. Once most entries carry in-taxonomy tags, the tags dimension will
revert to a lower apply rate (genuine semantic mismatches only, as in the
pre-expansion era). A sustained high apply rate after the P20 backlog is exhausted
would suggest either systematic new-entry creation with wrong tags or the reviewer
is over-flagging valid tags.

### 2. Self-check (§4) remains the most precise instrument at 34.3%

Reviewing exactly the entries a run just created or modified concentrates the model
on the small, coherent set where fresh errors actually live. This is ~2× more
precise than the whole-dictionary accuracy sweep (17.6%). Both roles are deliberate:
§4 catches errors at birth; the sweep is surveillance for dormant errors.

### 3. Accuracy sweep apply rate improved from 1.5% to 17.6%

The day-1 measurement (1.5%) was driven by pre-policy runs where out-of-taxonomy
tag flags were routinely rejected. Post-policy, the sweep's apply rate is more
representative. The 2026-06-10 15:54 polish run's high applies (41) came from an
accuracy-review pass over the 1201–1650 range (41 tag-migration applies and a few
content fixes).

### 4. Furigana screening precision remains very low (2.9%)

1 of 34 furigana flags was applied across 18 runs. Consistent with the standing
calibration finding: the screening model over-flags correct partial-okurigana
readings (果|は in 果てる, 跳|は, 上|あ) and occasionally hallucinates a kanji. On
format-only systemic-fix batches the screener still serves as a **regression check**
— a clean screen confirms the edit introduced nothing — but its flags on untouched
furigana are near-noise.

### 5. Review queue is now converging (reversal of day-1 finding)

The first snapshot (day 1) reported "the review queue is not converging" — net −152
in one day. With 18 runs the picture has changed: the queue is at **18,684**, down
from its peak of **19,607** on 2026-06-10 — a net −923 over two days. Two large
accuracy-review runs on 2026-06-11 each drained ~400–500 entries from the queue.

The queue converges when accuracy-review runs are frequent; it re-grows when other
modes dominate. The structural dynamic (every new/changed entry re-enters the queue)
remains, but the ~1-in-5-runs frequency for accuracy-review is sufficient for
gradual net convergence. At the current rate (~−460 per accuracy-review run, ~2 such
runs per day) the queue would reach zero in roughly 20 days of uninterrupted
operation — though in practice it will plateau once the new-entry and polishing
inflow balances the review outflow.

### 6. OpenRouter spend is well within the $5/day cap

Peak daily spend was $0.831 (2026-06-10) and $0.757 so far on 2026-06-11.
Accuracy-review dominates (~$0.5/run); self-checks cost pennies each. The cap has
not been approached. If the accuracy-review run frequency increases the spend would
need monitoring.

### 7. Ledger source-value inconsistency in early entries

62 flags (7%) in the decisions ledger use `src: "accuracy-review"` rather than the
§C-mandated `src: "accuracy"`. These are all from the 2026-06-10 accuracy-review run
(session 001) before the exact-value rule was enforced. The file is append-only and
cannot be retroactively corrected. Precision statistics above combine both spellings;
future entries should use `accuracy` consistently.

## Implications for je-dict-1

- **Weight reviewer dimensions by precision, with a policy-change awareness.** The
  `tags` dimension is now the highest-yield dimension by design (always-apply for
  out-of-taxonomy tags). After the P20 migration backlog is exhausted, expect tags
  apply rate to fall; a sustained high rate after that point warrants investigation.
  `gloss`/`translation` (6–8%) remain: apply only on a clear error. `furigana`
  screening remains near-noise.
- **§4 self-check is the primary quality gate.** At 34.3% it is ~2× more precise
  than the sweep and nearly free. The sweep provides coverage (surveillance), not
  hit rate.
- **Review queue convergence is real but fragile.** It depends on accuracy-review
  run frequency relative to new-entry and polishing volume. Watch for the queue
  stabilising or growing again as a signal to increase accuracy-review scheduling
  weight.
- **Fix the 62 early non-standard `accuracy-review` src values.** Because the ledger
  is append-only this is purely documentary — precision statistics that segment by
  source should combine `accuracy` and `accuracy-review` until a bulk of new `accuracy`
  entries dwarfs the 62 early outliers.

## How this page is maintained

`wiki`-mode Routine runs regenerate this page from the two ledgers when ≥10 new
`metrics-history.jsonl` lines have accumulated since the "Last updated" date
above (`prompts/routine2.md` §2, wiki row). Each refresh should: re-tabulate the
latest runs, recompute precision-by-dimension and by-source, and append a
`[pattern]` observation for any metric trending the wrong way. The per-dimension
precision table is the headline the curator uses to tune which reviewer dimensions
to trust, gate, or retire.
