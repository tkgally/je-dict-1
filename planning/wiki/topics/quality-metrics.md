# Quality Metrics Trend

**Last updated**: 2026-06-16 (fourth refresh: 60 runs spanning 7 days, 3,167 adjudicated flags)

## Overview

The Unified Improvement Routine v2 (`prompts/routine2.md`) instruments every run.
Two append-only ledgers accumulate a real time series instead of impressions:

- **`pipeline/metrics-history.jsonl`** — one line per Routine run (§5): mode,
  entries changed, flags applied/rejected/escalated, plus dictionary-wide
  counters (entry total, candidates, review-queue depth, observation lines,
  comprehensive-polish frontier, OpenRouter spend).
- **`reviews/decisions.jsonl`** — one line per *adjudicated external-model flag*
  (§C): the entry, the review source, the dimension, the severity, and the
  APPLY / REJECT / FLAG decision with a telegraphic note.

This page summarises both so the curator (and the weekly wiki review) can watch
two things that used to be anecdotal: **whether dictionary quality is improving**
and **how precise each external reviewer dimension is**. It is regenerated by the
`wiki` mode's metrics-trend activity whenever ≥10 new metrics lines have
accumulated since the last update.

> **Sample size.** This refresh covers **60 Routine runs (2026-06-10 through
> 2026-06-16) and 3,167 adjudicated flags**. A major policy change occurred on
> 2026-06-11 (curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 tags; out-of-taxonomy flags
> are now always applied). This makes the `tags` dimension pre-policy and
> post-policy numbers incomparable — see Findings §1. Per-dimension precision is
> more durable than daily counts as the sample grows. **The headline of this
> refresh: the `tags` apply rate did *not* keep collapsing — it rebounded to
> 23.0% in runs 47–60 (vs. 8.2% in runs 35–46)** because two of the three
> accuracy-review sweeps this period landed on ranges with genuine wrong-category
> tag errors (60.6% and 38.6% apply), while only one was the now-familiar
> `general`-noise-dominated sweep (9.3% apply). So the runs 35–46 collapse was
> partly range-specific; across more runs the `tags` dimension is settling into a
> low-20s apply rate, still split between genuine wrong-domain catches and the
> `general`/narrowness noise family (Finding §8 / Tooling Backlog item 17, now
> confirmed continuous up to entry 05703). The review queue kept converging
> (−524 over the period, **−3,714 from peak**), and OpenRouter spend stayed low
> (≤$0.82/day, never near the $5 cap).

## Findings

### 1. Tags dimension: policy change split pre-/post-2026-06-11

The **51.2%** all-time tags apply rate is not comparable across time. On 2026-06-10
(pre-policy), the tags apply rate was **~6.8%** (6 applied of ~88 total, driven by
the accuracy-review sweep over entries 00001–00650). On 2026-06-11, after the
curator expanded VALID_SEMANTIC with 30 established tags and mandated that
out-of-taxonomy flags are always applied, the apply rate jumped to
**~45–100% depending on source**: the new-entries §4 self-check applied all 16 tag
flags; the accuracy-review run (entries 1651–2086) applied 70 of 95 tag flags.

This is the policy working correctly, not noise. Every out-of-taxonomy tag is now
a migration candidate that must be corrected — the question is no longer "does this
tag exist in the dictionary somewhere?" (it may) but "is it in VALID_SEMANTIC?".
See [Schema Tag Reliability](schema-tag-reliability.md) → "The tag-vocabulary
contradiction and its resolution."

**Implication**: Watch for tags apply rate *falling* as the P20 migration backlog
is drained. Once most entries carry in-taxonomy tags, the tags dimension will
revert to a lower apply rate (genuine semantic mismatches only, as in the
pre-expansion era). A sustained high apply rate after the P20 backlog is exhausted
would suggest either systematic new-entry creation with wrong tags or the reviewer
is over-flagging valid tags.

**Update (2026-06-12)**: The 54.6% flag rate on range 03301–03800 (Finding §8)
suggests a new noise source is emerging as the P20 backlog is drained: the `general`
tag false-positive family. Watch for the tags apply rate declining while the
total-flags-per-run stays high — that would confirm the noise is swamping the signal.

**Update (2026-06-14) — prediction confirmed**: In runs 35–46 the tags apply rate
fell to **8.2%** (46 applied of 562) while tag flags per accuracy-review run stayed
high (312, 227, and 73 rejections in three runs). This is exactly the
declining-apply-rate-with-high-volume signature flagged above: the P20 migration
backlog is largely drained, so the remaining tag flags are dominated by the
`general`-too-broad noise family rather than genuine errors. The all-time tags apply
rate dropped from 51.2% to **35.4%** as a result. **The tags dimension has now
transitioned from "highest-yield" to "highest-noise"; suppressing the `general`-noise
at the prompt (Tooling Backlog item 17) is now the binding constraint on accuracy-review
adjudication cost.**

**Update (2026-06-16) — the collapse was range-specific, not terminal**: Runs 47–60
brought the tags apply rate back up to **23.0%** (79 applied of 343). The runs 35–46
trough (8.2%) turned out to reflect *which ranges were swept*, not a permanent floor:
two of this period's three accuracy-review sweeps hit ranges with genuine wrong-category
errors (60.6% and 38.6% apply), while only one was the `general`-noise case (9.3%). The
all-time tags rate is now **33.1%**. The practical reading is unchanged — the
`general`/narrowness noise still dominates *polished* ranges and must be suppressed at
the prompt (item 17, now confirmed continuous up to entry 05703) — but the dimension
still earns its keep on un-polished ranges, so it should not be retired. Expect the
per-sweep apply rate to keep oscillating with range polish-state until item 17 ships and
the un-reviewed backlog is exhausted.

### 2. Self-check (§4) is the most precise instrument at 49.3% all-time

Reviewing exactly the entries a run just created or modified concentrates the model
on the small, coherent set where fresh errors actually live. The all-time apply rate
(49.6%) remains well above the accuracy sweep (26.0%); §4 ran at 50.0% in runs 47–60,
its steadiest reading yet. §4 is the clear primary quality gate; the sweep is
surveillance.

Both roles remain deliberate: §4 catches errors at birth; the sweep provides
whole-dictionary surveillance for dormant errors.

### 3. Accuracy sweep apply rate peaked at 60% mid-migration, now ~26% all-time

The day-1 measurement (1.5%) reflected pre-policy runs that rejected out-of-taxonomy
flags. The sweep then peaked at 60.0% in runs 19–34, driven by the P20 tag-migration
pipeline producing high-confidence applies on entire cohorts. With the migration
largely drained, the sweep fell to 6.1% in runs 35–46 then recovered to 19.9% in runs
47–60 on genuine-catch ranges, leaving the all-time rate at **26.0%**. This is the
expected lifecycle of a migration: a burst of cheap, correct
applies followed by a long tail of noise once the backlog is gone. Gloss and translation
settled at a stable 14–15% all-time after the post-v3 spike — a more durable estimate
of their true precision than the runs 19–34 peak (53–64%).

### 4. Furigana screening precision is effectively noise (1.8% all-time)

12 of 670 furigana/screening flags were applied across 60 runs (5 of 43 in runs
47–60 — a small uptick on a few genuine catches, but still near-noise). The screening model
over-flags correct partial-okurigana readings, cross-reference index strings,
and rendaku in compounds — none of these are errors. On format-only systemic-fix
batches the screener still serves as a **regression check** (a clean screen confirms
the edit introduced nothing), but its flags on untouched furigana should be bulk-rejected
without detailed review.

### 5. Review queue convergence continues steadily

Review queue trajectory: 19,607 peak (2026-06-10) → 17,347 (end of run 34) →
16,417 (end of run 46) → **15,893 (end of run 60)**. **Net −3,714 from peak over 7
days**; the newest period (runs 47–60) drained another −524. The pace has eased from
the −1,004 single-day peak on 2026-06-12 to roughly −110 to −600 per day depending on
how many accuracy-review runs land, and the queue now wobbles slightly upward between
sweeps as CI re-adds changed entries (e.g. 06-15 09:27→12:22 ticked 16,009→16,019), but
the multi-day direction is unbroken — no day has shown net queue growth.

At the current rate the queue will reach the structural floor (inflow ≈ outflow) within
a few weeks of sustained operation. The structural dynamic is unchanged: every new or
changed entry re-enters the queue, so the floor is ~(new-entries-per-day +
polished-per-day) ≈ 40–60 entries/day; the accuracy-review outflow still comfortably
outpaces that inflow on review-heavy days.

### 6. OpenRouter daily spend remains within cap; 2026-06-11 was the peak day

Spend history: $0.831 (2026-06-10), $1.038 (2026-06-11, peak), $0.696 (2026-06-12),

### 7. Ledger source-value inconsistency in early entries

62 flags (7% of the first 874) in the decisions ledger use `src: "accuracy-review"`
rather than the §C-mandated `src: "accuracy"`. These are all from the 2026-06-10
accuracy-review run (session 001) before the exact-value rule was enforced. The file
is append-only and cannot be retroactively corrected. Precision statistics above
combine both spellings; future entries should use `accuracy` consistently.

Additionally, some screening-pass entries in runs 19–34 use `src: "screening"` or
`src: "furigana-screening"` instead of `src: "furigana"`. These are combined under
"furigana/screening" in the tables above. The `§C` standard is `src: "furigana"`.

`self-check` when a `session` field is present, else `accuracy`). The tables above
normalise all of these. The lesson is the same: §C's "use exactly these lowercase
field names and values" rule exists precisely so the precision stats stay countable
without per-refresh normalisation glue — adherence has improved but is not yet perfect.

### 8. New finding: `general`-tag false-positive noise emerging in accuracy sweep

The 2026-06-12 18:31 accuracy-review run (entries 03301–03800) generated a 54.6%
flag rate (273 flags on 500 entries) — far above the 20% noise threshold. The primary
noise family: the reviewer model flags any entry tagged `general` as needing a more
specific tag, even though `general` is a valid `VALID_SEMANTIC` tag and the standing
adjudication rule (§A) is to reject "too narrow/too broad" substitutions between
in-list tags. One bulk-reject line covered 180 flags from this family alone.

A secondary noise family: `formal` tags on words without explicit contradictions in
the entry's notes — many are genuine applies, but the reviewer infers neutrality from
frequency rather than reading the entry.

This is a **growing problem**: as the P20 migration backlog is drained, the remaining
tag flags will increasingly be `general`-noise, reducing the effective apply rate and
raising the per-run adjudication burden. Tooling Backlog item 17 proposes adding
explicit exceptions to the reviewer prompt for `general`, `descriptive`, `action`,
and `expression` tags. Until that fix ships, bulk-reject the `general`-noise family
in one aggregated §C line per run rather than reading each flag individually.

**Update (2026-06-14) — now confirmed and worsening**: Runs 35–46 made this the
dominant tag-flag pattern (Finding §1 update): three accuracy-review runs rejected 312,
227, and 73 flags respectively, the bulk of them `general`-too-broad. The 2026-06-14
4801–4982 run added a new wrinkle — the noise is now emitted at **`error` severity**
(73 error-level tag flags, only 13 genuine), which defeats the "work every
error-severity flag individually" triage rule and forces full adjudication on known
noise. Tooling Backlog item 17's 2026-06-14 update therefore proposes not only the
fallback-tag exception but an explicit **severity rule**: a more-specific in-list tag
existing is never an `error`. This is the single highest-leverage reviewer-prompt fix
outstanding.

## Implications for je-dict-1

- **Weight reviewer dimensions by precision, with range-state awareness.** The
  `tags` dimension's P20-migration window has closed, but its apply rate did not settle
  at the 8.2% trough (runs 35–46) — it rebounded to **23.0%** (runs 47–60) and sits at
  **33.1%** all-time, oscillating with whether the swept range has been polished
  (genuine wrong-domain catches on un-polished ranges; `general`-noise on polished ones,
  Finding §1/§8). Do not retire it; suppress its noise at the prompt (item 17) and let
  it keep working the un-reviewed backlog. `gloss` and `translation` hold a stable
  **13–15%** all-time — the most durable apply-on-clear-errors dimensions. `furigana`
  screening remains near-noise (1.8%).
- **§4 self-check is the primary quality gate.** At 49.3% all-time (70% in runs 35–46)
  it is the most precise instrument and nearly free. The accuracy sweep provides
  whole-dictionary surveillance (coverage), not hit rate — and its hit rate has fallen
  to 6.1% in the latest period as `general`-noise dominates.
- **Review queue is converging reliably.** At −3,714 net from peak over 7 days, the
  queue is heading toward a structural floor. No action needed unless a day shows net
  growth.
- **Fix the `general`-tag noise before the P20 backlog is exhausted.** Once out-of-
  taxonomy migrations are done, the `general`-noise family will dominate the tags
  dimension and inflate per-run adjudication effort. Ship Tooling Backlog item 17
  (reviewer prompt exception for fallback tags) before that transition.
- **Ledger consistency**: combine `accuracy` and `accuracy-review` source values
  when computing precision statistics (62 early non-standard entries). Similarly
  combine `furigana`, `screening`, and `furigana-screening`.
