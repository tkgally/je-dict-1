1. gargling
The act of rinsing the mouth and throat with liquid.
うがいをする。
To gargle.
帰(かえ)ったら手洗(てあら)いとうがいをしなさい。
Wash your hands and gargle when you get home.
うがい薬(ぐすり)を使(つか)う。
To use gargling medicine.
風邪(かぜ)の予防(よぼう)にはうがいが大切(たいせつ)です。
Gargling is important for preventing colds.
喉(のど)が痛(いた)いので、塩水(しおみず)でうがいをしている。
My throat hurts, so I've been gargling with salt water.

うがい refers to gargling — rinsing the throat with water or medicated liquid, usually for hygiene or to prevent illness.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive)
  • No standard transitive pair
  • Pattern: うがいする

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • うがいをする (to gargle)
  • うがい薬(ぐすり) (gargling medicine / medicated mouthwash)
  • 手洗(てあら)いとうがい (handwashing and gargling)

CULTURAL NOTE:
Gargling is strongly emphasized as a daily health practice in Japan, especially during cold and flu season. The phrase 手洗(てあら)い・うがい (handwashing and gargling) is taught from early childhood and appears on signs in schools and public facilities.

ETYMOLOGY:
The word comes from the cormorant (鵜(う)), referring to the way cormorants hold fish in their throat during 鵜飼(うか)い (cormorant fishing).
