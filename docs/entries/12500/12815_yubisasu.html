指(ゆび)さす

ゆびさす
godan verb
to point at; to indicate with one's finger
1. to point at, to indicate with one's finger
To extend one's finger toward a person, object, or direction to indicate it. Can also be used figuratively to mean singling someone out for criticism.
地図(ちず)を指(ゆび)さして説明(せつめい)した。
I pointed at the map and explained.
子供(こども)が空(そら)の虹(にじ)を指(ゆび)さした。
The child pointed at the rainbow in the sky.
人(ひと)を指(ゆび)さすのは失礼(しつれい)だと言(い)われている。
It is said to be rude to point at people.

USAGE:
Also written as 指差(ゆびさ)す or 指(ゆび)を差(さ)す. In Japanese culture, pointing directly at a person is considered impolite, so the expression 人(ひと)を指(ゆび)さす often carries a negative nuance, implying singling someone out or publicly criticizing them.

CONJUGATION:

Godan verb (五段動詞(ごだんどうし)), す-ending.

  • Negative: 指(ゆび)ささない
  • Polite: 指(ゆび)さします
  • Te-form: 指(ゆび)さして
  • Past: 指(ゆび)さした

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 人(ひと)を指(ゆび)さす: to point at a person (often negative)
  • 方向(ほうこう)を指(ゆび)さす: to point in a direction
  • 後(うし)ろ指(ゆび)をさされる: to be talked about behind one's back (set expression)
