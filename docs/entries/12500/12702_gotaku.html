御託(ごたく)

ごたく
noun
tedious talk, pretentious excuses
1. tedious talk, pretentious excuses, long-winded complaints
Self-important or tiresome talk, especially complaints, excuses, or justifications that others find annoying. Almost always used in the negative expression 御託を並べる (to spout tedious excuses).
御託(ごたく)を並(なら)べるな。
Stop making excuses.
彼(かれ)はいつも御託(ごたく)ばかり並(なら)べている。
He's always spouting tedious excuses.
御託(ごたく)を並(なら)べていないで、さっさと仕事(しごと)を始(はじ)めなさい。
Stop making excuses and get to work already.

USAGE:
Almost exclusively used in the set phrase 御託(ごたく)を並(なら)べる (to line up pretentious talk = to make tedious excuses or complaints). The word carries a dismissive, impatient tone toward what someone is saying. Originally from Buddhist terminology referring to oracles or divine pronouncements (御託宣(ごたくせん)), the modern usage is ironic — treating someone's talk as if they think their words are as important as divine proclamations.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 御託(ごたく)を並(なら)べる: to spout excuses, to go on and on
  • 御託(ごたく)はいい: enough with the excuses
