失(う)せる

うせる
ichidan verb
to vanish, to disappear; to get lost (rude imperative)
1. to vanish, to disappear
Describes something disappearing or ceasing to exist. Often used for abstract things like feelings, motivation, or interest fading away.
やる気(き)が失(う)せた。
I lost my motivation.
彼女(かのじょ)の態度(たいど)を見(み)て、食欲(しょくよく)が失(う)せた。
Seeing her attitude, I lost my appetite.
あまりにも長(なが)い会議(かいぎ)で、集中力(しゅうちゅうりょく)が完全(かんぜん)に失(う)せてしまった。
The meeting was so long that I completely lost my ability to concentrate.
2. to get lost, to go away (rude command)
Used in the imperative form 失せろ as a harsh way to tell someone to leave. Strongly rude and confrontational.
失(う)せろ！
Get lost!
「二度(にど)と俺(おれ)の前(まえ)に現(あらわ)れるな、失(う)せろ」と怒鳴(どな)った。
He shouted, "Don't ever appear before me again, get lost!"
邪魔(じゃま)だ、さっさと失(う)せろと言(い)わんばかりの顔(かお)をされた。
He gave me a look as if to say, "You're in the way, get lost now."

失(う)せる is an intransitive verb with two distinct uses.

SENSE 1 — VANISHING:

Used when feelings, qualities, or abstract things disappear. Commonly paired with:

  • やる気(き)が失(う)せる (lose motivation)
  • 食欲(しょくよく)が失(う)せる (lose one's appetite)
  • 興味(きょうみ)が失(う)せる (lose interest)

SENSE 2 — RUDE IMPERATIVE:
The command form 失(う)せろ is a very harsh way to tell someone to go away. It appears frequently in fiction, manga, and arguments but should be avoided in real-life situations.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 消(き)える: neutral term for vanishing or disappearing (physical or abstract)
  • 失(う)せる: more literary or emphatic for abstract things; also has the rude imperative use
