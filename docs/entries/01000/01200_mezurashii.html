1. rare, uncommon
Describes something that is not often seen or encountered.
これは珍(めずら)しい花(はな)ですね。
This is a rare flower, isn't it?
この鳥(とり)は日本(にほん)では珍(めずら)しい。
This bird is rare in Japan.
珍(めずら)しい名前(なまえ)ですね。
That's an unusual name.
珍(めずら)しい本(ほん)を見(み)つけた。
I found a rare book.
この地域(ちいき)では珍(めずら)しい動物(どうぶつ)が何種類(なんしゅるい)も生息(せいそく)している。
Several rare animal species live in this region.
2. unusual, surprising
Describes an unexpected occurrence, often used when someone acts out of character.
珍(めずら)しく早起(はやお)きした。
Unusually, I woke up early.
Adverbial form
彼(かれ)が料理(りょうり)するなんて珍(めずら)しい。
It's unusual for him to cook.
Expressing surprise at unexpected behavior
今日(きょう)は珍(めずら)しく雨(あめ)だ。
It's unusually rainy today.
珍(めずら)しく遅刻(ちこく)した。
Unusually, I was late.
お父(とう)さんが料理(りょうり)するのは珍(めずら)しい。
It's unusual for Dad to cook.
3. novel, curious
Describes something new and interesting that attracts attention.
外国(がいこく)の珍(めずら)しいお土産(みやげ)をもらった。
I received an unusual/exotic souvenir from abroad.
Modifier usage
珍(めずら)しいものを見(み)てみたい。
I want to see something novel.
珍(めずら)しい料理(りょうり)を食(た)べた。
I ate an unusual dish.
珍(めずら)しい体験(たいけん)をした。
I had a novel experience.
子供(こども)たちは珍(めずら)しそうに見(み)ていた。
The children were watching curiously.
珍(めずら)しいデザインの服(ふく)。
Clothes with a novel design.

珍(めずら)しい is an i-adjective meaning 'rare,' 'unusual,' or 'novel.' The three senses shade into one another: sense 1 is about low frequency in the world, sense 2 about an event departing from what is expected of a particular person or situation, and sense 3 about something being new and eye-catching to the speaker.

Forms:

  • Adverbial 珍(めずら)しく: modifies the whole event, not a noun — 'unusually, [it happened that]...' It is the normal way to say that someone acted out of character, as in 珍(めずら)しく早(はや)く起(お)きた 'unusually, I got up early.'
  • 珍(めずら)しそうに: 'as if [it were] something novel' — describes the looker, not the thing looked at (sense 3).
  • Noun form 珍(めずら)しさ: 'rarity, novelty.'

Usage pattern: often a predicate expressing surprise — 〜するなんて珍(めずら)しい, 'It's unusual for [someone] to ~.' The のは珍(めずら)しい version (ex11) is the neutral equivalent; the なんて version adds a note of astonishment.

Similar and contrasting words:

  • 貴重(きちょう): also 'rare,' but it foregrounds value — a 貴重(きちょう)な体験(たいけん) is one worth having, a 珍(めずら)しい体験(たいけん) merely an uncommon one.
  • 普通(ふつう) is the everyday opposite: what is 普通(ふつう) is the norm that 珍(めずら)しい departs from.

Note: unlike English 'rare,' 珍(めずら)しい is never used for meat doneness. For a steak, use レア.
