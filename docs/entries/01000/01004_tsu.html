1. general counter (1-9)
A native Japanese counter that can be used for various objects, especially when the specific counter is unknown. Only used for numbers 1-9.
一(ひと)つください。
Please give me one.
りんごを三(みっ)つください。
Please give me three apples.
問題(もんだい)が二(ふた)つあります。
There are two problems.
部屋(へや)には窓(まど)が四(よっ)つあります。
There are four windows in the room.
お菓子(かし)が八(やっ)つありましたが、五(いつ)つは子供(こども)にあげました。三(みっ)つ残(のこ)りました。
There were eight sweets, but I gave five to the children. Three remained.

つ is the most versatile Japanese counter. It uses native Japanese (やまとことば) numbers and can count almost anything when you don't know the specific counter. It only works for numbers 1-9.

COUNTING FORMS:

  • 一(ひと)つ (1), 二(ふた)つ (2), 三(みっ)つ (3)
  • 四(よっ)つ (4), 五(いつ)つ (5), 六(むっ)つ (6)
  • 七(なな)つ (7), 八(やっ)つ (8), 九(ここの)つ (9)

LIMITATIONS:

  • No form for 10 or above. For 10, use 十(とお). For higher numbers, use specific counters or the Chinese-derived number system.
  • Cannot count people (use 人(にん)) or long thin objects (use 本(ほん)).
  • For small discrete objects, 個(こ) is the common alternative and has no upper limit.

ASKING HOW MANY:
Use 幾(いく)つ (how many?) to ask for a count in this series. The same word asks a small child's age.

AGE OF YOUNG CHILDREN:
つ also counts years of age up to nine: 三(みっ)つの子(こ) means "a three-year-old child." From ten on, use 歳(さい).

USAGE:
This is the safest counter to use when unsure which specific counter applies. It sounds natural for small objects, abstract things, and items on a list.
