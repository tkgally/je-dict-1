どんどん

どんどん
adverb
rapidly; steadily; one after another
1. rapidly, steadily, at a fast pace
Describes continuous and rapid progress or change.
日本語(にほんご)がどんどん上手(じょうず)になっている。
Your Japanese is getting better and better.
Describing progress
仕事(しごと)がどんどん増(ふ)えていく。
Work keeps piling up.
技術(ぎじゅつ)がどんどん進(すす)んでいる。
Technology is advancing rapidly.
気温(きおん)がどんどん上(あ)がっている。
The temperature is rising steadily.
時間(じかん)がどんどん過(す)ぎていく。
Time keeps passing quickly.
2. one after another, in succession
Indicates things happening continuously without pause.
人(ひと)がどんどん入(はい)ってきた。
People kept coming in one after another.
Continuous action
客(きゃく)がどんどん来(き)た。
Customers kept coming one after another.
注文(ちゅうもん)がどんどん入(はい)る。
Orders keep coming in.
問題(もんだい)がどんどん出(で)てくる。
Problems keep coming up one after another.
新(あたら)しい店(みせ)がどんどんできて、町(まち)が変(か)わっていく。
New shops keep opening one after another, and the town is changing.
3. without hesitation, boldly
Encourages doing something without holding back.
どんどん食(た)べてください。
Please help yourself. / Keep eating!
Encouraging without restraint
質問(しつもん)があったらどんどん聞(き)いてください。
If you have questions, please ask freely.
Encouraging participation
どんどん使(つか)ってください。
Please use it freely.
どんどん挑戦(ちょうせん)してください。
Please keep challenging yourself.
どんどん言(い)ってください。
Please speak up freely.
遠慮(えんりょ)しないでどんどん取(と)ってください。
Don't hold back, help yourself.

どんどん is an onomatopoeic adverb describing rapid, continuous action or change. All three senses share the idea of momentum with nothing holding it back — a process that keeps going (sense 1), items arriving without pause (sense 2), or a listener told not to hold back (sense 3).

Position: usually immediately before the verb it modifies.

Onomatopoeia origin: originally mimics the sound of drums (ドンドン), extended to mean 'rapidly' or 'continuously.'

Similar expressions:

  • どんどん vs. だんだん: だんだん means 'gradually' (slow, incremental change); どんどん means 'rapidly.' Both fit 上手(じょうず)になる, but they describe different rates of improvement.
  • どんどん vs. 徐々(じょじょ)に: 徐々(じょじょ)に is the written-style equivalent of だんだん and likewise implies slowness.
  • どんどん vs. どしどし: both encourage action, but どしどし is more emphatic and casual, and is largely limited to that encouraging use.
  • どんどん vs. 次々(つぎつぎ)に: 次々(つぎつぎ)に specifically means 'one after another' and says nothing about speed; どんどん emphasizes the pace.

Common usage:

  • どんどん〜てください: encouragement to do something freely, without holding back. This is a warm, hospitable formula, the natural counterpart of telling a guest not to 遠慮(えんりょ).
  • どんどん〜ていく / どんどん〜ている: keep ~ing more and more; the 〜ていく version stresses that the change carries on into the future.

Orthography: usually written in hiragana.

Related Words

Contrast: だんだん (gradually)
Related: 次々(つぎつぎ) (one after another)
Synonym: どしどし (more emphatic)
