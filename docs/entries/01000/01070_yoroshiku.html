よろしく

よろしく
expression
please, best regards
1. nice to meet you, please treat me well
Used when meeting someone for the first time, often after はじめまして. Full form is よろしくお願(ねが)いします.
はじめまして。田中(たなか)です。よろしく。
Nice to meet you. I'm Tanaka. Pleased to meet you.
Casual introduction
よろしくお願(ねが)いします。
Thank you in advance. / Pleased to meet you.
Polite form
今日(きょう)からお世話(せわ)になります。よろしくお願(ねが)いします。
I'll be in your care from today. Nice to meet you.
Sense 1: meeting
新入社員(しんにゅうしゃいん)の佐藤(さとう)です。よろしくお願(ねが)いいたします。
I'm Sato, a new employee. Pleased to meet you.
Sense 1: formal introduction
これからもよろしく！
Looking forward to our future relationship!
Sense 1: casual
今後(こんご)ともよろしくお願(ねが)いします。
I look forward to working with you in the future.
Sense 1/2: ongoing relationship
2. please (do something for me)
Used when asking someone to do something or take care of something.
よろしくお願(ねが)いします。
Thank you in advance. / Pleased to meet you.
Polite form
この件(けん)はよろしくお願(ねが)いします。
Please take care of this matter.
来年(らいねん)もどうぞよろしくお願(ねが)いいたします。
I look forward to your continued support next year as well.
Often used at year-end
予約(よやく)の件(けん)、よろしくお願(ねが)いします。
Please take care of the reservation matter.
Sense 2: request
ご検討(ごけんとう)のほど、よろしくお願(ねが)いいたします。
I would appreciate your consideration.
Sense 2: business request
今後(こんご)ともよろしくお願(ねが)いします。
I look forward to working with you in the future.
Sense 1/2: ongoing relationship
3. give my regards to
Used when asking someone to pass along greetings.
山田(やまだ)さんによろしく伝(つた)えてください。
Please give my regards to Mr. Yamada.
お母(かあ)さんにもよろしく言(い)ってね。
Please give my regards to your mother too.
Sense 3: casual
皆様(みなさま)によろしくお伝(つた)えください。
Please give my regards to everyone.
Sense 3: polite
ご家族(ごかぞく)にもよろしく。
Please give my regards to your family as well.
Sense 3: regards
先輩(せんぱい)によろしくお伝(つた)えいただけますか。
Could you please give my regards to the senior?
Sense 3: polite request

よろしく is an extremely versatile expression. It comes from the adjective よろしい (good, fine). The polite form よろしくお願(ねが)いします is essential for business and formal situations. It can be used when meeting someone, ending an email, or asking for a favor.
