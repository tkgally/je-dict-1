チャーミング

ちゃーみんぐ
adjective-na
charming; attractive; cute
1. charming; attractive; cute; appealing
Having an attractive, appealing, or endearing quality — typically said of people (especially women or children), their facial expressions, their mannerisms, or their personalities. The word focuses on warmth and appeal rather than on formal beauty.
チャーミングな笑顔(えがお)ですね。
What a charming smile!
彼女(かのじょ)は明(あか)るくてチャーミングな人(ひと)だ。
She is a cheerful and charming person.
その女優(じょゆう)は年(とし)を取(と)ってもチャーミングな魅力(みりょく)を失(うしな)わなかった。
That actress did not lose her charming appeal even as she grew older.
彼(かれ)の少(すこ)しおどけた仕草(しぐさ)が、かえってチャーミングに感(かん)じられて、好感(こうかん)を持(も)った。
His slightly clownish mannerisms actually came across as charming, and I took a liking to him.

Loanword from English "charming." Used as a na-adjective: チャーミングな人(ひと) (a charming person), チャーミングだ (is charming).

USAGE:
チャーミング is used primarily to describe people — their appearance, smile, voice, personality, or small behaviors. It is almost never used for objects, landscapes, or abstract ideas. The connotation is warm and positive, suggesting someone is attractive not because of flashy beauty but because of a personal appeal: a nice smile, a friendly manner, or an endearing quirk.

The word is most often applied to women and children, though it can be used of men as well. It is considered a stylish, slightly old-fashioned compliment, sometimes carrying a hint of the 1960s–80s magazine vocabulary.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • チャーミングな笑顔(えがお): a charming smile
  • チャーミングな女性(じょせい): a charming woman
  • チャーミングな仕草(しぐさ): charming mannerisms
  • チャーミングに見(み)える: to look charming

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 可愛(かわい)い: cute, pretty — broader, used for people, animals, and objects; more everyday
  • 魅力的(みりょくてき)な: attractive, appealing — more formal and serious; can describe people, ideas, or things
  • 愛嬌(あいきょう)のある: having charm/warmth — very similar in feeling, native Japanese equivalent
  • キュート: cute — another loanword, more childlike/pop than チャーミング
