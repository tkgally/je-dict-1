友好的(ゆうこうてき)

ゆうこうてき
na-adjective
friendly; amicable
1. friendly; amicable; on good terms
Describes an atmosphere, attitude, or relationship characterized by goodwill and cooperation rather than hostility. Often used in formal settings — diplomacy, business, and public statements — to describe relations between countries, companies, or groups. Carries a more measured, official tone than the everyday word 親切(しんせつ) ('kind').
友好的(ゆうこうてき)な関係(かんけい)。
A friendly relationship.
両国(りょうこく)は友好的(ゆうこうてき)な雰囲気(ふんいき)の中(なか)で会談(かいだん)を行(おこな)った。
The two countries held talks in a friendly atmosphere.
交渉(こうしょう)は友好的(ゆうこうてき)に進(すす)み、最終的(さいしゅうてき)に合意(ごうい)に達(たっ)した。
The negotiations proceeded amicably and finally reached an agreement.

Compound of 友好(ゆうこう) ('friendship; amity') and the adjectival suffix 〜的(てき). The related noun 友好(ゆうこう) is itself common in compounds like 友好(ゆうこう)関係(かんけい) ('friendly relations'), 友好(ゆうこう)国(こく) ('friendly nation'), 日米(にちべい)友好(ゆうこう) ('Japan-US friendship').

USAGE:

  • Used as a な-adjective: 友好的(ゆうこうてき)な態度(たいど) ('a friendly attitude').
  • Very common adverbially in official reports: 友好的(ゆうこうてき)に解決(かいけつ)する ('to resolve amicably').
  • Has a formal, public-facing feel. For personal friendliness between individuals, 親(した)しい ('close') or 仲(なか)がいい ('getting along well') are more natural.
  • Often paired with nouns of relationship or interaction: 関係(かんけい), 雰囲気(ふんいき), 交流(こうりゅう).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 友好的(ゆうこうてき)な関係(かんけい): friendly relationship
  • 友好的(ゆうこうてき)な雰囲気(ふんいき): friendly atmosphere
  • 友好的(ゆうこうてき)な態度(たいど): friendly attitude
  • 友好的(ゆうこうてき)に解決(かいけつ)する: to resolve amicably
  • 友好的(ゆうこうてき)に接(せっ)する: to treat in a friendly manner

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 敵対的(てきたいてき): hostile — the common antonym, especially in diplomatic and business contexts.
  • 協力的(きょうりょくてき): cooperative — focuses on willingness to work together rather than on warmth of feeling.
  • 親密(しんみつ): intimate; close — used for personal closeness rather than polite friendliness.
  • 好意的(こういてき): favorable; well-disposed — emphasizes a positive appraisal rather than mutual friendliness.
