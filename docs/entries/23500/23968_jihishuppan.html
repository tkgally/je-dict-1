1. self-publishing; publishing at one's own expense
Publishing a book by paying the production and distribution costs oneself rather than through a commercial publisher's normal contract. In Japan the term covers both author-funded arrangements with specialty 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん) companies and fully independent self-publishing. Historically carried a vanity-publishing connotation, though digital platforms have broadened its image.
小説(しょうせつ)を自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)する。
To self-publish a novel.
祖父(そふ)の俳句集(はいくしゅう)を自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)した。
I self-published a collection of my grandfather's haiku.
最近(さいきん)は電子書籍(でんししょせき)として自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)する作家(さっか)が増(ふ)えている。
Recently, more and more authors are self-publishing as e-books.

Compound of 自費(じひ) ('one's own expense') and 出版(しゅっぱん) ('publication'). Contrasted with 商業(しょうぎょう)出版(しゅっぱん) ('commercial publishing'), in which the publisher bears the costs.

USAGE:

  • Used as a noun and with する to form a verb: 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)する ('to self-publish').
  • Historically tied to paying a subsidy press; in current usage increasingly covers print-on-demand and e-book self-publishing.
  • Can describe the book itself: 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)の本(ほん) ('a self-published book').
  • Common for memoirs, poetry collections, local histories, and niche academic works that commercial publishers will not take on.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)する: to self-publish
  • 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)の本(ほん): a self-published book
  • 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)社(しゃ): self-publishing house
  • 電子書籍(でんししょせき)で自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)する: to self-publish as an e-book
  • 自費(じひ)出版(しゅっぱん)費用(ひよう): self-publishing costs

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 商業(しょうぎょう)出版(しゅっぱん): commercial publishing — the standard publisher-funded model; the opposite pole.
  • 同人誌(どうじんし): doujinshi; self-published fan/hobbyist publication — overlaps in 'not from a major publisher', but implies small-circle, fan, or independent creative culture.
  • 個人出版(こじんしゅっぱん): personal/individual publishing — a newer, more neutral term used especially for digital self-publishing.
  • 自主(じしゅ)制作(せいさく): self-production — broader, covering music, film, and other creative works made without a commercial backer.
