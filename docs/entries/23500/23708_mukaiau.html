1. to face each other; to sit / stand opposite one another
For two or more people or things to be positioned facing one another directly. Also used figuratively for confronting or dealing squarely with a person, problem, or one's own feelings.
二人(ふたり)が向(む)かい合(あ)う。
The two face each other.
机(つくえ)を向(む)かい合(あ)わせに並(なら)べた。
We arranged the desks facing each other.
家族(かぞく)全員(ぜんいん)が食卓(しょくたく)で向(む)かい合(あ)って夕食(ゆうしょく)を食(た)べた。
The whole family sat around the dinner table facing each other and ate dinner.
自分(じぶん)の弱(よわ)さと正面(しょうめん)から向(む)かい合(あ)うことは、誰(だれ)にとっても簡単(かんたん)ではない。
Confronting one's own weakness head-on is not easy for anyone.

A compound verb formed from 向(む)かう (to face, to head toward) and 合(あ)う (the reciprocal auxiliary suffix 'to do mutually'). The reciprocal nuance is essential: one-sided facing is 向(む)かう, while mutual facing is 向(む)かい合(あ)う.

USAGE:

  • Physical meaning: two people, tables, buildings, etc. positioned facing each other. Subjects are typically plural or joined by と.
  • Figurative meaning: facing up to or directly engaging with a problem, emotion, person, or one's past. Takes the object with と or と+正面(しょうめん)から.
  • The て}-form {向(む)かい合(あ)って is very common as an adverbial: 向(む)かい合(あ)って話(はな)す (to talk face to face).
  • 向(む)かい合(あ)わせ (the noun form with -aせ ending) means 'opposite each other (in arrangement)'.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Intransitive. Partners are marked with と, or both parties appear as the joint subject.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 正面(しょうめん)から向(む)かい合(あ)う: to face (something) head-on
  • 問題(もんだい)と向(む)かい合(あ)う: to confront a problem
  • 自分(じぶん)自身(じしん)と向(む)かい合(あ)う: to face oneself
  • 向(む)かい合(あ)って座(すわ)る: to sit facing each other

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 向(む)かう: to head toward / face — one-directional, not reciprocal
  • 対(たい)する: to face / oppose — more abstract, often in formal or literary contexts
  • 対峙(たいじ)する: to confront / face off — stronger, often adversarial
  • 見(み)つめ合(あ)う: to gaze at each other — emphasizes eye contact rather than physical position
