押(お)し入(い)り

おしいり
noun
breaking and entering; home invasion; burglary (using force)
1. breaking and entering; home invasion; forced-entry robbery
Forced entry into a residence or business, typically to commit robbery, often while the occupants are inside. Also refers by extension to the person who does this. A standard term in crime news reports.
押(お)し入(い)りが発生(はっせい)した。
A break-in occurred.
深夜(しんや)、住宅(じゅうたく)街(がい)で押(お)し入(い)り事件(じけん)があった。
Late at night, there was a home invasion in the residential neighborhood.
警察(けいさつ)は近隣(きんりん)で相次(あいつ)いで発生(はっせい)している押(お)し入(い)り強盗(ごうとう)の犯人(はんにん)を捜査(そうさ)している。
The police are investigating the suspect behind the string of home-invasion robberies in the area.

Noun formed from the verb 押(お)し入(い)る ('to force one's way into'). Frequently appears in compounds and news headlines describing forced-entry crimes. Unlike a quiet burglary, 押(お)し入(い)り implies aggressive or violent entry, often with occupants present.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 押(お)し入(い)り強盗(ごうとう): home-invasion robbery (very common in news)
  • 押(お)し入(い)り事件(じけん): break-in incident
  • 押(お)し入(い)り犯(はん): the perpetrator of a break-in
  • 押(お)し入(い)りにあう: to be the victim of a break-in
  • 押(お)し入(い)りが発生(はっせい)する: a break-in occurs

RELATED TERMS:

  • 押(お)し入(い)る: to force one's way in (the base verb)
  • 強盗(ごうとう): robbery; robber — the broader crime category
  • 空(あ)き巣(す): burglary when the house is empty — contrasts with 押(お)し入(い)り, which often happens with occupants home
  • 侵入(しんにゅう): intrusion, unlawful entry — more general; includes non-violent trespass
  • 不法(ふほう)侵入(しんにゅう): illegal entry; trespassing

USAGE:
Standard term in crime reporting. In news stories it is usually followed by 強盗(ごうとう) or 事件(じけん). In casual speech, 強盗(ごうとう) alone is often enough; 押(お)し入(い)り emphasizes the forced, aggressive nature of the entry.
