紅色(べにいろ)

べにいろ
noun
crimson; deep red; rouge red
1. crimson; deep red; the red of safflower dye (beni)
A warm, vivid red with slight orange undertones — the traditional color of 紅(べに) ('rouge'), made from safflower petals and long used as a dye, lipstick, and pigment. Associated with Japanese aesthetic tradition, kimono, and the ink used on hanging scrolls and seals.
紅色(べにいろ)の花(はな)。
A crimson flower.
夕日(ゆうひ)で空(そら)が紅色(べにいろ)に染(そ)まった。
The sky was dyed crimson by the setting sun.
彼女(かのじょ)は紅色(べにいろ)の着物(きもの)を着(き)て祭(まつ)りに出(で)かけた。
She wore a crimson kimono and set out for the festival.
秋(あき)になると山(やま)のもみじが鮮(あざ)やかな紅色(べにいろ)に変(か)わる。
In autumn, the maple leaves in the mountains turn a vivid crimson.

Compound of 紅(べに) ('rouge; safflower dye; deep red') and 色(いろ) ('color'). The 紅(べに) was historically produced from safflower petals (紅花(べにばな)) and used as a cosmetic and dye since ancient times, so 紅色(べにいろ) evokes a traditional, slightly warm red rather than a pure primary red.

USAGE:
Used as both a noun (紅色(べにいろ)の〜 = 'crimson ~') and with の to modify other nouns. More evocative than 赤(あか) ('red'); often chosen for poetic, literary, or aesthetically refined descriptions — autumn leaves, sunsets, kimono, flowers, lips.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 紅色(べにいろ)の花(はな): a crimson flower
  • 紅色(べにいろ)に染(そ)まる: to be dyed crimson
  • 紅色(べにいろ)の紅葉(こうよう): crimson autumn leaves
  • 紅色(べにいろ)の口紅(くちべに): crimson lipstick
  • 鮮(あざ)やかな紅色(べにいろ): a vivid crimson

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 赤(あか): red — the basic color term, covers a wide range
  • 真(ま)っ赤(か): bright/deep red — vivid but not necessarily warm
  • 朱色(しゅいろ): vermillion; cinnabar red — more orange, used for torii gates and seals
  • 茜色(あかねいろ): madder red — deep red of sunset, literary
  • 臙脂(えんじ): dark red; wine red — darker and more subdued than 紅色(べにいろ)

CULTURAL NOTE:
紅(べに) has a rich cultural history in Japan. Traditional cosmetics (紅(べに) lipstick), dyed kimono fabric, and ink for personal seals (朱肉(しゅにく)) all draw on red tones. 紅白(こうはく) ('red and white') is a culturally significant color pair used for celebrations.
