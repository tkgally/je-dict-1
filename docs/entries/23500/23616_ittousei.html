一等星(いっとうせい)

いっとうせい
noun
first-magnitude star
1. first-magnitude star; brightest-class star
In astronomy, a star of the first magnitude — one of the brightest stars visible to the naked eye. The magnitude scale assigns lower numbers to brighter stars, so a 一等星(いっとうせい) is in the top tier of brightness. Sirius, Vega, and Betelgeuse are familiar examples.
シリウスは有名(ゆうめい)な一等星(いっとうせい)だ。
Sirius is a famous first-magnitude star.
都会(とかい)でも一等星(いっとうせい)なら肉眼(にくがん)で見(み)える。
Even in the city, first-magnitude stars are visible to the naked eye.
夏(なつ)の夜空(よぞら)には三(みっ)つの一等星(いっとうせい)が大(おお)きな三角形(さんかっけい)を描(えが)いている。
In the summer night sky, three first-magnitude stars form a large triangle.
天体観測(てんたいかんそく)に慣(な)れていない人(ひと)は、まず一等星(いっとうせい)を見(み)つけることから始(はじ)めるとよい。
Those unfamiliar with stargazing should start by locating first-magnitude stars.

Composed of 一等(いっとう) (first class, first rank) and 星(せい) (star). An astronomical term classifying stars by their apparent brightness.

BACKGROUND:
The 等級(とうきゅう) (magnitude) scale originally classified visible stars into six ranks: first-magnitude stars being the brightest, sixth-magnitude stars being the faintest visible to the naked eye under dark skies. Modern astronomy uses a precise mathematical definition, but for casual skywatching the 一等星(いっとうせい) category still means 'the brightest stars in the sky.'

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 一等星(いっとうせい)を見(み)つける: to spot a first-magnitude star
  • 夏(なつ)/冬(ふゆ)の大三角(だいさんかく): the Summer / Winter Triangle (each connecting three 一等星(いっとうせい))
  • 肉眼(にくがん)で見(み)える一等星(いっとうせい): first-magnitude stars visible to the naked eye
  • 北半球(きたはんきゅう)の一等星(いっとうせい): first-magnitude stars of the northern hemisphere

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 二等星(にとうせい): second-magnitude star — the next tier down in brightness
  • 等級(とうきゅう): magnitude — the general astronomical brightness scale
  • 恒星(こうせい): fixed star — a true star (as opposed to a planet)
  • 天体(てんたい): celestial body — any object in the sky, including stars, planets, and galaxies
