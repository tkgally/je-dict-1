棒術(ぼうじゅつ)

ぼうじゅつ
noun
the art of the staff (a Japanese martial art)
1. bōjutsu; the martial art of fighting with a staff
A traditional Japanese martial art that uses a wooden staff, typically around 180 cm long (the 六(ろく)尺(しゃく)棒(ぼう), 'six-foot staff'), as its principal weapon. Techniques include strikes, thrusts, sweeps, and blocks, and 棒術(ぼうじゅつ) is one component of classical weapons training (古武道(こぶどう)), often taught alongside or within schools of 柔術(じゅうじゅつ) or 剣術(けんじゅつ). Also refers to the broader category of staff-based martial techniques.
棒術(ぼうじゅつ)を習(なら)う。
To learn bōjutsu.
祖父(そふ)は若(わか)いころ棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の道場(どうじょう)に通(かよ)っていた。
My grandfather attended a bōjutsu dojo when he was young.
沖縄(おきなわ)には古(ふる)くから独自(どくじ)の棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の流派(りゅうは)が伝(つた)わっている。
A unique school of bōjutsu has been passed down in Okinawa since ancient times.
演武会(えんぶかい)では、剣術(けんじゅつ)や柔術(じゅうじゅつ)だけでなく棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の型(かた)も披露(ひろう)された。
At the demonstration, not only swordsmanship and jūjutsu but also bōjutsu forms were performed.

Compound of 棒(ぼう) ('stick; staff; rod') and 術(じゅつ) ('technique; art'). The 〜術(じゅつ) suffix names many martial and practical arts: 剣術(けんじゅつ) ('swordsmanship'), 柔術(じゅうじゅつ) ('jūjutsu'), 忍術(にんじゅつ) ('ninjutsu'), 弓術(きゅうじゅつ) ('archery'), 馬術(ばじゅつ) ('horseback riding'), 手術(しゅじゅつ) ('surgery').

USAGE:

  • Treated as a noun; verbs include 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)を習(なら)う ('learn'), を練習(れんしゅう)する ('practice'), の稽古(けいこ)をする ('train in').
  • Does not take する directly.
  • Often specified by region or school: 沖縄(おきなわ)棒術(ぼうじゅつ) ('Okinawan bōjutsu'), 神道(しんとう)夢想(むそう)流(りゅう)棒術(ぼうじゅつ) ('Shintō Musō-ryū bōjutsu').

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)を習(なら)う: to learn bōjutsu
  • 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の稽古(けいこ): bōjutsu practice / training
  • 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の型(かた): bōjutsu forms / kata
  • 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の流派(りゅうは): school / tradition of bōjutsu
  • 棒術(ぼうじゅつ)の演武(えんぶ): bōjutsu demonstration
  • 沖縄(おきなわ)棒術(ぼうじゅつ): Okinawan bōjutsu

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 杖術(じょうじゅつ): jōjutsu — art of the shorter 杖(じょう) (around 128 cm); related but distinct weapon.
  • 剣術(けんじゅつ): swordsmanship — classical sword arts.
  • 槍術(そうじゅつ): spearmanship — uses a spear (槍(やり)), not a staff.
  • 薙刀術(なぎなたじゅつ): naginata arts — glaive-style pole weapon.
  • 古武道(こぶどう): classical Japanese martial arts — the broader category that includes 棒術(ぼうじゅつ).

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
棒術(ぼうじゅつ) developed in feudal Japan as both a battlefield skill and a self-defense art for travelers, monks, and commoners who were not permitted to carry swords. Okinawan 古武道(こぶどう), influenced by Chinese weapon traditions, preserves a particularly rich repertoire of staff techniques that complement karate training.
