1. condolence visit; call of condolence; visit of mourning
A formal visit to a bereaved family to express condolences after someone's death. In Japan, a 弔問(ちょうもん) visitor may come to the family's home before the funeral or to the funeral venue itself to offer incense, prayers, and sympathy to the family of the deceased.
弔問(ちょうもん)に伺(うかが)う。
To pay a visit of condolence.
弔問(ちょうもん)客(きゃく)が次々(つぎつぎ)と訪(おとず)れた。
Condolence visitors came one after another.
故人(こじん)のご自宅(じたく)に弔問(ちょうもん)するときは、地味(じみ)な服装(ふくそう)で伺(うかが)う。
When making a condolence visit to the deceased's home, you should go in plain, subdued clothing.
通夜(つや)や葬儀(そうぎ)に参列(さんれつ)できない場合(ばあい)は、後日(ごじつ)弔問(ちょうもん)に伺(うかが)うこともある。
If you cannot attend the wake or funeral, you may pay a condolence visit at a later date.

Formed from 弔(ちょう) (to mourn, to condole) + 問(もん) (to visit, to inquire). A formal term used in contexts of bereavement.

USAGE:

  • Can be used as a suru-verb: 弔問(ちょうもん)する (to pay a condolence visit). The verb 伺(うかが)う is also common in the phrase 弔問(ちょうもん)に伺(うかが)う.
  • Typical timing is either before the wake (通夜(つや)) at the bereaved family's home, or after the funeral if the mourner could not attend in person.
  • Visitors usually bring 香典(こうでん) (a monetary condolence offering) and wear subdued 略式(りゃくしき)喪服(もふく) (semi-formal mourning clothes) rather than the full black suit worn at the funeral itself.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 弔問(ちょうもん)に伺(うかが)う: to go / call to pay condolences (humble form)
  • 弔問(ちょうもん)客(きゃく): a mourner calling to offer condolences
  • 弔問(ちょうもん)に訪(おとず)れる: to visit in condolence
  • 弔問(ちょうもん)を受(う)ける: to receive condolence visitors
  • 弔問(ちょうもん)の手紙(てがみ): letter of condolence

RELATED TERMS:

  • 通夜(つや): wake — the evening gathering held before a funeral
  • 葬儀(そうぎ) / 葬式(そうしき): funeral ceremony
  • 香典(こうでん): condolence money given at a funeral
  • お悔(く)やみ: expression of condolence — the words spoken or written
  • 焼香(しょうこう): the burning of incense before the deceased's altar
