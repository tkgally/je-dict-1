滝壺(たきつぼ)

たきつぼ
noun
plunge pool; basin at the foot of a waterfall
1. plunge pool; basin formed at the base of a waterfall
The deep pool of water at the foot of a waterfall, carved out by the long-term impact of falling water. A standard vocabulary item in travel writing about scenic spots and in descriptions of river geography.
滝壺(たきつぼ)は深(ふか)そうだ。
The plunge pool looks deep.
青(あお)く澄(す)んだ滝壺(たきつぼ)に虹(にじ)がかかっていた。
A rainbow hung over the clear blue plunge pool.
滝壺(たきつぼ)は水(みず)の流(なが)れが急(きゅう)で危険(きけん)なので、絶対(ぜったい)に入(はい)ってはいけない。
The plunge pool has strong currents and is dangerous, so you must never go in.

Composed of 滝(たき) (waterfall) and 壺(つぼ) (pot, jar, hollow). The 壺(つぼ) element captures the image of a rounded basin carved out of the rock by the falling water. The word is vivid and concrete — a 滝(たき) is the waterfall itself, while the 滝壺(たきつぼ) is specifically the pool it lands in.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 滝壺(たきつぼ)に落(お)ちる: fall into a plunge pool
  • 滝壺(たきつぼ)をのぞき込(こ)む: peer into the plunge pool
  • 深(ふか)い滝壺(たきつぼ): a deep plunge pool
  • 澄(す)んだ滝壺(たきつぼ): a clear plunge pool
  • 滝壺(たきつぼ)巡(めぐ)り: waterfall(-pool) tour

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 滝(たき): waterfall — the falling water itself, not the pool
  • 淵(ふち): deep pool, depths (of a river) — a deep spot in a river generally, not specifically at a waterfall
  • 瀬(せ): shallows, rapids — the opposite of 淵(ふち); shallow or fast-flowing water
  • 池(いけ): pond — a standing body of water, usually artificial or natural but not formed by a waterfall
