焼(や)き目(め)

やきめ
noun
browned surface; sear (on cooked food)
1. browned surface; sear marks; golden-brown color on grilled or fried food
The browned, slightly charred color and surface texture that develops on food when it is grilled, seared, or fried at high heat. A key element of both appearance and flavor in Japanese cooking, especially for fish, meat, and grilled vegetables.
魚(さかな)にきれいな焼(や)き目(め)がついた。
The fish got a nice sear on it.
両面(りょうめん)にしっかり焼(や)き目(め)をつけてから煮(に)込(こ)む。
Sear the surface firmly on both sides, then simmer.
強火(つよび)で短時間(たんじかん)焼(や)くと、香(こう)ばしい焼(や)き目(め)がついて肉汁(にくじゅう)を閉(と)じ込(こ)めることができる。
Searing briefly over high heat gives a fragrant browned surface and seals in the meat's juices.

Formed from 焼(や)く ('to grill, bake, roast') + 目(め) ('mark, look, appearance'). A common term in recipes and cooking shows, often paired with the verbs つく ('to form, develop') and つける ('to develop intentionally').

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 焼(や)き目(め)がつく: a sear forms
  • 焼(や)き目(め)をつける: to sear; to develop a browned surface
  • きれいな焼(や)き目(め): a nicely browned surface
  • 香(こう)ばしい焼(や)き目(め): a fragrant sear
  • 両面(りょうめん)に焼(や)き目(め)をつける: to sear both sides
  • 焼(や)き目(め)がつくまで焼(や)く: to cook until browned

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • こんがり: nicely browned (adverb) — describes the ideal level of browning, often in こんがり}{焼(や)く
  • 焦(こ)げ目(め): char marks — stronger, darker browning; closer to 'charred' than 'seared'
  • 焦(こ)げ: burn; charred part — food that has burned, usually unwanted
  • 香(こう)ばしさ: fragrant, toasty aroma — the pleasant smell that accompanies browning

USAGE:
焼(や)き目(め) is generally positive, suggesting attractive, appetizing browning. 焦(こ)げ目(め) is darker and can lean negative, and 焦(こ)げ is clearly negative (burnt).
