1. self-sufficiency; meeting one's own needs; being content with what one has
Supplying one's own needs or being satisfied with what one already has — without seeking more from outside. Most commonly seen in the four-character compound 自給自足(じきゅうじそく) (self-sufficiency).
自給(じきゅう)自足(じそく)の生活(せいかつ)。
A self-sufficient lifestyle.
小(ちい)さな畑(はたけ)で野菜(やさい)を自足(じそく)している。
We supply our own vegetables from a small field.
現代(げんだい)の都市(とし)生活(せいかつ)では、食料(しょくりょう)を自足(じそく)することはほぼ不可能(ふかのう)だ。
In modern urban life, it is nearly impossible to be self-sufficient in food.
彼(かれ)らは山(やま)の中(なか)で、電気(でんき)も水道(すいどう)も使(つか)わずに自足(じそく)の暮(く)らしを続(つづ)けている。
In the mountains, they continue a self-sufficient life without using electricity or running water.

Composed of 自(じ) (self) and 足(そく) (be sufficient, suffice). Refers to meeting one's own needs or being satisfied with what one has. In modern Japanese, 自足(じそく) rarely appears on its own — it is most commonly encountered as part of the four-character idiom 自給自足(じきゅうじそく) (self-sufficiency: producing and consuming one's own goods).

USAGE:

  • As a noun: 自足(じそく)の生活(せいかつ) (a self-sufficient life)
  • As a suru-verb: 野菜(やさい)を自足(じそく)する (to supply one's own vegetables)
  • Often combined: 自給(じきゅう)自足(じそく) (the standard idiomatic form)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 自給(じきゅう)自足(じそく): self-sufficiency (idiomatic four-character compound)
  • 自足(じそく)の生活(せいかつ): a self-sufficient life
  • 自足(じそく)できない: cannot supply one's own / cannot be satisfied
  • 食料(しょくりょう)を自足(じそく)する: to meet one's own food needs

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 自給(じきゅう): self-supply — the more common half of 自給自足(じきゅうじそく); emphasizes production
  • 独立(どくりつ): independence — broader; includes political and emotional independence
  • 自立(じりつ): independence, standing on one's own — focuses on personal autonomy rather than supplying one's needs
  • 満足(まんぞく): satisfaction, contentment — emphasizes feeling, not supply
