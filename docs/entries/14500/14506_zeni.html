銭(ぜに)

ぜに
noun
money; coin; cash (colloquial/old-fashioned)
1. money; coin; cash
An informal, somewhat old-fashioned or rough word for money or coins. Has a blunt, down-to-earth feel compared to the neutral okane (money).
銭(ぜに)がない。
I have no money.
銭(ぜに)を稼(かせ)ぐのは大変(たいへん)だ。
Making money is hard work.
あの男(おとこ)は銭(ぜに)のことしか頭(あたま)にないから、信用(しんよう)できない。
That man only thinks about money, so you can't trust him.

An old-fashioned, blunt word for money. In modern Japanese, お金(かね) is the standard neutral term, while 銭(ぜに) has a rougher, more colloquial tone. It can sound gruff, masculine, or old-fashioned.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 銭(ぜに)を稼(かせ)ぐ (to earn money)
  • 銭(ぜに)勘定(かんじょう) (counting money; being stingy)
  • 銭(ぜに)もうけ (making money; moneymaking)
  • びた一文(いちもん)の銭(ぜに)も (not a single cent)

Also appears as a unit of currency (銭(せん) = 1/100 of a yen), though this reading is distinct. The reading ぜに is specifically the colloquial word for money/coins. Common in period dramas, proverbs, and expressions with a traditional flavor.
