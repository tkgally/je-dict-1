1. to push into
To forcefully push something or someone into a space.
かばんに本(ほん)を押(お)し入(い)れた。
I shoved the book into my bag.
荷物(にもつ)を無理(むり)やり押(お)し入(い)れた。
I forcibly pushed the luggage in.
服(ふく)を引(ひ)き出(だ)しに押(お)し入(い)れた。
I shoved the clothes into the drawer.
満員(まんいん)の電車(でんしゃ)に体(からだ)を押(お)し入(い)れて、なんとか乗(の)った。
I squeezed my body into the packed train and managed to get on.
スーツケースに土産(みやげ)を全部(ぜんぶ)押(お)し入(い)れるのは大変(たいへん)だった。
It was hard to shove all the souvenirs into the suitcase.
2. to break into, to force one's way in
To enter a place by force, especially illegally.
強盗(ごうとう)が家(いえ)に押(お)し入(い)れた。
Robbers broke into the house.
群衆(ぐんしゅう)が建物(たてもの)に押(お)し入(い)れた。
The crowd forced their way into the building.
泥棒(どろぼう)が窓(まど)から押(お)し入(い)れた。
The thief broke in through the window.
犯人(はんにん)は裏口(うらぐち)から店(みせ)に押(お)し入(い)れたらしい。
The culprit apparently broke into the store through the back door.
深夜(しんや)に見知(みし)らぬ男(おとこ)が家(いえ)に押(お)し入(い)れてきて、警察(けいさつ)を呼(よ)んだ。
A stranger forced his way into the house late at night, and we called the police.

押(お)し入(い)れる is a compound verb from 押(お)す (to push) + 入(い)れる (to put in). It indicates forceful entry or insertion.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive) for pushing something in
  • Can describe a person forcing their way in

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 無理(むり)に押(お)し入(い)れる: to force in
  • 部屋(へや)に押(お)し入(い)る: to force one's way into a room
  • 荷物(にもつ)を押(お)し入(い)れる: to shove luggage in

CONFUSION NOTE:
Do not confuse with 押(お)し入(い)れ (おしいれ), which is a noun meaning a Japanese-style closet — a built-in storage space with sliding doors, typically found in 和室(わしつ) (Japanese-style rooms).

RELATED:

  • 押(お)し込(こ)む: to push/cram in (similar meaning)
  • 押(お)し入(い)る (intransitive): to force one's way in
