床(とこ)の間(ま)

とこのま
noun
alcove in Japanese room
1. alcove, recessed area in traditional Japanese room
A recessed area in a Japanese-style room where art, flowers, or scrolls are displayed.
床(とこ)の間(ま)のある和室(わしつ)。
A Japanese-style room with an alcove.
床(とこ)の間(ま)に掛(か)け軸(じく)を飾(かざ)る。
To display a hanging scroll in the alcove.
床(とこ)の間(ま)には花(はな)が生(い)けてある。
Flowers are arranged in the alcove.

CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The 床(とこ)の間(ま) is a raised alcove in a traditional Japanese room (和室(わしつ)), considered the spiritual and aesthetic focal point of the room.

TRADITIONAL DISPLAYS:

  • 掛(か)け軸(じく): hanging scroll
  • 生(い)け花(ばな): flower arrangement
  • 置物(おきもの): decorative objects

ETIQUETTE:
The seat of honor (上座(かみざ)) is closest to the 床(とこ)の間(ま). Guests are traditionally seated with their back to it as a sign of humility, while hosts offer the position facing it.

Note: Modern homes rarely have 床(とこ)の間(ま), but they remain important in traditional inns (旅館(りょかん)) and tea rooms.
