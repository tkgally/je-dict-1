1. to withdraw, to step back
To move back from a position; to retreat.
一歩(いっぽ)引(ひ)き下(さ)がった。
I stepped back one step.
一歩(いっぽ)引(ひ)き下(さ)がって考(かんが)える。
I step back and think about it.
危険(きけん)を感(かん)じて、少(すこ)し引(ひ)き下(さ)がった。
I sensed danger and stepped back a little.
2. to back down, to give in
To abandon one's position in an argument or negotiation.
ここは引(ひ)き下(さ)がった方(ほう)がいい。
It's better to back down here.
相手(あいて)が強硬(きょうこう)だったので引(ひ)き下(さ)がった。
I backed down because the other party was firm.
これ以上(いじょう)言(い)っても無駄(むだ)だと思(おも)って、引(ひ)き下(さ)がることにした。
I decided to back down, thinking it was pointless to say any more.

引(ひ)き下(さ)がる means to withdraw or step back, either physically or figuratively (backing down in an argument).

COMPOUND VERB:
引(ひ)く (to pull) + 下(さ)がる (to go down/back)

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive)
  • Transitive pair: 引(ひ)き下(さ)げる (to pull down, to lower)

ASPECT (ている):
引(ひ)き下(さ)がっている describes the state of having withdrawn or stepped back.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 一歩(いっぽ)引(ひ)き下(さ)がる: to step back (literal or figurative)
  • ここは引(ひ)き下(さ)がった方(ほう)がいい: it's better to back down here
  • 引(ひ)き下(さ)がることにする: to decide to back down

USAGE:
Often used in negotiations, arguments, or competitive situations where someone chooses not to press forward. Also used physically for stepping back from danger.

Related Words

Contrast: 引(ひ)き返(かえ)す (to turn back)
