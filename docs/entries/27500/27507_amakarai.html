1. sweet and salty; sweet-and-savory
A characteristic Japanese flavor profile combining sweetness (often from sugar or mirin) with the savory saltiness of soy sauce. Typical of teriyaki, simmered dishes, and many sauces — not "sweet and spicy" despite the literal kanji.
この味(あじ)は甘辛(あまから)い。
This has a sweet-and-savory flavor.
甘辛(あまから)いタレを肉(にく)にかけた。
I poured the sweet-and-savory sauce over the meat.
甘辛(あまから)い味付(あじつ)けは白(しろ)ご飯(はん)との相性(あいしょう)が抜群(ばつぐん)だ。
A sweet-and-savory seasoning goes exceptionally well with plain rice.

Despite containing 辛(から)い (spicy/salty), this word almost always refers to the soy-sauce-and-sugar flavor of Japanese home cooking, not chili heat. Think teriyaki, sukiyaki broth, or simmered fish.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 甘辛(あまから)いタレ: sweet-and-savory sauce
  • 甘辛(あまから)い味付(あじつ)け: sweet-and-savory seasoning
  • 甘辛(あまから)い煮物(にもの): sweet-and-savory simmered dish

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 甘辛(あまから): the noun form, used in compounds like 甘辛(あまから)煮(に) (sweet-savory simmered dish)
  • 甘酸(あまず)っぱい: sweet and sour — fruity tartness; very different flavor
  • 辛(から)い: salty; spicy — covers both "hot" and "salty" in Japanese, but context usually makes it clear
