1. to hire; to take on; to employ
To formally bring someone on as a worker or staff member. More deliberate and formal than plain 雇(やと)う, often used of a company taking on new personnel.
新(あたら)しい社員(しゃいん)を雇(やと)い入(い)れる。
We will take on a new employee.
会社(かいしゃ)は若(わか)い人材(じんざい)を雇(やと)い入(い)れた。
The company took on young talent.
忙(いそが)しい時期(じき)に備(そな)えて、パートを何人(なんにん)か雇(やと)い入(い)れることにした。
We decided to take on several part-timers in preparation for the busy season.

Compound of 雇(やと)う (to employ) + 入(い)れる (to take in). The 入(い)れる element stresses bringing the person into the organization, so it suits formal hiring of staff.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 社員(しゃいん)を雇(やと)い入(い)れる: to take on an employee
  • 人材(じんざい)を雇(やと)い入(い)れる: to bring on talent
  • 新(あら)たに雇(やと)い入(い)れる: to newly hire

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 雇(やと)う: to employ; to hire — the plain, everyday verb; 雇(やと)い入(い)れる sounds more formal and organizational
