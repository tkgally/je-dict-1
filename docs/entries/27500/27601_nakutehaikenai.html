なくてはいけない

なくてはいけない
expression
must; have to
1. must; have to; need to
A pattern expressing necessity or obligation, very common in spoken Japanese. Attaches to the negative te-form of verbs (〜(〜)なくて) plus はいけない. Literally "if not (doing it), it won't do."
薬(くすり)を飲(の)まなくてはいけない。
I have to take my medicine.
明日(あした)は早(はや)く起(お)きなくてはいけない。
Tomorrow I have to get up early.
試験(しけん)に合格(ごうかく)するためには、もっと勉強(べんきょう)しなくてはいけないと思(おも)う。
I think I have to study more in order to pass the exam.

Formed from the negative te-form (e.g. 飲(の)まなくて) + はいけない. Used for personal obligations, social rules, and situational necessity. Slightly more conversational in feel than なければならない.

FORMATION:

  • Verbs: plain negative → drop ない → add なくてはいけない (行(い)く → 行(い)かなくてはいけない)
  • I-adjectives: drop い → add くなくてはいけない
  • Nouns / Na-adjectives: + でなくてはいけない

CASUAL CONTRACTIONS:

  • なくては → なくちゃ (行(い)かなくちゃ)
  • なくてはいけない → なくちゃいけない / なくちゃ

NUANCE:

  • なくてはいけない: "must," often felt as a personal or local obligation
  • なくてはならない: "must," slightly more formal; describes what is generally required
  • なければならない: "must," the most written-style of the group

REGISTER:
Very common in spoken Japanese, including in polite conversation. The contracted form なくちゃ is everyday casual and frequent in conversation between friends or in self-talk.
