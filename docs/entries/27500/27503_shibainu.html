柴犬(しばいぬ)

しばいぬ
noun
Shiba Inu (dog breed)
1. Shiba Inu; Shiba (Japanese dog breed)
A small-to-medium Japanese spitz breed with a curled tail and a thick double coat, originally bred for hunting in mountainous terrain. The most popular native Japanese breed kept as a pet, and one of the country's six recognized indigenous dog breeds.
柴犬(しばいぬ)を飼(か)っている。
I have a Shiba Inu.
隣(となり)の柴犬(しばいぬ)は毎朝(まいあさ)散歩(さんぽ)している。
The neighbor's Shiba goes for a walk every morning.
柴犬(しばいぬ)は忠実(ちゅうじつ)で警戒心(けいかいしん)が強(つよ)く、番犬(ばんけん)としても人気(にんき)がある。
Shibas are loyal and alert, and are popular as watchdogs too.

Also read しばけん, though しばいぬ is more common in everyday speech. The name 柴(しば) refers to brushwood — the dog's traditional reddish-tan coat resembles dried brush. Often just called 柴(しば) for short.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 柴犬(しばいぬ)を飼(か)う: to have/raise a Shiba
  • 柴犬(しばいぬ)の子犬(こいぬ): Shiba puppy
  • 豆柴(まめしば): mini Shiba (smaller variety)

CULTURAL NOTE:
One of the six native Japanese breeds along with 秋田犬(あきたいぬ) and four others. Internet-famous through the "doge" meme, which features a Shiba.
