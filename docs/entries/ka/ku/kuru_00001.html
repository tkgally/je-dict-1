来(く)る

くる
verb (irregular)
to come

Definitions

1. to come, to arrive
Expresses movement toward the speaker's current location or reference point. The opposite of 行(い)く (to go). Used when the speaker is at or identifies with the destination.
2. to come about, to occur
When attached to the te-form of another verb (〜てくる), indicates an action that has been continuing up to the present, or movement toward the speaker.

Examples

友達(ともだち)が家(いえ)に来(き)ました。
My friend came to my house.
バスが来(き)た！
The bus is here! / The bus came!
日本語(にほんご)が分(わ)かるようになってきた。
I've come to understand Japanese. / I've been starting to understand Japanese.
〜てくる expressing gradual change up to the present

Notes

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). Counterpart: 行(い)く (to go). Pattern: Xに/へ来(く)る (come to X).

ASPECT: ている indicates resulting state or habitual. 来(き)ている = 'has come (and is here)' or 'comes regularly.'

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 家(いえ)に来(く)る (come to the house)
  • 遊(あそ)びに来(く)る (come to hang out)
  • 〜て来(く)る (come after doing ~, or change up to now)
  • 来(く)るようになる (come to do something regularly)

IRREGULAR VERB: One of only two irregular verbs (with する). Reading changes: く→こ (negative), く→き (te-form, past, polite).
