1. anchoring; mooring; lying at anchor
A ship or boat stopping and remaining in a harbor, port, or at sea. Used for both temporary stops and longer stays.
船(ふね)が港(みなと)に停泊(ていはく)している。
A ship is anchored in the harbor.
大型(おおがた)クルーズ船(せん)が三日間(みっかかん)停泊(ていはく)した。
A large cruise ship was moored for three days.
台風(たいふう)の影響(えいきょう)で、多(おお)くの船(ふね)が港内(こうない)に停泊(ていはく)を余儀(よぎ)なくされた。
Due to the typhoon, many ships were forced to remain moored in the harbor.

Compound of 停(てい) (stop) + 泊(はく) (stay overnight, anchor). Functions as a する verb (停泊(ていはく)する = to anchor, to moor).

USAGE:
Used exclusively for watercraft — ships, boats, and other vessels. Not used for vehicles on land or aircraft. Common in maritime contexts, travel writing, and news about port activities.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 停泊(ていはく)する — to anchor, to moor
  • 停泊(ていはく)中(ちゅう) — at anchor, while moored
  • 停泊(ていはく)地(ち) — anchorage, mooring place
  • 港(みなと)に停泊(ていはく)する — to anchor in a harbor

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 接岸(せつがん) — coming alongside a pier, docking (specifically touching the quay)
  • 碇泊(ていはく) — anchoring (variant spelling using 碇(いかり) "anchor")
  • 寄港(きこう) — calling at a port (the act of visiting, not the state of being moored)
