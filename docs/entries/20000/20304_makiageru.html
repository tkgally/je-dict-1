1. to roll up; to wind up; to hoist
To roll or wind something upward, such as sleeves, a rope, or a blind. Also used for wind or water whipping things upward.
袖(そで)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げる。
To roll up one's sleeves.
強風(きょうふう)が砂(すな)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げた。
The strong wind whipped up sand.
ブラインドを巻(ま)き上(あ)げて窓(まど)を開(あ)けた。
I rolled up the blinds and opened the window.
2. to fleece; to swindle; to extort money from
To take money or valuables from someone through deception, intimidation, or bullying. A colloquial usage with a strong negative connotation.
金(かね)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げられた。
I was fleeced of my money.
あいつに全財産(ぜんざいさん)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げられた。
That guy swindled me out of everything I had.
年寄(としよ)りから金(かね)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げる悪質(あくしつ)な詐欺(さぎ)が増(ふ)えている。
Malicious scams that fleece the elderly of their money are on the rise.

A compound verb from 巻(ま)く (to wind, to roll) + 上(あ)げる (to raise). Sense 1 is the literal meaning; sense 2 likely derives from the image of "rolling up" someone's possessions and taking them.

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive. The intransitive form is 巻(ま)き上(あ)がる (to swirl up, to be rolled up).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 袖(そで)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げる — to roll up sleeves
  • 砂埃(すなぼこり)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げる — to kick up dust
  • 金(かね)を巻(ま)き上(あ)げる — to fleece someone of money
  • ロープを巻(ま)き上(あ)げる — to wind up a rope

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 騙(だま)し取(と)る — to swindle, to cheat out of (sense 2; emphasizes deception)
  • たくし上(あ)げる — to tuck up, to hitch up (for fabric, like skirts)
  • 捲(まく)る — to turn up, to roll back (pages, sleeves; less upward emphasis)
