1. to stand in a row; to be lined up (of buildings, structures)
Used to describe buildings, houses, or structures standing side by side along a street or in an area. Evokes a visual image of a row of structures.
ビルが建(た)ち並(なら)ぶ。
Buildings stand in a row.
通(とお)りに沿(そ)ってお店(みせ)が建(た)ち並(なら)んでいる。
Shops line the street.
駅前(えきまえ)には高層(こうそう)マンションが建(た)ち並(なら)び、昔(むかし)の面影(おもかげ)はない。
High-rise apartments line the area in front of the station, and there's no trace of what it used to look like.

A compound verb of 建(た)つ (to be built, to stand) and 並(なら)ぶ (to line up). Specifically used for buildings and structures, not for people standing in a line. Common in descriptions of cityscapes, neighborhoods, and street scenes.

TRANSITIVITY:
Intransitive — the buildings are the subject.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ビルが建(た)ち並(なら)ぶ — buildings line up
  • 家(いえ)が建(た)ち並(なら)ぶ — houses stand in a row
  • 建(た)ち並(なら)ぶ商店街(しょうてんがい) — a shopping street lined with stores

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 並(なら)ぶ — to line up (general, not specific to buildings)
  • 立(た)ち並(なら)ぶ — to stand in a row (for people or trees; uses 立(た)つ not 建(た)つ)
  • 軒(のき)を連(つら)ねる — to stand side by side (literary, for buildings)
