恋(こい)に落(お)ちる

こいにおちる
expression
to fall in love
1. to fall in love
To suddenly or unexpectedly develop romantic feelings for someone. A direct parallel to the English expression 'fall in love,' conveying that love is something one falls into rather than chooses.
彼女(かのじょ)に恋(こい)に落(お)ちた。
I fell in love with her.
一目(ひとめ)で恋(こい)に落(お)ちてしまった。
I fell in love at first sight.
二人(ふたり)は大学(だいがく)で出会(であ)い、すぐに恋(こい)に落(お)ちた。
The two met at university and quickly fell in love.

A common expression combining 恋(こい) (romantic love) + に (into) + 落(お)ちる (to fall). Like the English "fall in love," it implies that love happens suddenly or irresistibly, not through deliberate choice.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 人(ひと)に恋(こい)に落(お)ちる: to fall in love with someone
  • 一目(ひとめ)で恋(こい)に落(お)ちる: to fall in love at first sight
  • 恋(こい)に落(お)ちてしまう: to end up falling in love (with a nuance of it being beyond one's control)

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 恋(こい)をする: to be in love, to have a crush — more neutral and everyday; doesn't carry the "falling" metaphor
  • 惚(ほ)れる: to fall for, to be smitten — more colloquial and direct
  • 好(す)きになる: to come to like — broader and milder; can apply to non-romantic contexts too

USAGE:
Common in romantic fiction, song lyrics, and everyday conversation. Slightly more dramatic or literary than 好(す)きになる, emphasizing the uncontrollable nature of romantic attraction.
