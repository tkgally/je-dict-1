1. continuous service; length of service; tenure
The unbroken period of time someone has been employed at the same company or organization. Often used when recognizing long-term employees.
勤続(きんぞく)20年(ねん)になる。
I've been with the company for 20 years.
勤続(きんぞく)年数(ねんすう)に応(おう)じて有給(ゆうきゅう)が増(ふ)える。
Paid leave increases according to years of service.
勤続(きんぞく)30年(ねん)を記念(きねん)して表彰(ひょうしょう)された。
She was recognized with an award to celebrate 30 years of continuous service.

Compound of 勤(きん) (work/serve) and 続(ぞく) (continue). Refers specifically to unbroken employment at the same organization, which is an important concept in Japanese workplace culture where long tenure has traditionally been valued and rewarded.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 勤続(きんぞく)〜年(ねん): X years of service
  • 勤続(きんぞく)年数(ねんすう): years of service, length of tenure
  • 勤続(きんぞく)表彰(ひょうしょう): long-service award
  • 長期(ちょうき)勤続(きんぞく): long-term service
  • 勤続(きんぞく)手当(てあて): longevity pay, tenure allowance

CULTURAL NOTE:
In the Japanese 終身雇用(しゅうしんこよう) (lifetime employment) system, 勤続(きんぞく)年数(ねんすう) has traditionally been a key factor in determining salary and rank. Companies often hold ceremonies to honor employees reaching milestones like 10, 20, or 30 years.

NOTE ON HOMOPHONES:
Not to be confused with 金属(きんぞく) (metal), which is a much more common word with the same reading.
