1. to hang down; to suspend from above; to dangle
To attach something to a higher point so that it hangs freely. Used for physical objects suspended from ceilings, hooks, branches, and similar supports.
ランプを天井(てんじょう)から吊(つ)り下(さ)げる。
To hang a lamp from the ceiling.
首(くび)からカメラを吊(つ)り下(さ)げていた。
He had a camera hanging from his neck.
木(き)の枝(えだ)から風鈴(ふうりん)を吊(つ)り下(さ)げると、涼(すず)しげな音(おと)がした。
When I hung a wind chime from a tree branch, it made a cool, refreshing sound.

A compound verb from 吊(つ)る (to hang, to suspend) + 下(さ)げる (to lower). The combination emphasizes that the object is hanging downward from its attachment point.

TRANSITIVITY:

Transitive. The intransitive counterpart is 吊(つ)り下(さ)がる (to hang down, to be suspended).

  • 吊(つ)り下(さ)げる — to hang something (transitive)
  • 吊(つ)り下(さ)がる — to be hanging (intransitive)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 天井(てんじょう)から吊(つ)り下(さ)げる — to hang from the ceiling
  • 首(くび)から吊(つ)り下(さ)げる — to hang from one's neck
  • 吊(つ)り下(さ)げ式(しき) — suspended type, hanging type
  • 吊(つ)り下(さ)げ広告(こうこく) — hanging advertisement (train ads)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 掛(か)ける — to hang (on a hook or wall; broader usage)
  • ぶら下(さ)げる — to dangle, to hang (more casual; emphasizes swinging)
  • 垂(た)らす — to let hang down, to droop (letting something drop loosely)
