1. to microwave; to heat up in the microwave
Casual, colloquial verb meaning to heat food in a microwave oven. Named after the 'chin' (ding) sound that microwaves make when finished.
お弁当(べんとう)をチンして。
Microwave the lunch box.
冷(つめ)たくなったら、チンすればいいよ。
If it gets cold, just pop it in the microwave.
昨日(きのう)の残(のこ)り物(もの)をチンして、夕飯(ゆうはん)にした。
I microwaved yesterday's leftovers and had them for dinner.

An onomatopoeic する-verb derived from the チン sound (ding) that Japanese microwaves make when finished heating. Extremely common in casual speech — most Japanese people use チンする rather than the formal 電子(でんし)レンジで温(あたた)める.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • チンして食(た)べる: to microwave and eat
  • チンするだけ: just microwave it (describing easy meals)
  • レンジでチンする: to ding it in the microwave

USAGE:
Used in casual, everyday conversation. Not appropriate in formal writing. The word is understood by virtually all Japanese speakers regardless of age. Convenience store staff may ask: 温(あたた)めますか？ (Shall I heat it up?) — the formal equivalent of チンする.

RELATED WORDS:

  • 電子(でんし)レンジ: microwave oven (the formal word for the appliance)
  • 温(あたた)める: to heat up, to warm (the formal verb)
