1. to eat one's way around; to try food at various places
To visit multiple restaurants, food stalls, or eating establishments to sample their food, often as a leisure activity or culinary exploration.
ラーメンを食(た)べ歩(ある)く。
To eat ramen at various places.
休日(きゅうじつ)は町(まち)のカフェを食(た)べ歩(ある)くのが趣味(しゅみ)だ。
My hobby is visiting different cafes around town on my days off.
京都(きょうと)の名店(めいてん)を食(た)べ歩(ある)いて、お気(き)に入(い)りの店(みせ)を見(み)つけた。
I ate my way around the famous restaurants of Kyoto and found a favorite.
2. to eat while walking; to snack on the go
To eat food while walking along a street, especially at tourist spots or food markets.
商店街(しょうてんがい)で食(た)べ歩(ある)きする。
To eat while walking through the shopping street.
屋台(やたい)の食(た)べ歩(ある)きが楽(たの)しい。
Eating at the street stalls while walking around is fun.
浅草(あさくさ)では仲見世通(なかみせどお)りで食(た)べ歩(ある)きを楽(たの)しむ観光客(かんこうきゃく)が多(おお)い。
In Asakusa, many tourists enjoy eating their way along Nakamise Street.

Compound verb of 食(た)べる (to eat) + 歩(ある)く (to walk). The noun form 食(た)べ歩(ある)き is extremely common and popular in Japanese food culture.

USAGE:
The noun form 食(た)べ歩(ある)き is used more frequently than the verb form and appears in travel guides, food blogs, and TV programs. Sense 1 (食(た)べ歩(ある)き as visiting multiple eateries) is a popular leisure activity in Japan, with dedicated TV shows, books, and online guides. Sense 2 (eating while walking) is common at tourist areas and festivals, though some traditional areas consider it poor manners.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 食(た)べ歩(ある)きグルメ — street food, food to eat while walking
  • 食(た)べ歩(ある)きマップ — food tour map
  • ラーメンの食(た)べ歩(ある)き — ramen restaurant hopping
  • 食(た)べ歩(ある)きスポット — food tour spot

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • はしご — hopping between multiple bars/restaurants (informal)
  • 食(た)べ比(くら)べ — taste comparison, trying the same dish at different places
