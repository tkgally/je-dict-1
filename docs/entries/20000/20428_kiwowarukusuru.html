1. to take offense; to feel bad; to be displeased
To become upset or offended by something said or done. Often used when apologizing in advance for something that might cause displeasure.
気(き)を悪(わる)くしないでね。
Don't take it the wrong way, okay?
気(き)を悪(わる)くされたらすみません。
I'm sorry if I offended you.
正直(しょうじき)に言(い)って気(き)を悪(わる)くするかもしれないけど、あの企画(きかく)はうまくいかないと思(おも)う。
You might take this badly, but honestly, I don't think that project is going to work out.

Literally "to make one's feeling/spirit bad." A very common expression used in everyday conversation, especially when delivering potentially unwelcome opinions or criticism.

USAGE:

Most commonly appears in cautionary or apologetic forms:

  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くしないで: don't take offense (preemptive)
  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くされたら: if I've offended you
  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くするかもしれないけど: you might take this badly, but...

The passive form 気(き)を悪(わる)くされる is often used to show deference.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くしないでください: please don't take offense
  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くしたらごめんね: sorry if I offended you
  • 気(き)を悪(わる)くするようなことを言(い)う: to say something offensive

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 気分(きぶん)を害(がい)する: to offend, to hurt one's feelings — more formal
  • 機嫌(きげん)を損(そこ)ねる: to put in a bad mood — emphasizes the resulting mood
  • 怒(おこ)らせる: to make angry — more direct and strong
