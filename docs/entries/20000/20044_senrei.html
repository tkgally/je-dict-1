洗礼(せんれい)

せんれい
noun
baptism; christening
1. baptism; christening
Religious sacrament
洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)ける。
To receive baptism.
赤(あか)ちゃんの洗礼式(せんれいしき)に招待(しょうたい)された。
I was invited to the baby's baptism ceremony.
教会(きょうかい)で洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)けてキリスト教徒(きょうと)になった。
I received baptism at a church and became a Christian.
2. initiation; ordeal; baptism by fire
Figurative: a challenging first experience
社会(しゃかい)の洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)ける。
To go through the baptism of the real world.
新人(しんじん)は厳(きび)しい洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)けた。
The newcomer went through a tough initiation.
海外(かいがい)赴任(ふにん)の初日(しょにち)に文化(ぶんか)の違(ちが)いという洗礼(せんれい)を浴(あ)びた。
On my first day of the overseas posting, I was baptized by the reality of cultural differences.

Originally a Christian religious term for the sacrament of baptism. In Japanese, the figurative sense of a harsh or transformative first experience is used much more commonly than the religious sense.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)ける: to receive baptism; to undergo an initiation
  • 洗礼(せんれい)を浴(あ)びる: to be hit with an ordeal (figurative)
  • 洗礼(せんれい)名(めい): baptismal name
  • 洗礼式(せんれいしき): baptism ceremony
  • 社会(しゃかい)の洗礼(せんれい): the baptism of the real world

USAGE:
The figurative sense 2 is far more common in everyday Japanese. It typically describes a newcomer's difficult first encounter with something — a new job, a new environment, or harsh reality. The phrase 洗礼(せんれい)を受(う)ける works for both senses.
