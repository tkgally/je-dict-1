1. to outsmart; to outwit; to get ahead of someone by trickery
To gain an advantage over someone by acting cleverly or sneakily before they can react. Implies cunning or underhanded tactics rather than honest competition.
ライバルを出(だ)し抜(ぬ)く。
To outsmart a rival.
彼(かれ)はいつも人(ひと)を出(だ)し抜(ぬ)こうとする。
He always tries to get the better of people.
競合他社(きょうごうたしゃ)に出(だ)し抜(ぬ)かれて、契約(けいやく)を取(と)られてしまった。
We were outmaneuvered by a competitor and lost the contract.

A godan verb compound of 出(だ)す (to put out) and 抜(ぬ)く (to pull out, to surpass). Carries a negative connotation of using cunning or underhandedness to get ahead of others. Often used in business and competitive contexts.

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive — takes を to mark the person being outsmarted.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ライバルを出(だ)し抜(ぬ)く — to outsmart a rival
  • 人(ひと)を出(だ)し抜(ぬ)く — to get the better of someone
  • 出(だ)し抜(ぬ)かれる — to be outsmarted (passive)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 裏(うら)をかく — to outwit, to catch off guard
  • 先手(せんて)を打(う)つ — to make the first move (more neutral)
  • 騙(だま)す — to deceive, to trick (more directly dishonest)
