1. to peel; to shell; to husk; to remove the shell/skin
To remove the outer covering of eggs, nuts, shellfish, or other foods with a hard outer layer.
ゆで卵(たまご)の殻(から)をむく。
To peel a boiled egg.
エビの殻(から)をむいてから炒(いた)める。
Peel the shrimp before stir-frying.
栗(くり)の殻(から)をむくのは大変(たいへん)だけど、焼(や)き栗(ぐり)はおいしい。
Peeling chestnuts is hard work, but roasted chestnuts are delicious.

A common cooking expression. The verb むく means to peel or strip off, and 殻(から) refers to any hard outer covering — eggshells, nutshells, crustacean shells, etc.

WHAT CAN BE PEELED:

  • ゆで卵(たまご)の殻(から): boiled egg shell
  • エビの殻(から): shrimp shell
  • 栗(くり)の殻(から): chestnut shell
  • 落花生(らっかせい)の殻(から): peanut shell
  • 貝(かい)の殻(から): shellfish shell

RELATED EXPRESSIONS:

  • 皮(かわ)をむく: to peel skin (of fruit, vegetables) — for soft outer layers
  • 殻(から)を割(わ)る: to crack a shell — when breaking rather than peeling
  • 殻(から)を取(と)る: to remove the shell — more general

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • むく: to peel — the base verb, used with various objects
  • 皮(かわ)をむく: to peel (fruit/vegetables) — for soft coverings like apple skin
  • 殻(から)つき: with shell on, unshelled — describes food still in its shell
