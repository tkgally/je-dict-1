1. to spoil; to pamper; to indulge someone too much
To be overly lenient or indulgent with someone, especially a child, often with negative implications of producing bad behavior.
子供(こども)を甘(あま)やかしすぎると良(よ)くない。
It's not good to spoil children too much.
祖父母(そふぼ)は孫(まご)を甘(あま)やかす傾向(けいこう)がある。
Grandparents tend to spoil their grandchildren.
彼(かれ)は甘(あま)やかされて育(そだ)った。
He was raised being spoiled.
かわいいからといって甘(あま)やかしてばかりいると、わがままな子(こ)になるよ。
If you keep spoiling them just because they're cute, they'll turn into a selfish child.
親(おや)が子供(こども)を甘(あま)やかしすぎた結果(けっか)、自分(じぶん)で何(なに)もできない大人(おとな)になってしまった。
As a result of the parents spoiling the child too much, they grew into an adult who can't do anything on their own.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: transitive (他動詞(たどうし))
  • Pair: 甘(あま)える (to act spoiled, to seek indulgence) - intransitive
  • Pattern: XがYを甘(あま)やかす - the person being indulged is the object

ASPECT:

  • 甘(あま)やかしている = is spoiling (an action in progress, or a habit)
  • 甘(あま)やかされて育(そだ)つ = to grow up spoiled - the passive plus a verb of growing is the usual way to say this about a person

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 子供(こども)を甘(あま)やかす (to spoil a child)
  • 甘(あま)やかして育(そだ)てる (to raise someone indulgently)
  • 甘(あま)やかしすぎる (to be too lenient)

NUANCE:
Unlike simple kindness, 甘(あま)やかす implies leniency that goes too far, and it is nearly always used with disapproval - it points at a failure to set limits. The word usually paired against it is しつけ (discipline, bringing a child up properly).

Related Words

Pair verb: 甘(あま)える (to act spoiled, to seek indulgence (intransitive))
Related: 溺愛(できあい) (excessive doting)
Contrast: 躾(しつけ) (discipline, upbringing)
