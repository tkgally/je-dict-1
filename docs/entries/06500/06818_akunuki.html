1. removing bitterness, removing harshness (cooking)
The cooking process of removing bitter, astringent, or harsh components from vegetables and other ingredients to improve their taste.
ほうれん草(そう)は灰汁(あく)抜(ぬ)きをしてから使(つか)う。
Remove the bitterness from spinach before using it.
たけのこの灰汁(あく)抜(ぬ)きには米(こめ)ぬかを使(つか)う。
Use rice bran to remove the harshness from bamboo shoots.
灰汁(あく)抜(ぬ)きを怠(おこた)ると苦味(にがみ)が残(のこ)る。
If you skip removing the bitterness, the bitter taste will remain.

FORMATION: 灰汁(あく) (lye; harsh/bitter components) + 抜(ぬ)き (removing).

COOKING TECHNIQUE: Essential preparation step in Japanese cuisine for many vegetables.

COMMON METHODS:

  • 茹(ゆ)でて灰汁(あく)を取(と)る (boil and skim off scum)
  • 水(みず)に晒(さら)す (soak in water)
  • 塩水(しおみず)に漬(つ)ける (soak in salt water)
  • 米(こめ)ぬかと茹(ゆ)でる (boil with rice bran)

COMMON INGREDIENTS REQUIRING 灰汁(あく)抜(ぬ)き:

  • ほうれん草(そう) (spinach)
  • たけのこ (bamboo shoots)
  • 蕨(わらび) (bracken fern)
  • ごぼう (burdock root)
  • なす (eggplant)

RELATED: 灰汁(あく)を取(と)る (skim off scum from simmering food)
