そこそこ

そこそこ
adverb, na-adjective (の/な)
so-so; reasonably; passable; moderate
1. so-so; reasonably; fairly; passable
Describes something that is acceptable or adequate, though not outstanding. Indicates a moderate, middle-ground level of quality or quantity.
給料(きゅうりょう)はそこそこだけど、仕事(しごと)は楽(らく)だ。
The salary is so-so, but the work is easy.
そこそこ人気(にんき)のあるレストランだ。
It's a reasonably popular restaurant.
そこそこの成績(せいせき)で卒業(そつぎょう)した。
I graduated with passable grades.
2. hurriedly; hastily (with に)
When used with に, indicates doing something quickly and moving on, without lingering.
朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)をそこそこに済(す)ませて出(で)かけた。
I hurriedly finished breakfast and left.
挨拶(あいさつ)をそこそこに切(き)り上(あ)げて、次(つぎ)の予定(よてい)に向(む)かった。
I cut the greetings short and headed to my next appointment.
準備(じゅんび)をそこそこに済(す)ませて出発(しゅっぱつ)したので、忘(わす)れ物(もの)をしてしまった。
I left after rushing through my preparations, so I forgot something.

USAGE:

Sense 1 (so-so, moderate):

As an adverb, そこそこ goes straight in front of the adjective or verb it grades:

  • そこそこ美味(おい)しい (fairly tasty)
  • そこそこ売(う)れている (selling reasonably well)

As a modifier it takes の before a noun; そこそこな is also heard but is the weaker option:

  • そこそこの値段(ねだん) (a reasonable price)
  • そこそこの成績(せいせき) (passable grades)

After a number, そこそこ means "just about that much, and no more": 30歳(さい)そこそこ (barely thirty).

Sense 2 (hurriedly):

〜をそこそこに + verb. The に is obligatory here, and this is what separates sense 2 from sense 1:

  • 挨拶(あいさつ)をそこそこに立(た)ち去(さ)る (to leave after a hasty greeting)

The thing marked with を is what got short-changed, so the sentence usually carries a hint of rudeness or regret.

NUANCE:
そこそこ says something is acceptable but not impressive — good enough to pass muster without standing out. Used of your own situation it sounds modest; used of someone else's work it can sound faintly dismissive, so it is safer in the first person.

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • まあまあ - so-so, not bad; the everyday spoken equivalent
  • ほどほど - in moderation; about restraint rather than about quality
  • それなりに - reasonably, in its own way; grants merit judged against expectations
