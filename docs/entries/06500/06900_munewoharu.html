胸(むね)を張(は)る

むねをはる
expression
to be proud, to hold one's head high
1. to be proud, to hold one's head high with confidence
To feel and display pride or confidence, often shown through upright posture.
自分(じぶん)の仕事(しごと)に胸(むね)を張(は)れる。
I can be proud of my work.
胸(むね)を張(は)って生(い)きていきたい。
I want to live with my head held high.
堂々(どうどう)と胸(むね)を張(は)って歩(ある)いた。
She walked confidently with her chest out.
胸(むね)を張(は)って言(い)えることだ。
It's something I can say proudly.
子供(こども)たちの前(まえ)で胸(むね)を張(は)れる大人(おとな)でいたい。ごまかしのない生(い)き方(かた)をしたいと思(おも)う。
I want to be an adult who can hold my head high in front of children. I want to live honestly.

STRUCTURE: 胸(むね) (chest) + を + 張(は)る (to stretch, to draw taut) — the posture of pride, chest out and shoulders back.

NUANCE: Pride that is earned, not arrogance. It is used for pride in work well done, for living without anything to hide, and for standing behind one's choices. Because the pride has to be justified, the phrase often appears as a test the speaker sets for themself: can I say this 胸(むね)を張(は)って?

POTENTIAL FORM: 胸(むね)を張(は)れる ('can hold one's head high') is at least as common as the plain form, and is the usual shape when talking about oneself:

  • 胸(むね)を張(は)って言(い)える: can say it proudly
  • 胸(むね)を張(は)れる仕事(しごと): work one can be proud of

RELATED EXPRESSIONS:

  • 誇(ほこ)りに思(おも)う: to feel proud of
  • 自信(じしん)を持(も)つ: to have confidence
  • 堂々(どうどう)と: confidently, with dignity — often used alongside it

OTHER 胸(むね) EXPRESSIONS:

  • 胸(むね)が痛(いた)む: to feel heartache
  • 胸(むね)を打(う)つ: to be moving, to touch the heart
  • 胸(むね)が一杯(いっぱい): to be overcome with emotion
