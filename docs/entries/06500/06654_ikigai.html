生(い)き甲斐(がい)

いきがい
noun
purpose in life; reason for living; something to live for
1. purpose in life; reason for living; something to live for
What gives one's life meaning and makes it worth living. Can be a passion, goal, relationship, or activity that motivates someone to keep going.
孫(まご)が私(わたし)の生(い)き甲斐(がい)だ。
My grandchildren are my reason for living.
退職(たいしょく)後(ご)は生(い)き甲斐(がい)を見(み)つけることが大切(たいせつ)だ。
After retirement, it's important to find a purpose in life.
音楽(おんがく)は彼(かれ)にとって生(い)き甲斐(がい)だ。
Music is what makes life worth living for him.

生(い)き甲斐(がい) is whatever makes a person's life feel worth living — it can be a person, an activity, or a role. Grammatically it behaves as a plain noun: Xが生(い)き甲斐(がい)だ identifies what that thing is for someone. It is formed from 生(い)きる plus the 〜甲斐(かい) suffix seen in やり甲斐(がい).

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 生(い)き甲斐(がい)を見(み)つける (to find one's purpose in life)
  • 生(い)き甲斐(がい)を感(かん)じる (to feel a sense of purpose)
  • 生(い)き甲斐(がい)を失(うしな)う (to lose one's reason for living)
  • 生(い)き甲斐(がい)のある人生(じんせい) (a life with purpose)

CULTURAL NOTE:
The four-circle diagram popular in English — the overlap of 好(す)きなこと (what you love), 得意(とくい)なこと (what you are good at), 必要(ひつよう)とされること (what the world needs), and 稼(かせ)げること (what you can be paid for) — is a foreign elaboration. In ordinary Japanese use the word is far humbler and carries no requirement that the thing be productive or paid: a grandchild or a garden qualifies.

WRITING NOTE:
Often written いきがい in hiragana. The word has been borrowed into English as 'ikigai,' usually in the four-circle sense rather than the everyday Japanese one.
