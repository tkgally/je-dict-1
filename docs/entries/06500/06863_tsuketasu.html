1. to add on; to supplement; to append
To add something extra to what already exists.
説明(せつめい)を付(つ)け足(た)してもいいですか。
May I add an explanation?
スープの味(あじ)が薄(うす)いので塩(しお)を付(つ)け足(た)した。
The soup was bland so I added more salt.
最後(さいご)に一言(ひとこと)付(つ)け足(た)しておく。
Let me add one more thing at the end.
資料(しりょう)に図(ず)を一(いち)つ付(つ)け足(た)しました。
I added one more diagram to the handout.
彼(かれ)は話(はな)し終(お)わった後(あと)で、言(い)い忘(わす)れたことを一(ひと)つ付(つ)け足(た)した。
After he finished speaking, he added one thing he had forgotten to say.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 付(つ)ける (to attach) + 足(た)す (to add). Both halves point the same way, and the result implies the addition is small and comes after the main thing is already in place — an afterthought rather than a planned part.

USAGE:

  • 情報(じょうほう)を付(つ)け足(た)す (to add information)
  • 注釈(ちゅうしゃく)を付(つ)け足(た)す (to add notes)
  • 調味料(ちょうみりょう)を付(つ)け足(た)す (to add seasoning)

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive, marking the added item with を and the thing added to with に: 資料(しりょう)に図(ず)を付(つ)け足(た)す.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 書(か)き足(た)す — to add in writing, specifically.
  • 言(い)い足(た)す — to add in speech, specifically.
  • 追加(ついか)する — more formal and neutral; the standard word on forms, menus, and in business writing, with no sense of afterthought.
  • 加(くわ)える — the plain verb "to add," used for ingredients, numbers, and conditions alike, without the "tacked on at the end" feel.
