1. to knead thoroughly
To work dough or similar material until it reaches the desired consistency.
生地(きじ)をしっかり練(ね)り上(あ)げてください。
Please knead the dough thoroughly.
粘土(ねんど)を練(ね)り上(あ)げて器(うつわ)を作(つく)る。
To knead clay and make a vessel.
餡(あん)をなめらかになるまで練(ね)り上(あ)げた。
I kneaded the bean paste until it was smooth.
2. to polish up; to refine; to perfect
To carefully develop and improve something through repeated effort.
企画(きかく)を練(ね)り上(あ)げるのに何(なん)週間(しゅうかん)もかかった。
It took weeks to refine the plan.
作家(さっか)は何度(なんど)も推敲(すいこう)して文章(ぶんしょう)を練(ね)り上(あ)げた。
The author polished the text through repeated revisions.
チーム全員(ぜんいん)で時間(じかん)をかけて戦略(せんりゃく)を練(ね)り上(あ)げた。
The whole team took their time to carefully develop the strategy.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 練(ね)る (to knead, to work over) + 上(あ)げる (to carry through to completion). The ～上(あ)げる half is what adds the sense that the work has been taken all the way to a finished state, not merely begun.

USAGE:

  • 戦略(せんりゃく)を練(ね)り上(あ)げる (to refine a strategy)
  • 脚本(きゃくほん)を練(ね)り上(あ)げる (to polish a script)
  • アイデアを練(ね)り上(あ)げる (to develop an idea)
  • 生地(きじ)を練(ね)り上(あ)げる (to knead dough thoroughly)

THE TWO SENSES:
Sense 1 is the literal one — physically working dough, clay, or bean paste until its texture is right. Sense 2 is the same action applied to a plan, a text, or a strategy, and is the more common use in everyday writing. Both share the image of prolonged, repeated working; the object tells you which sense is meant.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 練(ね)る alone describes the working itself with no implication that it is finished — 計画(けいかく)を練(ね)る is working on a plan, while 練(ね)り上(あ)げる says it came out fully formed.
  • 仕上(しあ)げる is the general verb for finishing something off; it says nothing about the repeated kneading-over that 練(ね)り上(あ)げる implies.
  • 磨(みが)く polishes a surface or a skill, not a plan or a dough.
