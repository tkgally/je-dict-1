1. to depend on someone's affection; to act spoiled; to presume upon someone's kindness
To seek or expect indulgence from someone in a close relationship, allowing oneself to be emotionally dependent.
子供(こども)が母親(ははおや)に甘(あま)えている。
The child is acting spoiled with their mother.
彼女(かのじょ)は彼(かれ)に甘(あま)えるのが上手(じょうず)だ。
She's good at acting dependent on him.
ご好意(こうい)に甘(あま)えて、お言葉(ことば)に甘(あま)えます。
I'll take you up on your kind offer.
子(こ)が甘(あま)えてきたときは、しっかり受(う)け止(と)めてあげたい。
When my child comes to me for affection, I want to accept and respond to it properly.

CULTURAL CONCEPT:
甘(あま)え (the noun form; see 甘(あま)え) is a key concept in Japanese 心理(しんり), analysed by the psychiatrist Doi Takeo in his 本(ほん) 「甘(あま)えの構造(こうぞう)」 (The Anatomy of Dependence). It names the wish to be looked after indulgently by someone close, and the confidence that they will allow it.

NOT SIMPLY NEGATIVE:
English 'spoiled' sounds like criticism, but 甘(あま)える is often warm: a child leaning on a parent this way, or a friend accepting a favour, is behaving in a way that confirms closeness. It turns critical only when someone presumes too far on a relationship, as in 甘(あま)えすぎる.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: intransitive (自動詞(じどうし))
  • Pair: 甘(あま)やかす (to spoil, to pamper) - transitive
  • Pattern: XがYに甘(あま)える - the person indulged is marked with に

ASPECT:
甘(あま)えている describes behaviour going on right now, not a resulting state - a child who is being clingy at this moment.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • Xに甘(あま)える (to depend on X, to act spoiled toward X)
  • 好意(こうい)に甘(あま)える (to accept someone's kindness)
  • お言葉(ことば)に甘(あま)えて (taking you up on your kind words - a set polite phrase for accepting an offer)
  • 親切(しんせつ)に甘(あま)える (to presume upon someone's kindness)
