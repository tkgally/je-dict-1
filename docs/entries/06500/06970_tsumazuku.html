1. to stumble; to trip
To catch one's foot on something and lose balance while walking or running. The obstacle causes an unintended forward motion that may or may not result in a fall.
石(いし)につまずいて転(ころ)んだ。
I tripped on a stone and fell.
暗(くら)い廊下(ろうか)でつまずかないように気(き)をつけてください。
Please be careful not to trip in the dark hallway.
段差(だんさ)につまずいて足首(あしくび)をひねってしまった。
I tripped on a step and twisted my ankle.
2. to encounter an obstacle; to hit a snag
Figuratively, to encounter an unexpected problem or difficulty that hinders progress in work, life, or a project.
新(あたら)しいプロジェクトは予算(よさん)問題(もんだい)でつまずいた。
The new project hit a snag due to budget issues.
出(で)だしからつまずいてしまった。
I stumbled right from the start.
受験勉強(じゅけんべんきょう)で数学(すうがく)につまずいて、塾(じゅく)に通(かよ)い始(はじ)めた。
I was struggling with math in my exam prep, so I started going to a cram school.

PARTICLE: the thing you trip over takes に, not を — 石(いし)につまずく. In the figurative sense the same に marks the subject or stage that defeated you (数学(すうがく)につまずく), while で marks the cause or the point at which things went wrong.

ORTHOGRAPHY:
Can be written 躓(つまず)く, but that kanji is outside the standard list and hiragana is much more common in everyday writing.

WHAT IT DOES AND DOESN'T MEAN:
つまずく is only the catching of the foot and the loss of balance. Whether you actually go down is a separate verb, which is why 転(ころ)ぶ is so often added after it, as in example 1.

USAGE:
The physical meaning (sense 1) belongs to everyday conversation; the figurative meaning (sense 2) is frequent in news and business writing about projects, negotiations, and studies that ran into trouble early.

COLLOCATIONS:

  • 石(いし)につまずく: to trip on a stone
  • 段差(だんさ)につまずく: to trip on a step
  • 最初(さいしょ)の一歩(いっぽ)でつまずく: to stumble at the very first step
  • 出(で)だしからつまずく: to stumble from the start
