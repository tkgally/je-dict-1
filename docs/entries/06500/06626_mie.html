見栄(みえ)

みえ
noun
vanity; showing off; keeping up appearances
See also: 見得(みえ) (dramatic pose in kabuki - same reading, different kanji)
1. vanity; showing off; putting on airs
The act of trying to appear better, wealthier, or more capable than one actually is.
見栄(みえ)を張(は)って高(たか)い車(くるま)を買(か)った。
I bought an expensive car to show off.
見栄(みえ)っ張(ぱ)りな性格(せいかく)を直(なお)したい。
I want to fix my habit of showing off.
見栄(みえ)を張(は)らずに素直(すなお)に生(い)きたい。
I want to live honestly without putting on airs.
友(とも)だちの前(まえ)で見栄(みえ)を張(は)って嘘(うそ)をついてしまった。
I ended up telling a lie in front of my friends to keep up appearances.
給料(きゅうりょう)が少(すく)ないのに見栄(みえ)を張(は)って高級(こうきゅう)店(みせ)で食事(しょくじ)するのはやめた方(ほう)がいい。
You should stop eating at expensive restaurants to keep up appearances when your salary is low.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 見栄(みえ)を張(は)る (to show off, to put on airs)
  • 見栄(みえ)っ張(ぱ)り (noun / na-adjective: a show-off, a vain person)

TWO WORDS, ONE READING - 見栄(みえ) AND 見得(みえ):

Both are read みえ, and the kabuki phrase is often miswritten with the kanji of this entry.

  • 見栄(みえ)を張(は)る - to put on airs (this entry)
  • 見得(みえ)を切(き)る - to strike a dramatic pose on stage, or by extension to make a bold public declaration

Note the different verbs as well: 張(は)る for vanity, 切(き)る for the pose.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 虚栄心(きょえいしん) (vanity, conceit) - the inner feeling, more abstract and more formal.
  • 見栄(みえ) is the outward display: the car, the restaurant, the story told to impress.

NUANCE:
見栄(みえ)を張(は)る usually implies spending beyond one's means or claiming more success than one has, and it is mildly critical - including when speakers apply it to themselves.
