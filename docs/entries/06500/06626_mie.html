見栄(みえ)

みえ
noun
vanity; showing off; keeping up appearances
1. vanity; showing off; putting on airs
The act of trying to appear better, wealthier, or more capable than one actually is.
見栄(みえ)を張(は)って高(たか)い車(くるま)を買(か)った。
I bought an expensive car to show off.
見栄(みえ)っ張(ぱ)りな性格(せいかく)を直(なお)したい。
I want to fix my habit of showing off.
2. pose; dramatic pause (in kabuki)
A stylized, dramatic pose struck by kabuki actors at climactic moments.
歌舞伎(かぶき)役者(やくしゃ)が見栄(みえ)を切(き)った。
The kabuki actor struck a dramatic pose.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 見栄(みえ)を張(は)る (to show off, to put on airs)
  • 見栄(みえ)っ張(ぱ)り (noun/na-adj: show-off, vain person)
  • 見栄(みえ)を切(き)る (to strike a dramatic pose - kabuki term)

KABUKI ORIGIN:
The theatrical term (見栄(みえ)を切(き)る) refers to the stylized pose where actors cross their eyes, turn their head sharply, and freeze at a dramatic moment. This pose emphasizes emotion and is unique to kabuki.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 虚栄心(きょえいしん) (vanity, conceit) - more abstract, internal feeling
  • 見栄(みえ) focuses on the outward display of vanity

NUANCE:
見栄(みえ)を張(は)る often implies spending beyond one's means or pretending to be more successful than one is.
