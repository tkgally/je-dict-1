1. to break in; to force one's way in; to burst in
Used for unauthorized entry, often with criminal or aggressive implications. Intransitive verb formed from 押す (to push) + 入る (to enter).
強盗(ごうとう)が家(いえ)に押(お)し入(い)った。
A robber broke into the house.
不審者(ふしんしゃ)が店(みせ)に押(お)し入(い)り、現金(げんきん)を奪(うば)った。
A suspicious person broke into the store and stole cash.
警察(けいさつ)が容疑者(ようぎしゃ)の部屋(へや)に押(お)し入(い)った。
The police forced their way into the suspect's room.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). No transitive pair. Formed from 押(お)す (to push) + 入(い)る (to enter), so the entry is forced rather than merely unauthorized.

PARTICLE PATTERNS:

  • [place]に押(お)し入(い)る - to break into [place]. The place takes に, never を.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 家(いえ)に押(お)し入(い)る - to break into a house
  • 店(みせ)に押(お)し入(い)る - to break into a store
  • 強盗(ごうとう)が押(お)し入(い)る - a robber breaks in

REGISTER:
Almost entirely a crime-reporting word. It appears in news copy and police statements about robberies and forced entries; in everyday speech people say 入(はい)る or 侵入(しんにゅう)する instead.

RELATED:

  • 押(お)し込(こ)む - to force something into a space (transitive; also used of a break-in)
  • 侵入(しんにゅう)する - to intrude, trespass (formal; covers unauthorized entry without implying force)
