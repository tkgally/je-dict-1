渋(しぶ)い

しぶい
i-adjective
astringent; refined, understated
1. astringent
Having a dry, puckering taste, like unripe persimmons or strong tea.
2. refined, understated, cool
Having a subtle, tasteful elegance; sophisticated in a low-key way. Often used to praise someone's style or choices.
3. reluctant, stingy
Unwilling to spend money or give generously; having a sour or reluctant expression.
この柿(かき)はまだ熟(う)れていなくて渋(しぶ)い。
This persimmon isn't ripe yet and is astringent.
渋(しぶ)いお茶(ちゃ)が好(す)きです。
I like astringent tea.
お父(とう)さんの着物(きもの)の色(いろ)、渋(しぶ)くてかっこいいね。
The color of your father's kimono is refined and cool.
彼(かれ)は渋(しぶ)い声(こえ)で歌(うた)う俳優(はいゆう)だ。
He's an actor who sings with a deep, refined voice.
社長(しゃちょう)は賃上(ちんあ)げに渋(しぶ)い顔(かお)をした。
The company president made a reluctant face at the wage increase.

渋(しぶ)い is an i-adjective with distinct meanings.

FORMS:

  • Adverbial: 渋(しぶ)く
  • Noun form: 渋(しぶ)さ (both astringency and refined taste)
  • Related noun: 渋(しぶ) (astringent juice, tannin)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 渋(しぶ)い vs. 地味(じみ): 渋(しぶ)い implies positive understated elegance; 地味(じみ) is neutral or negative (plain, drab)
  • 渋(しぶ)い vs. 苦(にが)い: 渋(しぶ)い is astringent (puckering); 苦(にが)い is bitter

CULTURAL NOTE:
The aesthetic sense of 渋(しぶ)い is deeply rooted in Japanese culture, valuing understated elegance over flashiness. It's often used to praise older men's fashion sense, traditional crafts, or classic design choices. This meaning expanded from the original taste sensation.
