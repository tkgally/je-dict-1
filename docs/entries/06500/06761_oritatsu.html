1. to step down onto; to alight and stand; to descend upon (a place)
Compound verb emphasizing the moment of setting foot somewhere, often used for significant arrivals.
飛行機(ひこうき)から降(お)り立(た)つと、暖(あたた)かい風(かぜ)が吹(ふ)いていた。
When I stepped off the plane, a warm wind was blowing.
初(はじ)めて日本(にほん)の地(ち)に降(お)り立(た)った。
I set foot on Japanese soil for the first time.
宇宙(うちゅう)飛行士(ひこうし)が月(つき)に降(お)り立(た)った瞬間(しゅんかん)は歴史的(れきしてき)だった。
The moment the astronaut stepped onto the moon was historic.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). Formed from 降(お)りる (to descend) + 立(た)つ (to stand), so the picture is of coming down and standing on the spot — the arrival, not the journey.

PARTICLE PATTERNS:

  • [place]に降(お)り立(た)つ - to set foot at [place]
  • [vehicle]から降(お)り立(た)つ - to step down from [vehicle]

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 地(ち)に降(お)り立(た)つ - to set foot on the ground
  • 空港(くうこう)に降(お)り立(た)つ - to arrive at an airport
  • 異国(いこく)の地(ち)に降(お)り立(た)つ - to set foot in a foreign land

USAGE:
Literary and dramatic where 降(お)りる is plain. Reserve it for arrivals that matter — a first visit to a country, a landing on the moon, a celebrity emerging at an airport. Getting off a bus at your usual stop is 降(お)りる, not 降(お)り立(た)つ.
