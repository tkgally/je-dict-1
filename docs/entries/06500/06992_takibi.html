焚(た)き火(び)

たきび
noun
bonfire; campfire
1. bonfire; campfire; open fire
A fire made outdoors, typically for warmth, cooking, burning waste, or enjoyment. Associated with camping, autumn activities, and traditional Japanese winter scenes.
キャンプ場(じょう)で焚(た)き火(び)を囲(かこ)んだ。
We gathered around the campfire at the campsite.
焚(た)き火(び)で焼(や)き芋(いも)を作(つく)った。
We made roasted sweet potatoes over the bonfire.
住宅地(じゅうたくち)では焚(た)き火(び)が禁止(きんし)されている。
Open fires are prohibited in residential areas.

ORTHOGRAPHY:
Also written 焚火(たきび) or たき火(び). 焚(た)く is outside the standard kanji list, so the mixed and all-kana spellings are common in ordinary writing.

COLLOCATIONS:

  • 焚(た)き火(び)をする: to have a fire going
  • 焚(た)き火(び)を囲(かこ)む: to gather around a fire
  • 焚(た)き火(び)を消(け)す: to put a fire out
  • 焚(た)き火(び)台(だい): a portable fire stand, standard camping equipment now that burning directly on the ground is discouraged

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
焚(た)き火(び) carries strong nostalgic associations with winter — the 1941 children's song 「たきび」 has children warming their hands at a neighbour's fire, with 焼(や)き芋(いも) roasting in the embers. In practice, open burning is now restricted in most urban and residential areas on fire-safety and air-quality grounds, so the word is met more often at campsites than in gardens.
