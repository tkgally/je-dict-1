億劫(おっくう)

おっくう
na-adjective
bothersome; troublesome; tiresome
1. bothersome; troublesome; tiresome; tedious
Feeling that something is too much effort or bother to do. Describes the reluctance to do something because it seems tiresome or inconvenient.
外出(がいしゅつ)するのが億劫(おっくう)だ。
I find it too bothersome to go out.
雨(あめ)の日(ひ)は買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)くのが億劫(おっくう)になる。
On rainy days, going shopping becomes tiresome.
歳(とし)を取(と)ると、何(なに)をするのも億劫(おっくう)になってくる。
As you get older, everything starts to feel like too much effort.

億劫(おっくう) describes a task that feels like more effort than one can face — not that it is difficult, but that getting started is unappealing. The person who feels this way is the topic; what feels burdensome is marked with が, usually a nominalized clause ending in の.

USAGE:

  • 〜するのが億劫(おっくう)だ (it's bothersome to do 〜)
  • 億劫(おっくう)に思(おも)う (to find something tiresome)
  • 億劫(おっくう)になる (to come to feel like too much effort)
  • As a na-adjective it also modifies nouns directly (億劫(おっくう)な仕事(しごと)), though the predicate use is far more common.

NUANCE:
Expresses 'I know I should do this, but I really don't want to make the effort.' It is about lacking motivation, not about being unable — and it is a statement of one's own feeling, so it is rarely used to describe someone else.

COMMON CONTEXTS:

  • 外出(がいしゅつ)が億劫(おっくう) (going out is a bother)
  • 料理(りょうり)が億劫(おっくう) (cooking is tedious)
  • 掃除(そうじ)が億劫(おっくう) (cleaning is tiresome)

ETYMOLOGY:
Originally a Buddhist term meaning 'one hundred million kalpas,' an inconceivably long stretch of time — hence something that feels as though it would take forever to get through.

RELATED WORDS:

  • 面倒(めんどう) (troublesome — about the task itself being fiddly, rather than about one's own reluctance)
  • 面倒臭(めんどうくさ)い (a blunter, more colloquial version of the same complaint)

Related Words

Synonym: 面倒(めんどう) (bothersome)
