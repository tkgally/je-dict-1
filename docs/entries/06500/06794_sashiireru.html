1. to insert, to put in
To insert or put something into a space or opening.
鍵(かぎ)を鍵穴(かぎあな)に差(さ)し入(い)れる。
Insert the key into the keyhole.
手紙(てがみ)を封筒(ふうとう)に差(さ)し入(い)れた。
I slipped the letter into the envelope.
コインを自動販売機(じどうはんばいき)に差(さ)し入(い)れた。
I inserted a coin into the vending machine.
2. to bring refreshments or gifts (to someone working)
To bring food, drinks, or gifts to people who are working hard or to someone in detention. The noun form 差(さ)し入(い)れ is commonly used.
残業(ざんぎょう)している同僚(どうりょう)に差(さ)し入(い)れをした。
I brought refreshments to my colleagues working overtime.
留置場(りゅうちじょう)にいる友人(ゆうじん)に本(ほん)を差(さ)し入(い)れた。
I brought books to my friend in detention.
部活(ぶかつ)の練習(れんしゅう)に差(さ)し入(い)れとしてジュースを持(も)って行(い)った。
I brought juice as refreshments to the club practice.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). The thing inserted or brought takes を and the destination or recipient takes に.

COMPOUND VERB: Formed from 差(さ)す (to insert/extend) + 入(い)れる (to put in).

NOUN FORM: 差(さ)し入(い)れ is commonly used as a noun meaning 'refreshments/gifts brought to someone' or 'the act of bringing such items.' In sense 2 the noun form is in fact far more common than the verb: people say 差(さ)し入(い)れをする more often than 差(さ)し入(い)れる.

SENSE 1 vs SENSE 2: Sense 1 (physical insertion) is neutral and slightly formal — everyday speech usually prefers plain 入(い)れる or 差(さ)す. Sense 2 is the sense learners meet most often.

CULTURAL NOTE: Bringing 差(さ)し入(い)れ to people working hard (such as during overtime, sports practice, or moving) is a common Japanese custom showing care and appreciation.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 差(さ)し入(い)れを持(も)って行(い)く (bring refreshments)
  • おやつを差(さ)し入(い)れる (bring snacks)
  • 差(さ)し入(い)れをもらう (receive refreshments)
