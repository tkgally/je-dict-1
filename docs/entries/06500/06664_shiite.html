強(し)いて

しいて
adverb
forcibly; compulsorily; if pressed; if I must say
1. forcibly; by force; compulsorily
Doing something against one's will or nature, or making someone do something unwillingly.
強(し)いて参加(さんか)する必要(ひつよう)はない。
You don't need to force yourself to participate.
嫌(いや)がる人(ひと)に強(し)いて飲(の)ませるのはよくない。
It's not right to force someone to drink when they don't want to.
自分(じぶん)の意見(いけん)を他人(たにん)に強(し)いてはいけない。
You shouldn't force your opinions on others.
2. if pressed; if I must say; dare I say
Used when offering an opinion reluctantly or when pressed to give an answer even though one doesn't have a strong opinion.
強(し)いて選(えら)ぶなら、こっちのほうがいいかもしれない。
If I had to choose, this one might be better.
強(し)いて言(い)えば、赤(あか)より青(あお)のほうが好(す)きかな。
If I had to say, I guess I like blue more than red.
強(し)いて挙(あ)げるなら、時間(じかん)が足(た)りなかったことが問題(もんだい)だった。
If I had to point out something, the problem was that we didn't have enough time.

ETYMOLOGY:
The て-form of the verb 強(し)いる (to force, to compel), now fixed as an adverb.

SENSE 1 - FORCIBLY:

Doing something, or making someone do something, against their inclination:

  • 強(し)いて〜する (to do ~ by force, to force oneself to ~)
  • 強(し)いて〜させる (to force someone to ~)

It is most often negated: 強(し)いて〜必要(ひつよう)はない (there is no need to force it), 強(し)いてはいけない (you must not force it). A plain affirmative sounds stiff and literary.

SENSE 2 - IF PRESSED:

Followed by a conditional, this is one of the most useful hedges in spoken Japanese:

  • 強(し)いて言(い)えば (if I had to say)
  • 強(し)いて挙(あ)げるなら (if I had to name one)
  • 強(し)いて選(えら)ぶなら (if I had to choose)

The speaker is signalling that they have no strong preference and are answering only because they were asked, so the opinion that follows should not be weighed too heavily. Sentence-final かな or かもしれない often softens it further.

SIMILAR ADVERBS:

  • あえて - to do something deliberately despite the difficulty or the reasons not to; a positive choice, where 強(し)いて is closer to reluctance
  • 無理(むり)やり - forcibly, by brute force; blunter and more physical than sense 1, and never used for the hedging sense
