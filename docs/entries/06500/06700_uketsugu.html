1. to inherit; to succeed to
To receive something (property, traditions, skills, etc.) from a predecessor and continue with it.
父(ちち)の仕事(しごと)を受(う)け継(つ)いだ。
I took over my father's work.
祖父(そふ)から家業(かぎょう)を受(う)け継(つ)いだ。
I inherited the family business from my grandfather.
母(はは)の料理(りょうり)の腕(うで)を受(う)け継(つ)ぎたい。
I want to inherit my mother's cooking skills.
この伝統(でんとう)は何(なん)世代(せだい)にもわたって受(う)け継(つ)がれてきた。
This tradition has been passed down through many generations.
先代(せんだい)の社長(しゃちょう)が築(きず)いた信頼(しんらい)を受(う)け継(つ)ぎ、会社(かいしゃ)をさらに成長(せいちょう)させた。
He inherited the trust built by the previous president and grew the company further.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 受(う)ける (to receive) + 継(つ)ぐ (to succeed, to inherit)

USAGE:

  • What is inherited takes を; the predecessor it comes from takes から (example 2).
  • The object is typically something intangible and continuing — a trade, a tradition, a skill, a temperament — rather than a one-off physical object. For simply receiving a thing, 受(う)け取(と)る is the ordinary verb.
  • Very often passive: 受(う)け継(つ)がれる describes traditions or qualities being passed down without naming who did the passing.
  • 技術(ぎじゅつ)が受(う)け継(つ)がれる (skills are passed down)
  • 意志(いし)を受(う)け継(つ)ぐ (to carry on someone's will)
  • 財産(ざいさん)を受(う)け継(つ)ぐ (to inherit property)
  • DNAを受(う)け継(つ)ぐ (to inherit genes)

ASPECT:
受(う)け継(つ)いでいる and the passive 受(う)け継(つ)がれている both name a resulting state — "has taken it over and now holds it", "is still being carried on today" — not an action in progress. Example 4's 受(う)け継(つ)がれてきた adds きた to trace that state back through time.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 引(ひ)き継(つ)ぐ - handing over duties and responsibilities at a changeover; neutral about lineage, and normal in office contexts
  • 継(つ)ぐ - simpler and broader; 家(いえ)を継(つ)ぐ is the standard phrase for succeeding to a household
