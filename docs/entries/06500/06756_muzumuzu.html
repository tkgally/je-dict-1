1. itching; ticklish
An itchy or tickling sensation on the body.
背中(せなか)がむずむずする。
My back is itching.
鼻(はな)がむずむずしてくしゃみが出(で)そうだ。
My nose is tickling and I feel like I'm going to sneeze.
花粉(かふん)の季節(きせつ)になると、目(め)や鼻(はな)がむずむずする。
When pollen season comes, my eyes and nose start to itch.
2. restless; impatient; eager
A feeling of being unable to stay still due to impatience or anticipation.
早(はや)く行(い)きたくてむずむずしている。
I'm restless and eager to go.
何(なに)かしたくてむずむずする。
I'm itching to do something.
新(あたら)しいゲームが発売(はつばい)されるのが待(ま)ちきれなくて、むずむずしている。
I can't wait for the new game to come out and I'm getting restless.

USAGE:
むずむず(と) + verb / むずむずする

The shared image is a small, persistent irritation just under the surface — literal on the skin, figurative in the mind. Both senses describe a state you want to end, not one you enjoy.

SENSE 1 — physical itching:

  • 肌(はだ)がむずむずする (skin is itching)
  • 虫刺(むしさ)されがむずむずする (a bug bite is itching)
  • 花粉症(かふんしょう)で鼻(はな)がむずむずする (nose tickles from hay fever)

Typically a light, creeping itch — the kind that makes you want to squirm rather than scratch hard. かゆい is the plain adjective for a strong itch.

SENSE 2 — psychological restlessness:

  • 〜たくてむずむずする (to be itching to do something)
  • 何(なに)かしたくてむずむずする (to feel an urge to do something)

The body-level metaphor stays alive here: the restlessness is felt physically, as an inability to sit still.

MEDICAL:
むずむず脚(あし)症候群(しょうこうぐん) is the standard Japanese name for Restless Leg Syndrome — one of the few places this onomatopoeia appears in formal writing.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • うずうず: itching to act. Only the psychological sense, and the eagerness is stronger and more forward-looking.
  • かゆい: itchy. A plain adjective describing the sensation directly, with no restlessness reading.
  • そわそわ: fidgety. Restlessness from nerves, and visible to others; むずむず is felt from inside.
