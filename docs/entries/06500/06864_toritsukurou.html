1. to patch up; to mend temporarily
To make quick repairs or fixes to something.
壊(こわ)れた椅子(いす)をとりあえず取(と)り繕(つくろ)った。
I patched up the broken chair for now.
破(やぶ)れた服(ふく)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)って着(き)ている。
I'm wearing clothes that have been patched up.
雨漏(あまも)りを応急(おうきゅう)的(てき)に取(と)り繕(つくろ)った。
I temporarily patched up the roof leak.
2. to keep up appearances; to gloss over
To hide problems or faults to maintain a good impression.
失敗(しっぱい)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)おうとしたが、バレてしまった。
I tried to cover up my mistake, but it was found out.
体裁(ていさい)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)っても意味(いみ)がない。
There's no point in just keeping up appearances.
笑顔(えがお)で取(と)り繕(つくろ)っても、本心(ほんしん)は隠(かく)せない。
Even if you put on a smile to cover it up, you can't hide your true feelings.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 取(と)る (to take, here little more than a prefix adding briskness) + 繕(つくろ)う (to mend). The base verb 繕(つくろ)う already carries both the literal mending sense and the "keeping up appearances" sense; 取(と)り繕(つくろ)う adds the suggestion of doing it hastily, on the spot.

USAGE:

  • その場(ば)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)う (to smooth over the situation)
  • 見栄(みえ)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)う (to keep up appearances for vanity)
  • 欠点(けってん)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)う (to gloss over faults)

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive, taking を for the damage, fault, or situation being patched over.

NUANCE:
Sense 2 is by far the more common in modern use, and it is unflattering: the speaker is pointing out that only the surface was dealt with. The fixed collocation その場(ば)を取(と)り繕(つくろ)う — smoothing over an awkward moment so the conversation can move on — is worth learning as a unit.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ごまかす is the everyday verb for fudging or covering something up, and is stronger on the deception side.
  • 繕(つくろ)う alone is the ordinary verb for mending torn clothes, and is the neutral choice when the meaning is literal repair.
