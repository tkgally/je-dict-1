1. to kick away; to kick hard
To kick something or someone forcefully.
邪魔(じゃま)な石(いし)を蹴飛(けと)ばした。
I kicked away the stone that was in the way.
空(あ)き缶(かん)を蹴飛(けと)ばして歩(ある)く。
To walk while kicking an empty can.
布団(ふとん)を蹴飛(けと)ばして寝(ね)ている。
He's sleeping with the blanket kicked off.
2. to reject; to turn down flatly
To refuse an offer or proposal dismissively.
彼(かれ)は高額(こうがく)のオファーを蹴飛(けと)ばした。
He turned down the generous offer.
せっかくの昇進(しょうしん)のチャンスを蹴飛(けと)ばした。
She turned down a rare chance for promotion.
条件(じょうけん)が気(き)に入(い)らなくて、契約(けいやく)を蹴飛(けと)ばした。
I didn't like the terms, so I rejected the contract.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 蹴(け)る (to kick) + 飛(と)ばす (to send flying). The second half supplies the force — the object does not merely receive a kick, it goes flying.

USAGE:

  • 申(もう)し出(で)を蹴飛(けと)ばす (to reject an offer)
  • 提案(ていあん)を蹴飛(けと)ばす (to reject a proposal)
  • 布団(ふとん)を蹴飛(けと)ばす (to kick off the blanket)

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive, taking を for the thing kicked or the offer refused. There is no intransitive counterpart; the intransitive image is carried by 飛(と)ぶ on its own.

NUANCE:
Stronger and more physical than plain 蹴(け)る, and distinctly casual. In sense 2 it is a speaker's dismissive characterisation of a refusal — you would not use it in the wording of a formal notice, where 断(ことわ)る or 拒否(きょひ)する belongs. Saying someone 蹴飛(けと)ばした an offer suggests they threw away something others would have taken.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 断(ことわ)る is the neutral verb for declining, usable in any register.
  • 拒否(きょひ)する is formal refusal, the word of documents and news reports.

Related Words

Related: 蹴(け)る (to kick (base verb))
