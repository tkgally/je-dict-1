1. to change (footwear or legwear)
To take off one pair of shoes, slippers, pants, or other items worn on the lower body and put on a different pair. Commonly used for changing shoes when entering a building.
玄関(げんかん)でスリッパに履(は)き替(か)えてください。
Please change into slippers at the entrance.
運動靴(うんどうぐつ)に履(は)き替(か)えて体育館(たいいくかん)に行(い)った。
I changed into athletic shoes and went to the gym.
冬(ふゆ)タイヤに履(は)き替(か)える時期(じき)だ。
It's time to change to winter tires.
汚(よご)れたズボンを履(は)き替(か)えてから出(で)かけた。
I changed out of my dirty pants before going out.
学校(がっこう)に着(つ)いたらまず上履(うわば)きに履(は)き替(か)えなければならない。
When you arrive at school, you have to change into indoor shoes first.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). Two patterns, distinguished by particle:

  • Xを履(は)き替(か)える - change out of X (X is what you take off)
  • Xに履(は)き替(か)える - change into X (X is what you put on)

The に pattern is the more common one.

ASPECT: ている gives the resulting state as often as the ongoing action — もう履(は)き替(か)えている normally means 'has already changed (and is now wearing them)'.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 履(は)く (to wear on the lower body or feet) + 替(か)える (to change, exchange).

USAGE:

Applies to anything worn on the lower body that is being swapped:

  • 靴(くつ)を履(は)き替(か)える (to change shoes)
  • スリッパに履(は)き替(か)える (to change into slippers)
  • ズボンを履(は)き替(か)える (to change pants)
  • タイヤを履(は)き替(か)える (to change tires - extended usage, since a car 'wears' tires)

JAPANESE CULTURE:
Changing shoes is an important custom in Japan. At schools, workplaces, homes, and many public buildings, visitors change from outdoor shoes (外履(そとば)き) to indoor shoes (内履(うちば)き / 上履(うわば)き).

SIMILAR COMPOUND VERBS:

  • 着替(きが)える - to change clothes (upper body or general)
  • 乗(の)り換(か)える - to transfer (vehicles)
