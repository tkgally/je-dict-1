1. to jump at; to pounce on
To physically leap onto someone or something.
猫(ねこ)が獲物(えもの)に飛(と)び付(つ)いた。
The cat pounced on its prey.
子供(こども)たちがお菓子(かし)に飛(と)び付(つ)いた。
The children pounced on the sweets.
犬(いぬ)が帰(かえ)ってきた飼(か)い主(ぬし)に飛(と)び付(つ)いた。
The dog jumped on its owner who had just come home.
2. to jump at; to eagerly seize
To eagerly accept an offer or opportunity without hesitation.
チャンスに飛(と)び付(つ)く。
To jump at a chance.
そんな怪(あや)しい話(はなし)にすぐ飛(と)び付(つ)いてはいけない。
You shouldn't jump at such a suspicious offer.
安(やす)いからといって飛(と)び付(つ)くと、後(あと)で後悔(こうかい)することがある。
If you eagerly jump at something just because it's cheap, you may regret it later.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 飛(と)ぶ (to jump, to fly) + 付(つ)く (to attach, to stick)

TRANSITIVITY:

自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). The target takes に, never を:

  • Xに飛(と)び付(つ)く (to jump at X)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 話(はなし)に飛(と)び付(つ)く (to jump at an offer)
  • 獲物(えもの)に飛(と)び付(つ)く (to pounce on prey)
  • チャンスに飛(と)び付(つ)く (to seize an opportunity)

NUANCE:
Sense 1 is a single sudden movement ending in contact, so it is normally reported in the past (飛(と)び付(つ)いた) rather than as an ongoing action. Used figuratively (sense 2), it carries clear disapproval: the person grabbed the offer too fast to think it through, which is why it so often appears in warnings (examples 5 and 6).
