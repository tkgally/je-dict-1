振(ふ)り切(き)る

verb (godan, transitive)

1. to shake off; to break free from
To forcefully remove or escape from someone or something holding on.
彼女(かのじょ)は追(お)いすがる彼(かれ)の手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)って逃(に)げた。
She shook off his clinging hand and fled.
警官(けいかん)の手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)って逃走(とうそう)した。
He shook off the police officer's grip and fled.
過去(かこ)のしがらみを振(ふ)り切(き)る。
Break free from past entanglements.
子供(こども)が母親(ははおや)の手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)って走(はし)り出(だ)した。
The child shook off their mother's hand and ran off.
引(ひ)き止(と)める声(こえ)を振(ふ)り切(き)って出(で)て行(い)った。
She left, shaking off the voices trying to stop her.
2. to outrun; to leave behind
To run faster than a pursuer and escape.
ランナーは追跡者(ついせきしゃ)を振(ふ)り切(き)ってゴールした。
The runner outran the pursuer and crossed the finish line.
最後(さいご)のスパートで他(ほか)の選手(せんしゅ)を振(ふ)り切(き)った。
In the final sprint, she outran the other athletes.
パトカーを振(ふ)り切(き)るのは不可能(ふかのう)だ。
It's impossible to outrun a police car.
追(お)っ手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)って山(やま)に逃(に)げ込(こ)んだ。
He outran his pursuers and escaped into the mountains.
ライバルチームを振(ふ)り切(き)って優勝(ゆうしょう)した。
They left the rival team behind and won the championship.
3. to overcome; to push past
To overcome emotions, doubts, or opposition.
迷(まよ)いを振(ふ)り切(き)って決断(けつだん)した。
I overcame my hesitation and made a decision.
不安(ふあん)を振(ふ)り切(き)って一歩(いっぽ)を踏(ふ)み出(だ)した。
I pushed past my anxiety and took the first step.
反対(はんたい)を振(ふ)り切(き)って計画(けいかく)を実行(じっこう)した。
They pushed past the opposition and carried out the plan.
未練(みれん)を振(ふ)り切(き)って新(あたら)しい生活(せいかつ)を始(はじ)めた。
I overcame my lingering attachment and started a new life.
眠気(ねむけ)を振(ふ)り切(き)って仕事(しごと)を続(つづ)けた。
I pushed through my drowsiness and continued working.

振(ふ)り切(き)る means to break free from something that is holding you back — a physical grip, a pursuer, or an emotional attachment. It always implies force and a clean, decisive break.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 振(ふ)る (to shake, to swing) + 切(き)る (to cut). The 切(き)る half supplies the sense of finishing the action off completely.

USAGE:
Transitive: whatever is broken away from takes を. The three uses shade into one another — physical (a hand, a grip), competitive (a pursuer, a rival), and emotional (hesitation, attachment, drowsiness). In sports reporting it is the standard word for pulling away from the field.

The て form 振(ふ)り切(き)って is by far the most common shape, because breaking free is usually the setup for a following action: 逃(に)げる (flee), 走(はし)り出(だ)す (start running), 決断(けつだん)する (make up one's mind).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)る: to shake off someone's hand
  • 追(お)っ手(て)を振(ふ)り切(き)る: to shake off pursuers
  • 未練(みれん)を振(ふ)り切(き)る: to overcome lingering attachment
  • 反対(はんたい)を振(ふ)り切(き)る: to push past opposition
