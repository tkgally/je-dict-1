1. to hold out, to endure, to withstand
To continue to resist or survive under difficult conditions; to hang on despite hardship.
会社(かいしゃ)はなんとか不況(ふきょう)を持(も)ち堪(こた)えた。
The company somehow managed to hold out through the recession.
堤防(ていぼう)が洪水(こうずい)に持(も)ち堪(こた)えるか心配(しんぱい)だ。
I'm worried whether the levee will hold against the flood.
あと少(すこ)し持(も)ち堪(こた)えれば、救援(きゅうえん)が来(く)る。
If we can just hold out a little longer, help will arrive.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). The subject is whatever is holding out, marked with が or は.

PARTICLES: Although the verb is intransitive, the hardship weathered is regularly marked with を (不況(ふきょう)を持(も)ち堪(こた)える, 'weather the recession') — a traversal を rather than a true direct object. The pressure resisted can also be marked with に (攻撃(こうげき)に持(も)ち堪(こた)える).

COMPOUND VERB: Formed from 持(も)つ (to hold) + 堪(こた)える (to endure). The combination emphasizes sustained endurance or resistance.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 不況(ふきょう)を持(も)ち堪(こた)える (weather a recession)
  • 攻撃(こうげき)に持(も)ち堪(こた)える (withstand an attack)
  • 困難(こんなん)に持(も)ち堪(こた)える (endure hardships)
  • なんとか持(も)ち堪(こた)える (somehow hold out)

SIMILAR WORDS: 耐(た)える emphasizes bearing pain or difficulty; 持(も)ち堪(こた)える emphasizes maintaining a position or continuing to function. 堪(こら)える (same kanji, read こらえる) is a different verb meaning to hold back a feeling or urge.
