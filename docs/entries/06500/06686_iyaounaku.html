否応(いやおう)なく

いやおうなく
adverb
inevitably; willy-nilly; like it or not
1. inevitably; willy-nilly; whether one likes it or not
Describes something that happens regardless of one's wishes or consent. Indicates that there is no choice in the matter—it will happen whether you want it to or not.
否応(いやおう)なく決断(けつだん)を迫(せま)られた。
I was forced to make a decision whether I liked it or not.
グローバル化(か)で否応(いやおう)なく英語(えいご)が必要(ひつよう)になった。
With globalization, English became inevitably necessary.
時代(じだい)の流(なが)れに否応(いやおう)なく巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる。
One gets swept up in the currents of the times, like it or not.

FORMATION:
否(いや) (no, refusal) + 応(おう) (yes, consent) + なく (without)
Literally "without a yes or a no" — that is, without asking whether you agree.

VARIANTS:

  • 否応(いやおう)なく - the standard adverbial form
  • 否応(いやおう)なしに - same meaning, slightly more emphatic and a little more literary
  • 有無(うむ)を言(い)わさず - without letting anyone object; stresses an agent overriding you, where 否応(いやおう)なく usually points at circumstances

USAGE:

否応(いやおう)なく modifies a verb, and that verb is nearly always one of being acted upon — a passive, or a change that happens to the subject:

  • 否応(いやおう)なく巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる (to be drawn in regardless)
  • 否応(いやおう)なく従(したが)う (to comply whether one likes it or not)
  • 否応(いやおう)なく変(か)わる (to change inevitably)

It is not used of things the subject chose to do, so 否応なく行った sounds wrong unless the going was imposed.

NUANCE:
The force is external — social change, an employer, the passage of time — and the speaker's own preference has been bypassed rather than overruled in argument. It is common in writing about society and history, and carries a note of resignation.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 必然(ひつぜん) - inevitability as a logical outcome; states that something had to follow, without the sense of a will being ignored

Related Words

Related: 必然(ひつぜん) (inevitability, necessity)
