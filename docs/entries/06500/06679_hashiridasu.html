1. to start running; to break into a run
To suddenly begin running. Describes the moment when someone transitions from standing or walking into a running motion.
子供(こども)たちが校庭(こうてい)に走(はし)り出(だ)した。
The children broke into a run toward the schoolyard.
信号(しんごう)が青(あお)になったら走(はし)り出(だ)した。
When the light turned green, I started running.
彼(かれ)は急(きゅう)に走(はし)り出(だ)して姿(すがた)が見(み)えなくなった。
He suddenly started running and disappeared from sight.
犬(いぬ)がリードを引(ひ)っ張(ぱ)って走(はし)り出(だ)したので、転(ころ)びそうになった。
The dog pulled the leash and started running, and I nearly fell over.
遅刻(ちこく)しそうだと気(き)づいて、慌(あわ)てて走(はし)り出(だ)したが、電車(でんしゃ)にはもう間(ま)に合(あ)わなかった。
I realized I was going to be late and frantically broke into a run, but I still missed the train.

ASPECT: 〜出(だ)す marks the onset of the motion, so 走(はし)り出(だ)す normally appears in the past (走(はし)り出(だ)した) or in て-form leading into what happened next. For running that is under way, use 走(はし)っている.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 走(はし)る (to run) + 出(だ)す (to begin).

The 〜出(だ)す pattern indicates the beginning of an action. With motion verbs it emphasizes the moment of transition into that motion, often abruptly and without warning.

SIMILAR COMPOUND VERBS:

  • 歩(ある)き出(だ)す - to start walking
  • 泳(およ)ぎ出(だ)す - to start swimming
  • 飛(と)び出(だ)す - to jump/rush out
  • 笑(わら)い出(だ)す - to start laughing
  • 泣(な)き出(だ)す - to start crying

USAGE NOTE:
走(はし)り出(だ)す focuses on the moment of starting to run; 走(はし)り出(で)る (different reading of the second element) means to run out of a place.
