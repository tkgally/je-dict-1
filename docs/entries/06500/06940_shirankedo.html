知(し)らんけど

しらんけど
expression
I don't know though; just saying
1. I don't know though; just saying; but don't quote me on that
A casual expression used as a hedge after making a statement, often from Kansai dialect.
あの店(みせ)、おいしいらしいよ。知(し)らんけど。
Apparently that restaurant is good. I don't know though.
明日(あした)は晴(は)れるって。知(し)らんけど。
They say it'll be sunny tomorrow. Just saying.
あの二人(ふたり)、付(つ)き合(あ)ってるらしい。知(し)らんけど。
I heard those two are dating. But don't quote me on that.

Originally 関西弁(かんさいべん), this phrase spread nationwide through social media and TV during the 2010s and is now used by speakers with no connection to Kansai.

STRUCTURE: 知(し)らん is the Kansai (and wider western) negative of 知(し)る, where standard Japanese uses 知(し)らない, plus the concessive けど ("though").

USAGE: Tacked onto the end of a statement to disclaim responsibility for its accuracy. The speaker passes information along while signalling they cannot vouch for it. It follows a complete sentence and is usually spoken as its own short unit, after a pause.

NUANCE:

  • Softens an assertion and avoids sounding too authoritative
  • Standard when relaying rumor or hearsay, often after らしい or って
  • Frequently a joke - the speaker has just given confident advice and then undercuts it, which is much of the appeal
  • Casual only: it would be evasive or flippant in a work or service setting

VARIATIONS:

  • 知(し)らんけど - the Kansai-origin form, and the one that spread
  • 知(し)らないけど - the standard-Japanese wording; grammatical, but without the set-phrase flavor
  • 分(わ)からんけど - "not that I understand it"; less fixed as a catchphrase

Related Words

Related: 知(し)る (to know)
Related: けど (but; although)
