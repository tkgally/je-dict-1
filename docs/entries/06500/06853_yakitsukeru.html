1. to burn in; to brand
To burn an image or mark onto something.
鉄(てつ)に印(しるし)を焼(や)き付(つ)ける。
To brand a mark onto iron.
牛(うし)に焼印(やきいん)を焼(や)き付(つ)ける。
To brand cattle with a branding iron.
木(き)の板(いた)に模様(もよう)を焼(や)き付(つ)けた。
I burned a pattern into a wooden board.
2. to sear into memory; to imprint
To make a strong impression that stays in one's mind.
その光景(こうけい)を目(め)に焼(や)き付(つ)けた。
I seared that scene into my memory.
彼女(かのじょ)の笑顔(えがお)を心(こころ)に焼(や)き付(つ)けた。
I imprinted her smile in my heart.
最後(さいご)の試合(しあい)を目(め)に焼(や)き付(つ)けておきたい。
I want to sear the final game into my memory.

A compound verb: 焼(や)く (to burn) + 付(つ)ける (to attach). Literally to fix an image or mark onto a surface by burning it there; sense 2 carries that image over to memory.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive)
  • The mark or image takes を; the surface it goes onto takes に: 板(いた)に模様(もよう)を焼(や)き付(つ)ける
  • Its intransitive partner is 焼(や)き付(つ)く, where the image itself is the subject: 光景(こうけい)が目(め)に焼(や)き付(つ)く

SENSE 2 (memory):

In the figurative sense the speaker deliberately fixes something in mind, so the verb usually appears with volitional endings — 焼(や)き付(つ)けたい, 焼(や)き付(つ)けておく. The involuntary counterpart ("it stuck with me whether I wanted it to or not") is the intransitive 焼(や)き付(つ)く.

  • 目(め)に焼(や)き付(つ)ける (to fix in one's eyes)
  • 心(こころ)に焼(や)き付(つ)ける (to imprint on one's heart)
  • 記憶(きおく)に焼(や)き付(つ)ける (to burn into memory)

ASPECT (ている):
焼(や)き付(つ)けている describes the act in progress. For the resulting state — the image is already fixed there — Japanese uses the intransitive 焼(や)き付(つ)いている, which is why that form is far more common than the transitive one in everyday speech.
