よね

よね
particle
right?, isn't it? (seeking confirmation)
1. right?, isn't it?
A compound sentence-final particle combining よ (assertion) and ね (agreement-seeking), used to seek confirmation while softly asserting something.
明日(あした)は日曜日(にちようび)だよね。
Tomorrow is Sunday, right?
この店(みせ)、前(まえ)に来(き)たことあるよね。
We've been to this shop before, haven't we?
彼女(かのじょ)、綺麗(きれい)だよね。
She's beautiful, don't you think?
これ、高(たか)すぎるよね。
This is too expensive, isn't it?
夏休(なつやす)みはもう終(お)わったよね。早(はや)かったよね。
Summer vacation is already over, right? It went by fast, didn't it?

COMPOSITION: よ (assertion) + ね (agreement-seeking)

  • よ alone: "I'm telling you" (informing)
  • ね alone: "isn't it?" (pure agreement)
  • よね: "I think X, don't you agree?"

NUANCE: Softer than よ alone. The speaker:

  • Believes something to be true
  • Wants confirmation or agreement
  • Leaves room for the listener to disagree

Because よね invites the listener to weigh in, it is the safer choice when you are fairly sure but not certain. Using よ alone in the same slot can sound like you are correcting the listener.

COMMON USES:

  • Confirming shared information: 約束(やくそく)は3時(じ)だよね (The appointment is at 3, right?)
  • Seeking agreement: いい天気(てんき)だよね (Nice weather, isn't it?)
  • Rhetorical confirmation: 嘘(うそ)でしょ、これ高(たか)いよね (No way, this is expensive, right?)

ATTACHMENT: よね attaches to a complete plain-form predicate. After a noun or na-adjective, the copula だ is required in casual speech (学生(がくせい)だよね), and the polite equivalent is ですよね (学生(がくせい)ですよね). Verbs and i-adjectives take よね directly (行(い)くよね、高(たか)いよね).

VS. でしょう:

  • よね: casual, conversational
  • でしょう: slightly more formal, can be used for predictions

INTONATION:

  • Rising: genuinely seeking confirmation
  • Flat/falling: rhetorical, expecting agreement

Related Words

Related: よ (assertion particle (first half of よね))
Related: ね (agreement-seeking particle (second half of よね))
Contrast: でしょう (more formal confirmation-seeking form)
Contrast: でしょ (casual でしょう; stronger expectation of agreement)
