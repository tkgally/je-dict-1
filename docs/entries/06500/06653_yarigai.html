やり甲斐(がい)

やりがい
noun
sense of fulfillment; worth doing; rewarding
1. sense of fulfillment; being worth doing; rewarding quality
The feeling that an activity is meaningful and worth the effort. Something that makes one feel satisfied and motivated when doing or accomplishing it.
この仕事(しごと)はやり甲斐(がい)がある。
This job is rewarding.
やり甲斐(がい)を感(かん)じられる仕事(しごと)を探(さが)している。
I'm looking for a job where I can feel a sense of fulfillment.
難(むずか)しい課題(かだい)だからこそ、やり甲斐(がい)がある。
Precisely because it's a difficult challenge, it's worth doing.

やり甲斐(がい) is the sense that an effort is worth making — the reward one gets back from doing something demanding. It is a property of the activity as experienced, so it takes ある (this job has it) rather than a verb of doing.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • やり甲斐(がい)がある (to be rewarding/worth doing)
  • やり甲斐(がい)を感(かん)じる (to feel a sense of fulfillment)
  • やり甲斐(がい)のある仕事(しごと) (a rewarding job)

PRODUCTIVE PATTERN 〜甲斐(がい):

甲斐(かい) attaches to a verb's masu-stem — voiced to -がい in this position — to mean 'worth doing [verb]'. The base here is やる:

  • 生(い)き甲斐(がい) (reason for living, purpose in life)
  • 読(よ)む → 読(よ)み甲斐(がい) (worth reading)
  • 見(み)る → 見(み)甲斐(がい) (worth seeing)
  • 食(た)べる → 食(た)べ甲斐(がい) (worth eating, filling)

WORK CULTURE:
やり甲斐(がい) comes up constantly in Japanese workplace talk, and employee surveys treat it as a headline metric. Note the critical coinage やり甲斐(がい)搾取(さくしゅ) ('fulfillment exploitation') — using an appeal to meaningful work to justify poor pay.

WRITING NOTE:
Usually written all in hiragana as やりがい outside formal writing.
