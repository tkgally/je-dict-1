1. respectful, reverent, deferential
Describes behavior or manner that shows deep respect, often through formal gestures, careful handling, or humble demeanor.
うやうやしく頭(あたま)を下(さ)げた。
They bowed their head respectfully.
神主(かんぬし)がうやうやしく祝詞(のりと)を奏上(そうじょう)した。
The Shinto priest reverently recited the prayer.
うやうやしい態度(たいど)で接(せっ)する。
To treat someone with a respectful attitude.
賞状(しょうじょう)をうやうやしく受(う)け取(と)った。
They received the certificate with great reverence.

KANJI: Usually written in hiragana; the kanji spelling 恭(うやうや)しい also occurs, mainly in formal or literary writing.

NUANCE: Describes formal, ceremonial, or deeply respectful conduct - deliberate and careful, often traditional or ritual, and occasionally so elaborate that it tips into stiffness. It describes how someone acts, not how they feel, so it attaches to gestures and bearing rather than to inner states.

COMMON CONTEXTS:

  • Religious ceremonies
  • Formal receptions and award presentations
  • Handling precious or sacred objects
  • Meeting people of high status

GRAMMAR: The adverbial form うやうやしく is by far the most common - it modifies a verb to say the action was performed reverently. The plain form before a noun (うやうやしい態度(たいど)) is also natural; as a bare predicate it is rare.

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 丁寧(ていねい) - polite and careful; the everyday word, with no ceremonial weight
  • 慇懃(いんぎん) - courteous in a stiff, formal way; often heard in 慇懃(いんぎん)無礼(ぶれい) (politeness so exaggerated it insults)
  • 敬虔(けいけん) - pious, devout; about religious feeling rather than outward manner

CAUTION: Because the word marks conspicuous formality, it can be used ironically to suggest the respect is overdone or insincere.
