1. to intervene; to step in; to meddle
To become involved in a situation, dispute, or process, especially one that does not directly concern oneself. Often used in political, military, or economic contexts, but also in personal relationships.
政府(せいふ)が市場(しじょう)に介入(かいにゅう)する。
The government intervenes in the market.
他人(たにん)の問題(もんだい)に介入(かいにゅう)すべきではない。
You should not meddle in other people's problems.
国連(こくれん)が紛争(ふんそう)の解決(かいけつ)のために軍事(ぐんじ)介入(かいにゅう)を決定(けってい)した。
The United Nations decided on military intervention to resolve the conflict.

A formal word that often carries the nuance of stepping into something uninvited. Depending on context, can be neutral (legitimate intervention) or negative (unwelcome meddling).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 軍事(ぐんじ)介入(かいにゅう): military intervention
  • 為替(かわせ)介入(かいにゅう): currency market intervention
  • 内政(ないせい)介入(かいにゅう): interference in internal affairs
  • 介入(かいにゅう)を控(ひか)える: to refrain from intervening
  • 過度(かど)な介入(かいにゅう): excessive intervention

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 干渉(かんしょう)する: to interfere — more strongly negative, implying unwanted meddling, while 介入(かいにゅう) can be either positive or negative depending on context
  • 仲裁(ちゅうさい)する: to mediate, to arbitrate — implies a neutral role in resolving a dispute, while 介入(かいにゅう) can mean taking sides or asserting power
  • 口出(くちだ)しする: to butt in, to offer unsolicited opinions — colloquial and negative, focused on verbal interference
