1. to withdraw; to retreat; to pull out
To pull back from a position, market, or activity, whether in a military, business, or general context.
軍(ぐん)が撤退(てったい)した。
The army withdrew.
その企業(きぎょう)は海外(かいがい)市場(しじょう)から撤退(てったい)した。
That company pulled out of the overseas market.
赤字(あかじ)が続(つづ)いたため、事業(じぎょう)からの撤退(てったい)を決(き)めた。
Due to continued losses, they decided to withdraw from the business.
被災(ひさい)地域(ちいき)から安全(あんぜん)に撤退(てったい)するよう指示(しじ)が出(で)た。
Orders were given to safely withdraw from the disaster-affected area.

Originally a military term meaning to retreat from a battlefield. Now widely used in business contexts for pulling out of a market or terminating operations. Implies a strategic, deliberate decision rather than a panicked flight.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 市場(しじょう)から撤退(てったい)する: to pull out of a market
  • 撤退(てったい)戦略(せんりゃく): withdrawal strategy
  • 完全(かんぜん)撤退(てったい): complete withdrawal
  • 段階的(だんかいてき)撤退(てったい): phased withdrawal
  • 撤退(てったい)を余儀(よぎ)なくされる: to be forced to withdraw

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 退却(たいきゃく)する: to retreat — more military-specific, implies retreating under pressure
  • 引(ひ)き揚(あ)げる: to pull out, to withdraw — can be used for people or investments
  • 離脱(りだつ)する: to break away, to leave — emphasizes leaving an organization or group
