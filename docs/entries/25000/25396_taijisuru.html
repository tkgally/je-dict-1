1. to exterminate; to slay; to get rid of
To defeat and eliminate monsters, pests, villains, or other undesirable things. Used both in folklore and legends (slaying demons) and in everyday life (getting rid of pests).
鬼(おに)を退治(たいじ)する。
To slay the demon.
台所(だいどころ)のゴキブリを退治(たいじ)した。
I got rid of the cockroach in the kitchen.
桃太郎(ももたろう)は犬(いぬ)と猿(さる)ときじを連(つ)れて、鬼(おに)ヶ島(しま)の鬼(おに)を退治(たいじ)しに行(い)った。
Momotaro took a dog, a monkey, and a pheasant with him and went to Onigashima to slay the demons.

A word deeply rooted in Japanese folklore, where heroes slay monsters and demons. In everyday use, commonly applied to getting rid of household pests. Has a somewhat dramatic or heroic tone even in mundane contexts.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 鬼(おに)退治(たいじ): demon slaying
  • 害虫(がいちゅう)退治(たいじ): pest extermination
  • ゴキブリ退治(たいじ): cockroach extermination
  • 悪者(わるもの)を退治(たいじ)する: to defeat the villain

CULTURAL NOTE:
Many famous Japanese folktales center on 退治(たいじ): 桃太郎(ももたろう)の鬼(おに)退治(たいじ) (Momotaro's demon slaying), 大江山(おおえやま)の酒呑童子(しゅてんどうじ)退治(たいじ) (slaying Shuten-doji), and 蜘蛛(くも)退治(たいじ) (spider slaying) are among the most well-known.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 駆除(くじょ)する: to exterminate (pests) — more technical and practical, used by pest control professionals, while 退治(たいじ) has a more narrative or dramatic quality
  • 倒(たお)す: to defeat — broader meaning of defeating an opponent, without the specific "eliminate the threat" nuance of 退治(たいじ)
