1. to stand out; to be set off; to look more attractive (by contrast or context)
to stand out; to be set off; to look more attractive (by contrast or context)
白(しろ)い皿(さら)で料理(りょうり)の色(いろ)が引(ひ)き立(た)つ。
The colors of the food stand out on a white plate.
赤(あか)いドレスが彼女(かのじょ)の美(うつく)しさを引(ひ)き立(た)てている。
The red dress sets off her beauty.
シンプルな額縁(がくぶち)のおかげで、絵(え)そのものの魅力(みりょく)が引(ひ)き立(た)っている。
Thanks to the simple frame, the appeal of the painting itself stands out.
わさびを少(すこ)し加(くわ)えると、刺身(さしみ)の味(あじ)が引(ひ)き立(た)つ。
Adding a little wasabi brings out the flavor of the sashimi.

Describes something looking more attractive, prominent, or noticeable thanks to its context, contrast, or an accompanying element. Used for visual appearance, flavors, talents, and other qualities. The transitive counterpart is 引(ひ)き立(た)てる (to set off; to make something stand out).

TRANSITIVITY:

  • 引(ひ)き立(た)つ (intransitive): to stand out
  • 引(ひ)き立(た)てる (transitive): to make something stand out; to set off; to promote

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 色(いろ)が引(ひ)き立(た)つ: colors stand out
  • 味(あじ)が引(ひ)き立(た)つ: flavor is brought out
  • 魅力(みりょく)が引(ひ)き立(た)つ: charm stands out
  • お互(たが)いを引(ひ)き立(た)てる: to bring out the best in each other

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 目立(めだ)つ: to stand out; to be conspicuous — can be positive or negative, while 引(ひ)き立(た)つ always implies looking better or more attractive
  • 映(は)える: to look good; to be photogenic — focuses on visual appeal, especially in photography or against a background
