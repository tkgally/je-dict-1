1. to accept a loss silently; to give up without protest; to swallow one's grievance
To resign oneself to an unfair situation without taking action or complaining, usually because one feels powerless to change it.
泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りするしかなかった。
I had no choice but to accept it silently.
被害(ひがい)に遭(あ)っても泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りする人(ひと)が多(おお)い。
Many people who suffer harm just accept it without protest.
詐欺(さぎ)に遭(あ)ったのに泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りせず、警察(けいさつ)に届(とど)けたほうがいい。
If you've been scammed, rather than giving up silently, you should report it to the police.

泣(な)き寝入(ねい)り literally means "crying oneself to sleep" (泣(な)く + 寝入(ねい)る), evoking the image of someone who can only cry and then fall asleep without resolving their problem. The expression conveys resignation in the face of injustice or mistreatment.

USAGE:
Used both as a noun (泣(な)き寝入(ねい)り) and as a suru verb. Often appears with しかない (have no choice but to) or せず (without doing).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りするしかない: have no choice but to accept it
  • 泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りせずに: without giving up quietly
  • 泣(な)き寝入(ねい)りさせない: not let someone be taken advantage of silently
  • 消費者(しょうひしゃ)の泣(な)き寝入(ねい)り: consumers quietly accepting unfair treatment

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 諦(あきら)める: to give up — more neutral; can be used for positive acceptance too
  • 我慢(がまん)する: to endure; to put up with — emphasizes patience and endurance rather than resigned defeat
  • 泣(な)く泣(な)く: reluctantly; tearfully — an adverb describing doing something unwillingly
