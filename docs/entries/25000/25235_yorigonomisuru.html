1. to be picky; to be choosy; to be fussy (about selecting)
to be picky; to be choosy; to be fussy (about selecting)
食(た)べ物(もの)を選(よ)り好(ごの)みしてはいけない。
You shouldn't be picky about food.
仕事(しごと)を選(よ)り好(ごの)みしている場合(ばあい)ではない。
This is no time to be choosy about jobs.
あまり選(よ)り好(ごの)みせずに、まずは挑戦(ちょうせん)してみよう。
Without being too picky, let's just give it a try first.
彼女(かのじょ)は洋服(ようふく)を選(よ)り好(ごの)みするので、買(か)い物(もの)に時間(じかん)がかかる。
She is so fussy about clothes that shopping takes a long time.

Describes being overly selective or fussy when choosing something, usually with a mildly negative nuance suggesting the person is being unreasonable or impractical. Often used in contexts where someone should be less selective — job hunting, food, dating, etc.

USAGE:

  • Often used in negative or admonishing contexts: 選(よ)り好(ごの)みするな (don't be picky)
  • The noun form 選(よ)り好(ごの)み is also common

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 食(た)べ物(もの)を選(よ)り好(ごの)みする: be picky about food
  • 仕事(しごと)を選(よ)り好(ごの)みする: be choosy about jobs
  • 選(よ)り好(ごの)みが激(はげ)しい: very picky

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 好(す)き嫌(きら)い: likes and dislikes (especially about food) — describes having preferences, while 選(よ)り好(ごの)み emphasizes the act of being overly selective
  • こだわる: to be particular about; to be finicky — can be positive (attention to quality) or negative, while 選(よ)り好(ごの)み is almost always negative
  • わがまま: selfish; willful — describes demanding behavior in general, not specifically about choosing
