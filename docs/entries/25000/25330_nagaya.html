長屋(ながや)

A type of traditional Japanese dwelling where multiple housing units share continuous walls under one long roof. Composed of 長(なが) (long) and 屋(や) (building, house). They were the standard housing for commoners in Edo-period cities and symbolize the tight-knit community life of that era.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 長屋(ながや)暮(ぐ)らし: tenement living
  • 長屋(ながや)の住人(じゅうにん): tenement residents
  • 長屋(ながや)門(もん): long gate (a type of gate building)

CULTURAL NOTE:
長屋(ながや) are a fixture of 落語(らくご) (traditional comic storytelling), where they serve as the setting for humorous tales of Edo-period commoners. The expression 長屋(ながや)の花見(はなみ) is a famous 落語(らくご) story about tenement dwellers improvising a cherry blossom viewing party.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 町家(まちや): traditional townhouse — a merchant's home with shop front
  • 一戸建(いっこだ)て: detached house — a standalone residence
  • テラスハウス: terraced house — the modern equivalent
