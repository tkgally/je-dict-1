1. to rebel; to resist; to defy
To actively oppose or resist authority, rules, or someone in a position of power. Often describes adolescent rebelliousness or resistance to oppressive systems.
親(おや)に反抗(はんこう)する。
To rebel against one's parents.
息子(むすこ)は最近(さいきん)、何(なに)を言(い)っても反抗(はんこう)する。
My son rebels no matter what I say to him lately.
不当(ふとう)な権力(けんりょく)に対(たい)して反抗(はんこう)することは、時(とき)に必要(ひつよう)なことだ。
Resisting unjust power is sometimes necessary.

Describes active resistance or defiance directed at authority figures or systems. Frequently used to describe the rebellious phase of adolescence.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 反抗(はんこう)期(き): rebellious phase (especially adolescence)
  • 反抗(はんこう)的(てき)な態度(たいど): defiant attitude
  • 反抗(はんこう)心(しん): spirit of defiance
  • 権力(けんりょく)に反抗(はんこう)する: to resist authority

USAGE:
The phrase 反抗(はんこう)期(き) is commonly used in parenting contexts to describe the typical rebellious phase children go through, roughly equivalent to the English "terrible twos" (第一(だいいち)反抗(はんこう)期(き)) and teenage rebellion (第二(だいに)反抗(はんこう)期(き)).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 抵抗(ていこう)する: to resist — broader in meaning and can include passive resistance or physical resistance, while 反抗(はんこう) specifically implies active defiance against authority
  • 逆(さか)らう: to go against, to disobey — more colloquial and often used for defying individuals rather than systems
