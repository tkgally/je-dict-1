One of the most frequently encountered words in polite Japanese, functioning both as a na-adjective meaning 'rude' and as a set expression for apologies and greetings. Literally means 'losing propriety' (失(しつ) = lose + 礼(れい) = courtesy).

KEY SET PHRASES:

  • 失礼(しつれい)します: used when entering a room, leaving, or beginning something that may impose on the other person
  • お先(さき)に失礼(しつれい)します: the standard phrase for leaving the office before colleagues
  • 失礼(しつれい)しました: I'm sorry (for what I just did)
  • 失礼(しつれい)ですが...: excuse me, but... (polite preface to a potentially imposing question)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 失礼(しつれい)な人(ひと): a rude person
  • 失礼(しつれい)な発言(はつげん): a rude remark
  • 大変(たいへん)失礼(しつれい)しました: I'm very sorry (more formal apology)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 無礼(ぶれい): rude; impolite — a stronger, more formal word that implies a more serious breach of etiquette
  • 不躾(ぶしつけ): blunt; tactless — describes behavior lacking in social finesse, often used self-deprecatingly
  • 非常識(ひじょうしき): lacking common sense — focuses on behavior that defies social norms more broadly
