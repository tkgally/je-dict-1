1. to cut off; to lop off; to chop off
To cut something so it falls away from the main body — a branch, hair, finger, edge of paper, etc. Implies a clean, complete separation.
枝(えだ)を切(き)り落(お)とす。
To lop off a branch.
紙(かみ)のはしを切(き)り落(お)とす。
To trim the edge of the paper.
庭師(にわし)が伸(の)びすぎた枝(えだ)を丁寧(ていねい)に切(き)り落(お)としていった。
The gardener carefully lopped off the overgrown branches.

Compound of 切(き)る (to cut) + 落(お)とす (to drop, let fall). The 落(お)とす element emphasizes that what is cut falls away — distinguishing it from a simple cut where the piece remains attached.

TRANSITIVITY:

Transitive. The thing cut off takes を; the body it is cut from takes から or appears as the modifier.

  • 木(き)から枝(えだ)を切(き)り落(お)とす: to lop a branch off the tree
  • 髪(かみ)を切(き)り落(お)とす: to chop one's hair off

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 枝(えだ)を切(き)り落(お)とす: to lop off a branch
  • 首(くび)を切(き)り落(お)とす: to behead
  • 余分(よぶん)を切(き)り落(お)とす: to trim away the excess
  • バッサリと切(き)り落(お)とす: to chop off in one decisive cut

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 切(き)る: to cut — basic, does not specify whether the piece separates
  • 切(き)り取(と)る: to cut out — emphasizes removing a piece for later use
  • 切(き)り離(はな)す: to detach by cutting — emphasizes the separation itself, not the falling
