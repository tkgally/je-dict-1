グループホーム

ぐるーぷほーむ
noun
group home (small-scale care facility)
1. group home; small-scale residential care facility
A small residential facility where a few people with dementia, disabilities, or other care needs live together with support from staff. Distinct from a large nursing home in scale and atmosphere.
母(はは)はグループホームで暮(く)らしている。
My mother lives in a group home.
近所(きんじょ)に認知症(にんちしょう)のグループホームができた。
A group home for dementia patients has opened in our neighborhood.
グループホームでは少人数(しょうにんずう)で家庭的(かていてき)な雰囲気(ふんいき)の中(なか)で生活(せいかつ)できる。
In a group home, residents live in a small-group, family-like atmosphere.

From English "group home." In Japan the term most often refers to facilities under the 介護(かいご)保険(ほけん) system for people with dementia (認知症(にんちしょう)対応型(たいおうがた)共同(きょうどう)生活(せいかつ)介護(かいご)), though it is also used for facilities serving people with intellectual or developmental disabilities.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • グループホームに入(はい)る: to move into a group home
  • 認知症(にんちしょう)のグループホーム: dementia group home
  • グループホームの職員(しょくいん): group-home staff

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 老人(ろうじん)ホーム: nursing home — larger-scale, more institutional
  • 特別(とくべつ)養護(ようご)老人(ろうじん)ホーム (特養(とくよう)): special nursing home — public, for people needing heavy care
  • デイサービス: day service — daytime-only care, residents go home at night
