大志(たいし)

たいし
noun
great ambition; lofty aspiration
1. great ambition; lofty aspiration
A grand, far-reaching goal for one's life — bigger than ordinary career or personal goals. A formal, somewhat literary word, famous from William S. Clark's saying 少年(しょうねん)よ、大志(たいし)を抱(いだ)け ("Boys, be ambitious").
大志(たいし)を抱(いだ)く。
To hold a great ambition.
若(わか)いうちに大志(たいし)を持(も)つことが大切(たいせつ)だ。
It's important to have great ambitions while young.
彼(かれ)は大志(たいし)を抱(いだ)いて故郷(こきょう)を離(はな)れ、都会(とかい)へ向(む)かった。
Holding lofty ambitions, he left his hometown and headed for the city.

Compound of 大(たい) (great) + 志(し) (will, intention). A formal noun, often paired with 抱(いだ)く (to hold, to harbor).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 大志(たいし)を抱(いだ)く: to harbor great ambitions
  • 大志(たいし)を持(も)つ: to have great ambitions
  • 少年(しょうねん)よ、大志(たいし)を抱(いだ)け: "Boys, be ambitious" (famous Clark quote)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 志(こころざし): aspiration; will — broader, also formal
  • 野望(やぼう): ambition — often with a negative ring of selfish or excessive desire
  • 野心(やしん): ambition; aspiration — can be neutral or negative depending on context
  • 夢(ゆめ): dream — everyday word, less formal
