1. to get tired; to be worn out; to be exhausted
To become physically or mentally exhausted, especially after sustained effort. Slightly literary or old-fashioned, with a worn-down nuance compared to 疲(つか)れる.
今日(きょう)はくたびれた。
I'm worn out today.
長旅(ながたび)でくたびれてしまった。
I got exhausted from the long trip.
毎日(まいにち)残業(ざんぎょう)続(つづ)きで、すっかりくたびれた顔(かお)をしている。
After day after day of overtime, he has a thoroughly worn-out look on his face.
2. to become worn-out (of clothes, objects)
For an object — typically clothing, shoes, or a bag — to become shabby and aged through long use.
くたびれた靴(くつ)を捨(す)てた。
I threw out my worn-out shoes.
父(ちち)のくたびれたコートが物置(ものおき)に残(のこ)っていた。
My father's worn-out coat was still in the storage room.
長年(ながねん)使(つか)って、鞄(かばん)もずいぶんくたびれてきた。
After using it for years, the bag has gotten quite worn-looking.

An intransitive ichidan verb. Slightly literary or old-fashioned compared to 疲(つか)れる, with an extra nuance of wear and weariness rather than acute fatigue. The past form くたびれた often serves as an adjective for shabby, worn-looking things.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • くたびれた顔(かお): a worn-out face
  • くたびれた服(ふく): shabby clothes
  • すっかりくたびれる: to be completely exhausted
  • くたびれ果(は)てる: to be utterly worn out (intensified)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 疲(つか)れる: to get tired — neutral and standard; くたびれる carries a more drained, lasting nuance
  • へとへとになる: to be exhausted — emphasizes acute, intense fatigue
