1. to melt; to dissolve in the mouth
Of food, especially fatty or rich food, to soften and melt smoothly — often used as a sign of high quality.
この肉(にく)は口(くち)の中(なか)でとろける。
This meat melts in your mouth.
とろけるような食感(しょっかん)のチーズケーキ。
A cheesecake with a melt-in-your-mouth texture.
脂(あぶら)がよくのったマグロが、舌(した)の上(うえ)でとろけていった。
The well-marbled tuna melted away on my tongue.
2. to be entranced; to be melted (by pleasure, beauty, or affection)
Figurative use: to be so charmed, moved, or relaxed that one feels one's body or mind is melting. Common in descriptions of music, faces, hot springs, and romantic moments.
彼(かれ)の笑顔(えがお)にとろけてしまった。
I was completely melted by his smile.
温泉(おんせん)に浸(つ)かると疲(つか)れがとろけていく気(き)がする。
When I soak in a hot spring, I feel my fatigue melting away.
彼女(かのじょ)は子犬(こいぬ)を抱(だ)っこして、とろけるような顔(かお)をしていた。
Holding the puppy, she had an utterly melted look on her face.

Intransitive ichidan verb. The pair 溶(と)かす / 溶(と)く 'to dissolve' shares the same physical idea, but とろける specifically emphasizes a soft, smooth, pleasurable melting.

KANJI:
Usually written in hiragana. Less common kanji forms are 蕩(とろ)ける (especially for sense 2) and 溶(とろ)ける.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 口(くち)の中(なか)でとろける: to melt in the mouth
  • とろけるような食感(しょっかん): a melt-in-the-mouth texture
  • とろけるチーズ: melting cheese (product label)
  • とろけるような笑顔(えがお): a melting smile

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 溶(と)ける: to melt; to dissolve — broader, physical/chemical sense
  • 蕩(とろ)ける: same word, less common kanji form
  • ほぐれる: to come loose; to be relieved — for tension/fatigue without the 'melting' nuance
