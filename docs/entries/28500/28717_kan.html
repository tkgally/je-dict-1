貫(かん)

かん
counter
counter for pieces of sushi
1. (counter) piece of sushi (especially nigiri)
Used after a number to count individual pieces of sushi, typically nigiri. Standard in sushi restaurants and on menus.
マグロを二(に)貫(かん)注文(ちゅうもん)した。
I ordered two pieces of tuna sushi.
このセットは十(じゅっ)貫(かん)入(はい)っている。
This set contains ten pieces of sushi.
大将(たいしょう)、今日(きょう)のおすすめを五(ご)貫(かん)握(にぎ)ってください。
Chef, please make me five pieces of today's recommendation.

A counter used almost exclusively in sushi contexts. Origin is debated, but it likely comes from older units of weight or coinage. A piece of nigiri sushi is sometimes (originally) two pieces served together as one 貫(かん), but modern usage treats one piece as one 貫(かん).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 一(いっ)貫(かん), 二(に)貫(かん), 三(さん)貫(がん): 1, 2, 3 pieces (counting changes with sound shifts)
  • 一(いち)人前(にんまえ)十(じゅっ)貫(かん): ten pieces per serving
  • 握(にぎ)り寿司(ずし)二(に)貫(かん): two pieces of nigiri sushi

USAGE NOTE:
For maki rolls (cut rolls), the counter is usually 切(き)れ or 本(ほん) (for the whole roll). 貫(かん) is reserved mainly for nigiri.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 個(こ): piece (general counter) — works for sushi too in casual speech but lacks the specialist feel
  • 人前(にんまえ): portion; serving — for full sets, not individual pieces
