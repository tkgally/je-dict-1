1. to sharpen; to make pointed; to purse (lips)
To make something pointy at the tip. Also used figuratively with 口(くち) (mouth/lips) to mean pouting or sulking, and with 神経(しんけい) (nerves) to mean being on edge.
鉛筆(えんぴつ)を尖(とが)らせる。
To sharpen a pencil.
弟(おとうと)は口(くち)を尖(とが)らせて文句(もんく)を言(い)った。
My little brother pursed his lips and complained.
試合(しあい)前(まえ)の選手(せんしゅ)たちは神経(しんけい)を尖(とが)らせていた。
The players were on edge before the match.

The transitive/causative form of 尖(とが)る (to become pointed).

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive (takes を). Pairs with intransitive 尖(とが)る.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 鉛筆(えんぴつ)を尖(とが)らせる: to sharpen a pencil
  • 口(くち)を尖(とが)らせる: to pout; to purse one's lips (in displeasure)
  • 神経(しんけい)を尖(とが)らせる: to be on edge; to be highly alert
  • 耳(みみ)を尖(とが)らせる: to listen sharply

FIGURATIVE USAGE:
口(くち)を尖(とが)らせる is the standard way to describe a sulky or complaining facial expression. 神経(しんけい)を尖(とが)らせる describes heightened sensitivity or vigilance, often about a specific issue.
