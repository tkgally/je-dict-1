おきに

おきに
suffix
every other ~; at intervals of ~
1. every other ~; at intervals of ~; leaving a gap of ~
Attaches to a number-plus-counter phrase to state the size of the gap between one occurrence and the next. Because it names the gap rather than the cycle, 一日(いちにち)おきに means every second day, not every day. Used for gaps in time and in space alike.
薬(くすり)は一日(いちにち)おきに飲(の)んでください。
Please take the medicine every other day.
この駅(えき)からは十五分(じゅうごふん)おきにバスが出(で)ている。
Buses leave this station every fifteen minutes.
道(みち)の両側(りょうがわ)に十(じゅう)メートルおきに桜(さくら)の木(き)が植(う)えられている。
Cherry trees are planted at ten-meter intervals along both sides of the road.
祖母(そぼ)の様子(ようす)が心配(しんぱい)なので、二日(ふつか)おきに電話(でんわ)をかけるようにしている。
I'm worried about how my grandmother is doing, so I make a point of phoning every third day.

From 置(お)く (to place, to leave), so the literal sense is "leaving ~ in between."

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 一日(いちにち)おきに: every other day (one day skipped)
  • 一週間(いっしゅうかん)おきに: every two weeks
  • 一行(いちぎょう)おきに: on every other line
  • 一(ひと)つおきに: every other one
  • 三十分(さんじゅっぷん)おきに: every thirty minutes

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ごとに: every ~ — counts the unit itself, so 一日(いちにち)ごとに is every single day, while 一日(いちにち)おきに is every second day. With durations such as 三十分(さんじゅっぷん), the two come out the same, and speakers often use them interchangeably there.

COMMON MISTAKES:
With counters of two or more, native usage is not fully consistent; 二日(ふつか)おきに is normally every third day, but some speakers say it for every second day. When precision matters, say 三日(みっか)に一度(いちど) (once every three days) instead.
