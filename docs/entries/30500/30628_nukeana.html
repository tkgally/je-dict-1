抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)

ぬけあな
noun
loophole; secret passage
1. loophole; gap in the rules
A flaw in a law, contract, or system that lets someone evade its intent without technically breaking it. The most common sense in modern use, especially in news and business.
法律(ほうりつ)の抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)をふさぐ。
To close a legal loophole.
この規則(きそく)には抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)がある。
There's a loophole in this rule.
制度(せいど)の抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)を利用(りよう)して税金(ぜいきん)を逃(のが)れる企業(きぎょう)が問題(もんだい)になっている。
Companies exploiting loopholes in the system to avoid taxes have become a problem.
2. secret passage; escape tunnel; hole leading through
A hidden or unofficial passage that leads out of or through somewhere — a tunnel under a castle, a gap in a fence, a shortcut people have worn through.
塀(へい)に抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)があった。
There was a hole through the wall.
城(しろ)には昔(むかし)、抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)があったと言(い)われている。
The castle is said to have had a secret passage long ago.
子(こ)どもたちは生垣(いけがき)の抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)を通(とお)って公園(こうえん)へ近道(ちかみち)していた。
The children were taking a shortcut to the park through a gap in the hedge.

The literal sense 2 (a hole you can slip through) is the older one; sense 1 applies the same image to rules.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 法律(ほうりつ)の抜(ぬ)け穴(あな): a legal loophole
  • 抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)をふさぐ: to close a loophole
  • 抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)を突(つ)く: to exploit a loophole
  • 抜(ぬ)け穴(あな)だらけ: full of holes

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 盲点(もうてん): a blind spot — something overlooked, not necessarily exploitable
  • 隠(かく)し通路(つうろ): a hidden passage — the plain descriptive term for sense 2
