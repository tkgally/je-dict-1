手(て)が足(た)りない

てがたりない
expression
to be short-handed; to not have enough help
1. to be short-handed; to lack enough people to do the work
For there not to be enough people available for the work at hand. Literally 'hands are insufficient' — 手(て) stands for working hands, as in English 'we're short-handed.'
今日(きょう)は手(て)が足(た)りない。
We're short-handed today.
手(て)が足(た)りないので、アルバイトを増(ふ)やすことにした。
We're short-handed, so we decided to take on more part-timers.
年末(ねんまつ)は注文(ちゅうもん)が増(ふ)えて、どうしても手(て)が足(た)りなくなる。
Orders pick up at the end of the year, so we inevitably end up short-handed.
引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの当日(とうじつ)は手(て)が足(た)りないと思(おも)うので、友人(ゆうじん)にも来(き)てもらう予定(よてい)だ。
I expect we'll be short-handed on moving day, so I'm planning to have friends come too.

STRUCTURE: 手(て) (hand, worker) + が + 足(た)りない (to be insufficient).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 手(て)が足(た)りない: we're short-handed
  • 手(て)が足(た)りなくなる: to end up short-handed
  • 人手(ひとで)が足(た)りない: to be short of staff (the same idea with the noun 人手(ひとで); slightly more formal, common in news writing)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 人手不足(ひとでぶそく): labor shortage — the noun used for the long-term, industry-wide problem
  • 猫(ねこ)の手(て)も借(か)りたい: so busy one would borrow a cat's paws — a vivid idiom for the same situation

USAGE:
Use it for a temporary shortfall on a particular day or job; a chronic shortage is 人手不足(ひとでぶそく).

Related Words

Antonym: 手(て)が空(あ)く (to become free, to have a moment)
