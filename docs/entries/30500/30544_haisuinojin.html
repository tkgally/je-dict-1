背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)

はいすいのじん
expression
a last stand with no way back; burning one's bridges
1. a last stand with no retreat; a do-or-die position
A situation deliberately entered with no line of retreat, so that the only option is to win. Used both of circumstances forced on someone and of a resolve someone takes on themselves.
背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)で臨(のぞ)む。
To go into it with no way back.
今回(こんかい)の試合(しあい)は背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)だ。
This match is a do-or-die situation for us.
会社(かいしゃ)は全(すべ)ての資金(しきん)を新製品(しんせいひん)につぎ込(こ)み、背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)を敷(し)いた。
The company poured all its funds into the new product, leaving itself no way back.

Literally "a battle formation with one's back to the water." From a Chinese account of the general 韓信(かんしん), who drew his troops up against a river so that they could not flee and had to fight to win.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)を敷(し)く: to take up a no-retreat position
  • 背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)で臨(のぞ)む: to face something with no way back
  • まさに背水(はいすい)の陣(じん)だ: it is truly a last stand

USAGE:
Common in sports reporting and business writing about a decisive final attempt. It describes a deliberate commitment, so it fits poorly where someone is simply unlucky or cornered by accident.
