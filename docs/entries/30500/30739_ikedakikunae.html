池田菊苗(いけだきくなえ)

いけだきくなえ
noun (proper)
Kikunae Ikeda (chemist who identified umami)
1. Kikunae Ikeda; the chemist who discovered umami
A chemist (1864–1936) at Tokyo Imperial University who in 1908 isolated glutamate from 昆布(こんぶ) and named the taste うま味(み). His work led directly to the commercial seasoning 味(あじ)の素(もと).
うま味(み)を発見(はっけん)したのは池田菊苗(いけだきくなえ)だ。
It was Kikunae Ikeda who discovered umami.
池田菊苗(いけだきくなえ)は昆布(こんぶ)のだしからグルタミン酸(さん)を取(と)り出(だ)した。
Ikeda extracted glutamate from kelp stock.
池田菊苗(いけだきくなえ)が1908年(ねん)に名付(なづ)けた「うま味(み)」は、今(いま)では umami として世界中(せかいじゅう)で通用(つうよう)する。
The word umami, coined by Kikunae Ikeda in 1908, is now understood worldwide.

The name is read 池田(いけだ) (family name) + 菊苗(きくなえ) (given name); 菊苗(きくなえ) is unusual enough that the reading is often given in furigana even in Japanese texts.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 池田菊苗(いけだきくなえ)のうま味(み)発見(はっけん): Ikeda's discovery of umami
  • 日本(にほん)の十大(じゅうだい)発明(はつめい): "Japan's ten great inventions," a list his seasoning patent is always on

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
He is a fixture of school science lessons and of any discussion of Japanese food abroad, because umami is the one taste term Japanese has given the world's languages. He also studied in Leipzig under Wilhelm Ostwald and roomed in London with the novelist 夏目漱石(なつめそうせき).
