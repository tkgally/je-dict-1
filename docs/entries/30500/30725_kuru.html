1. to reel in; to turn over one by one; to count off in order
To move something along one length or one layer at a time with the hands — pulling in a rope hand over hand, flipping through the pages of a book or calendar, or ticking off days and items in order. The shared image is steady, unit-by-unit handling.
ページを繰(く)る。
To turn the pages.
糸(いと)をゆっくり繰(く)っている。
She is slowly reeling in the thread.
辞書(じしょ)を繰(く)って意味(いみ)を調(しら)べた。
I leafed through the dictionary and looked up the meaning.
祖母(そぼ)はカレンダーを繰(く)りながら、孫(まご)が来(く)る日(ひ)を指折(ゆびお)り数(かぞ)えていた。
My grandmother turned the calendar pages and counted on her fingers the days until her grandchild would come.

A somewhat literary godan verb. Note that it is a homophone of 来(く)る (to come) in the dictionary form only — the conjugations differ completely (繰(く)らない vs 来(こ)ない).

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive; the thing handled takes を: ページを繰(く)る, 糸(いと)を繰(く)る.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ページを繰(く)る: to turn the pages
  • 辞書(じしょ)を繰(く)る: to leaf through a dictionary
  • 糸(いと)を繰(く)る: to reel thread
  • 日(ひ)を繰(く)る: to count off the days

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • めくる: to turn over, to flip — the everyday word for turning pages; 繰(く)る sounds more bookish
  • 手繰(たぐ)る: to pull in hand over hand — keeps only the rope-pulling sense, and is used figuratively of tracing memories
