たこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)

たこあしはいせん
noun
octopus wiring; overloading one outlet
1. octopus wiring; overloading a single electrical outlet
Running many appliances off one wall socket through stacked adapters and power strips, so the cords spread out like an octopus's legs. Treated in Japan as a well-known fire hazard.
たこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)は危(あぶ)ない。
Octopus wiring is dangerous.
机(つくえ)の下(した)がたこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)になっている。
Under the desk it's turned into a tangle of octopus wiring.
火事(かじ)の原因(げんいん)はたこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)だったと消防署(しょうぼうしょ)が発表(はっぴょう)した。
The fire department announced that the cause of the fire was octopus wiring.

From たこ (octopus) plus 配線(はいせん) (wiring): one socket with cords radiating from it.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • たこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)になる: to end up as octopus wiring
  • たこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)を避(さ)ける: to avoid overloading an outlet
  • たこ足(あし)配線(はいせん)は危険(きけん): octopus wiring is hazardous

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
The phrase appears constantly in fire-prevention posters and household safety advice, so most Japanese speakers meet it as a warning rather than a technical term. It is also used figuratively for any overloaded single point, such as one worker handling too many jobs.
