街宣車(がいせんしゃ)

がいせんしゃ
noun
sound truck; loudspeaker van
1. sound truck; van fitted with loudspeakers for street campaigning
A vehicle equipped with loudspeakers used to broadcast political messages on the street. The word most often calls to mind the black vans of right-wing groups, but it also covers the campaign vans used by candidates during elections.
街宣車(がいせんしゃ)がうるさい。
The sound truck is noisy.
駅前(えきまえ)に街宣車(がいせんしゃ)が止(と)まっている。
A sound truck is parked in front of the station.
選挙(せんきょ)が近(ちか)づくと、候補者(こうほしゃ)の街宣車(がいせんしゃ)が町中(まちじゅう)を走(はし)り回(まわ)る。
As the election approaches, the candidates' sound trucks drive all over town.

Made up of 街頭(がいとう) (on the street) + 宣伝(せんでん) (publicity) + 車(しゃ) (vehicle).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 街宣車(がいせんしゃ)が回(まわ)る: a sound truck drives around
  • 街宣車(がいせんしゃ)の音(おと): the noise from a sound truck
  • 右翼(うよく)の街宣車(がいせんしゃ): a right-wing sound truck
  • 選挙(せんきょ)カー: election campaign car — the neutral term used at election time

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Black 街宣車(がいせんしゃ) playing military music and slogans at high volume are a familiar sight near stations and government offices in Japanese cities. Because of that association the word carries a faintly negative tone, so news coverage of ordinary campaigning usually prefers 選挙(せんきょ)カー.
