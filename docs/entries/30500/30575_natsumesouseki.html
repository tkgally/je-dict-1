夏目漱石(なつめそうせき)

なつめそうせき
noun (proper)
Natsume Soseki (1867-1916), novelist
1. Natsume Soseki, Meiji-era novelist
Japan's most widely read modern novelist, author of 吾輩(わがはい)は猫(ねこ)である, 坊(ぼ)っちゃん, and こころ. His works are standard reading in Japanese schools, so his name and his characters come up constantly in ordinary conversation about literature and education.
夏目漱石(なつめそうせき)の小説(しょうせつ)が好(す)きだ。
I like Natsume Soseki's novels.
国語(こくご)の授業(じゅぎょう)で漱石(そうせき)の「こころ」を読(よ)んだ。
We read Soseki's Kokoro in Japanese class.
昔(むかし)の千円札(せんえんさつ)には夏目漱石(なつめそうせき)の肖像(しょうぞう)が使(つか)われていた。
The old thousand-yen note used a portrait of Natsume Soseki.
漱石(そうせき)は英語(えいご)の教師(きょうし)をしてから作家(さっか)になった人(ひと)だ。
Soseki was someone who became a writer after working as an English teacher.

In everyday speech he is usually called just 漱石(そうせき) — the pen name alone — the way English speakers say "Shakespeare." 夏目(なつめ) is the family name and 漱石(そうせき) the pen name.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 漱石(そうせき)の作品(さくひん): Soseki's works
  • 漱石(そうせき)を読(よ)む: to read Soseki
  • 文豪(ぶんごう)漱石(そうせき): the great writer Soseki

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
His portrait appeared on the 1,000-yen note from 1984 to 2004, so older speakers may refer to that bill as 漱石(そうせき). 坊(ぼ)っちゃん (the hot-headed young teacher of his novel) is used as a nickname for a naive, impulsive young man.
