関(せき)ヶ原(はら)

せきがはら
noun (proper)
Sekigahara; a decisive showdown
1. Sekigahara (battlefield in Gifu Prefecture); the 1600 battle fought there
A basin in western Gifu Prefecture where Tokugawa Ieyasu's eastern army defeated the western coalition in 1600, opening the way to the Edo shogunate.
関(せき)ヶ原(はら)は岐阜県(ぎふけん)にある。
Sekigahara is in Gifu Prefecture.
関(せき)ヶ原(はら)の戦(たたか)いは１６００年(ねん)に起(お)きた。
The Battle of Sekigahara took place in 1600.
関(せき)ヶ原(はら)の戦(たたか)いの後(あと)、徳川家康(とくがわいえやす)は全国(ぜんこく)の大名(だいみょう)を従(したが)えた。
After the Battle of Sekigahara, Tokugawa Ieyasu brought the country's feudal lords under his control.
2. decisive showdown; make-or-break moment
By extension, any contest that will settle everything — a final match, a crucial negotiation, a career-defining exam.
明日(あした)の試合(しあい)が関(せき)ヶ原(はら)だ。
Tomorrow's match is our Sekigahara.
今回(こんかい)の交渉(こうしょう)が天下(てんか)分(わ)け目(め)の関(せき)ヶ原(はら)になる。
These negotiations will be the decisive showdown.
受験生(じゅけんせい)にとって、二月(にがつ)の一週間(いっしゅうかん)はまさに関(せき)ヶ原(はら)だと言(い)われている。
For students taking entrance exams, that one week in February is said to be their Sekigahara.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 天下(てんか)分(わ)け目(め)の関(せき)ヶ原(はら): the showdown that decides everything (the set phrase behind sense 2)
  • 関(せき)ヶ原(はら)の戦(たたか)い: the Battle of Sekigahara
  • 人生(じんせい)の関(せき)ヶ原(はら): the turning point of one's life

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
The battle is the best-known single day in Japanese history, retold constantly in novels, films, and taiga dramas. Because everyone knows how much rode on it, the name works in ordinary conversation as shorthand for a decisive moment, much as "Waterloo" does in English — though Sekigahara names the contest itself rather than a defeat.
