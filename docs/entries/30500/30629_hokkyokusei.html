北極星(ほっきょくせい)

ほっきょくせい
noun
the North Star; Polaris
1. the North Star; Polaris
The star almost directly above the north pole, which therefore stays nearly fixed in the sky while the others turn around it. Long used for finding direction at night, and often invoked figuratively as a guiding light.
北極星(ほっきょくせい)を探(さが)す。
To look for the North Star.
北極星(ほっきょくせい)はほとんど動(うご)かないように見(み)える。
The North Star appears to stay almost still.
昔(むかし)の船乗(ふなの)りは北極星(ほっきょくせい)を頼(たよ)りに方角(ほうがく)を確(たし)かめていた。
Sailors of old relied on the North Star to check their bearings.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 北極星(ほっきょくせい)を見(み)つける: to spot the North Star
  • 北極星(ほっきょくせい)を頼(たよ)りに: guided by the North Star
  • 北斗七星(ほくとしちせい): the Big Dipper — the usual landmark for locating it

USAGE:
In speeches and business writing the star serves as a metaphor for a fixed goal or guiding principle: 会社(かいしゃ)の北極星(ほっきょくせい)となる理念(りねん) (a philosophy that serves as the company's north star).
