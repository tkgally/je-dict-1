松尾芭蕉(まつおばしょう)

まつおばしょう
noun (proper)
Matsuo Basho (haiku poet)
1. Matsuo Basho, Edo-period poet who shaped haiku
The seventeenth-century poet who raised the seventeen-syllable form to serious literature and wrote the travel journal 奥(おく)の細道(ほそみち). Usually called simply 芭蕉(ばしょう).
松尾芭蕉(まつおばしょう)は俳句(はいく)の名人(めいじん)だ。
Matsuo Basho was a master of haiku.
奥(おく)の細道(ほそみち)は松尾芭蕉(まつおばしょう)の紀行文(きこうぶん)だ。
The Narrow Road to the Deep North is Matsuo Basho's travel journal.
松尾芭蕉(まつおばしょう)は東北(とうほく)や北陸(ほくりく)を歩(ある)きながら、各地(かくち)で句(く)を詠(よ)んだ。
Walking through the Tohoku and Hokuriku regions, Matsuo Basho composed verses in each place he visited.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 芭蕉(ばしょう)の句(く): a verse by Basho
  • 奥(おく)の細道(ほそみち): The Narrow Road to the Deep North, his best-known work
  • 古池(ふるいけ)や蛙(かわず)飛(と)びこむ水(みず)の音(おと): his most quoted poem, known to nearly everyone

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Basho is to haiku what Shakespeare is to English verse: schoolchildren memorise his poems, and towns along his walking route market themselves through him. His name comes up whenever someone writes a haiku, seriously or as a joke about a trip.
