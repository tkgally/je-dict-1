銀座(ぎんざ)

ぎんざ
noun (proper)
Ginza (upscale Tokyo shopping district)
1. Ginza; Tokyo's premier shopping and dining district
The district in central Tokyo known for department stores, luxury boutiques, galleries, and expensive restaurants. The name comes from the Edo-period silver mint that stood there, and it now stands generally for high-end shopping.
銀座(ぎんざ)で買(か)い物(もの)をした。
I went shopping in Ginza.
銀座(ぎんざ)の店(みせ)は値段(ねだん)が高(たか)い。
Shops in Ginza are expensive.
この商店街(しょうてんがい)は地元(じもと)の銀座(ぎんざ)と呼(よ)ばれ、いつも買(か)い物客(ものきゃく)でにぎわっている。
This shopping street is called the local Ginza and is always crowded with shoppers.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 銀座(ぎんざ)通(どお)り: Ginza's main avenue
  • 銀(ぎん)ぶら: strolling around Ginza (a coined word from 銀座(ぎんざ) + ぶらぶら)
  • 〜の銀座(ぎんざ): the "Ginza" of somewhere — any bustling, prosperous shopping street
  • 歩行者天国(ほこうしゃてんごく): the weekend pedestrian zone Ginza is famous for

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Because the name signals prestige, towns across Japan have borrowed it for their own main streets, and businesses use it to suggest quality. Saying a shop is 銀座(ぎんざ)価格(かかく) implies its prices are high even for what it sells.
