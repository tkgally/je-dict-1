ねぎま

ねぎま
noun
chicken-and-leek yakitori skewer
1. a yakitori skewer of chicken alternating with pieces of leek
The standard yakitori skewer on which chunks of chicken thigh alternate with sections of 長(なが)ねぎ. Usually the first thing ordered at a yakitori shop and the benchmark by which the shop is judged.
ねぎまをタレでお願(ねが)いします。
Negima with sauce, please.
この店(みせ)のねぎまは肉(にく)が厚(あつ)くておいしい。
The negima at this place has thick pieces of meat and tastes great.
とりあえずねぎまと砂肝(すなぎも)を頼(たの)んで、飲(の)み物(もの)はあとで決(き)めよう。
Let's order negima and gizzard for now and decide on drinks later.

Written in kana on almost every menu. The name comes from ねぎ (leek) plus ま, and originally referred to an Edo-period dish of leek and tuna (鮪(まぐろ)); the chicken skewer took over the name later.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ねぎまをタレで: negima with the sweet soy sauce
  • ねぎまを塩(しお)で: negima with salt
  • ねぎま二本(にほん): two negima skewers

USAGE:
Ordering yakitori means choosing タレ (sauce) or 塩(しお) (salt) for each item, so ねぎまを塩(しお)で is a complete order on its own.
