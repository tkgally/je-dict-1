1. to loaf on the job; to waste time chatting instead of working
To slack off in the middle of a task, typically by stopping to talk. The idiom implies the person is supposed to be working and has wandered off or lingered somewhere.
どこで油(あぶら)を売(う)っていたんだ。
Where have you been loafing around?
油(あぶら)を売(う)っていないで早(はや)く戻(もど)りなさい。
Stop idling and get back here.
買(か)い物(もの)を頼(たの)んだのに、近所(きんじょ)の人(ひと)と油(あぶら)を売(う)っていて一時間(いちじかん)も帰(かえ)ってこなかった。
I sent him shopping, but he stood chatting with a neighbor and didn't come back for a whole hour.

ETYMOLOGY:
From Edo-period hair-oil sellers, whose trade involved long chats with customers while the thick oil was measured out — so their work looked like standing around talking.

USAGE:
Used to scold or tease, most often in the {~ている} form or as a rhetorical question about where someone has been. Saying it of oneself sounds self-deprecating and light.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 油(あぶら)を売(う)っていないで: instead of loafing about
  • どこで油(あぶら)を売(う)っていた: where were you dawdling
  • 途中(とちゅう)で油(あぶら)を売(う)る: to dawdle on the way

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • サボる: to skip out, to shirk — to skip the work entirely; 油(あぶら)を売(う)る is stopping partway, usually to talk
