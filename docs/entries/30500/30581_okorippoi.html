1. quick-tempered; prone to anger; irritable
Describes someone who gets angry easily and often. Built from 怒(おこ)る (to get angry) plus the suffix 〜っぽい, which marks a tendency, so it points at a habit rather than a single outburst.
父(ちち)は怒(おこ)りっぽい。
My father is quick-tempered.
寝不足(ねぶそく)だと怒(おこ)りっぽくなる。
I get irritable when I haven't had enough sleep.
怒(おこ)りっぽい上司(じょうし)の下(した)で働(はたら)くのは疲(つか)れる。
Working under a short-fused boss is exhausting.
最近(さいきん)怒(おこ)りっぽくなったと妻(つま)に言(い)われて、少(すこ)し反省(はんせい)している。
My wife told me I've become short-tempered lately, and I'm reflecting on it a bit.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 怒(おこ)りっぽい人(ひと): a quick-tempered person
  • 怒(おこ)りっぽくなる: to become irritable (often about a temporary state — tiredness, illness, age)
  • 怒(おこ)りっぽい性格(せいかく): an irritable disposition

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 気(き)が短(みじか)い: short-tempered — the same trait stated as an idiom; slightly more neutral in tone
  • 短気(たんき): short temper — the noun, used in 短気(たんき)を起(お)こす (to lose one's temper)
  • 怒(おこ)りやすい: easily angered — plainer and more clinical; 〜っぽい carries a hint of criticism

USAGE:
Because it sounds mildly critical, speakers rarely apply it to a superior's face; it is normal in describing oneself or a family member.
