伊勢(いせ)

いせ
noun (proper)
Ise (region in Mie Prefecture, site of the Ise Grand Shrine)
1. Ise; the old province and city around the Ise Grand Shrine
A region on the coast of Mie Prefecture, and the name of the old province there. It is known above all for 伊勢神宮(いせじんぐう), the most senior shrine in Shinto, which has drawn pilgrims from all over Japan for centuries.
伊勢(いせ)に参(まい)りたい。
I want to make a pilgrimage to Ise.
伊勢(いせ)名物(めいぶつ)の赤福(あかふく)を買(か)って帰(かえ)った。
I bought Ise's famous akafuku sweets to take home.
江戸時代(えどじだい)には、一生(いっしょう)に一度(いちど)はお伊勢(いせ)参(まい)りをするのが庶民(しょみん)の夢(ゆめ)だった。
In the Edo period, making the pilgrimage to Ise once in a lifetime was the dream of ordinary people.

In everyday speech the shrine and the place are usually referred to with the honorific お: お伊勢(いせ)さん, お伊勢(いせ)参(まい)り.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 伊勢神宮(いせじんぐう): the Ise Grand Shrine
  • お伊勢(いせ)参(まい)り: a pilgrimage to Ise
  • 伊勢(いせ)海老(えび): spiny lobster (literally "Ise shrimp"), a celebratory food
  • 伊勢(いせ)うどん: Ise udon, served in a dark, thick sauce

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Every twenty years the shrine buildings are rebuilt from scratch in the 式年遷宮(しきねんせんぐう) ceremony, a practice often cited in Japan as an example of renewing tradition rather than preserving the original materials.
