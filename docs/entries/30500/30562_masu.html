ます
auxiliary
polite verb ending
1. polite verb ending
An auxiliary attached to the stem of a verb to make the sentence polite toward the listener. It carries no meaning of its own: 行(い)く and 行(い)きます both mean "go", but the second is addressed politely.
毎朝(まいあさ)走(はし)ります。
I run every morning.
昨日(きのう)は何(なに)も食(た)べませんでした。
I didn't eat anything yesterday.
会議(かいぎ)は三時(さんじ)に始(はじ)まりますので、十分(じゅっぷん)前(まえ)にお越(こ)しください。
The meeting starts at three, so please come ten minutes beforehand.

Attaches to the 連用形(れんようけい) (the ます-stem): 書(か)く → 書(か)きます、食(た)べる → 食(た)べます、する → します、来(く)る → 来(き)ます.

MAIN FORMS:

  • ます: present/future (行(い)きます)
  • ません: negative (行(い)きません)
  • ました: past (行(い)きました)
  • ませんでした: past negative (行(い)きませんでした)
  • ましょう: let's (行(い)きましょう)
  • ませんか: won't you? (行(い)きませんか)

USAGE:
The ます-form is the safe default with strangers, customers, teachers, and anyone senior. Plain forms are used with family and close friends, and inside relative clauses and most writing, where politeness is carried by the final verb of the sentence only.
