几帳(きちょう)

きちょう
noun
curtained standing screen (Heian-era furnishing)
1. curtained standing screen; portable partition
A T-shaped wooden stand hung with silk panels, used in Heian-period aristocratic houses to divide a room and to shield noblewomen from view. It appears constantly in classical literature and in period drama sets.
几帳(きちょう)の陰(かげ)に隠(かく)れる。
She hides behind the standing screen.
部屋(へや)の中(なか)を几帳(きちょう)で仕切(しき)っていた。
The room was partitioned with curtained screens.
平安(へいあん)時代(じだい)の女性(じょせい)は几帳(きちょう)越(ご)しに客(きゃく)と話(はな)すのが普通(ふつう)だった。
Heian-period women normally spoke with visitors from the other side of a curtained screen.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 几帳(きちょう)越(ご)しに話(はな)す: to speak through a screen
  • 几帳(きちょう)を立(た)てる: to set up a screen
  • 几帳(きちょう)の陰(かげ): behind the screen

RELATED WORD:
The adjective 几帳面(きちょうめん) (methodical, meticulous) is said to derive from the precise decorative joinery of the {几帳}'s posts — a useful memory hook, since the two words look related but describe very different things.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 屏風(びょうぶ): folding screen — rigid, hinged panels rather than hanging cloth
  • 襖(ふすま): sliding door panel — a fixed part of the building, not portable
