熱海(あたみ)

あたみ
noun (proper)
Atami (hot-spring resort town in Shizuoka)
1. Atami; the seaside hot-spring resort town
A hot-spring town on the coast of Shizuoka Prefecture, less than an hour from Tokyo by Shinkansen. It was the archetypal destination for company trips and honeymoons during the postwar boom, and its name still evokes that era of resort tourism.
週末(しゅうまつ)は熱海(あたみ)に行(い)く。
I'm going to Atami this weekend.
熱海(あたみ)の温泉(おんせん)旅館(りょかん)を予約(よやく)した。
I booked a hot-spring inn in Atami.
一時(いちじ)は寂(さび)れていた熱海(あたみ)も、若(わか)い観光客(かんこうきゃく)が戻(もど)って活気(かっき)を取(と)り戻(もど)した。
Atami, which had declined for a time, has recovered its energy as young tourists have come back.

The name is written with 熱(あつ)い (hot) and 海(うみ) (sea) — hot springs once bubbled up in the shore itself.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 熱海(あたみ)温泉(おんせん): the Atami hot springs
  • 熱海(あたみ)の花火大会(はなびたいかい): Atami's fireworks displays, held many times a year
  • 社員旅行(しゃいんりょこう)で熱海(あたみ): "Atami for the company trip" — a stock image of Showa-era corporate life

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Atami's decline in the 1990s and its revival as a weekend destination are a familiar case study in Japanese discussions of regional tourism. It is also the setting of the classic melodrama 金色夜叉(こんじきやしゃ), whose seaside scene is marked by a statue on the beach.
