1. to carry in; to bring in; to haul into a place
To move something bulky or heavy into a building or enclosed space. The compound stresses the endpoint of the movement: the object ends up inside.
荷物(にもつ)を運(はこ)び込(こ)んだ。
We carried the luggage in.
けが人(にん)が病院(びょういん)に運(はこ)び込(こ)まれた。
The injured person was rushed into the hospital.
業者(ぎょうしゃ)が朝早(あさはや)くから機材(きざい)を会場(かいじょう)に運(はこ)び込(こ)んでいた。
The contractors had been hauling equipment into the venue since early morning.

Compound of 運(はこ)ぶ (to carry) + 込(こ)む (into). Transitive: the thing carried takes を and the destination takes に.

ASPECT (ている):
運(はこ)び込(こ)んでいる can mean either an action in progress (is carrying things in) or a resulting state (has brought them in and they are there); context decides.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 病院(びょういん)に運(はこ)び込(こ)まれる: to be rushed to hospital
  • 荷物(にもつ)を部屋(へや)に運(はこ)び込(こ)む: to carry luggage into a room
  • 機材(きざい)を運(はこ)び込(こ)む: to haul in equipment

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 持(も)ち込(こ)む: to bring in — smaller items carried by hand, often where permission is at issue
  • 搬入(はんにゅう)する: to deliver in — formal, used of freight and exhibition setup
