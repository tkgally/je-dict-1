へた

へた
noun
calyx; stem end (of a tomato, persimmon, or eggplant)
1. calyx; the green cap and stem end of a fruit
The small leafy cap where the stem joins a fruit such as a tomato, persimmon, eggplant, or strawberry. In cooking it is the part you cut or pull off before eating.
トマトのへたを取(と)る。
Remove the stem end of the tomato.
柿(かき)のへたが乾(かわ)いている。
The calyx of the persimmon is dry.
苺(いちご)はへたを付(つ)けたまま洗(あら)うと水(みず)っぽくならない。
If you wash strawberries with the calyx still attached, they do not get watery.

Written 蒂(へた) or 萼(へた) in kanji, but almost always kana. A kitchen word rather than a botanical one; the technical term is 萼(がく).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • へたを取(と)る: to remove the calyx
  • へたを切(き)り落(お)とす: to cut off the stem end
  • へたが付(つ)いたまま: with the calyx still on
  • へたが青(あお)い: the calyx is green (a freshness sign)

USAGE:
A green, firm へた is the standard way of judging whether a tomato or persimmon is fresh, so the word turns up often in shopping and cooking advice.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 芯(しん): core — the inner part of an apple or cabbage, not the outer cap
  • 下手(へた): unskillful — an unrelated homophone written with kanji
