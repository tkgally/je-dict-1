甥(おい)っ子(こ)

おいっこ
noun
nephew
1. nephew (affectionate, colloquial)
The son of one's brother or sister. The 〜っ子(こ) ending adds warmth and is the form most people use in conversation, especially when the nephew is still a child.
甥(おい)っ子(こ)が生(う)まれた。
My nephew was born.
甥(おい)っ子(こ)に誕生日(たんじょうび)のプレゼントを送(おく)った。
I sent my nephew a birthday present.
正月(しょうがつ)に甥(おい)っ子(こ)が遊(あそ)びに来(き)て、家(いえ)の中(なか)がにぎやかになった。
My nephew came to visit at New Year's and the house got lively.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 甥(おい)っ子(こ)が生(う)まれる: to have a nephew born
  • 甥(おい)っ子(こ)の面倒(めんどう)を見(み)る: to look after one's nephew
  • 甥(おい)っ子(こ)にお年玉(としだま)をあげる: to give one's nephew New Year's money

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 甥(おい): nephew — the plain term; used in writing and formal speech, where 甥(おい)っ子(こ) would sound too cosy
  • 姪(めい)っ子(こ): niece — the matching form
  • 孫(まご): grandchild — often mentioned alongside when talking about the younger generation

The same 〜っ子(こ) pattern appears in words like 江戸(えど)っ子(こ) (a born-and-bred Tokyoite), where it likewise marks familiarity rather than small size.
