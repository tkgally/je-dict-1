アミノ酸(さん)

あみのさん
noun
amino acid
1. amino acid
An organic compound that serves as a building block of proteins. Commonly encountered in contexts such as nutrition, health foods, supplements, cosmetics, and biology. Twenty amino acids make up the proteins of the human body, and a subset (必須(ひっす)アミノ酸(さん)) must be obtained from food.
肉(にく)にはアミノ酸(さん)が多(おお)く含(ふく)まれている。
Meat contains a lot of amino acids.
このシャンプーはアミノ酸(さん)系(けい)なので髪(かみ)にやさしい。
This shampoo is amino-acid-based, so it's gentle on the hair.
運動後(うんどうご)にアミノ酸(さん)を摂取(せっしゅ)すると回復(かいふく)が早(はや)いと言(い)われている。
It is said that taking amino acids after exercise speeds up recovery.
人間(にんげん)の体(からだ)は約(やく)20種類(しゅるい)のアミノ酸(さん)から作(つく)られる様々(さまざま)なタンパク質(しつ)で構成(こうせい)されている。
The human body is made up of various proteins built from about twenty kinds of amino acids.

Loanword compound: the katakana stem アミノ (from 'amino') + the kanji 酸(さん) ('acid'). The katakana portion is a scientific borrowing, and the Japanese word always mixes katakana with the kanji ending.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 必須(ひっす)アミノ酸(さん): essential amino acid
  • 非必須(ひひっす)アミノ酸(さん): non-essential amino acid
  • 遊離(ゆうり)アミノ酸(さん): free amino acid
  • アミノ酸(さん)飲料(いんりょう): amino acid drink (sports drink)
  • アミノ酸(さん)サプリメント: amino acid supplement
  • アミノ酸(さん)系(けい)シャンプー(しゃんぷー): amino-acid-based shampoo
  • アミノ酸(さん)を摂取(せっしゅ)する: to take in amino acids
  • アミノ酸(さん)が豊富(ほうふ): rich in amino acids

USAGE:

  • A fully naturalized scientific term that appears frequently on food labels, supplement ads, and cosmetics packaging. In advertising copy, it often implies 'natural' or 'body-friendly.'
  • In the food industry it is strongly associated with 旨味(うまみ) ('savoriness'), since glutamic acid (グルタミン{酸(さん)}) is the amino acid most responsible for umami taste.

RELATED TERMS:

  • タンパク{質(しつ)} / 蛋白{質(しつ)}: protein — the macromolecule built from chains of amino acids.
  • 酸(さん): acid — the general chemistry term.
  • アミノ{酸(さん)配列(はいれつ)}: amino acid sequence — biological/genetics term.
  • 旨味(うまみ): umami — the savory taste produced by certain amino acids such as glutamic acid.
