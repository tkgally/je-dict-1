1. stroll; ramble; leisurely walk without a destination
Walking around aimlessly and at a relaxed pace, enjoying the surroundings rather than heading toward a specific goal. A somewhat literary word.
街(まち)を漫歩(まんぽ)する。
Stroll through the town.
秋(あき)の公園(こうえん)を漫歩(まんぽ)した。
I took a leisurely walk through the park in autumn.
仕事(しごと)を忘(わす)れて異国(いこく)の路地(ろじ)を漫歩(まんぽ)するのが、旅(たび)の醍醐味(だいごみ)だ。
The real pleasure of travel is forgetting about work and strolling through foreign alleyways.

Composed of 漫(まん) (aimless, rambling) + 歩(ぽ) (walk, step). A literary and somewhat refined word for walking without purpose.

USAGE:
漫歩(まんぽ) is used primarily in written language — essays, travel writing, and literary descriptions. In everyday conversation, 散歩(さんぽ) or ぶらぶら歩(ある)く are more natural. 漫歩(まんぽ) emphasizes the aimless, unhurried quality of the walk more than 散歩(さんぽ) does.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 街(まち)を漫歩(まんぽ)する: to stroll through a town
  • 漫歩(まんぽ)を楽(たの)しむ: to enjoy a stroll
  • 都市(とし)漫歩(まんぽ): urban strolling
  • 漫歩(まんぽ)記(き): rambling notes, strolling diary

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 散歩(さんぽ): walk, stroll — the everyday word for taking a walk; can be purposeful (exercise, dog walking) or leisurely
  • 散策(さんさく): leisurely walk, exploring on foot — similar in register to 漫歩(まんぽ), but implies more active looking around and exploring
  • ぶらぶら歩(ある)く: to wander around — casual, conversational expression for aimless walking
