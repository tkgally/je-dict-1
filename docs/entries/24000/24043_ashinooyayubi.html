足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)

あしのおやゆび
noun
big toe
1. big toe
The large innermost toe on the foot. In Japanese, toes are usually referred to using the names of the fingers of the hand prefixed with 足(あし)の ('of the foot'), so the big toe is literally 'the thumb of the foot.' Frequently mentioned in medical, sports, and footwear contexts.
足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)が痛(いた)い。
My big toe hurts.
靴(くつ)が小(ちい)さくて、足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)が当(あ)たる。
The shoes are small, and they're pressing against my big toe.
階段(かいだん)で足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)の爪(つめ)をぶつけてしまった。
I bumped the toenail of my big toe on the stairs.
足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)の付(つ)け根(ね)が腫(は)れて、歩(ある)くのもつらい。
The base of my big toe is swollen, and even walking is painful.

Literally 'the thumb of the foot.' Japanese does not have distinct one-word names for each toe; instead, toes are described by taking the corresponding finger name and prefixing it with 足(あし)の.

TOE NAMES:

  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび): big toe (= thumb of foot)
  • 足(あし)の人差(ひとさ)し指(ゆび): second toe (= index finger of foot)
  • 足(あし)の中指(なかゆび): middle toe
  • 足(あし)の薬指(くすりゆび): fourth toe (= ring finger of foot)
  • 足(あし)の小指(こゆび): little toe

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)をぶつける: to stub one's big toe
  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)の爪(つめ): the nail of the big toe
  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)の付(つ)け根(ね): the base of the big toe
  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)が痛(いた)い: one's big toe hurts
  • 足(あし)の親指(おやゆび)を骨折(こっせつ)する: to break one's big toe

USAGE:

  • In everyday speech, people often just say 親指(おやゆび) when context makes it clear whether the hand or foot is meant — e.g., when pointing to one's foot or discussing shoes.
  • The technical medical term is 母趾(ぼし) or 第一趾(だいいっし), but these are rarely used outside hospitals.
  • Common medical conditions named with this word include 巻(ま)き爪(づめ) ('ingrown toenail') and 外反母趾(がいはんぼし) ('bunion; hallux valgus').
