1. drawing a sword; unsheathing a blade
The act of pulling a sword from its scabbard, especially as a technique in Japanese martial arts.
素早(すばや)く抜刀(ばっとう)した。
He drew his sword swiftly.
抜刀(ばっとう)の構(かま)えを取(と)った。
He took a sword-drawing stance.
抜刀術(ばっとうじゅつ)は、鞘(さや)から刀(かたな)を抜(ぬ)く瞬間(しゅんかん)に斬(き)りつける武術(ぶじゅつ)だ。
Battōjutsu is a martial art in which one strikes at the moment of drawing the blade from its scabbard.

A compound of 抜(ばつ) (to draw out) and 刀(とう) (sword). This term is closely associated with Japanese swordsmanship traditions. The art of drawing and cutting in one motion is called 抜刀術(ばっとうじゅつ) or 居合(いあい) (iaido), and is still practiced as a martial art today.

The word frequently appears in historical dramas (時代劇(じだいげき)), manga, anime, and games featuring samurai themes.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 抜刀(ばっとう)する: to draw a sword
  • 抜刀術(ばっとうじゅつ): the art of drawing and cutting
  • 抜刀(ばっとう)の構(かま)え: sword-drawing stance
  • 抜刀隊(ばっとうたい): sword-drawing unit (historical military term)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 居合(いあい): iaido (the art/way of drawing the sword; more emphasis on the spiritual discipline)
  • 納刀(のうとう): sheathing the sword (the opposite action)
  • 帯刀(たいとう): wearing a sword at one's side
