1. to strum; to play loudly; to bang out (on a stringed instrument)
To play a stringed instrument — especially a guitar, mandolin, or traditional Japanese instrument like the biwa — by sweeping or scraping the strings vigorously to produce bold, continuous sound. Suggests energetic, often rough or expressive playing rather than careful plucking.
ギターをかき鳴(な)らす。
To strum a guitar.
彼(かれ)はステージでギターを激(はげ)しくかき鳴(な)らした。
He strummed his guitar wildly on the stage.
夜(よる)、公園(こうえん)で誰(だれ)かがギターをかき鳴(な)らしているのが聞(き)こえた。
At night, I could hear someone strumming a guitar in the park.
琵琶(びわ)をかき鳴(な)らしながら物語(ものがたり)を語(かた)る盲目(もうもく)の法師(ほうし)の姿(すがた)が描(えが)かれている。
The scene depicts a blind monk telling tales while strumming a biwa.

Compound of the prefix かき (from the verb 掻(か)く, 'to scratch; to scrape,' functioning as an intensifier) and 鳴(な)らす ('to make something sound'). The かき prefix adds a forceful, sweeping quality to the action. Though sometimes written 掻(か)き鳴(な)らす, the kana-only かき鳴(な)らす form is far more common in modern usage.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive) — takes a direct object marked with を.
  • There is no paired intransitive verb; for the instrument itself sounding, use 鳴(な)る.

ASPECT (ている):

  • かき鳴(な)らしている: is strumming (right now) — ongoing action in progress.
  • Often appears in the て-form with subsidiary verbs: かき鳴(な)らしながら ('while strumming'), かき鳴(な)らしていた ('was strumming').

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ギターをかき鳴(な)らす: to strum a guitar
  • 琵琶(びわ)をかき鳴(な)らす: to play a biwa (traditional lute)
  • 三味線(しゃみせん)をかき鳴(な)らす: to strum a shamisen
  • 弦(げん)をかき鳴(な)らす: to strum the strings
  • 激(はげ)しくかき鳴(な)らす: to strum wildly
  • 思(おも)いのままにかき鳴(な)らす: to strum as the mood takes one

USAGE:

  • Strongly associated with stringed instruments played with a sweeping motion. Not used for plucking individual notes (弾(ひ)く) or for wind or percussion instruments.
  • Conveys energy, passion, or roughness — compared with the neutral 弾(ひ)く ('to play,' for strings or piano), かき鳴(な)らす emphasizes boldness or intensity.
  • Common in literary, musical, and narrative contexts: rock performances, folk music, or descriptions of traditional Japanese music.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 弾(ひ)く: to play (a stringed instrument or piano) — the neutral, general verb; does not imply vigor.
  • 奏(かな)でる: to play (an instrument); to produce music — more literary and elegant; often used for producing beautiful melodies.
  • 鳴(な)らす: to sound; to make ring — the base verb; works for any object that produces sound (bells, horns), not just strings.
  • 掻(か)き立(た)てる: to stir up; to rouse — a different かき compound, used figuratively for emotions, not music.
