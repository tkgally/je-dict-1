1. pretending to be asleep; playing possum
Often used humorously or with mild criticism.
タヌキ寝入(ねい)りしてるでしょ。
You're just pretending to be asleep, aren't you?
子供(こども)はタヌキ寝入(ねい)りが下手(へた)だ。
Children are bad at pretending to be asleep.
父(ちち)はテレビの前(まえ)でタヌキ寝入(ねい)りをして、話(はなし)を聞(き)いていなかった。
My father was pretending to sleep in front of the TV and wasn't listening to the conversation.

Literally "tanuki sleep," from the folk belief that tanuki (狸(たぬき), raccoon dogs) play dead to deceive people. Also written as 狸(たぬき)寝入(ねい)り. The expression captures the idea of feigning sleep to avoid a situation or responsibility.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • タヌキ寝入(ねい)りをする: to pretend to be asleep
  • タヌキ寝入(ねい)りを決(き)め込(こ)む: to stubbornly pretend to be asleep

CULTURAL NOTES:
Tanuki are prominent in Japanese folklore as tricksters and shape-shifters. Many Japanese idioms reference their supposed cunning, such as 狸(たぬき)親父(おやじ) (sly old man). The English equivalent "playing possum" draws on a similar animal-based metaphor.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 寝(ね)たふり: pretending to sleep (more neutral, no cultural connotation)
  • 知(し)らんぷり: pretending not to know (different type of pretense)
