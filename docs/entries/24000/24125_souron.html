総論(そうろん)

そうろん
noun
general remarks; overview; introduction
1. general remarks; overview; general discussion
A broad, high-level discussion or summary of a topic before going into specific details. Used in academic writing, textbooks, and formal presentations to refer to the introductory or overview section that covers principles and framework before detailed case-by-case analysis.
まず総論(そうろん)から始(はじ)めよう。
Let's start with the overview first.
教科書(きょうかしょ)の第一章(だいいっしょう)は総論(そうろん)にあたる。
The first chapter of the textbook corresponds to the general overview.
総論(そうろん)賛成(さんせい)、各論(かくろん)反対(はんたい)という態度(たいど)では何(なに)も進(すす)まない。
Nothing will get done with an attitude of agreeing in principle but opposing every detail.

Compound of 総(そう) ('general; overall') and 論(ろん) ('discussion; argument'). Contrasts with 各論(かくろん) ('detailed discussion of individual topics'). The pair 総論(そうろん)/各論(かくろん) is a fundamental organizing concept in Japanese academic and professional writing — textbooks, legal codes, and reports often begin with a 総論(そうろん) section before proceeding to 各論(かくろん).

USAGE:

  • The set phrase 総論(そうろん)賛成(さんせい)、各論(かくろん)反対(はんたい) ('agree in general, oppose in specifics') describes a very common pattern in Japanese consensus-building where everyone supports a policy in principle but objects to specific implementation measures.
  • In medical and legal textbooks, 総論(そうろん) is a formal structural label for the introductory portion.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 総論(そうろん)と各論(かくろん): general overview and specific topics
  • 総論(そうろん)賛成(さんせい): agreement in principle
  • 総論(そうろん)的(てき)な議論(ぎろん): general-level discussion
  • 総論(そうろん)部分(ぶぶん): overview section

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 各論(かくろん): detailed discussion of specific topics — the counterpart to 総論(そうろん), covering individual cases
  • 概論(がいろん): introductory survey — a general overview of a field, often used in course titles (e.g., 経済学(けいざいがく)概論(がいろん) 'Introduction to Economics')
  • 序論(じょろん): introduction — the opening section of a paper or book, distinct from 総論(そうろん) in that it sets up the work rather than surveying principles
