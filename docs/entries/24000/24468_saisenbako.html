賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)

さいせんばこ
noun
offertory box
1. offertory box; donation box at a shrine or temple
Found at the front of Japanese shrines and temples for monetary offerings.
賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)にお金(かね)を入(い)れた。
I put money into the offertory box.
神社(じんじゃ)の賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)に五円(ごえん)玉(だま)を投(な)げ入(い)れた。
I tossed a five-yen coin into the shrine's offertory box.
正月(しょうがつ)には賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)の前(まえ)に長(なが)い行列(ぎょうれつ)ができる。
During New Year's, long lines form in front of the offertory box.

Composed of 賽銭(さいせん) (monetary offering) + 箱(ばこ) (box). The large wooden box placed in front of the main hall at Japanese shrines and temples where visitors toss coins as part of their prayer or wish.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)にお金(かね)を入(い)れる: to put money in the offertory box
  • 賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)の前(まえ)で手(て)を合(あ)わせる: to put hands together in front of the offertory box
  • 賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ)泥棒(どろぼう): offertory box thief

CULTURAL NOTES:
The standard worship procedure at a Shinto shrine is to toss a coin into the 賽銭(さいせん)箱(ばこ), ring the bell, bow twice, clap twice, pray, and bow once more. Five-yen coins (五円(ごえん)玉(だま)) are popular because 五円(ごえん) sounds like 御縁(ごえん) (divine connection). At Buddhist temples, the procedure differs slightly, with no clapping.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 賽銭(さいせん): the offering itself (the money)
  • 奉納(ほうのう)箱(ばこ): votive offering box (for non-monetary offerings)
  • 募金(ぼきん)箱(ばこ): donation box, collection box (secular)
