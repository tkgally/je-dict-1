朱印(しゅいん)

しゅいん
noun
red seal
1. red seal; vermillion stamp
Refers to both historical official seals and modern temple/shrine stamps.
朱印(しゅいん)をいただけますか。
May I receive a red seal stamp?
この神社(じんじゃ)の朱印(しゅいん)は美(うつく)しいデザインだ。
The red seal stamp at this shrine has a beautiful design.
旅行(りょこう)中(ちゅう)に十(じゅう)か所(しょ)の寺(てら)で朱印(しゅいん)を集(あつ)めた。
I collected red seal stamps from ten temples during my trip.
朱印(しゅいん)帳(ちょう)を忘(わす)れてしまったので、書(か)き置(お)きをもらった。
I forgot my stamp book, so I received a pre-written one.

A red ink stamp given at Japanese temples and shrines as proof of a pilgrim's visit. Collecting these in a dedicated book (朱印(しゅいん)帳(ちょう)) has become extremely popular in recent years, both among religious visitors and tourists.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 朱印(しゅいん)帳(ちょう): red seal stamp book
  • 朱印(しゅいん)をもらう: to receive a red seal stamp
  • 朱印(しゅいん)巡(めぐ)り: pilgrimage to collect stamps
  • 御朱印(ごしゅいん): red seal stamp (honorific form, very common)

CULTURAL NOTES:
The honorific form 御朱印(ごしゅいん) is more commonly used in everyday speech. Each stamp typically includes the temple or shrine name, the date, and a hand-brushed calligraphy element, making each one unique. The practice dates back centuries when pilgrims received stamps as proof of copying sutras. Today most places charge 300-500 yen per stamp.

HISTORICAL USAGE:
In the Edo period, 朱印(しゅいん)状(じょう) referred to official documents bearing the shogun's red seal, granting trade privileges or land rights.
