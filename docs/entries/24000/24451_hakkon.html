1. root emergence; taking root; rooting
the process of a plant putting out roots, especially from seeds, cuttings, or transplanted plants
種(たね)をまいてから一週間(いっしゅうかん)で発根(はっこん)した。
The seeds took root one week after sowing.
挿(さ)し木(き)の発根(はっこん)を促(うなが)すために発根(はっこん)剤(ざい)を使(つか)う。
Use rooting agent to promote root growth in cuttings.
温度(おんど)と湿度(しつど)を適切(てきせつ)に管理(かんり)すれば、発根(はっこん)率(りつ)を大幅(おおはば)に向上(こうじょう)させることができる。
By properly managing temperature and humidity, you can significantly improve the rooting rate.

A botanical and gardening term for the process of roots emerging from seeds, cuttings, or transplanted plants. Important in horticulture and agriculture, where successful rooting determines whether plants survive transplanting.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 発根(はっこん)する: to put out roots, to take root
  • 発根(はっこん)剤(ざい): rooting agent/hormone
  • 発根(はっこん)率(りつ): rooting rate/success rate
  • 発根(はっこん)を促(うなが)す: to encourage root growth

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 根付(ねづ)く: to take root/become established — more general, also used figuratively
  • 発芽(はつが): germination — the sprouting of a seed above ground, the complementary process to rooting
  • 活着(かっちゃく): successful establishment — specifically for transplanted plants that survive and thrive
