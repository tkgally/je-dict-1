掌(たなごころ)

たなごころ
noun
palm of the hand
1. palm of the hand
The flat inner surface of the hand. A literary and elegant word for the same body part that is called 手のひら in everyday speech. Carries a poetic, classical tone and appears in literature, idioms, and figurative expressions.
掌(たなごころ)を返(かえ)す。
To flip one's palm — to reverse one's attitude completely.
掌(たなごころ)に汗(あせ)を握(にぎ)る展開(てんかい)だった。
It was a development that had me gripping my palms with sweat.
彼(かれ)は会社(かいしゃ)を掌(たなごころ)の上(うえ)で転(ころ)がすように動(うご)かしていた。
He was running the company as if rolling it in the palm of his hand.

An old Japanese word derived from 手(た)の心(こころ) ('the heart of the hand'). Elegant and literary in register, it is used mainly in set phrases, idioms, and poetic contexts rather than in everyday description of body parts. The kanji 掌(しょう) can also be read as しょう in Sino-Japanese compounds like 掌握(しょうあく) ('grasping; control').

USAGE:

  • The everyday word for 'palm' is 手(て)のひら — use 掌(たなごころ) when a literary or formal tone is desired.
  • Appears frequently in the idiom 掌(たなごころ)を返(かえ)す ('to flip one's palm'), meaning to suddenly reverse one's attitude or loyalty.
  • The expression 掌(たなごころ)の上(うえ)で転(ころ)がす ('to roll on one's palm') means to manipulate someone or something with complete control.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 掌(たなごころ)を返(かえ)す: to completely reverse one's position
  • 掌(たなごころ)を合(あ)わせる: to press palms together (in prayer)
  • 掌(たなごころ)の上(うえ): in the palm of one's hand (figurative: under one's control)
  • 掌(たなごころ)に汗(あせ)を握(にぎ)る: to grip sweat in one's palms (with tense excitement)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 手(て)のひら: palm — the standard everyday word for the same body part
  • 掌握(しょうあく): grasping; taking control — a Sino-Japanese compound using the same kanji, meaning to seize or hold power
  • 手(て)の甲(こう): back of the hand — the opposite surface
