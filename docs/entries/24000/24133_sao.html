竿(さお)

さお
noun
pole; rod; fishing rod
1. pole; rod; long stick
A long, thin stick or pole used for various purposes — hanging laundry, pushing a boat, or reaching things. One of the basic tools in traditional Japanese daily life.
物干(ものほ)し竿(ざお)に洗濯物(せんたくもの)を干(ほ)す。
To hang laundry on a drying pole.
長(なが)い竿(さお)で柿(かき)の実(み)を落(お)とした。
I knocked persimmons down with a long pole.
船頭(せんどう)が竿(さお)で川底(かわぞこ)を突(つ)いて船(ふね)を進(すす)めた。
The boatman pushed the boat forward by poling the riverbed.
2. fishing rod; fishing pole
A rod used for fishing, especially a traditional bamboo fishing pole. In fishing contexts, 竿 alone commonly means 'fishing rod.'
新(あたら)しい竿(さお)を買(か)った。
I bought a new fishing rod.
竿(さお)を垂(た)れて魚(さかな)を待(ま)つ。
To drop the rod and wait for a bite.
祖父(そふ)は竹(たけ)の竿(さお)で釣(つ)りをするのが好(す)きだった。
My grandfather liked fishing with a bamboo rod.

A fundamental word in traditional Japanese life. The voiced form ざお appears in compounds: 物干(ものほ)し竿(ざお) ('laundry pole'), 釣(つ)り竿(ざお) ('fishing rod'). Originally referred to bamboo poles, which were the standard material for centuries.

USAGE:

  • In compound words, the reading changes to ざお through rendaku (sequential voicing): 釣(つ)り竿(ざお), 物干(ものほ)し竿(ざお).
  • 竿(さお)を差(さ)す ('to pole a boat') is the traditional expression for propelling a flat-bottomed boat.
  • In everyday modern life, the most common encounter with 竿(さお) is the 物干(ものほ)し竿(ざお) used to hang laundry outdoors.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 物干(ものほ)し竿(ざお): laundry drying pole
  • 釣(つ)り竿(ざお): fishing rod
  • 竿(さお)を差(さ)す: to pole a boat
  • 竿(さお)を垂(た)れる: to cast a line (fishing)
  • 竹(たけ)竿(ざお): bamboo pole

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 棒(ぼう): stick; bar — a general term for a rigid stick or rod, without the long-and-thin connotation of 竿(さお)
  • 杖(つえ): cane; walking stick — a stick used for support while walking
  • ロッド: rod — the English loanword, used mainly for modern fishing rods
