朱(しゅ)

しゅ
noun
vermillion; cinnabar red
1. vermillion; cinnabar red
A vivid red-orange color traditionally derived from cinnabar (mercury sulfide), associated with shrines and traditional art.
朱(しゅ)に染(そ)まった鳥居(とりい)。
A torii gate painted in vermillion.
朱(しゅ)の色(いろ)が鮮(あざ)やかだ。
The vermillion color is vivid.
先生(せんせい)が朱(しゅ)で答案(とうあん)を添削(てんさく)した。
The teacher corrected the exam paper in red ink.
漆器(しっき)に朱(しゅ)を塗(ぬ)って仕上(しあ)げる。
Finish the lacquerware by applying vermillion.

A vivid red-orange color that has deep cultural significance in Japan. Originally referred to cinnabar, the mineral pigment used to produce the color.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 朱(しゅ)に染(そ)まる: be dyed vermillion
  • 朱色(しゅいろ): vermillion (the color name)
  • 朱(しゅ)塗(ぬ)り: vermillion-lacquered
  • 朱(しゅ)を入(い)れる: add corrections in red; also, to make corrections or revisions
  • 朱肉(しゅにく): red ink pad (for stamps)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 赤(あか): red — the general word for red; 朱(しゅ) is more orange-tinged and culturally specific
  • 紅(べに)/紅(くれない): crimson, deep red — a cooler, bluer red than 朱(しゅ)
  • 丹(に): red earth pigment — an older, more literary word for red pigment

CULTURAL NOTE:
Vermillion is the iconic color of Shinto 鳥居(とりい) gates and many shrine buildings. It was believed to ward off evil spirits. The expression 朱(しゅ)に交(まじ)われば赤(あか)くなる ("if you mix with vermillion, you turn red") is a proverb meaning you are influenced by the company you keep.
