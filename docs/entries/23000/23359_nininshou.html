二人称(ににんしょう)

ににんしょう
noun
second person (grammar); second-person pronoun
1. second person (grammatical category); second-person pronoun
In grammar, the category that refers to the listener or addressee. In Japanese, 二人称(ににんしょう) covers pronouns like あなた}, {君(きみ), お{前(まえ)} and the addressee's name when used in place of 'you.' A technical term used in language textbooks, writing guides, and linguistic discussion.
「あなた」は二人称(ににんしょう)の代名詞(だいめいし)だ。
"Anata" is a second-person pronoun.
日本語(にほんご)では二人称(ににんしょう)を使(つか)わないことが多(おお)い。
In Japanese, the second person is often left unspoken.
英語(えいご)では二人称(ににんしょう)は「you」の一語(いちご)だけだ。
In English, the second person is just the single word "you."
小説(しょうせつ)を二人称(ににんしょう)で書(か)くと読者(どくしゃ)が物語(ものがたり)の中(なか)に引(ひ)き込(こ)まれる効果(こうか)がある。
Writing a novel in the second person has the effect of drawing the reader into the story.

Composed of 二(に) (two) + 人称(にんしょう) (grammatical person). A grammatical term for the 'you' category. In Japanese, where explicit pronouns are often omitted, the class is defined more as a functional category (how the speaker refers to the person they are addressing) than as a fixed list of words.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 二人称(ににんしょう)代名詞(だいめいし): second-person pronoun
  • 二人称(ににんしょう)単数(たんすう): second-person singular
  • 二人称(ににんしょう)複数(ふくすう): second-person plural
  • 二人称(ににんしょう)の視点(してん): second-person point of view (in fiction)
  • 二人称(ににんしょう)で書(か)く: to write in the second person

RELATED TERMS:

  • 一人称(いちにんしょう): first person (I, we) — see 08311_ichininshou if present.
  • 三人称(さんにんしょう): third person (he, she, they)
  • 人称(にんしょう): grammatical person (the parent category)
  • 代名詞(だいめいし): pronoun

CULTURAL NOTE:
Japanese has many possible second-person words — あなた}, {君(きみ), お{前(まえ)}, あんた}, {貴殿(きでん), お{宅(たく)} and so on — and each carries its own social nuance. Overusing them can sound rude; in most polite situations, Japanese speakers either use the addressee's name and title or simply leave the 'you' unspoken. A grammar book discussing 二人称(ににんしょう) will therefore spend more time on when to avoid pronouns than on which form to choose.

REGISTER:
A technical term from grammar and linguistics; not used in everyday conversation outside of language study.
