自然数(しぜんすう)

しぜんすう
noun
natural number
1. natural number
In mathematics, one of the whole, non-negative counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, and so on. In most Japanese school textbooks the set starts from 1, though some advanced or international treatments include 0. A technical term used in math class and any writing about arithmetic, number theory, or logic.
1、2、3は自然数(しぜんすう)だ。
1, 2, and 3 are natural numbers.
自然数(しぜんすう)の集合(しゅうごう)は無限(むげん)だ。
The set of natural numbers is infinite.
小学校(しょうがっこう)では最初(さいしょ)に自然数(しぜんすう)の計算(けいさん)を学(まな)ぶ。
In elementary school, students first learn arithmetic with natural numbers.
日本(にほん)の数学(すうがく)の教科書(きょうかしょ)では、自然数(しぜんすう)は一般(いっぱん)的(てき)に1から始(はじ)まる正(せい)の整数(せいすう)と定義(ていぎ)されている。
In Japanese math textbooks, natural numbers are generally defined as the positive integers starting from 1.

Composed of 自然(しぜん) (natural) + 数(すう) (number). A direct translation of the mathematical term 'natural number.' The everyday word 自然(しぜん) ('nature') is not the intuition behind this use — 自然数(しぜんすう) refers specifically to the 'naturally occurring' counting numbers that any child begins with: 1, 2, 3, and so on.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 自然数(しぜんすう)の集合(しゅうごう): the set of natural numbers
  • 自然数(しぜんすう)の和(わ): the sum of natural numbers
  • 自然数(しぜんすう)の積(せき): the product of natural numbers
  • 正(せい)の自然数(しぜんすう): positive natural numbers
  • 任意(にんい)の自然数(しぜんすう): any natural number / an arbitrary natural number
  • 自然数(しぜんすう)列(れつ): a sequence of natural numbers

RELATED NUMBER TYPES:

  • 整数(せいすう): integer — includes 0 and negatives (..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...).
  • 正(せい)の整数(せいすう): positive integer — in Japanese school math, usually equivalent to 自然数(しぜんすう).
  • 有理数(ゆうりすう): rational number — any number expressible as p/q.
  • 無理数(むりすう): irrational number.
  • 実数(じっすう): real number.
  • 虚数(きょすう): imaginary number.
  • 複素数(ふくそすう): complex number.
  • 素数(そすう): prime number.

USAGE NOTE:
Whether 自然数(しぜんすう) includes 0 is a point of divergence between traditions. Japanese high-school and college entrance exams generally treat 自然数(しぜんすう) as starting from 1; university-level set theory and computer science, influenced by ISO and Bourbaki conventions, often include 0. When writing or reading a math problem, always check how the text defines the term.

REGISTER:
A technical term from mathematics. It has no casual uses, but it is basic vocabulary that every Japanese schoolchild meets early.
