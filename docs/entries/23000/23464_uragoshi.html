1. straining; sieving; passing food through a fine mesh
A cooking technique in which food is pressed through a fine mesh sieve to create a smooth, uniform texture. Used to make purees, smooth sauces, and strained soups.
かぼちゃを裏漉(うらご)しする。
To strain pumpkin through a sieve.
裏漉(うらご)ししたじゃがいもは滑(なめ)らかになる。
Sieved potatoes become smooth.
なめらかなプリンを作(つく)るには、卵液(らんえき)を裏漉(うらご)しするのが大切(たいせつ)だ。
To make smooth pudding, it's important to strain the egg mixture through a sieve.

A compound of 裏(うら) (back, reverse side) and 漉(こ)し (straining). The name refers to pressing food through a sieve from the back side. This is a fundamental technique in Japanese cooking, used to achieve a refined, smooth texture.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 裏漉(うらご)しする: to strain, to sieve
  • 裏漉(うらご)し器(き): a straining tool, a sieve for this purpose
  • 裏漉(うらご)しした野菜(やさい): strained vegetables
  • 裏漉(うらご)しにかける: to pass through a sieve

USAGE:

Often used as a suru verb (裏漉(うらご)しする). The technique is common in:

  • Japanese confections (和菓子(わがし)): straining 餡(あん) (sweet bean paste)
  • Western-style cooking: making smooth soups and sauces
  • Baby food preparation: creating smooth purees for infants

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 濾(こ)す: to strain, to filter — a general verb for filtering liquids; 裏漉(うらご)し specifically refers to pushing solid food through a mesh
  • 漉(こ)す: to strain — the base verb; 裏漉(うらご)し is the nominal compound used as a cooking term
