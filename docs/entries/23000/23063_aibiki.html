合挽(あいび)き

あいびき
noun
mixed ground meat (beef and pork)
1. mixed ground meat; ground meat blend of beef and pork
Ground meat that combines beef and pork, typically in roughly equal proportions. A staple in Japanese home cooking, used for hamburger steaks, meatballs, and other dishes.
合挽(あいび)き肉(にく)を買(か)う。
To buy mixed ground meat.
ハンバーグには合挽(あいび)き肉(にく)がよく合(あ)う。
Mixed ground meat works well for hamburger steaks.
合挽(あいび)き肉(にく)に塩(しお)コショウと卵(たまご)を加(くわ)えてよくこねる。
Add salt, pepper, and egg to the mixed ground meat and knead well.

A compound of 合(あい) (combined/together) + 挽(び)き (ground/minced). Almost always used with 肉(にく) as 合挽(あいび)き肉(にく). The standard ratio is roughly 50-50 or 60-40 beef to pork.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 合挽(あいび)き肉(にく): mixed ground meat
  • 合挽(あいび)きミンチ: mixed minced meat
  • 合挽(あいび)き肉(にく)のハンバーグ: hamburger steak made with mixed ground meat

USAGE:
In Japanese supermarkets, ground meat is typically sold as 牛(ぎゅう)ひき肉(にく) (beef), 豚(ぶた)ひき肉(にく) (pork), 鶏(とり)ひき肉(にく) (chicken), or 合挽(あいび)き肉(にく) (mixed beef and pork). The blend gives dishes more richness than either meat alone.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ひき肉(にく): ground meat — the general term for any ground/minced meat
  • ミンチ: mince — loanword for ground meat, used interchangeably with ひき肉(にく)
