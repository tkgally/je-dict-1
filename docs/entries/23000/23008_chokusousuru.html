1. to ship directly; to send straight to the destination
To send goods or products directly from the source (producer, factory, farm) to the customer or destination without going through intermediaries.
産地(さんち)から直送(ちょくそう)する。
To ship directly from the production area.
北海道(ほっかいどう)から新鮮(しんせん)な海産物(かいさんぶつ)を直送(ちょくそう)します。
We ship fresh seafood directly from Hokkaido.
工場(こうじょう)から直送(ちょくそう)された商品(しょうひん)なので、中間(ちゅうかん)マージンがかからない。
Since the products are shipped directly from the factory, there's no middleman markup.

A common word in commerce and food marketing. Very frequently seen on food packaging and restaurant advertising to emphasize freshness and quality by highlighting the direct-from-source supply chain.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 産地(さんち)直送(ちょくそう): shipped straight from the production area
  • 工場(こうじょう)直送(ちょくそう): factory-direct shipping
  • 漁港(ぎょこう)直送(ちょくそう): shipped straight from the fishing port
  • 農家(のうか)直送(ちょくそう): shipped directly from the farm
  • 直送(ちょくそう)便(びん): direct shipment service

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 発送(はっそう)する: to ship, dispatch — general word for sending out goods; does not imply direct sourcing
  • 配送(はいそう)する: to deliver — focuses on the delivery process rather than the directness of the route
  • 直販(ちょくはん)する: to sell directly — selling directly to consumers, not just shipping

USAGE:
The noun form 産地(さんち)直送(ちょくそう) is extremely common in advertising and menus, functioning almost as a quality label.
