1. castration; neutering; spaying
The surgical removal or deactivation of reproductive organs in an animal or, historically, in a person. In modern Japanese, most commonly used in the context of pet care and livestock management.
猫(ねこ)の去勢(きょせい)手術(しゅじゅつ)を受(う)けさせた。
I had the cat neutered.
去勢(きょせい)した犬(いぬ)は性格(せいかく)が穏(おだ)やかになることが多(おお)い。
Neutered dogs often become calmer in temperament.
野良猫(のらねこ)の増加(ぞうか)を防(ふせ)ぐため、地域(ちいき)で去勢(きょせい)活動(かつどう)が行(おこな)われている。
Neutering activities are being conducted in the community to prevent the increase of stray cats.
2. emasculation; rendering powerless (figurative)
Used figuratively to describe stripping someone or something of power, influence, or vitality.
野党(やとう)は法案(ほうあん)によって議会(ぎかい)が去勢(きょせい)されると批判(ひはん)した。
The opposition criticized the bill, saying it would emasculate the parliament.
規制(きせい)によってメディアが去勢(きょせい)されてはならない。
The media must not be rendered powerless by regulations.
改革(かいかく)の精神(せいしん)が去勢(きょせい)され、形(かたち)だけの制度(せいど)になってしまった。
The spirit of reform was emasculated, and it became a system in name only.

A compound of 去(きょ) (remove, get rid of) and 勢(せい) (vigor, vitality — here referring to reproductive power). The word can function as a noun or as a verb with する.

In modern usage, sense 1 (neutering/spaying) is by far the most common, especially in the context of pet ownership. Japanese veterinary clinics routinely recommend 去勢(きょせい)手術(しゅじゅつ) for male animals; the equivalent procedure for female animals is technically 避妊(ひにん)手術(しゅじゅつ) (spaying), though 去勢(きょせい) is sometimes used loosely for both.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 去勢(きょせい)手術(しゅじゅつ): neutering surgery
  • 去勢(きょせい)する: to neuter, to castrate
  • 去勢(きょせい)済(ず)み: already neutered

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 避妊(ひにん)手術(しゅじゅつ): spaying (for female animals) — the female-specific equivalent
  • 虚勢(きょせい): bluff, bravado — a homophone with completely different meaning; uses 虚(きょ) (empty) instead of 去(きょ) (remove)
