生(なま)ビール

なまびーる
noun
draft beer; draught beer
1. draft beer; draught beer
Beer that has not been heat-pasteurized, served on tap or in unpasteurized bottles and cans. In everyday restaurant and bar usage, it typically refers to beer served from a tap (on draft).
生(なま)ビールを一(いっ)つください。
One draft beer, please.
まずは生(なま)ビールで乾杯(かんぱい)しよう。
Let's start with a toast with draft beer.
この居酒屋(いざかや)の生(なま)ビールは一杯(いっぱい)490円(えん)だ。
Draft beer at this izakaya is 490 yen a glass.
仕事(しごと)の後(あと)に飲(の)む冷(つめ)たい生(なま)ビールは格別(かくべつ)だ。
A cold draft beer after work is something special.

Composed of 生(なま) (raw, fresh, unpasteurized) and ビール (beer). The 生(なま) prefix indicates that the beer has not undergone heat pasteurization (加熱処理(かねつしょり)). In practice, most Japanese beer — including canned and bottled varieties — is technically 生(なま)ビール, but in conversation the term usually means "beer on tap."

In restaurants and izakaya, 生(なま)ビール is the standard first drink ordered. It is extremely common to hear とりあえず生(なま)で ("draft beer for now") as the default opening order. The abbreviated form 生(なま) alone is widely understood to mean draft beer in dining contexts.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 生(なま)ビールを注文(ちゅうもん)する: to order a draft beer
  • とりあえず生(なま)で: draft beer for starters
  • 生(なま)ビール飲(の)み放題(ほうだい): all-you-can-drink draft beer
  • 生(なま)中(ちゅう): medium draft (abbreviated ordering term)

CULTURAL NOTE:
Ordering 生(なま)ビール is deeply embedded in Japanese drinking culture. At izakaya and restaurants, it is the overwhelmingly common first order, often before anyone has even looked at the menu. The phrase とりあえず生(なま) has become almost a catchphrase of after-work socializing.
