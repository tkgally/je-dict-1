乗(の)り越(こ)し

のりこし
noun
riding past one's stop
1. riding past one's stop; overshooting one's station
The act of traveling beyond one's intended destination on a train, bus, or other public transport, requiring adjustment of the fare.
乗(の)り越(こ)しに気(き)をつけて。
Be careful not to ride past your stop.
寝過(ねす)ごして二(ふた)つも乗(の)り越(こ)しをしてしまった。
I fell asleep and rode two stops past my station.
乗(の)り越(こ)し精算機(せいさんき)で差額(さがく)を払(はら)った。
I paid the difference at the fare adjustment machine.

Derived from the verb 乗(の)り越(こ)す (to ride past). This is an extremely common occurrence in daily Japanese train travel, and the railway system is well set up to handle it.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 乗(の)り越(こ)し精算(せいさん): fare adjustment (for riding past one's stop)
  • 乗(の)り越(こ)し精算機(せいさんき): fare adjustment machine
  • 乗(の)り越(こ)し料金(りょうきん): excess fare
  • 乗(の)り越(こ)しをする: to ride past one's stop

USAGE:
At Japanese train stations, 乗(の)り越(こ)し精算機(せいさんき) (fare adjustment machines) are located near the ticket gates. If you ride past your ticketed destination, you insert your ticket into the machine and pay the difference before exiting. With IC cards like Suica or PASMO, the correct fare is automatically deducted, so 乗(の)り越(こ)し精算(せいさん) is mainly relevant for paper ticket holders.
