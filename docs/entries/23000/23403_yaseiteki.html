野性的(やせいてき)

やせいてき
adjective
wild; primal; untamed; rough-hewn (in personality or appearance)
1. having the rough, raw, unrefined quality of a wild animal or something that has not been domesticated; wild, untamed, primal, rough-hewn
Describes a person — or sometimes an animal — whose appearance, voice, manner, or attitude feels raw, instinctive, and uncivilized in an attractive or impressive way. Often applied to actors, athletes, and models whose looks give a sense of animal energy, sharp features, deep tans, or a muscular build. Unlike 野蛮(やばん) ('barbaric'), which is strongly negative, 野性的(やせいてき) typically has a positive or admiring tone — it praises the subject as charismatic, masculine, or primally attractive.
彼(かれ)は野性的(やせいてき)な顔立(かおだ)ちをしている。
He has rugged, wild-looking features.
その俳優(はいゆう)は野性的(やせいてき)な魅力(みりょく)で人気(にんき)がある。
That actor is popular for his wild, primal charm.
日焼(ひや)けした肌(はだ)と鋭(するど)い目(め)が、野性的(やせいてき)な印象(いんしょう)を与(あた)える。
The tanned skin and sharp eyes give him a wild, untamed impression.
都会的(とかいてき)な洗練(せんれん)されたスタイルよりも、野性的(やせいてき)で力強(ちからづよ)い雰囲気(ふんいき)が好(この)まれる場面(ばめん)もある。
There are situations where a wild, powerful atmosphere is preferred over a polished, urban style.

Composed of 野性(やせい) (wild nature, animal instinct) + 的(てき) (-like, characterized by). The base noun 野性(やせい) specifically refers to the innate, instinctive nature of wild creatures, so 野性的(やせいてき) literally means 'having the nature of a wild animal.'

GRAMMAR:

Na-adjective. Before a noun it takes {な}; used adverbially with {に}.

  • 野性的(やせいてき)な男性(だんせい): a wild-looking / rugged man
  • 野性的(やせいてき)に見(み)える: to look wild / primal

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 野性的(やせいてき)な魅力(みりょく): primal / wild charm
  • 野性的(やせいてき)な顔立(かおだ)ち: rugged features
  • 野性的(やせいてき)な目(め)つき: a sharp, animal look in one's eyes
  • 野性的(やせいてき)な雰囲気(ふんいき): a wild / untamed atmosphere
  • 野性的(やせいてき)な美(うつく)しさ: a wild beauty
  • 野性的(やせいてき)なイケメン: a rugged good-looking man

NUANCE:

The word is generally positive and admiring. It often describes:

  • Men with strong, sharp, or sun-tanned features.
  • Athletes with raw physical power.
  • Women whose beauty feels primal rather than delicate.
  • Animals that still display their natural, unbred instincts.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION — 野性(やせい) vs. 野生(やせい):

Both are read やせい, but they are different words:

  • 野生(やせい): wild (not domesticated) — refers to the objective state of living freely in nature, e.g., 野生(やせい)動物(どうぶつ) 'wild animals.'
  • 野性(やせい): wild nature, animal instincts — refers to the innate, untamed character or instincts of a creature.
  • 野性的(やせいてき), with 性(せい) 'nature, character,' therefore means 'having wild instincts or a primal character,' not merely 'undomesticated.' For 'wild animal,' use 野生(やせい).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ワイルド}: wild (loanword) — a near-synonym, frequently used in magazines and fashion talk (ワイルドな{男性(だんせい)).
  • 荒々(あらあら)しい: rough, violent — emphasizes a harsh, forceful manner; less necessarily attractive.
  • 男(おとこ)らしい: manly — emphasizes masculine strength and dependability rather than animal primality.
  • 粗野(そや): crude, uncouth — negative, suggesting poor manners.
  • 野蛮(やばん): barbaric, savage — strongly negative.

REGISTER:
Neutral to colloquial. Very common in pop culture, celebrity and fashion magazines, and casual conversation about people's appearance. Not typical in scholarly or formal contexts.
