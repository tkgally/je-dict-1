開拓者(かいたくしゃ)

かいたくしゃ
noun
pioneer; settler; trailblazer
1. pioneer (literal); settler; one who opens up new land for cultivation
A person who clears and develops previously undeveloped land for farming or settlement. Historically used for those who settled in Hokkaido or overseas colonies.
北海道(ほっかいどう)の開拓者(かいたくしゃ)。
The pioneers of Hokkaido.
開拓者(かいたくしゃ)たちは厳(きび)しい冬(ふゆ)を耐(た)え抜(ぬ)いた。
The settlers endured the harsh winters.
明治(めいじ)時代(じだい)、多(おお)くの開拓者(かいたくしゃ)が未開(みかい)の土地(とち)に入(はい)った。
In the Meiji era, many settlers moved into undeveloped land.
2. pioneer (figurative); trailblazer; person who breaks new ground
A person who is the first to explore or develop a new field, idea, or industry. Used for pioneers of science, technology, art, business, and so on.
この分野(ぶんや)の開拓者(かいたくしゃ)。
A pioneer in this field.
彼女(かのじょ)は女性(じょせい)研究者(けんきゅうしゃ)の開拓者(かいたくしゃ)と呼(よ)ばれている。
She is called a pioneer among female researchers.
AI分野(ぶんや)の開拓者(かいたくしゃ)として、彼(かれ)は世界的(せかいてき)に知(し)られている。
As a pioneer in the field of AI, he is known around the world.

Originally literal, referring to those who cultivated new land, but now more commonly used figuratively for pioneers in any field. The verb 開拓(かいたく)する means both "to cultivate land" and "to pioneer a new field."

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 〜の開拓者(かいたくしゃ): a pioneer of ~
  • 開拓者(かいたくしゃ)精神(せいしん): pioneering spirit; frontier spirit
  • この分野(ぶんや)の開拓者(かいたくしゃ): a pioneer in this field
  • 開拓者(かいたくしゃ)として知(し)られる: to be known as a pioneer
  • 女性(じょせい)開拓者(かいたくしゃ): a pioneering woman (in a field)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • パイオニア: pioneer (borrowed word) — often interchangeable with the figurative sense
  • 先駆者(せんくしゃ): forerunner; pioneer — emphasizes being the first to do something, slightly more formal
  • 草分(くさわ)け: pioneer; founder — more literary; suggests someone who "parted the grass" to make a path
  • 入植者(にゅうしょくしゃ): settler; colonist — stronger focus on moving into new land for settlement

CULTURAL NOTE:
Japan's frontier narrative centers on the colonization of Hokkaido in the Meiji era, and "Boys, be ambitious" (attributed to William S. Clark) is closely associated with the 開拓者(かいたくしゃ) spirit. Sapporo's 開拓神社(かいたくじんじゃ) honors the early settlers of Hokkaido.
