1. to clam up; to keep silent; to refuse to speak
To deliberately stop talking or refuse to discuss something, typically because of fear, secrecy, or reluctance to reveal information.
彼(かれ)は口(くち)を閉(と)ざした。
He clammed up.
その件(けん)について関係者(かんけいしゃ)は口(くち)を閉(と)ざしている。
The people involved are keeping silent about that matter.
記者(きしゃ)に質問(しつもん)されたが、彼女(かのじょ)は過去(かこ)のことについて固(かた)く口(くち)を閉(と)ざした。
When questioned by reporters, she firmly refused to speak about the past.

Literally "to close one's mouth." A set expression using 閉(と)ざす (to shut, to close off), which is more literary than 閉(し)める. The expression implies a deliberate, often stubborn refusal to talk, not merely being quiet.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 固(かた)く口(くち)を閉(と)ざす: to keep one's mouth firmly shut
  • 一切(いっさい)口(くち)を閉(と)ざす: to refuse to say anything at all
  • 口(くち)を閉(と)ざしたまま: while remaining silent

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 黙(だま)る: to fall silent — more general; simply stopping talking
  • 口(くち)をつぐむ: to shut one's mouth — very similar, slightly less literary
  • 沈黙(ちんもく)する: to remain silent — more formal; emphasizes the state of silence itself
