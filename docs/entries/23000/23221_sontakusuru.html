1. to surmise; to conjecture someone's thoughts or wishes
To guess or infer what someone else is thinking or feeling, especially a superior's unspoken wishes, and to act accordingly.
上司(じょうし)の意向(いこう)を忖度(そんたく)する。
To surmise the boss's intentions.
相手(あいて)の気持(きも)ちを忖度(そんたく)して行動(こうどう)した。
I acted after reading the other person's feelings.
官僚(かんりょう)が政治家(せいじか)の意向(いこう)を忖度(そんたく)して手続(てつづ)きを進(すす)めたとされる。
It is alleged that bureaucrats pushed through procedures after surmising the politician's wishes.

An old literary word that gained massive popular attention around 2017 due to a Japanese political scandal (森友(もりとも)問題(もんだい)), where bureaucrats were accused of acting on the unspoken wishes of powerful politicians. Before this, 忖度(そんたく) was a relatively obscure word known mainly to educated readers.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 意向(いこう)を忖度(そんたく)する: to surmise someone's intentions
  • 気持(きも)ちを忖度(そんたく)する: to guess someone's feelings
  • 政治的(せいじてき)忖度(そんたく): political deference to unspoken wishes
  • 忖度(そんたく)政治(せいじ): politics of unspoken influence

USAGE:
Originally neutral or even positive (showing sensitivity to others' feelings), the word has acquired a strongly negative connotation in political contexts, suggesting sycophantic behavior — acting to please superiors without being explicitly instructed. In everyday usage, it can still be neutral, meaning simply to be considerate of others' unspoken needs.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 察(さっ)する: to sense, to perceive — more everyday, broader in meaning
  • 配慮(はいりょ)する: to show consideration — focuses on the action of being considerate, not the guessing
  • 空気(くうき)を読(よ)む: to read the room — colloquial, similar concept of sensing unspoken social dynamics
