1. to omit; to leave out reluctantly; to cut for brevity
To reluctantly leave something out, typically due to time or space constraints. Implies that the omitted content has value and the speaker regrets not being able to include it.
詳細(しょうさい)は割愛(かつあい)します。
I'll omit the details.
時間(じかん)の都合(つごう)で一部(いちぶ)割愛(かつあい)させていただきます。
Due to time constraints, I will have to leave some parts out.
紹介(しょうかい)したいことはたくさんあるが、紙面(しめん)の関係(かんけい)で割愛(かつあい)する。
There's a lot I'd like to introduce, but I'll have to leave it out due to limited space.

Originally meant to part reluctantly with something one loves (愛(あい) = love, 割(か)つ = to cut). The modern usage retains this nuance of reluctant omission — you would include the content if you could, but constraints prevent it. Very common in presentations, speeches, and written articles.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 説明(せつめい)を割愛(かつあい)する: to omit the explanation
  • 詳細(しょうさい)は割愛(かつあい): details omitted
  • 紙面(しめん)の都合(つごう)で割愛(かつあい): omitted due to space constraints
  • 時間(じかん)の都合(つごう)で割愛(かつあい): omitted due to time constraints

USAGE:
This is a formal, polite way to tell an audience or reader that you are skipping something. Using 割愛(かつあい) signals respect for both the omitted content and the audience. It is a staple of academic presentations, business meetings, and journalistic writing.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 省略(しょうりゃく)する: to abbreviate, to omit — more neutral; no connotation of reluctance
  • 省(はぶ)く: to cut, to leave out — more casual and direct
