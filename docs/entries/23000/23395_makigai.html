巻(ま)き貝(がい)

まきがい
noun
univalve; gastropod; snail-type shellfish (with a spiral shell)
1. a mollusk that has a single, typically spiral-coiled shell into which it can retract its body, such as a sea snail, whelk, turban shell, or conch; a univalve, gastropod, or snail-type shellfish
The Japanese everyday term for gastropod mollusks that have a single spiral (coiled) shell — as opposed to 二枚貝(にまいがい), which have two hinged shells. The category includes familiar edible sea snails such as サザエ} (turban shell), {バイ{貝(がい)} (Japanese whelk), and つぶ{貝(がい)} (whelks), as well as non-edible snails. Although the biological class Gastropoda also includes terrestrial land snails (カタツムリ}) and slugs, in everyday Japanese {巻(ま)き貝(がい) is used mainly for marine snails with a visible coiled shell.
巻(ま)き貝(がい)を浜辺(はまべ)で拾(ひろ)った。
I picked up a spiral shell on the beach.
サザエは代表的(だいひょうてき)な巻(ま)き貝(がい)だ。
Sazae is a representative univalve.
巻(ま)き貝(がい)は殻(から)の中(なか)に身(み)を隠(かく)して身(み)を守(まも)る。
Univalves protect themselves by hiding their bodies inside their shells.
巻(ま)き貝(がい)の殻(から)は、ほとんどが右巻(みぎま)きだが、まれに左巻(ひだりま)きのものも見(み)つかる。
Almost all spiral shells coil to the right, but left-coiling ones are occasionally found.

Composed of the native verb 巻(ま)く ('to wind, to coil') + 貝(かい) ('shellfish'). The connecting form 巻(ま)き + 貝(がい) (rendaku) gives 'coiling shellfish,' a vivid description of the spiral shape of the shell. Often written with the okurigana 巻(ま)き貝(がい), but also commonly seen as 巻貝(まきがい) without the okurigana.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 巻(ま)き貝(がい)の殻(から): a univalve shell
  • 巻(ま)き貝(がい)類(るい): univalves (as a biological group)
  • 右巻(みぎま)きの巻(ま)き貝(がい): a right-coiling univalve
  • 巻(ま)き貝(がい)を煮(に)る: to simmer univalve shellfish (as a cooking method)
  • 巻(ま)き貝(がい)を拾(ひろ)う: to pick up a spiral shell (on the beach)

COMMON EDIBLE 巻(ま)き貝(がい):

  • サザエ (栄螺(さざえ)): turban shell — often grilled in the shell (壺焼(つぼや)き).
  • アワビ (鮑(あわび)): abalone — technically a 巻(ま)き貝(がい) though its shell is flat and ear-shaped.
  • バイ貝(がい) (貝(ばい)): Japanese whelk — often simmered in soy sauce.
  • つぶ貝(がい): whelk — common in northern Japan; eaten boiled or grilled.
  • 法螺貝(ほらがい): conch — famously used as a signal horn in mountain Buddhism.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 二枚貝(にまいがい): bivalve — the main biological counterpart; shellfish with two hinged shells, such as clams and oysters.
  • 貝(かい) / 貝類(かいるい): shellfish (in general) — the umbrella word for both univalves and bivalves.
  • カタツムリ (蝸牛(かたつむり)): land snail — biologically also a gastropod, but in everyday Japanese it is not normally called a 巻(ま)き貝(がい).
  • ナメクジ (蛞蝓(なめくじ)): slug — a shell-less gastropod; not called a 巻(ま)き{がい}.

SCIENCE NOTE:
The overwhelming majority of 巻(ま)き貝(がい) species have shells that coil clockwise when viewed from the tip (右巻(みぎま)き). Left-coiling shells (左巻(ひだりま)き) are rare and prized by collectors. This fact gives rise to the metaphorical expression 左巻(ひだりま)き used to describe someone whose thinking is 'backward' or eccentric.
