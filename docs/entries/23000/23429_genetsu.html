1. the lowering of a fever by medication or other means; the action of bringing a feverishly raised body temperature back down toward normal
A medical/health term referring specifically to the act of reducing a fever, not the natural disappearance of fever as a person recovers. Most often used in the context of fever-reducing medication (解熱(げねつ)剤(ざい) or 解熱(げねつ)薬(やく)), in pediatric and home medicine, and on packaging for over-the-counter cold and flu drugs. Used both as a plain noun and as a する-verb (解熱(げねつ)する 'to bring down a fever / for a fever to come down').
解熱(げねつ)剤(ざい)を飲(の)んだ。
I took fever-reducing medicine.
薬(くすり)のおかげで解熱(げねつ)した。
Thanks to the medicine, my fever came down.
熱(ねつ)が高(たか)いので、解熱(げねつ)剤(ざい)を使(つか)って様子(ようす)を見(み)ましょう。
His temperature is high, so let's give him a fever reducer and watch his condition.
子(こ)どもの熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がらず、解熱(げねつ)するまで病院(びょういん)で付(つ)き添(そ)っていた。
My child's fever wouldn't come down, so I stayed by his side at the hospital until it went down.

Composed of 解(げ) ('to undo, to dissolve, to relieve') + 熱(ねつ) ('fever, heat'). Literally 'undoing a fever'. The reading is the on'yomi げ, which is also seen in words like 解毒(げどく) ('detoxification') and 解熱剤(げねつざい) ('fever-reducing drug').

USAGE:
解熱(げねつ) is a medical term, but it appears regularly in everyday life because of its use on cold-medicine packaging and pediatric advice columns. It can be used as a noun, as a する-verb meaning 'to bring down a fever' (transitive: e.g. of a drug acting on fever) or 'for a fever to come down' (intransitive: of a person whose fever subsides).

The most familiar collocation is 解熱(げねつ)剤(ざい) or 解熱(げねつ)薬(やく) ('fever reducer'), which is the standard category name for over-the-counter and prescription drugs that reduce fever.

GRAMMAR:

The verb 解熱(げねつ)する is somewhat unusual in that it can be used both transitively (e.g. of medicine acting on a fever) and intransitively (of a person whose fever has come down):

  • 薬(くすり)が熱(ねつ)を解熱(げねつ)する (transitive use, less common)
  • 彼(かれ)は解熱(げねつ)した (intransitive: 'his fever has gone down')

In practice the intransitive use, treating 解熱(げねつ)する as 'to recover from fever', is more common in everyday speech.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 解熱(げねつ)剤(ざい): fever reducer, antipyretic drug
  • 解熱(げねつ)薬(やく): fever-reducing medicine
  • 解熱(げねつ)作用(さよう): antipyretic action (medical)
  • 解熱(げねつ)鎮痛(ちんつう)剤(ざい): fever-reducing painkiller (a common drug category)
  • 解熱(げねつ)する: to bring down a fever; for a fever to come down
  • 解熱(げねつ)後(ご): after the fever has come down
  • 解熱(げねつ)後(ご) 24時間(じかん): 24 hours after the fever has come down

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がる: for a fever to go down — the everyday spoken expression. Less formal than 解熱(げねつ)する.
  • 熱(ねつ)冷(さ)まし: a fever cooler — colloquial term for a fever-reducing drug (especially the kind given to children); alternatively the cold-pack patches stuck to the forehead.
  • 下熱(げねつ): same as 解熱(げねつ); an alternative kanji writing, less common in modern usage.
  • 解毒(げどく): detoxification — same 解(げ) prefix, applied to poisons.
  • 鎮痛(ちんつう): pain relief — frequently combined with 解熱(げねつ) in drug-category names.
  • 発熱(はつねつ): fever, the development of a fever — the opposite event.

DOMAIN NOTE:
In Japanese health-care guidance for influenza and infectious diseases, a common rule is that a patient may return to school or work 解熱(げねつ)後(ご) 48 時間(じかん) ('48 hours after the fever has gone down'). This phrasing is widely used in school notices and corporate sick-leave policies, especially for children.
