眠(ねむ)れる

ねむれる
verb (ichidan)
to be able to sleep; sleeping
1. to be able to sleep; can sleep
The potential form of 眠(ねむ)る, indicating the ability to fall asleep or sleep well.
昨日(きのう)はよく眠(ねむ)れた。
I was able to sleep well last night.
コーヒーを飲(の)みすぎて眠(ねむ)れない。
I drank too much coffee and can't sleep.
静(しず)かな環境(かんきょう)でないと眠(ねむ)れない人(ひと)もいる。
Some people can't sleep unless the environment is quiet.
2. sleeping; dormant; lying dormant
Used as an attributive adjective (連体形(れんたいけい)) meaning "sleeping" or "dormant," as in 眠(ねむ)れる森(もり) (sleeping forest).
眠(ねむ)れる森(もり)の美女(びじょ)。
Sleeping Beauty (literally: the sleeping forest's beauty).
この地域(ちいき)には眠(ねむ)れる資源(しげん)がまだたくさんある。
There are still many dormant resources in this region.
眠(ねむ)れる才能(さいのう)を目覚(めざ)めさせるきっかけが必要(ひつよう)だ。
A trigger is needed to awaken dormant talent.

USAGE:
Sense 1 is the potential form of 眠(ねむ)る and is very common in everyday speech, especially in the negative (眠(ねむ)れない — can't sleep).

Sense 2 is a literary usage where 眠(ねむ)れる functions as an attributive form meaning "sleeping" or "dormant." This is the classical Japanese 已然形(いぜんけい) (realis form) used as a modifier, most famously seen in 眠(ねむ)れる森(もり)の美女(びじょ) (Sleeping Beauty).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • よく眠(ねむ)れる (to sleep well / to be able to sleep well)
  • 眠(ねむ)れない夜(よる) (a sleepless night)
  • 眠(ねむ)れる森(もり) (sleeping forest)
  • 眠(ねむ)れる獅子(しし) (sleeping lion — figurative for hidden potential)
