家紋(かもん)

かもん
noun
family crest
1. family crest; family emblem
A hereditary emblem used to identify a Japanese family, clan, or household. Originating in the Heian period, family crests feature stylized designs based on plants, animals, geometric shapes, or other motifs. They appear on formal clothing, tombstones, and traditional items.
うちの家紋(かもん)は桐(きり)だ。
Our family crest is the paulownia.
着物(きもの)に家紋(かもん)が入(はい)っている。
The kimono has a family crest on it.
自分(じぶん)の家紋(かもん)を知(し)らない人(ひと)が最近(さいきん)は増(ふ)えているらしい。
Apparently, more and more people these days don't know their own family crest.

USAGE: 家紋(かもん) are found on formal kimono (紋付(もんつき)), gravestones, 提灯(ちょうちん) (paper lanterns), and family-owned items. Common motifs include 桐(きり) (paulownia), 菊(きく) (chrysanthemum), and 梅(うめ) (plum blossom).

Historically, samurai families used 家紋(かもん) to identify themselves on the battlefield. Today, they are most commonly seen at weddings, funerals, and other formal ceremonies.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 家紋(かもん)が入(はい)る: to bear a family crest
  • 家紋(かもん)入(い)りの着物(きもの): kimono with a family crest
  • 家紋(かもん)を調(しら)べる: to look up one's family crest
