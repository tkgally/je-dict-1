道草(みちくさ)

みちくさ
noun
detour; dawdling on the way
1. detour; dawdling; loitering on the way
Stopping or lingering along the way instead of going directly to one's destination. Often used with する or 食(く)う. Originally referred to a horse stopping to eat grass on the roadside.
道草(みちくさ)を食(く)った。
I dawdled on the way.
子(こ)どもが道草(みちくさ)をして帰(かえ)りが遅(おそ)くなった。
The child dawdled on the way and got home late.
道草(みちくさ)しないでまっすぐ帰(かえ)ってきなさい。
Come straight home without dawdling.

USAGE:
Most commonly used in the set phrase 道草(みちくさ)を食(く)う (to dawdle, literally "to eat roadside grass"). Can also be used with する. Typically describes children loitering on the way home from school, but applies to anyone making unnecessary stops.

ETYMOLOGY:
From 道(みち) (road) + 草(くさ) (grass). Originally described a horse stopping to graze on grass along the road during a journey, delaying the traveler.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 道草(みちくさ)を食(く)う (to dawdle on the way)
  • 道草(みちくさ)する (to loiter, to make detours)
