目(め)を伏(ふ)せる

めをふせる
expression / verb (ichidan)
to look down; to cast one's eyes down; to avert one's gaze
1. to look down; to cast one's eyes downward
To lower one's gaze, typically out of shyness, sadness, shame, or deference. A common expression in literary and everyday descriptions of body language.
彼女(かのじょ)は目(め)を伏(ふ)せた。
She looked down.
恥(は)ずかしくて目(め)を伏(ふ)せてしまった。
I was so embarrassed that I cast my eyes down.
先生(せんせい)に叱(しか)られて、生徒(せいと)たちは黙(だま)って目(め)を伏(ふ)せるしかなかった。
Scolded by the teacher, the students could only remain silent and look down.

USAGE:
目(め)を伏(ふ)せる describes lowering the eyes to avoid eye contact. It often conveys emotions such as shame, shyness, sadness, or respect. It is frequently used in literary descriptions and narratives.

CONJUGATION:
The verb 伏(ふ)せる is ichidan: 伏(ふ)せる → 伏(ふ)せない → 伏(ふ)せた → 伏(ふ)せて.

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 目(め)を逸(そ)らす (to avert one's eyes — more active avoidance)
  • 俯(うつむ)く (to hang one's head — involves tilting the whole head down)
  • 目(め)を落(お)とす (to drop one's gaze — similar but slightly more literary)
