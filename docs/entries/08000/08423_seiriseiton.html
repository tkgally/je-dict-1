1. tidying up; organizing; putting things in order
the act of arranging items neatly and systematically, often used as a set phrase emphasizing thorough organization
部屋(へや)の整理整頓(せいりせいとん)をする。
To tidy up the room.
整理整頓(せいりせいとん)が苦手(にがて)だ。
I'm bad at organizing things.
仕事(しごと)を始(はじ)める前(まえ)に机(つくえ)の整理整頓(せいりせいとん)をした。
I tidied up my desk before starting work.

A 四字熟語(よじじゅくご) combining 整理(せいり) (sorting, organizing — deciding what to keep) and 整頓(せいとん) (arranging neatly — putting things in their place). Together they emphasize thorough organization.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 整理整頓(せいりせいとん)する: to organize and tidy up
  • 整理整頓(せいりせいとん)が行(い)き届(とど)く: to be thoroughly organized
  • 整理整頓(せいりせいとん)が苦手(にがて): to be bad at organizing

CULTURAL NOTE:
Often seen on signs in schools and workplaces. 整理整頓(せいりせいとん) is considered a fundamental virtue in Japanese culture, taught from elementary school onward.

RELATED:

  • 片(かた)づける: to tidy up (casual, everyday)
  • 断捨離(だんしゃり): decluttering (modern concept, minimalism-oriented)
  • 整理(せいり): organizing/sorting (used alone as well)
