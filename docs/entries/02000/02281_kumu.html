1. to draw (water)
To collect water from a well or source.
井戸(いど)から水(みず)を汲(く)む。
To draw water from a well.
川(かわ)から水(みず)を汲(く)んできた。
I drew water from the river.
山(やま)の湧(わ)き水(みず)を汲(く)みに行(い)く。
To go draw spring water from the mountain.
2. to scoop
To take up liquid with a container.
お風呂(ふろ)の湯(ゆ)を汲(く)む。
To scoop bath water.
バケツで水(みず)を汲(く)む。
To scoop water with a bucket.
ひしゃくで水(みず)を汲(く)んで手(て)を洗(あら)った。
I scooped water with a ladle and washed my hands.

汲(く)む means to draw or scoop liquid, typically water, from a source.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive)
  • Takes を for the liquid and から for the source

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 水(みず)を汲(く)む: to draw water
  • 井戸(いど)から汲(く)む: to draw from a well
  • 湯(ゆ)を汲(く)む: to scoop hot water
  • 心(こころ)を汲(く)む: to understand someone's feelings (figurative)

DISTINCTION (homophones):

  • 汲(く)む: to draw/scoop water
  • 組(く)む: to put together, cross (arms), team up
  • 酌(く)む: to pour drinks (for someone)

The figurative use 心(こころ)を汲(く)む or 意(い)を汲(く)む means to understand and appreciate someone's feelings or intentions.
