1. to fret; to worry impatiently; to be anxious and restless
To feel restless anxiety while waiting for or worrying about something one cannot control. Built on the mimetic やきもき, which conveys nervous, fidgety impatience.
結果(けっか)を待(ま)ってやきもきする。
I fret while waiting for the results.
子供(こども)の帰(かえ)りが遅(おそ)くて、親(おや)はやきもきした。
The parents fretted because their child was late coming home.
返事(へんじ)がなかなか来(こ)ないので、一日中(いちにちじゅう)やきもきして過(す)ごした。
Since the reply just wouldn't come, I spent the whole day fretting.

Describes nervous, impatient worry, especially while waiting for an outcome one cannot influence. The subject is often watching someone else's situation and unable to help.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 待(ま)ってやきもきする: to fret while waiting
  • 内心(ないしん)やきもきする: to fret inwardly
  • やきもきさせる: to keep (someone) on edge; to make (someone) anxious

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • いらいらする: to be irritated — focuses on annoyance rather than anxious waiting
  • はらはらする: to be on tenterhooks — nervous suspense while watching, often more momentary
