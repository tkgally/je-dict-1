脱色剤(だっしょくざい)

だっしょくざい
noun
decolorizing agent; bleach (for hair)
1. decolorizing agent; bleach used to remove color
A chemical product that removes color from hair, fabric, or other material. Especially common for the hair bleach used to lighten hair before dyeing.
脱色剤(だっしょくざい)を使(つか)う。
To use a decolorizing agent.
髪(かみ)を染(そ)める前(まえ)に脱色剤(だっしょくざい)で明(あか)るくする。
Before dyeing the hair, you lighten it with a decolorizing agent.
脱色剤(だっしょくざい)は刺激(しげき)が強(つよ)いので、使(つか)いすぎに注意(ちゅうい)する。
Decolorizing agents are harsh, so be careful not to overuse them.

Compound of 脱色(だっしょく) (removing color) + 剤(ざい) (agent, preparation). Most often refers to hair bleach, but also to color removers for cloth or other materials.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 脱色剤(だっしょくざい)を塗(ぬ)る: to apply the decolorizing agent
  • 市販(しはん)の脱色剤(だっしょくざい): a commercially sold bleach

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 漂白剤(ひょうはくざい): bleach — mainly for whitening laundry and removing stains
  • 染料(せんりょう): dye — adds color rather than removing it
