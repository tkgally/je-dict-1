仮(かり)に

かりに
adverb
supposing; even if; hypothetically
1. supposing; assuming; hypothetically
Used to introduce a hypothetical situation for discussion or consideration.
仮(かり)に宝(たから)くじが当(あ)たったら、何(なに)を買(か)う？
Supposing you won the lottery, what would you buy?
仮(かり)に百万(ひゃくまん)円(えん)あったら、旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きたい。
Supposing I had a million yen, I'd want to go on a trip.
仮(かり)に明日(あした)地震(じしん)が来(き)たら、どうするか考(かんが)えておこう。
Let's think about what we'd do supposing an earthquake hit tomorrow.
2. even if; even supposing
Used to introduce a concessive condition, often followed by ても.
仮(かり)にそれが本当(ほんとう)だとしても、信(しん)じられない。
Even if that were true, I wouldn't believe it.
仮(かり)に失敗(しっぱい)しても、諦(あきら)めない。
Even if I fail, I won't give up.
仮(かり)に彼(かれ)の言(い)うことが正(ただ)しいとしても、やり方(かた)に問題(もんだい)がある。
Even supposing what he says is correct, there's a problem with the way he does things.

仮(かり)に sets up a supposition the speaker is not asserting — 'suppose that', 'let's say'. It is placed at the head of the clause and is picked up later by a conditional ending, so it never stands alone.

Formation: 仮(かり) 'provisional, tentative' + に, the particle that makes it adverbial.

Two patterns, differing in what follows:

  • 仮(かり)に〜たら / 〜ば: pure supposition — 'supposing X happened, then…' (examples 1–3)
  • 仮(かり)に〜としても / 〜ても: concessive — 'even granting X, still…' (examples 4–6)

The second pattern is the more distinctive one: it concedes a premise for the sake of argument and then rejects the conclusion drawn from it, which makes 仮(かり)に common in debate and written reasoning.

Similar words:

  • もし: the neutral, everyday 'if'; 仮(かり)に is more deliberate and marks the premise as hypothetical rather than merely uncertain
  • たとえ: 'even if', emphatic, and always concessive
  • 万(まん)が一(いち): 'if by any chance', reserved for unlikely and usually unwelcome possibilities
