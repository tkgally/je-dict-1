酸味(さんみ)

さんみ
noun
sourness; acidity; tartness
1. sourness; acidity; tartness
The sour taste quality, one of the five basic tastes. Found in citrus fruits, vinegar, and fermented foods.
このりんごは酸味(さんみ)が強(つよ)い。
This apple has a strong sourness.
レモンの酸味(さんみ)が料理(りょうり)を引(ひ)き立(た)てる。
The acidity of lemon enhances the dish.
酸味(さんみ)と甘味(あまみ)のバランスがいい。
The balance between sourness and sweetness is good.

酸味(さんみ) is sourness or acidity considered as a quality of a food or drink — one of the five basic tastes. It is the standard word in cooking, and in describing coffee, wine, and fruit.

Formation: 酸(さん) 'acid' + 味(み) 'taste.' The same 〜味(み) suffix names the other tastes below.

Note the part of speech: 酸味(さんみ) is a noun naming the quality, so it is something a food 強(つよ)い or weak in. To call something sour directly, use the i-adjective 酸(す)っぱい. 酸味(さんみ) is also the more appreciative of the two: 酸(す)っぱい often suggests something has gone off, while 酸味(さんみ) usually describes a flavour worth having.

Collocations:

  • 酸味(さんみ)が強(つよ)い: strongly sour
  • 酸味(さんみ)を加(くわ)える: to add acidity
  • 酸味(さんみ)が利(き)いている: has a pleasant tang

The five basic tastes:

  • 甘味(あまみ): sweetness
  • 酸味(さんみ): sourness
  • 塩味(しおあじ) / 塩気(しおけ): saltiness
  • 苦味(にがみ): bitterness
  • 旨味(うまみ): umami, savouriness

Related Words

Contrast: 甘味(あまみ) (sweetness)
