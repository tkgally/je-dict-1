1. to arise; to break out; to be stirred up
For something like controversy, emotion, or phenomena to suddenly arise or develop with momentum.
大(おお)きな問題(もんだい)が巻(ま)き起(お)こった。
A big problem arose.
大(おお)きな拍手(はくしゅ)が巻(ま)き起(お)こった。
A big round of applause erupted.
その発言(はつげん)で大(おお)きな議論(ぎろん)が巻(ま)き起(お)こった。
That statement sparked a huge debate.
ネットで批判(ひはん)の嵐(あらし)が巻(ま)き起(お)こった。
A storm of criticism arose online.
観客席(かんきゃくせき)から拍手喝采(はくしゅかっさい)が巻(ま)き起(お)こり、会場(かいじょう)は興奮(こうふん)に包(つつ)まれた。
Applause and cheers erupted from the audience, and the venue was filled with excitement.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Intransitive. The reaction is the subject and arises on its own; nobody 巻(ま)き起(お)こる anything.
  • The transitive partner 巻(ま)き起(お)こす means to stir something up deliberately.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 議論(ぎろん)が巻(ま)き起(お)こる (debate breaks out)
  • 批判(ひはん)が巻(ま)き起(お)こる (criticism erupts)
  • 拍手(はくしゅ)が巻(ま)き起(お)こる (applause breaks out)

REGISTER:
What 巻(ま)き起(お)こる is almost always a reaction that spreads through a crowd or a society — debate, criticism, applause, a craze. A single small event simply 起(お)こる. The compound is built from 巻(ま)く (to wind, to roll) plus 起(お)こる, and keeps the image of something rolling outward as it arises; it is common in news writing.
