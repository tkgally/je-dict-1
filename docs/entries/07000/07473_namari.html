訛(なま)り

なまり
noun
accent; dialectal speech

Definitions

1. regional accent; dialectal influence in speech
The distinctive pronunciation or intonation patterns that reveal a speaker's regional origin, or dialectal elements mixed into standard speech.

Examples

東北(とうほく)の訛(なま)りがある。
She has a Tohoku accent.
訛(なま)りが強(つよ)くて、どこの出身(しゅっしん)かすぐわかる。
His accent is so strong that you can immediately tell where he's from.
訛(なま)りを直(なお)そうとしたが、なかなか直(なお)らない。
I tried to get rid of my accent, but it's hard to change.
故郷(こきょう)に帰(かえ)ると訛(なま)りが出(で)る。
When I go back to my hometown, my accent comes out.

Notes

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 訛(なま)りがある: have an accent
  • 訛(なま)りが強(つよ)い: have a strong accent
  • 訛(なま)りが出(で)る: one's accent comes out
  • 訛(なま)りを直(なお)す: fix/lose one's accent
  • お国(くに)訛(なま)り: one's regional accent

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 訛(なま)り vs. 方言(ほうげん)
  • 訛(なま)り: focuses on pronunciation/accent features; often refers to dialectal traces in otherwise standard speech
  • 方言(ほうげん): the full dialect including vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation

NOTE:
Unlike English where "accent" is neutral, 訛(なま)り in Japanese can sometimes carry a nuance of "deviation from standard," though this varies by context. The word comes from the verb 訛(なま)る (to speak with an accent).

REGISTER:
Neutral. However, telling someone directly that their speech has 訛(なま)り may be perceived as pointing out a flaw in formal contexts.

Related Words

Related: 方言(ほうげん) (dialect)
Contrast: 標準語(ひょうじゅんご) (standard language)
