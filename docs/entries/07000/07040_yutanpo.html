湯(ゆ)たんぽ

ゆたんぽ
noun
hot water bottle
1. hot water bottle
A container filled with hot water, used to warm the body, especially in bed.
冬(ふゆ)は湯(ゆ)たんぽを布団(ふとん)に入(い)れて寝(ね)る。
In winter, I put a hot water bottle in the futon when I sleep.
湯(ゆ)たんぽにお湯(ゆ)を入(い)れた。
I filled the hot water bottle with hot water.
最近(さいきん)の湯(ゆ)たんぽは電子(でんし)レンジで温(あたた)められる。
Modern hot water bottles can be heated in the microwave.

A 湯(ゆ)たんぽ is a hot water bottle, put into the bedding an hour before turning in so the futon is warm on a cold night. It remains a normal winter object in Japanese homes rather than an old-fashioned one.

Formation: 湯(ゆ) 'hot water' + たんぽ, from the Chinese-derived 湯婆(たんぽ) 'warming vessel'. Also written 湯湯婆(ゆたんぽ) or 湯婆(ゆたんぽ), though the mixed form above is what people actually write.

Types, by material and heating method:

  • 金属(きんぞく)製(せい): metal, the traditional kind, which can go straight on a stove
  • 樹脂(じゅし)製(せい) / ゴム製(せい): plastic and rubber, the everyday kinds
  • 電気(でんき)湯(ゆ)たんぽ: an electrically heated version
  • レンジで温(あたた)めるタイプ: gel-filled, microwaved rather than filled

Collocations:

  • 湯(ゆ)たんぽを入(い)れる: to put a hot water bottle in the bed
  • 湯(ゆ)たんぽで足(あし)を温(あたた)める: to warm one's feet with it

Prolonged contact with the skin causes 低温(ていおん)やけど, a low-temperature burn, so a 湯(ゆ)たんぽ is always used inside a cover.
