1. to bounce back; to rebound; to spring back
For a physical object to bounce off a surface and return.
ボールが壁(かべ)に当(あ)たって跳(は)ね返(かえ)ってきた。
The ball hit the wall and bounced back.
弾丸(だんがん)が装甲(そうこう)に跳(は)ね返(かえ)った。
The bullet ricocheted off the armor.
石(いし)が地面(じめん)に落(お)ちて跳(は)ね返(かえ)った。
The stone hit the ground and bounced back.
2. to backfire; to have a negative effect return
For negative consequences to return to the person who caused them, or for something to have an unintended rebound effect.
悪口(わるくち)は自分(じぶん)に跳(は)ね返(かえ)る。
Bad-mouthing others comes back to you.
無理(むり)な値下(ねさ)げは結局(けっきょく)品質(ひんしつ)の低下(ていか)として跳(は)ね返(かえ)ってくる。
Unreasonable price cuts ultimately come back as a decline in quality.
人(ひと)をだますと、いつか自分(じぶん)に跳(は)ね返(かえ)ってくるものだ。
If you deceive others, it will eventually come back to haunt you.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Intransitive. The transitive partner 跳(は)ね返(かえ)す means to repel something or throw it back.

ASPECT:

  • 跳(は)ね返(かえ)ってくる is the usual form for sense 2. The てくる makes the consequence arrive back at the person responsible, which is the whole point of that sense.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • ボールが跳(は)ね返(かえ)る (a ball bounces back)
  • 光(ひかり)が跳(は)ね返(かえ)る (light reflects off a surface)
  • 自分(じぶん)に跳(は)ね返(かえ)る (to rebound on oneself)
  • コストに跳(は)ね返(かえ)る (to feed through into costs)

Sense 2 is a live metaphor rather than a fixed idiom: what comes back is the consequence of what you did, and it is the sense you meet most often in business and news writing. The compound is 跳(は)ねる (to spring) + 返(かえ)る (to go back).
