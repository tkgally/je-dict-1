お世辞(せじ)

おせじ
noun
flattery; compliment; lip service
1. flattery; compliment; lip service
Words of praise that may not be entirely sincere, often said to please someone or be polite.
お世辞(せじ)でもうれしいです。
Even if it's just flattery, I'm happy to hear it.
お世辞(せじ)を言(い)うのが上手(じょうず)な人(ひと)だ。
They're good at giving compliments.
お世辞(せじ)にも上手(うま)いとは言(い)えない。
I can't say it's good, even as a compliment.
Set phrase for 'not even being polite can make it good.'

お世辞(せじ) is praise offered to please the listener rather than to report an honest opinion — the compliment that smooths a social occasion. Calling something お世辞(せじ) implies it may not be sincere, so it is not normally used of one's own praise except to disclaim it.

Formation: お (polite prefix) + 世辞(せじ) 'compliment'. The bare 世辞(せじ) exists but is rarely used alone; the お form is the ordinary word.

Collocations:

  • お世辞(せじ)を言(い)う: to pay someone a compliment
  • お世辞(せじ)が上手(じょうず): good at flattery
  • お世辞(せじ)抜(ぬ)きで: frankly, flattery aside
  • お世辞(せじ)にも〜ない: can't call it ~ even generously — a fixed frame for polite understatement, as in example 3

Similar words:

  • 褒(ほ)め言葉(ことば): words of praise, with no implication of insincerity
  • おべっか: brown-nosing, sucking up — openly negative
  • 追従(ついしょう): fawning, sycophancy (formal, strongly negative)
  • 社交辞令(しゃこうじれい): social pleasantries — the wider category of things said for form's sake
