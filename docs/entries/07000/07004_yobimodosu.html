1. to call back; to summon back
To ask or order someone who has left to return.
子供(こども)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
I called the child back.
出(で)て行(い)った息子(むすこ)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
I called back my son who had left.
会社(かいしゃ)は海外(かいがい)にいた社員(しゃいん)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
The company recalled employees who were overseas.
医者(いしゃ)が患者(かんじゃ)の意識(いしき)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)そうとした。
The doctor tried to bring the patient back to consciousness.
危険(きけん)だと感(かん)じて、先(さき)に行(い)った仲間(なかま)を大声(おおごえ)で呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
Sensing danger, I called back my companions who had gone ahead in a loud voice.
2. to recall; to bring back (memories, feelings)
To cause something from the past to come back to mind or existence.
思(おも)い出(で)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)す。
To bring back memories.
その写真(しゃしん)が昔(むかし)の思(おも)い出(で)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
That photo brought back old memories.
その曲(きょく)は青春時代(せいしゅんじだい)の思(おも)い出(で)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)す。
That song brings back memories of my youth.
春(はる)の花(はな)の香(かお)りが、子供(こども)の頃(ころ)の楽(たの)しい日々(ひび)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)してくれた。
The scent of spring flowers brought back the happy days of my childhood.
懐(なつ)かしい味(あじ)が、家族(かぞく)で食卓(しょくたく)を囲(かこ)んだ日(ひ)のことを呼(よ)び戻(もど)した。
The nostalgic taste brought back memories of the days we gathered around the dinner table as a family.

FORMATION:
A compound verb: 呼(よ)ぶ (to call) + 戻(もど)す (to send back) — to call someone or something so that it comes back. In sense 2 the subject is typically an inanimate trigger (a photograph, a song, a scent) and the object is a memory or a feeling.

COLLOCATIONS:

  • 人(ひと)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)す — to call someone back
  • 記憶(きおく)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)す — to recall a memory
  • 意識(いしき)を呼(よ)び戻(もど)す — to bring someone back to consciousness
  • 本国(ほんごく)に呼(よ)び戻(もど)す — to recall someone to their home country

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 呼(よ)び出(だ)す summons someone *to* the speaker — to an office, to the phone. 呼(よ)び戻(もど)す returns them to where they were before.
  • 取(と)り戻(もど)す is what you recover for yourself (health, confidence, a lead); 呼(よ)び戻(もど)す works by calling out, whether to a person or, figuratively, to the past.
