負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)い

まけずぎらい
noun, na-adjective
competitive; hating to lose; determined not to be beaten
1. competitive; hating to lose; strong-willed
A personality trait characterized by refusing to give up and striving to win or succeed. Generally seen as a positive quality in Japanese culture.
彼女(かのじょ)は負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)いな性格(せいかく)だ。
She has a competitive personality.
負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)いだから絶対(ぜったい)に諦(あきら)めない。
Because I hate losing, I never give up.
子供(こども)の頃(ころ)から負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)いだった。
I've been competitive since I was a child.

負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)い describes someone who cannot stand to come off worse — in a game, an argument, an exam, or just a comparison. It is a standing character trait, not a mood, so it is normally predicated of a person or a 性格(せいかく) rather than of a single occasion.

Formation: 負(ま)けず 'not losing' (a literary negative of 負(ま)ける) + 嫌(きら)い 'dislike' — literally 'hating not to win'. The apparent double negative is a historical quirk; the meaning is simply 'hates losing'.

As a na-adjective it takes な before a noun:

  • 負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)いな人(ひと): a competitive person
  • 負(ま)けず嫌(ぎら)いな性格(せいかく): a competitive streak

The word is usually approving or at worst affectionate — it suggests drive and persistence, and is a stock compliment for athletes and hard-working students. To criticise the same trait a speaker would reach for something else, such as 'a sore loser'.

Compare 勝(か)ち気(き), a near synonym focused on the wish to win rather than the dislike of losing, and used more often of women in older writing.
