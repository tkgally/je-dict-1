1. bar (drinking establishment)
A drinking establishment typically featuring a counter where customers sit and order drinks, often with a more intimate atmosphere than an izakaya.
駅前(えきまえ)にいいバーがある。
There's a nice bar in front of the station.
バーでカクテルを注文(ちゅうもん)した。
I ordered a cocktail at the bar.
彼(かれ)は行(い)きつけのバーで友人(ゆうじん)と飲(の)んでいる。
He's drinking with friends at his regular bar.

A バー is a Western-style drinking place built around a counter: cocktails and spirits, a quiet room, and no set meal. Japanese has several words for places you drink, and they are chosen by the kind of place, not by formality.

SIMILAR PLACES:

  • 居酒屋(いざかや): food and drink together, groups at tables, lively and cheap. The default for a night out with colleagues.
  • バー: drinks come first, usually at the counter, and the room is quiet enough to talk.
  • スナック: a small counter bar run by a ママ who talks with the customers; normally a set charge rather than per-drink pricing.
  • 酒場(さかば): the general cover term, now old-fashioned. It appears on signs and in song titles more than in conversation.

COLLOCATIONS:

  • バーに行(い)く: go to a bar
  • バーで飲(の)む: drink at a bar
  • 行(い)きつけのバー: one's regular bar
  • バーテンダー: bartender (also shortened to バーテン)
