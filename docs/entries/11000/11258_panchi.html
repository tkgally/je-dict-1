パンチ

ぱんち
noun, verb (suru)
punch; impact, force; hole punch
1. punch (strike with the fist)
A blow delivered with the fist, as in boxing or fighting.
強(つよ)いパンチを食(く)らった。
I took a hard punch.
ボクサーが相手(あいて)にパンチを繰(く)り出(だ)した。
The boxer threw punches at his opponent.
2. impact, punch, force
Figurative strength or striking quality, used to describe food with bold flavor, designs with visual impact, or statements with force.
彼(かれ)のジョークはいつもパンチが効(き)いている。
His jokes always pack a punch.
この料理(りょうり)はパンチがあって美味(おい)しい。
This dish has a bold punch of flavor and is delicious.
もう少(すこ)しパンチの効(き)いたデザインにしたい。
I'd like a design with a bit more impact.
3. hole punch
A tool used to make holes in paper, typically for filing in binders.
書類(しょるい)にパンチで穴(あな)を開(あ)けてファイルに綴(と)じた。
I punched holes in the documents and filed them in a binder.
二(に)つ穴(あな)パンチを貸(か)してもらえますか。
Could I borrow a two-hole punch?
パンチで一度(いちど)に穴(あな)を開(あ)けられるのは十枚(じゅうまい)までだ。
You can punch holes in up to ten sheets at once.

USAGE:
The figurative sense 2 is very common in everyday Japanese. パンチが効(き)いている / パンチがある describes something with strong impact — spicy food, bold designs, memorable jokes, etc.

Sense 3 (hole punch) is common in office settings. The full form is 穴(あな)あけパンチ, but パンチ alone is usually sufficient in context.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • パンチを食(く)らう: to take a punch
  • パンチを繰(く)り出(だ)す: to throw punches
  • パンチが効(き)いている: to pack a punch, to have impact
  • パンチが強(つよ)い: to have strong impact
  • パンチで穴(あな)を開(あ)ける: to punch holes
