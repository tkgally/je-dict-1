装束(しょうぞく)

しょうぞく
noun
costume, garb, attire
1. costume, garb, attire, ceremonial clothing
Clothing or costume, especially traditional, ceremonial, or period-specific attire. Often refers to the formal garments worn in traditional Japanese arts, religious ceremonies, or historical contexts.
神主(かんぬし)が白(しろ)い装束(しょうぞく)を身(み)につけた。
The Shinto priest put on white vestments.
平安(へいあん)時代(じだい)の装束(しょうぞく)を再現(さいげん)した展示(てんじ)が人気(にんき)だ。
An exhibit recreating Heian-period costumes is popular.
能(のう)の舞台(ぶたい)では、役柄(やくがら)に合(あ)わせた装束(しょうぞく)が用(もち)いられ、その色(いろ)や柄(がら)にも意味(いみ)がある。
On the noh stage, costumes suited to each role are used, and their colors and patterns carry meaning.

USAGE:
Primarily used for traditional, historical, or ceremonial clothing rather than everyday attire. Commonly encountered in discussions of traditional performing arts (能(のう), 狂言(きょうげん)), Shinto rituals, and historical dramas.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 装束(しょうぞく)を身(み)につける: to wear ceremonial garb
  • 狩衣(かりぎぬ)装束(しょうぞく): hunting robe attire (Heian nobility)
  • 白(しろ)装束(しょうぞく): white vestments/robes
