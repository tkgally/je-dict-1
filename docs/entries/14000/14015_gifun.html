義憤(ぎふん)

ぎふん
noun
righteous indignation
1. righteous indignation; righteous anger
Anger or outrage motivated by a sense of justice, rather than personal grievance. The feeling of fury at witnessing injustice, corruption, or wrongdoing.
不正(ふせい)に対(たい)して義憤(ぎふん)を感(かん)じた。
I felt righteous indignation at the injustice.
市民(しみん)たちは義憤(ぎふん)に駆(か)られてデモに参加(さんか)した。
The citizens, driven by righteous anger, participated in the demonstration.
弱(よわ)い者(もの)がいじめられているのを見(み)て、義憤(ぎふん)を抑(おさ)えられなかった。
Seeing the weak being bullied, I couldn't suppress my righteous anger.

A compound of 義(ぎ) (righteousness, justice) and 憤(ふん) (indignation, anger).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 義憤(ぎふん)を感(かん)じる (feel righteous indignation)
  • 義憤(ぎふん)に駆(か)られる (be driven by righteous anger)
  • 義憤(ぎふん)を抑(おさ)える (suppress one's righteous anger)
  • 義憤(ぎふん)に燃(も)える (burn with righteous anger)

Distinguished from ordinary 怒(いか)り (anger) by its moral dimension — 義憤(ぎふん) is anger on behalf of justice or others, not personal frustration. Frequently used in written and formal contexts, especially in journalism and political commentary.
