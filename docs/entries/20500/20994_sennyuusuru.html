1. to infiltrate; to sneak in; to slip into; to go undercover
To secretly enter a place, organization, or group, often for the purpose of gathering information or carrying out a covert mission.
スパイが敵(てき)の基地(きち)に潜入(せんにゅう)した。
The spy infiltrated the enemy base.
記者(きしゃ)が潜入(せんにゅう)取材(しゅざい)を行(おこな)った。
The journalist conducted undercover reporting.
警察官(けいさつかん)が犯罪(はんざい)グループに潜入(せんにゅう)して、内部(ないぶ)情報(じょうほう)を集(あつ)めた。
A police officer infiltrated the criminal group and gathered inside information.

A する verb composed of 潜(せん) (hide, lurk) + 入(にゅう) (enter). Implies secrecy and stealth — entering without being detected or while concealing one's true purpose.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 潜入(せんにゅう)捜査(そうさ): undercover investigation
  • 潜入(せんにゅう)取材(しゅざい): undercover reporting/journalism
  • 潜入(せんにゅう)工作員(こうさくいん): infiltrator, covert agent
  • ～に潜入(せんにゅう)する: to infiltrate ~

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 忍(しの)び込(こ)む: to sneak in, to creep in — everyday word; physical stealth without necessarily a mission
  • 侵入(しんにゅう)する: to intrude, to break in — focuses on unauthorized entry; not necessarily secret
  • 潜伏(せんぷく)する: to lie low, to go into hiding — hiding after entry rather than the act of entering
