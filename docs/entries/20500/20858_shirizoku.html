1. to retreat; to withdraw; to step back
To move back or withdraw from a position, especially in a military or physical sense.
敵(てき)が退(しりぞ)いた。
The enemy retreated.
危険(きけん)を感(かん)じて一歩(いっぽ)退(しりぞ)いた。
Sensing danger, I stepped back one step.
大軍(たいぐん)を前(まえ)にして退(しりぞ)くしかなかった。
Faced with a large army, there was no choice but to retreat.
2. to retire; to step down; to resign
To withdraw from a position, office, or role. To leave the front stage of activity.
社長(しゃちょう)の座(ざ)を退(しりぞ)いた。
He stepped down from the position of company president.
政界(せいかい)から退(しりぞ)く決意(けつい)をした。
He decided to retire from politics.
長年(ながねん)務(つと)めた役職(やくしょく)を退(しりぞ)き、後進(こうしん)に道(みち)を譲(ゆず)った。
He retired from the position he had held for many years and gave way to the next generation.

A formal verb for withdrawing, retreating, or stepping down. The retreat sense is common in historical and military contexts, while the retirement sense is used in business and politics.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 一線(いっせん)を退(しりぞ)く: to retire from the front line
  • 政界(せいかい)から退(しりぞ)く: to retire from politics
  • 社長(しゃちょう)の座(ざ)を退(しりぞ)く: to step down as president
  • 敵(てき)が退(しりぞ)く: the enemy retreats

TRANSITIVITY:
Intransitive. The transitive counterpart is 退(しりぞ)ける (to repel, to drive back, to reject).

KANJI NOTE:
The same kanji 退(しりぞ)く is also read as ひく (to retreat, to recede) and どく (to get out of the way), but these are treated as separate words with different nuances.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 引(ひ)き退(さ)がる: to withdraw, to back off (less formal)
  • 辞任(じにん)する: to resign (from a specific position)
  • 引退(いんたい)する: to retire (especially from sports or entertainment)
  • 撤退(てったい)する: to withdraw, to pull out (military/business)
