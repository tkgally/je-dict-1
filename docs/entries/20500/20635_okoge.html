お焦(こ)げ

おこげ
noun
scorched rice crust; crispy rice at the bottom of the pot
1. scorched rice crust; crispy rice at the bottom of the pot
The thin, crispy layer of rice that forms at the bottom of a pot or rice cooker when rice is cooked over direct heat. Considered a delicacy in many cuisines.
お焦(こ)げが好(す)きです。
I like the crispy rice crust.
土鍋(どなべ)で炊(た)くと美味(おい)しいお焦(こ)げができる。
When you cook rice in a clay pot, you get delicious scorched rice.
お焦(こ)げにお茶(ちゃ)をかけて食(た)べるのが祖母(そぼ)の楽(たの)しみだった。
Pouring tea over the scorched rice and eating it was my grandmother's little pleasure.

The お is an honorific/beautifying prefix attached to 焦(こ)げ (burnt part), from the verb 焦(こ)げる (to scorch, to burn). Also written as おこげ in hiragana. The kanji form 御焦(おこ)げ is rarely used.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • お焦(こ)げができる (for scorched rice to form)
  • お焦(こ)げにお湯(ゆ)をかける (to pour hot water over scorched rice)
  • 土鍋(どなべ)のお焦(こ)げ (clay pot scorched rice)
  • パリパリのお焦(こ)げ (crispy scorched rice)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 焦(こ)げ — burnt part, scorch; the general term without the honorific prefix, can refer to any burnt food
  • 焦(こ)げ目(め) — browned surface, grill marks; the intentional browning on food
  • 煎餅(せんべい) — rice cracker; a separate snack made from rice

CULTURAL NOTE:
In traditional Japanese cooking with 釜(かま) (iron pots) or 土鍋(どなべ) (clay pots), お焦(こ)げ was a natural byproduct. Many people consider it a treat. A classic way to enjoy it is お焦(こ)げ湯(ゆ) — pouring hot water or tea over the crispy rice to make a simple, comforting drink-food.
