学(まな)び舎(や)

まなびや
noun
school; place of learning
1. school; place of learning; alma mater
A literary or poetic term for a school or educational institution. Carries a warm, nostalgic nuance and is often used when reminiscing about one's school days.
懐(なつ)かしい学(まな)び舎(や)を訪(たず)ねた。
I visited my dear old school.
この学(まな)び舎(や)で過(す)ごした日々(ひび)は忘(わす)れられない。
I can never forget the days I spent at this school.
卒業式(そつぎょうしき)の日(ひ)、生徒(せいと)たちは涙(なみだ)を流(なが)しながら学(まな)び舎(や)に別(わか)れを告(つ)げた。
On graduation day, the students said farewell to their school with tears in their eyes.

Composed of 学(まな)び (learning) + 舎(や) (building/house). A literary and somewhat poetic word for school, carrying a strong sense of nostalgia and emotional attachment.

USAGE:
Unlike the neutral 学校(がっこう), 学(まな)び舎(や) is used in sentimental contexts — graduation speeches, memoirs, school songs, and reflections on one's youth. It emphasizes the emotional bond between students and their school building.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 学(まな)び舎(や)を巣立(すだ)つ: to leave one's school (graduate)
  • 懐(なつ)かしい学(まな)び舎(や): one's fondly remembered school
  • 学(まな)び舎(や)に別(わか)れを告(つ)げる: to say farewell to one's school

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 学校(がっこう): school — the standard, neutral term
  • 母校(ぼこう): alma mater — emphasizes the relationship to a graduate
  • 校舎(こうしゃ): school building — refers to the physical structure without emotional connotation

REGISTER:
Literary and formal. Common in speeches, essays, and song lyrics. Rarely used in casual conversation.
