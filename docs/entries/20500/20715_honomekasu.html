1. to hint; to suggest; to insinuate; to imply
To communicate something indirectly without stating it explicitly.
辞職(じしょく)を仄(ほの)めかす。
To hint at resignation.
彼(かれ)は不満(ふまん)を仄(ほの)めかした。
He hinted at his dissatisfaction.
社長(しゃちょう)は記者会見(きしゃかいけん)で新(あら)たな計画(けいかく)を仄(ほの)めかした。
The company president hinted at a new plan during the press conference.

A transitive godan verb meaning to communicate indirectly. The kanji 仄(ほの) means 'faint' or 'dim,' giving the word a nuance of something only dimly visible or barely perceptible.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 辞職(じしょく)を仄(ほの)めかす: to hint at resignation
  • 不満(ふまん)を仄(ほの)めかす: to hint at dissatisfaction
  • 引退(いんたい)を仄(ほの)めかす: to hint at retirement
  • それとなく仄(ほの)めかす: to subtly suggest

USAGE:
Often used in news reporting and formal contexts to describe indirect communication by public figures. The word carries a somewhat literary or formal tone compared to casual expressions like ちらっと言(い)う.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 匂(にお)わせる: to hint, to suggest — more colloquial; often used for deliberately dropping hints, especially on social media
  • 示唆(しさ)する: to suggest, to imply — more formal and academic; used in official or analytical contexts
  • 暗示(あんじ)する: to imply, to allude to — emphasizes hidden or symbolic meaning
