1. fading, discoloration, loss of color
The gradual loss or change of color in materials such as fabrics, photographs, paintings, or dyed items, typically caused by exposure to light, washing, or the passage of time.
日光(にっこう)で布(ぬの)が退色(たいしょく)した。
The fabric faded from sunlight.
この染料(せんりょう)は退色(たいしょく)しにくい。
This dye is resistant to fading.
古(ふる)い写真(しゃしん)が退色(たいしょく)して、色(いろ)がほとんど分(わ)からなくなっていた。
The old photograph had faded so much that the colors were barely discernible.

A somewhat technical or literary word for the loss of color over time. Commonly used in textile, art preservation, photography, and material science contexts. In everyday speech, 色(いろ)が落(お)ちる or 色(いろ)が褪(あ)せる are more common ways to express fading.

Note: This is a homophone of 退職(たいしょく) (retirement from a job), which is far more common.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 退色(たいしょく)する: to fade, to lose color
  • 退色(たいしょく)しにくい: resistant to fading
  • 退色(たいしょく)防止(ぼうし): prevention of fading
  • 退色(たいしょく)試験(しけん): colorfastness test
  • 日光(にっこう)による退色(たいしょく): fading due to sunlight

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 褪色(たいしょく): fading — written with a different first character but same reading and meaning; 褪色(たいしょく) is considered more precise but less commonly written
  • 変色(へんしょく): discoloration, color change — the color changes to a different hue rather than simply fading
  • 色褪(いろあ)せる: to fade — a native Japanese verb expression more common in everyday speech
