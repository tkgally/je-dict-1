1. to poke; to prod; to jab
To repeatedly push at something with a finger, stick, or pointed object.
指(ゆび)で突(つ)っつく。
To poke with one's finger.
棒(ぼう)で虫(むし)を突(つ)っついた。
I poked the bug with a stick.
隣(となり)の人(ひと)を肘(ひじ)で突(つ)っついて合図(あいず)した。
I nudged the person next to me with my elbow to signal them.
2. to peck at; to pick at (food)
To eat in small amounts, poking at food without much appetite. Also used for birds pecking.
鳥(とり)が餌(えさ)を突(つ)っつく。
A bird pecks at food.
食欲(しょくよく)がなくて、料理(りょうり)を突(つ)っつくだけだった。
I had no appetite and just picked at my food.
鍋(なべ)を突(つ)っつきながら話(はなし)をした。
We chatted while picking at a hot pot.
3. to nitpick; to find fault with
To criticize minor flaws or point out small problems.
細(こま)かいことを突(つ)っつく。
To nitpick about small things.
上司(じょうし)にミスを突(つ)っつかれた。
My boss picked on my mistakes.
弱点(じゃくてん)を突(つ)っつかれると困(こま)る。
It's troublesome when someone pokes at your weak points.

A colloquial godan verb that is an emphatic or repeated-action form of 突(つ)く (to poke). The っ gemination adds a sense of repeated, light action.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 指(ゆび)で突(つ)っつく: to poke with a finger
  • 鍋(なべ)を突(つ)っつく: to share a hot pot (picking at it together)
  • 細(こま)かいことを突(つ)っつく: to nitpick
  • 痛(いた)いところを突(つ)っつく: to touch on a sore spot

USAGE:
The hot-pot usage (鍋(なべ)を突(つ)っつく) is a common casual expression for sharing a communal dish, conveying a friendly, informal atmosphere.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 突(つ)く: to poke, to thrust — the base verb; more general and can indicate a single, stronger action
  • つつく: to peck, to poke — a softer variant without the geminate っ; essentially the same word
  • 指摘(してき)する: to point out — formal equivalent of the nitpicking sense
