撥(ばち)

ばち
noun
plectrum, drumstick, pick (for traditional instruments)
1. plectrum, pick, drumstick (for traditional Japanese instruments)
A tool used to strike or pluck the strings of traditional Japanese instruments such as the shamisen, biwa, or taiko. The shape varies by instrument — fan-shaped for shamisen, thin and flat for biwa, and thick wooden sticks for taiko drums.
三味線(しゃみせん)の撥(ばち)で弦(げん)を弾(はじ)く。
To pluck the strings with a shamisen plectrum.
太鼓(たいこ)の撥(ばち)を力強(ちからづよ)く振(ふ)り下(お)ろした。
He brought the drumstick down powerfully onto the taiko.
津軽三味線(つがるじゃみせん)では、大(おお)きな撥(ばち)を使(つか)って激(はげ)しく演奏(えんそう)する奏法(そうほう)が特徴的(とくちょうてき)だ。
In Tsugaru shamisen, a characteristic technique involves playing intensely with a large plectrum.

The shape and material of the 撥(ばち) varies greatly depending on the instrument. For 三味線(しゃみせん), it is a fan-shaped plectrum made of ivory, wood, or plastic. For 太鼓(たいこ), it refers to thick wooden drumsticks. Also written as バチ in katakana in casual contexts.

The character 撥(ばち) is also used in the linguistic term 撥音(はつおん) (the moraic nasal ん), though in that case the reading is はつ.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 三味線(しゃみせん)の撥(ばち): shamisen plectrum
  • 太鼓(たいこ)の撥(ばち): taiko drumstick
  • 撥(ばち)さばき: plectrum/stick technique
  • 撥(ばち)を持(も)つ: to hold a plectrum/stick

CULTURAL NOTE:
The 撥(ばち) is central to the sound and technique of traditional Japanese music. Different schools and genres use different sizes and materials, which significantly affect the tone. The 撥(ばち) used in 津軽三味線(つがるじゃみせん) is notably larger and heavier than those used in other shamisen styles.
