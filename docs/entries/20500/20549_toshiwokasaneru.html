1. to grow older; to age; to accumulate years
To advance in age over time. A softer, more dignified way to express aging than direct words like 'to get old.' Often carries a positive or neutral nuance of gaining experience and maturity.
年(とし)を重(かさ)ねるごとに味(あじ)が出(で)てくる。
With each passing year, more character emerges.
年(とし)を重(かさ)ねても元気(げんき)でいたい。
I want to stay healthy even as I grow older.
年(とし)を重(かさ)ねて初(はじ)めて分(わ)かることがたくさんある。
There are many things you can only understand after growing older.

Literally 'to pile up years.' A euphemistic and respectful way to describe aging. The verb 重(かさ)ねる (to pile up, to accumulate) emphasizes the gradual accumulation of years and experience.

USAGE:
Preferred over 老(ふ)ける (to age, to grow old) or 年(とし)を取(と)る (to get older) in polite contexts because it avoids negative connotations. Often used in reflective or philosophical contexts about life.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 年(とし)を重(かさ)ねるごとに: with each passing year
  • 年(とし)を重(かさ)ねても: even as one ages
  • 年(とし)を重(かさ)ねた人(ひと): an older person (respectful)

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 年(とし)を取(と)る: to get older — more neutral, everyday expression
  • 老(ふ)ける: to age, to grow old — can sound negative
  • 歳月(さいげつ)を経(へ)る: for years to pass — more literary
