1. unification of the country; national unification
The act of bringing an entire country or realm under one ruler's control. Most commonly associated with the unification of Japan during the Sengoku period by Oda Nobunaga, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and Tokugawa Ieyasu.
天下統一(てんかとういつ)を目指(めざ)す。
To aim for unification of the country.
豊臣秀吉(とよとみひでよし)は天下統一(てんかとういつ)を成(な)し遂(と)げた。
Toyotomi Hideyoshi achieved the unification of Japan.
戦国時代(せんごくじだい)には多(おお)くの武将(ぶしょう)が天下統一(てんかとういつ)の野望(やぼう)を抱(いだ)いていた。
During the Warring States period, many military commanders harbored ambitions of unifying the country.

A four-character compound frequently encountered in Japanese history, literature, and popular culture (games, manga, TV dramas). 天下(てんか) means 'the realm' or 'the whole country,' and 統一(とういつ) means 'unification.'

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 天下統一(てんかとういつ)を成(な)し遂(と)げる: to achieve national unification
  • 天下統一(てんかとういつ)を目指(めざ)す: to aim for national unification
  • 天下統一(てんかとういつ)の野望(やぼう): ambition for national unification
  • 天下統一(てんかとういつ)への道(みち): the road to unification

HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
The three great unifiers of Japan are 織田信長(おだのぶなが), 豊臣秀吉(とよとみひでよし), and 徳川家康(とくがわいえやす). The process of unification spanned the late 16th century, ending the century-long 戦国時代(せんごくじだい) (Warring States period).
