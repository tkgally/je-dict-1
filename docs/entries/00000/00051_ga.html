が

が
particle
subject marker
1. subject marker
Marks the grammatical subject of a sentence—who or what performs the action or is being described. Unlike は, が typically introduces new information or emphasizes the identity of the subject.
2. object marker (with certain predicates)
With certain verbs and adjectives expressing ability, desire, or emotion (できる, 分(わ)かる, 好(す)き, 欲(ほ)しい, etc.), が marks what would be the object in English. 日本語(にほんご)が分(わ)かる (I understand Japanese), 猫(ねこ)が好(す)き (I like cats).
3. but, although (conjunction)
Connects two clauses with a contrastive or concessive meaning. Similar to 'but' or 'although' in English.
誰(だれ)が来(き)ましたか。田中(たなか)さんが来(き)ました。
Who came? Tanaka-san came.
が identifies the subject in a 'who' question and answer
日本語(にほんご)が分(わ)かりますか。
Do you understand Japanese?
が with 分(わ)かる (understand)
猫(ねこ)が好(す)きです。
I like cats.
が with 好(す)き (like)
行(い)きたいですが、時間(じかん)がありません。
I want to go, but I don't have time.
が as a conjunction meaning 'but'
私(わたし)が行(い)きます。
I'll go. (I'm the one who'll go)
Exhaustive-listing が: emphasizes 'I' as the one (not others)

The は vs. が distinction is notoriously difficult for learners. Key differences: (1) が introduces new information, は marks known/topical information; (2) が is used in subordinate clauses; (3) が is used after question words (誰(だれ)が, 何(なに)が); (4) certain predicates require が for their 'object' (好(す)き, 分(わ)かる, できる, 欲(ほ)しい, etc.).
