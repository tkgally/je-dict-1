1. to write
To form letters, characters, or symbols on a surface. Used for writing by hand, typing, or composing text. Covers both the physical act of writing and the creative act of composing.
名前(なまえ)を書(か)く。
I write my name.
手紙(てがみ)を書(か)きました。
I wrote a letter.
ここに名前(なまえ)を書(か)いてください。
Please write your name here.
日本語(にほんご)で書(か)けますか。
Can you write in Japanese?
毎日(まいにち)日記(にっき)を書(か)いている。一年(いちねん)続(つづ)けている。
I write in my diary every day. I've kept it up for a year.
2. to draw, to paint
Can also mean to draw or paint pictures, though 描(えが)く/描(か)く is more specific for artistic drawing.
絵(え)を書(か)く。
I draw a picture.
地図(ちず)を書(か)いてください。
Please draw a map.
子供(こども)が絵(え)を書(か)いている。
The child is drawing a picture.
猫(ねこ)の絵(え)を書(か)いた。
I drew a picture of a cat.
小(ちい)さい時(とき)から絵(え)を書(か)くのが好(す)きだった。今(いま)は仕事(しごと)で絵(え)を書(か)いている。
I've liked drawing since I was little. Now I draw for work.

TRANSITIVITY:
他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). No pair verb. Pattern: Xを書(か)く.

ASPECT:
ている marks the ongoing action: 書(か)いている = "is writing." Note the te-form 書(か)いて (not 書(か)きて) — the regular pattern for godan verbs ending in く.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 手紙(てがみ)を書(か)く — to write a letter
  • 名前(なまえ)を書(か)く — to write one's name
  • 日記(にっき)を書(か)く — to keep a diary
  • 書(か)き方(かた) — how to write something

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 絵(え)を書(か)く is common in casual writing, but 絵(え)を描(か)く is the conventional spelling when a picture is meant. 描(えが)く is the same kanji read more formally, used of depicting scenes and feelings in art or writing. Keep 書(か)く for letters, characters, and text.
  • 記入(きにゅう)する is what official forms say where conversation would use 書(か)く.
