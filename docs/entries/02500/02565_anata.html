あなた

あなた
pronoun
you
1. you (polite, neutral)
Second person pronoun used in polite or neutral contexts.
あなたのお名前(なまえ)は?
What is your name?
Polite inquiry
あなたはどう思(おも)いますか。
What do you think?
あなたの意見(いけん)を聞(き)かせてください。
Please let me hear your opinion.
Sense 1: you (polite)
あなたは日本語(にほんご)が上手(じょうず)ですね。
You're good at Japanese.
Sense 1: you
あなたはどこから来(き)ましたか。
Where are you from?
Sense 1: you (neutral)
2. you, dear (to spouse)
Term of address used by wives when speaking to their husbands.
あなた、今日(きょう)は早(はや)いのね。
You're early today, dear.
Wife to husband
あなた、夕飯(ゆうはん)は何(なに)がいい？
Dear, what would you like for dinner?
Sense 2: wife to husband
あなた、疲(つか)れてない？
Dear, aren't you tired?
Sense 2: wife to husband
あなた、聞(き)いてる？
Dear, are you listening?
Sense 2: wife to husband
あなた、大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)？
Dear, are you okay?
Sense 2: wife to husband

あなた is the standard second person pronoun, but its usage is more limited than English "you."

USAGE NOTES:

  • In daily conversation, Japanese speakers often avoid pronouns entirely, using names or titles instead
  • Using あなた too frequently can sound cold or confrontational
  • In formal settings, use the person's name + さん/様(さま) instead

SPOUSAL USAGE:

  • Wives commonly address husbands as あなた (dear)
  • Husbands typically don't use あなた for wives; they use names or お前(まえ)/きみ

WHEN TO AVOID:

  • With superiors (use name/title)
  • With strangers (can sound rude)
  • When the subject is clear from context (omit entirely)

OTHER "YOU" WORDS:

  • きみ: casual, male speaker to close friend/subordinate
  • お前(まえ): casual/rough, between close friends
  • 貴方(あなた)様(さま): very formal written form
