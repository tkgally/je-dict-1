私(わたし)

わたし
pronoun
I, myself
See also: 私(わたくし) (very formal), 僕(ぼく) (I (masculine casual))
1. I, myself
First-person pronoun, neutral to polite.
私(わたし)は学生(がくせい)です。
I am a student.
私(わたし)の名前(なまえ)は山田(やまだ)です。
My name is Yamada.
私(わたし)も行(い)きたいです。
I want to go too.
私(わたし)が作(つく)ったケーキを食(た)べてください。
Please eat the cake I made.
私(わたし)は毎朝(まいあさ)6時(じ)に起(お)きて、学校(がっこう)に行(い)く前(まえ)に少(すこ)し勉強(べんきょう)します。
I wake up at 6 every morning and study a little before going to school.

私(わたし) is the most standard and versatile first-person pronoun in Japanese, appropriate for nearly all situations. Used by both men and women.

READINGS:

  • わたし: standard polite form
  • わたくし: very formal (business, speeches, ceremonies)

FIRST-PERSON PRONOUN HIERARCHY:

  • 私(わたくし): very formal, both genders
  • 私(わたし): polite/neutral, both genders (safest default)
  • あたし: casual, mainly female
  • 僕(ぼく): casual, male (softer tone)
  • 俺(おれ): very casual, male (rough/masculine)

USAGE NOTES:

  • In formal writing and business, 私(わたし) or 私(わたくし) is always appropriate
  • In casual conversation, men often switch to 僕(ぼく) or 俺(おれ)
  • Japanese often drops the subject entirely when context makes it clear, so 私(わたし) is used less frequently than "I" in English
