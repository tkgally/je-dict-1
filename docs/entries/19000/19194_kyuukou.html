1. leaving farmland fallow; resting agricultural land
The practice of leaving farmland uncultivated for a period to restore soil fertility, or the state of farmland being unused. Also refers to government-mandated reduction of rice production.
休耕(きゅうこう)地(ち)が増(ふ)えている。
Fallow land is increasing.
土壌(どじょう)を休(やす)ませるために一年間(いちねんかん)休耕(きゅうこう)する。
The land will lie fallow for one year to rest the soil.
高齢化(こうれいか)で農家(のうか)が減(へ)り、休耕(きゅうこう)田(でん)が目立(めだ)つようになった。
As the population ages and farmers decrease, fallow rice paddies have become conspicuous.

Composed of 休(きゅう) (rest) + 耕(こう) (cultivate, plow). An agricultural term. Common collocations: 休耕(きゅうこう)地(ち) (fallow land), 休耕(きゅうこう)田(でん) (fallow rice paddy), 休耕(きゅうこう)田(た)んぼ (fallow paddy field). In Japan, 休耕(きゅうこう)田(でん) became widespread after the government's 減反(げんたん)政策(せいさく) (rice acreage reduction policy) starting in 1970 to combat rice overproduction. Rural depopulation and aging farmers have further increased 休耕(きゅうこう)地(ち). Not to be confused with the homophone 急行(きゅうこう) (express train).
