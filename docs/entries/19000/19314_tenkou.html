1. conversion; change of ideology or allegiance
A fundamental shift in one's political beliefs, religious faith, or ideological stance. Historically associated with the forced ideological conversions during prewar Japan.
彼(かれ)は共産主義(きょうさんしゅぎ)から転向(てんこう)した。
He converted from communism.
戦前(せんぜん)には思想(しそう)転向(てんこう)を強(し)いられた知識人(ちしきじん)が多(おお)かった。
Before the war, many intellectuals were forced to abandon their ideological beliefs.
政治的(せいじてき)転向(てんこう)は裏切(うらぎ)りと見(み)なされることもある。
Political conversion is sometimes seen as betrayal.
2. switching; changing course or direction
Changing from one field, career, or activity to another. Also used for changes in wind direction in meteorology.
文系(ぶんけい)から理系(りけい)に転向(てんこう)した。
I switched from the humanities to the sciences.
野球(やきゅう)からサッカーに転向(てんこう)する選手(せんしゅ)もいる。
Some athletes switch from baseball to soccer.
午後(ごご)から風向(かざむ)きが転向(てんこう)して北風(きたかぜ)になった。
In the afternoon the wind shifted direction and became a north wind.

Composed of 転(てん) (turn, change) and 向(こう) (direction). The word carries different weight depending on the sense: sense 1 often implies a serious, sometimes controversial ideological shift, while sense 2 is more neutral.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 思想(しそう)転向(てんこう) — ideological conversion
  • 政治的(せいじてき)転向(てんこう) — political conversion
  • 転向(てんこう)する — to convert, to switch
  • 転向者(てんこうしゃ) — a convert, one who has changed sides

HISTORICAL NOTE:
In Japanese history, 転向(てんこう) specifically refers to the government-forced abandonment of leftist and communist ideologies during the 1930s. This historical usage gives the word a heavier connotation than simple 'change.'

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 転換(てんかん): conversion, shift — broader and more neutral; used for policy changes, energy transitions, etc.
  • 転校(てんこう): school transfer — same reading but different meaning and kanji
