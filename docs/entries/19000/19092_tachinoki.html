立(た)ち退(の)き

たちのき
noun
eviction; vacating
1. eviction; vacating; moving out (under compulsion)
The act of being forced to leave a building or land, typically by legal or official order.
立(た)ち退(の)きを命(めい)じられた。
They were ordered to vacate.
住民(じゅうみん)は立(た)ち退(の)きに反対(はんたい)している。
The residents are opposing the eviction.
再開発(さいかいはつ)のため、このビルの住民(じゅうみん)は来月(らいげつ)までに立(た)ち退(の)きを求(もと)められている。
Due to redevelopment, the residents of this building are being asked to vacate by next month.

Derived from the verb 立(た)ち退(の)く (to vacate, to move out). Common collocations include 立(た)ち退(の)きを命(めい)じる (to order an eviction), 立(た)ち退(の)き料(りょう) (eviction compensation money), 立(た)ち退(の)き要求(ようきゅう) (eviction demand), and 強制(きょうせい)立(た)ち退(の)き (forced eviction). Often appears in news about urban redevelopment and landlord-tenant disputes.
