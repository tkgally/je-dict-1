1. to run into difficulties; to make slow progress; to be stalled
To encounter obstacles that slow or halt progress. Originally a nautical term meaning rough sailing, now widely used for negotiations, projects, and any process that hits snags.
交渉(こうしょう)が難航(なんこう)している。
The negotiations are stalled.
人手不足(ひとでぶそく)で工事(こうじ)が難航(なんこう)している。
The construction is having difficulties due to a labor shortage.
与野党(よやとう)の意見(いけん)が対立(たいりつ)して、法案(ほうあん)の審議(しんぎ)が難航(なんこう)を極(きわ)めている。
With the ruling and opposition parties at odds, deliberation on the bill is facing extreme difficulties.

Originally a nautical term — 難(なん) (difficulty) + 航(こう) (voyage) — meaning a rough sea crossing. Now used almost exclusively in the figurative sense of any process encountering serious obstacles.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 交渉(こうしょう)が難航(なんこう)する — negotiations hit difficulties
  • 審議(しんぎ)が難航(なんこう)する — deliberations stall
  • 調整(ちょうせい)が難航(なんこう)する — coordination runs into trouble
  • 難航(なんこう)を極(きわ)める — to face extreme difficulties
  • 難航(なんこう)が予想(よそう)される — difficulties are expected

USAGE NOTE:
Very common in news reporting, especially about political negotiations, business deals, and large projects. The noun form 難航(なんこう) is also used without する: 難航(なんこう)が続(つづ)く (difficulties continue).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 行(い)き詰(づ)まる — to reach a deadlock (more complete stoppage)
  • 苦戦(くせん)する — to struggle, fight an uphill battle (more about effort)
  • 停滞(ていたい)する — to stagnate (emphasizes lack of movement)
