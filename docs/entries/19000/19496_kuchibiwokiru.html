口火(くちび)を切(き)る

くちびをきる
expression
to be the first to speak; to start things off
1. to be the first to speak; to start things off; to break the ice
To be the one who initiates a discussion, debate, or action. Often used when someone is the first to speak up or take action in a group setting.
彼(かれ)が議論(ぎろん)の口火(くちび)を切(き)った。
He started off the discussion.
誰(だれ)も発言(はつげん)しないので、私(わたし)が口火(くちび)を切(き)ることにした。
Since nobody was speaking up, I decided to start things off.
反対(はんたい)派(は)の代表(だいひょう)が口火(くちび)を切(き)り、次(つぎ)々と意見(いけん)が出(で)された。
The representative of the opposition started things off, and opinions poured out one after another.

Originally a military term: 口火(くちび) refers to the fuse or primer of a firearm, so 口火(くちび)を切(き)る literally meant 'to light the fuse' or 'to open fire.' Now used figuratively to mean being the first to speak or act, triggering a chain of events.

ETYMOLOGY:
口火(くちび) is the opening of a furnace or the touch hole of a cannon — the small ignition point that sets off the main charge. The metaphor extends to being the catalyst that sets a discussion or movement in motion.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 議論(ぎろん)の口火(くちび)を切(き)る — to kick off a debate
  • 批判(ひはん)の口火(くちび)を切(き)る — to be the first to criticize
  • 改革(かいかく)の口火(くちび)を切(き)る — to spark reform

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 先陣(せんじん)を切(き)る — to take the lead, to be at the vanguard; broader in scope, not limited to speech
  • 火蓋(ひぶた)を切(き)る — to open hostilities; similar military origin, often used for the start of a battle or fierce competition
