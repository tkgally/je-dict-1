1. ground subsidence; sinking of land
The gradual sinking or settling of the ground surface, often caused by extraction of groundwater, natural gas, or minerals. A recognized environmental problem in Japan.
地下水(ちかすい)のくみ上(あ)げで地盤沈下(じばんちんか)が起(お)きた。
Groundwater extraction caused ground subsidence.
地盤沈下(じばんちんか)で建物(たてもの)が傾(かたむ)いた。
Buildings tilted due to ground subsidence.
地盤沈下(じばんちんか)対策(たいさく)として地下水(ちかすい)の使用(しよう)が規制(きせい)された。
Groundwater usage was regulated as a countermeasure against ground subsidence.
2. decline in influence or power (figurative)
A figurative use describing the weakening or decline of a person's, organization's, or region's power or influence.
党(とう)の地盤沈下(じばんちんか)が止(と)まらない。
The party's declining influence shows no sign of stopping.
製造業(せいぞうぎょう)の地盤沈下(じばんちんか)が地方経済(ちほうけいざい)に影響(えいきょう)している。
The decline of manufacturing is affecting the regional economy.
商店街(しょうてんがい)の地盤沈下(じばんちんか)は深刻(しんこく)で、シャッター通(どお)りと化(か)している。
The decline of the shopping district is serious — it has turned into a shuttered street.

Composed of 地盤(じばん) (ground, foundation) + 沈下(ちんか) (sinking, subsidence). One of Japan's seven major 公害(こうがい) (pollution) types. Historically severe in areas like 東京(とうきょう)下町(したまち) (eastern Tokyo lowlands) and 大阪(おおさか), where excessive groundwater pumping caused widespread subsidence. The figurative sense (decline of influence) is common in political and business journalism — often describing erosion of a politician's local 地盤(じばん) (support base/constituency), playing on the dual meaning of 地盤(じばん).
