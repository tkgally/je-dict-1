1. to drill into; to instill; to train thoroughly; to teach persistently
To teach something so repeatedly and thoroughly that it becomes deeply ingrained. The ～込(こ)む suffix adds a sense of thoroughness and persistence to the base verb 教(おし)える.
子供(こども)にマナーを教(おし)え込(こ)む。
To instill manners in a child.
基本(きほん)を徹底的(てっていてき)に教(おし)え込(こ)まれた。
The basics were thoroughly drilled into me.
祖母(そぼ)に小(ちい)さい頃(ころ)から「感謝(かんしゃ)の気持(きも)ちを忘(わす)れるな」と教(おし)え込(こ)まれた。
My grandmother drilled into me from a young age to never forget to be grateful.

A compound verb: 教(おし)える (to teach) + 込(こ)む (into, thoroughly). The ～込(こ)む suffix conveys the idea of something being driven in deeply through repetition.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • マナーを教(おし)え込(こ)む — to instill manners
  • 基本(きほん)を教(おし)え込(こ)む — to drill in the basics
  • 小(ちい)さい頃(ころ)から教(おし)え込(こ)まれる — to have something drilled in from childhood

USAGE:
Often used in the passive (教(おし)え込(こ)まれる) to describe values or skills that were instilled by parents, teachers, or mentors. Can have a slightly forceful nuance — the teaching is persistent and thorough, not casual.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 教(おし)える — to teach (neutral; 教(おし)え込(こ)む implies repeated, thorough instruction)
  • 叩(たた)き込(こ)む — to hammer in, to beat into (stronger, more physical nuance)
  • 仕込(しこ)む — to train, to coach (often used for apprentices or animals)
