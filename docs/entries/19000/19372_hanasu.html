1. to separate; to keep apart; to set apart
To create distance between two things or move something away from something else.
席(せき)を離(はな)して座(すわ)ってください。
Please sit with your seats spaced apart.
喧嘩(けんか)している犬(いぬ)を引(ひ)き離(はな)すのは大変(たいへん)だった。
It was difficult to pull the fighting dogs apart.
試験(しけん)のとき、カンニングを防(ふせ)ぐために机(つくえ)を一(いち)メートルずつ離(はな)して並(なら)べた。
During the exam, the desks were spaced one meter apart to prevent cheating.
2. to release; to let go of
To release one's grip or hold on something.
子供(こども)から目(め)を離(はな)さないでください。
Please don't take your eyes off the children.
犬(いぬ)の手綱(たづな)を離(はな)したら、走(はし)って行(い)ってしまった。
When I let go of the dog's leash, it ran off.
人混(ひとご)みの中(なか)では子供(こども)の手(て)を離(はな)さないようにしてください。
Please make sure not to let go of your child's hand in a crowd.

離(はな)す describes the physical action of moving things apart or letting go of something held. It covers both creating distance between objects and releasing one's grip.

INTRANSITIVE PAIR:

  • 離(はな)す (transitive) — to separate/release something
  • 離(はな)れる (intransitive) — to become separated; to leave

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 目(め)を離(はな)す — to take one's eyes off (often used with negatives: 目(め)を離(はな)さない)
  • 手(て)を離(はな)す — to let go (of something held in hand)
  • 引(ひ)き離(はな)す — to pull apart; to separate by force

CONTRAST WITH 話(はな)す:

  • 離(はな)す — to separate; to release
  • 話(はな)す — to speak; to talk
  • Both are read はなす but have completely different meanings
