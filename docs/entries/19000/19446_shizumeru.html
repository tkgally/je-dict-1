1. to calm, to quiet, to pacify
To make something that is agitated, noisy, or turbulent become calm and quiet. Used for emotions, noise, or disturbances.
心(こころ)を静(しず)める。
To calm one's mind.
怒(いか)りを静(しず)めてください。
Please calm your anger.
深呼吸(しんこきゅう)をして気持(きも)ちを静(しず)めた。
I took a deep breath and calmed my feelings.
先生(せんせい)が教室(きょうしつ)を静(しず)めた。
The teacher quieted the classroom.
2. to suppress, to subdue, to put down
To bring something under control by force or authority, such as a rebellion or unrest.
政府(せいふ)は軍(ぐん)を送(おく)って反乱(はんらん)を静(しず)めた。
The government sent the army to suppress the rebellion.
騒(さわ)ぎを静(しず)めるために警察(けいさつ)が来(き)た。
The police came to put down the disturbance.
民衆(みんしゅう)の怒(いか)りはなかなか静(しず)められなかった。
The anger of the people could not easily be subdued.

静(しず)める is a transitive verb meaning to calm or quiet something.

TRANSITIVITY PAIR:

  • 静(しず)める (transitive, ichidan): to calm something
  • 静(しず)まる (intransitive, godan): to become calm

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 心(こころ)を静(しず)める (to calm one's mind)
  • 怒(いか)りを静(しず)める (to calm one's anger)
  • 気持(きも)ちを静(しず)める (to calm one's feelings)
  • 騒(さわ)ぎを静(しず)める (to quiet a commotion)

KANJI NOTE:

  • Usually written with 静(しず)める (quiet, calm)
  • Not to be confused with 鎮(しず)める, which emphasizes suppression by force and is more common for sense 2 in formal writing
