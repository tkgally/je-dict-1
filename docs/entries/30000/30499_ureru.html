1. to ripen; to become ripe (of fruit or grain)
For fruit, grain, or seeds to reach full maturity, becoming sweet, soft, and ready to eat. Intransitive: the fruit is the subject, and there is no object.
柿(かき)がよく熟(う)れている。
The persimmons are nicely ripe.
この桃(もも)はまだ熟(う)れていない。
This peach is not ripe yet.
熟(う)れた果物(くだもの)は甘(あま)いが傷(いた)みやすいので、早(はや)めに食(た)べたほうがいい。
Ripe fruit is sweet but spoils easily, so it is better to eat it soon.

TRANSITIVITY:
Intransitive. The fruit ripens by itself: 桃(もも)が熟(う)れる. To say that someone ripens fruit, use 熟(じゅく)させる or 追熟(ついじゅく)させる.

ASPECT:
熟(う)れている normally describes the resulting state ('is ripe'), not an action in progress, so it is the form learners meet most often. 熟(う)れる on its own points to the future or to a general truth.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • よく熟(う)れた: nicely ripened
  • 熟(う)れた果物(くだもの): ripe fruit
  • 熟(う)れすぎる: to become overripe
  • まだ熟(う)れていない: not ripe yet

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 熟(じゅく)す: to ripen — same meaning, more formal, and also used figuratively of a time being ripe (機(き)が熟(じゅく)す)
  • 実(みの)る: to bear fruit — focuses on fruit forming, and is common figuratively for efforts paying off
  • 売(う)れる: to sell — an unrelated homophone; the kanji tells them apart
