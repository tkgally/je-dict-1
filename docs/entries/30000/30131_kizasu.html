1. to show signs; to begin to appear
For something not yet visible — a season, a feeling, a social change — to give its first faint indication. The subject is the thing coming into being, not the person noticing it.
春(はる)が兆(きざ)す。
Spring is beginning to show itself.
胸(むね)の中(なか)に不安(ふあん)が兆(きざ)し始(はじ)めた。
Unease began to stir in my chest.
長(なが)い冬(ふゆ)が終(お)わり、野原(のはら)のあちこちに新(あたら)しい命(いのち)が兆(きざ)していた。
The long winter had ended, and new life was beginning to appear here and there in the fields.

A literary verb, most at home in writing about nature, moods, and social currents. The related noun 兆(きざ)し (a sign, an omen) is far more common in everyday Japanese: 回復(かいふく)の兆(きざ)しが見(み)える (signs of recovery are visible).

TRANSITIVITY:
Intransitive; the thing appearing is the subject and takes が (不安(ふあん)が兆(きざ)す).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 春(はる)が兆(きざ)す: for spring to show its first signs
  • 不安(ふあん)が兆(きざ)す: for unease to stir
  • 芽(め)が兆(きざ)す: for buds to start to appear

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 芽生(めば)える: to sprout; to bud — used both of plants and of feelings, and much more common
  • 現(あらわ)れる: to appear — neutral and general; the thing is already visible, not merely hinted at
