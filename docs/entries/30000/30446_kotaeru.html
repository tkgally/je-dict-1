1. to hold out; to withstand; to endure
To keep standing under pressure, attack, or hardship without giving way. Somewhat literary on its own; most learners meet it inside the compound verb 持(も)ち堪(こた)える.
敵(てき)の攻撃(こうげき)によく堪(こた)えた。
They held out well against the enemy's attack.
古(ふる)い橋(はし)が台風(たいふう)の風(かぜ)に堪(こた)えた。
The old bridge withstood the typhoon winds.
苦(くる)しい時期(じき)によく堪(こた)えたからこそ、今(いま)の成功(せいこう)がある。
It is precisely because they held out through the hard times that they are successful now.

TRANSITIVITY: Intransitive. The thing withstood is marked with に (攻撃(こうげき)に堪(こた)える), and the one holding out is the subject.

USAGE:
On its own the verb sounds formal or written. In ordinary speech it appears mainly in 持(も)ち堪(こた)える (to hold out, to weather something), which is far more common.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 堪(こら)える: to endure; to hold back — the same kanji read こらえる, used of putting up with pain or suppressing tears and anger
  • 耐(た)える: to bear; to withstand — the everyday word for enduring hardship or for a material standing up to stress

NOTE ON こたえる:
The こたえる of 寒(さむ)さが身(み)にこたえる ('the cold really gets to me') is a different verb, usually written 応(こた)える or in kana. Do not confuse it with this one.
