たい

たい
auxiliary
want to (do)
1. want to (do); would like to (do)
Attached to a verb stem to express the speaker's own desire to perform the action: 食(た)べる → 食(た)べたい (want to eat). The result inflects like an i-adjective (食(た)べたくない, 食(た)べたかった).
水(みず)が飲(の)みたい。
I want to drink some water.
今日(きょう)はどこにも行(い)きたくない。
I don't want to go anywhere today.
将来(しょうらい)は海外(かいがい)で働(はたら)きたいと思(おも)っています。
I am thinking that I want to work abroad in the future.
子(こ)どものころは医者(いしゃ)になりたかったが、今(いま)は別(べつ)の道(みち)を選(えら)んでよかったと思(おも)う。
As a child I wanted to be a doctor, but now I'm glad I chose a different path.

Attaches to the ます-stem: 飲(の)みます → 飲(の)みたい, 見(み)ます → 見(み)たい. The object may take either を or が, with が emphasizing the desired thing itself (水(みず)が飲(の)みたい).

FORMS:

  • 行(い)きたい: want to go
  • 行(い)きたくない: don't want to go
  • 行(い)きたかった: wanted to go
  • 行(い)きたくて: wanting to go (te-form, linking)

USAGE LIMITS:

  • Used for the speaker's own desire, or in questions about the listener's.
  • Do not use plain 〜たい of a third person; say 行(い)きたがっている (looks like they want to go) or 行(い)きたいそうだ (they say they want to go).
  • Saying 〜たいですか to a superior can sound blunt; {いかがですか} or 〜ませんか is safer.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 〜たがる: shows signs of wanting to — the third-person counterpart
  • 〜ほしい: want (a thing) — takes a noun, not a verb
  • 〜たいと思(おも)う: I think I'd like to — softer and more common in formal speech
