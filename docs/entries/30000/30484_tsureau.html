1. to go along together; to accompany each other
For two or more people to travel through something together — literally along a road, or figuratively through life as a married couple. Now a dated, literary word; the derived noun 連(つ)れ合(あ)い (spouse) is far more common.
長年(ながねん)連(つ)れ合(あ)った夫婦(ふうふ)。
A couple who have spent many years together.
二人(ふたり)は若(わか)い頃(ころ)から連(つ)れ合(あ)ってきた。
The two of them have been together since they were young.
苦(くる)しい時代(じだい)を連(つ)れ合(あ)って生(い)きてきた二人(ふたり)の絆(きずな)は強(つよ)い。
The bond between the two, who lived through hard times side by side, is strong.

連(つ)れる (to take along) + 合(あ)う (to do mutually), so literally 'to take each other along.' The verb itself is old-fashioned and mostly appears in writing; learners meet it chiefly as the source of the everyday noun 連(つ)れ合(あ)い.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 長年(ながねん)連(つ)れ合(あ)う: to be together for many years
  • 連(つ)れ合(あ)って歩(ある)く: to walk along together

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 連(つ)れ添(そ)う: to be married to — the usual modern verb for living as a married couple
  • 付(つ)き合(あ)う: to associate with, to date — everyday word for keeping company
