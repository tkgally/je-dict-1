千羽鶴(せんばづる)

せんばづる
noun
thousand folded paper cranes
1. thousand folded paper cranes (strung together as a wish or prayer)
A thousand origami cranes folded and strung on threads, given as a wish for recovery from illness, for victory, or for peace. Often made collectively by a class, a team, or a group of friends.
千羽鶴(せんばづる)を折(お)る。
We fold a thousand paper cranes.
クラスのみんなで千羽鶴(せんばづる)を作(つく)った。
Our whole class made a thousand paper cranes together.
入院中(にゅういんちゅう)の友(とも)だちに千羽鶴(せんばづる)を届(とど)けた。
We delivered a thousand paper cranes to our friend in the hospital.
広島(ひろしま)の平和記念公園(へいわきねんこうえん)には、世界中(せかいじゅう)から送(おく)られた千羽鶴(せんばづる)が飾(かざ)られている。
At the Peace Memorial Park in Hiroshima, paper cranes sent from all over the world are on display.

From 千(せん) (thousand) + 羽(わ) (counter for birds) + 鶴(つる) (crane); つる becomes づる in the compound.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 千羽鶴(せんばづる)を折(お)る: to fold a thousand cranes
  • 千羽鶴(せんばづる)を贈(おく)る: to present a thousand cranes
  • 快復(かいふく)を願(ねが)って千羽鶴(せんばづる)を作(つく)る: to make cranes wishing for someone's recovery

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
The crane is said to live a thousand years, and folding a thousand of them is a way of praying for long life or recovery. The custom is closely associated with Sadako Sasaki, a girl who folded cranes while ill after the Hiroshima bombing, and with the peace movement that grew around her story.
