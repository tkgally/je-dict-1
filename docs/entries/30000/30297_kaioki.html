1. stocking up in advance; a stock kept on hand
Buying everyday items before they are needed so that a supply is always available, or the supply itself. Used of household staples such as rice, detergent, and toilet paper.
洗剤(せんざい)の買(か)い置(お)きがある。
We have detergent in stock.
買(か)い置(お)きしておいてよかった。
I'm glad I stocked up beforehand.
買(か)い置(お)きが切(き)れたので、帰(かえ)りにスーパーに寄(よ)ってきます。
We're out of our stock, so I'll stop by the supermarket on the way home.

Composed of 買(か)う (to buy) + 置(お)く (to leave in place). Takes する to form a verb: 買(か)い置(お)きする.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 買(か)い置(お)きしておく: to buy some to keep on hand
  • 買(か)い置(お)きがある: to have a stock of something
  • 買(か)い置(お)きが切(き)れる: to run out of one's stock

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 買(か)いだめ: hoarding — larger quantities, often driven by anxiety about shortages
  • 買(か)い足(た)す: to buy more — restocking after running short, the opposite timing
  • 備蓄(びちく): stockpile — formal, about emergency or national reserves
