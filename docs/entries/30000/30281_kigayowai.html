1. timid; faint-hearted; unable to assert oneself
Describes someone who cannot say no, backs down under pressure, or is easily rattled. The judgment is usually mildly critical or self-deprecating rather than harsh.
弟(おとうと)は気(き)が弱(よわ)い。
My younger brother is timid.
気(き)が弱(よわ)くて断(ことわ)れなかった。
I was too faint-hearted to say no.
気(き)の弱(よわ)い人(ひと)ほど、無理(むり)な仕事(しごと)を引(ひ)き受(う)けてしまいがちだ。
The more timid a person is, the more likely they are to take on impossible work.

A fixed 気(き) expression: the subject of 弱(よわ)い is 気(き), so the person takes は or が in a double-subject pattern (彼(かれ)は気(き)が弱(よわ)い). In attributive position the が becomes の: 気(き)の弱(よわ)い人(ひと).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 気(き)の弱(よわ)い人(ひと): a timid person (attributive form)
  • 気(き)が弱(よわ)くて断(ことわ)れない: too timid to refuse
  • 気(き)が弱(よわ)そう: to look faint-hearted

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 気(き)が強(つよ)い: strong-willed, assertive — the direct opposite
  • 臆病(おくびょう)な: cowardly — about fear itself, stronger and more negative
  • 内気(うちき)な: shy, reserved — about reticence with people, not about caving in to pressure
