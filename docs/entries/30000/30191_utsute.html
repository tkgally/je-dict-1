打つ手

うつて
noun
move one can make; recourse; available countermeasure
1. move one can make; recourse; available countermeasure
A course of action still open in a difficult situation. Taken from board games, where 手(て) is a move and 打(う)つ is to play a stone or piece, so the word frames a problem as a game position with moves left or none.
もう打(う)つ手(て)がない。
There is nothing more we can do.
次(つぎ)に打(う)つ手(て)をみんなで考(かんが)えた。
We all thought about what move to make next.
売上(うりあげ)は落(お)ち続(つづ)けているが、会社(かいしゃ)にはまだ打(う)つ手(て)が残(のこ)っている。
Sales keep falling, but the company still has moves left to make.
早(はや)い段階(だんかい)で打(う)つ手(て)を間違(まちが)えると、あとで取(と)り返(かえ)すのが難(むずか)しくなる。
If you make the wrong move at an early stage, it becomes hard to recover later.

Nearly always used in a small set of patterns rather than freely, and most often in the negative — the phrase 打(う)つ手(て)がない is far more frequent than the positive statement.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 打(う)つ手(て)がない: to have no options left
  • 打(う)つ手(て)を考(かんが)える: to think about what to do
  • 次(つぎ)の打(う)つ手(て): the next move
  • 打(う)つ手(て)が遅(おそ)い: to be too slow to act

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 対策(たいさく): countermeasure — neutral and concrete; the word used in plans and documents
  • 手(て)を打(う)つ: to take action — the verb phrase from which this noun comes
