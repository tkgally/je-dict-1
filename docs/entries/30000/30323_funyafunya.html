ふにゃふにゃ

ふにゃふにゃ
onomatopoeia
soft and limp; floppy; flabby
1. soft and limp; floppy; without firmness
Describes something that has lost its stiffness and gives way when touched — soggy food, overcooked noodles, a deflated cushion. Also used of a body that has gone weak, and of a person with no backbone.
パンがふにゃふにゃになった。
The bread has gone soft and limp.
疲(つか)れて足(あし)がふにゃふにゃだ。
I'm so tired my legs have gone wobbly.
時間(じかん)を置(お)きすぎて、せっかくの天(てん)ぷらがふにゃふにゃになってしまった。
I left it too long, and the tempura I'd gone to such trouble over went soggy.

A mimetic word (擬態語(ぎたいご)). Most often used with なる (to become) or だ, and with に before a verb.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • ふにゃふにゃになる: to go soft and limp
  • ふにゃふにゃした〜: soft, floppy (attributive)
  • ふにゃふにゃの体(からだ): a limp body

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ぐにゃぐにゃ: bendy, rubbery — emphasizes bending out of shape rather than losing firmness
  • ふわふわ: fluffy, light — pleasantly soft, whereas ふにゃふにゃ usually sounds negative
  • べちゃべちゃ: soggy and wet — stresses excess moisture
