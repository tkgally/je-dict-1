1. to go for tea; to have a coffee; to hang out at a café
To spend time at a café or tea shop, chatting over a drink. Casual spoken Japanese; the point is the socializing rather than the tea itself, so it covers coffee and cake just as well.
ちょっとお茶(ちゃ)しない?
Want to grab a coffee?
買(か)い物(もの)のあとで友(とも)だちとお茶(ちゃ)した。
After shopping I had tea with a friend.
時間(じかん)があるなら、駅前(えきまえ)のカフェでお茶(ちゃ)しながら話(はな)しませんか。
If you have time, shall we talk over tea at the café in front of the station?

A casual suru-verb made from the everyday noun お茶(ちゃ) (tea). The お is fixed here — 茶(ちゃ)する is not used.

USAGE:
Most often heard as an invitation: お茶(ちゃ)しない?, お茶(ちゃ)しよう. It is too casual for business settings, where 打(う)ち合(あ)わせ or お茶(ちゃ)でも飲(の)みませんか would be used instead. Common among friends and especially among younger speakers.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • お茶(ちゃ)しない?: want to get tea?
  • お茶(ちゃ)でもする: to have tea or something
  • カフェでお茶(ちゃ)する: to hang out at a café

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • お茶(ちゃ)を飲(の)む: to drink tea — literal, about the drink rather than the outing
  • 一服(いっぷく)する: to take a break — a short rest, often alone
