1. to start walking; to set off on foot
To begin walking after standing still, or to set out toward somewhere. Also used of a baby taking its first steps.
黙(だま)って歩(ある)き出(だ)した。
He started walking without a word.
信号(しんごう)が青(あお)になったので歩(ある)き出(だ)した。
The light turned green, so I started walking.
娘(むすめ)が一歳(いっさい)の誕生日(たんじょうび)の前(まえ)に歩(ある)き出(だ)した。
My daughter started walking just before her first birthday.
雨(あめ)がやむのを待(ま)っていたが、いつまでも止(や)まないので傘(かさ)を差(さ)して歩(ある)き出(だ)した。
I was waiting for the rain to stop, but it never let up, so I put up my umbrella and set off.

ASPECT: 〜出(だ)す marks the onset of the motion, so 歩(ある)き出(だ)す normally appears in the past (歩(ある)き出(だ)した) or in て-form leading into what happened next. For walking that is under way, use 歩(ある)いている.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 歩(ある)く (to walk) + 出(だ)す (to begin). Figuratively it can describe a project or a life finally getting under way: 新(あたら)しい道(みち)を歩(ある)き出(だ)す (to set out on a new path).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 走(はし)り出(だ)す: to start running — the same pattern with a faster motion
  • 歩(ある)き始(はじ)める: to begin walking — neutral, without the sense of abruptness
  • 出発(しゅっぱつ)する: to depart — about leaving for a destination, by any means of travel
