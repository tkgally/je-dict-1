〜たがる

たがる
auxiliary
show signs of wanting to (of someone else)
1. to appear to want to; to be eager to
Verb stem plus たがる. Reports a desire that is visible from the outside, so it is used about a third person rather than about oneself: 弟(おとうと)は行(い)きたがっている (my brother wants to go).
子(こ)どもが外(そと)に出(で)たがる。
The child wants to go outside.
彼(かれ)は理由(りゆう)を話(はな)したがらない。
He doesn't want to talk about the reason.
妹(いもうと)は新(あたら)しいゲームをやりたがっていて、毎日(まいにち)その話(はなし)ばかりする。
My little sister is dying to play the new game and talks about nothing else every day.

Attaches to the verb stem and then conjugates like a godan verb: 行(い)きたがる, 行(い)きたがらない, 行(い)きたがった. The object of the wanted action takes を, not が.

USAGE:

  • 〜たがっている describes what someone wants at this moment; plain 〜たがる describes a standing tendency.
  • Do not use it about yourself — use 〜たい instead.
  • About a superior or a customer it can sound presumptuous; 〜たいとおっしゃっています is safer.

SIMILAR FORMS:

  • 〜たい: want to — used for one's own desire, or in questions to the listener
  • 欲(ほ)しがる: to want (a thing) — the same third-person logic applied to objects rather than actions
