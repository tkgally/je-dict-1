1. to give one's all; to put out everything one has
To hold nothing back — to expend all of one's strength, skill, or ideas. Transitive, and most often used of 力(ちから) or 実力(じつりょく) in sports and work.
力(ちから)を出(だ)し切(き)った。
I gave everything I had.
練習(れんしゅう)の成果(せいか)を出(だ)し切(き)れなかった。
I couldn't fully bring out the results of my practice.
最後(さいご)の試合(しあい)で実力(じつりょく)を出(だ)し切(き)れたので、負(ま)けても悔(く)いはない。
I was able to give my full ability in the final match, so I have no regrets even though I lost.

出(だ)す (to put out) + 切(き)る (the compound-verb suffix meaning to do completely). The 切(き)る ending is what supplies the sense of exhausting the supply.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 力(ちから)を出(だ)し切(き)る: to give all one's strength
  • 実力(じつりょく)を出(だ)し切(き)る: to show one's full ability
  • 全(すべ)てを出(だ)し切(き)る: to give absolutely everything

USAGE:
The potential negative 出(だ)し切(き)れない (couldn't give it all) is very common after a disappointing performance.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 使(つか)い切(き)る: to use up — of supplies and money rather than effort
  • やり切(き)る: to see through to the end — emphasizes finishing the task, not exhausting oneself
