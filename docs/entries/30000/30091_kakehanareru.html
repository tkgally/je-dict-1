1. to be far apart; to differ greatly
To be greatly separated — usually figurative, describing a wide gap between two things in degree, quality, or nature rather than physical distance. Intransitive.
理想(りそう)と現実(げんじつ)がかけ離(はな)れている。
The ideal and reality are far apart.
二人(ふたり)の意見(いけん)はかけ離(はな)れていた。
The two people's opinions were worlds apart.
彼(かれ)の説明(せつめい)は事実(じじつ)とかけ離(はな)れていて、誰(だれ)も信(しん)じなかった。
His explanation was so far removed from the facts that no one believed it.

An intransitive verb describing a wide gap between two things in degree, quality, or nature — usually figurative rather than physical distance.

USAGE:

  • The thing compared against takes と: 現実(げんじつ)とかけ離(はな)れる.
  • Most often used in the ている form, or as かけ離(はな)れた before a noun: 現実(げんじつ)とかけ離(はな)れた計画(けいかく) (a plan far removed from reality).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 異(こと)なる: to differ — neutral, does not stress the size of the gap
  • 食(く)い違(ちが)う: to be at odds — about conflicting details rather than a wide gap
