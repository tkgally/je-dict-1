1. to take a short break; to pause for a breather
To stop work briefly and relax. 一服(いっぷく) literally means one dose or one serving, so the break is typically accompanied by a cigarette or a cup of tea — often implied even when not stated.
ちょっと一服(いっぷく)しよう。
Let's take a short break.
仕事(しごと)の合間(あいま)に一服(いっぷく)する。
I take a breather between tasks.
昼食(ちゅうしょく)のあと、庭(にわ)に出(で)て一服(いっぷく)するのが父(ちち)の習慣(しゅうかん)だ。
Going out to the garden for a smoke after lunch is my father's habit.
荷物(にもつ)を全部(ぜんぶ)運(はこ)び終(お)えてから、みんなでお茶(ちゃ)を飲(の)んで一服(いっぷく)した。
After carrying in all the luggage, we all had tea and took a break.

Slightly old-fashioned and warm in tone, and strongly associated with smokers, though it is used for any short rest. 一服(いっぷく) also works as a plain noun.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 一服(いっぷく)入(い)れる: to take a breather
  • 一服(いっぷく)つける: to light a cigarette
  • ここらで一服(いっぷく): time for a break about now

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 休憩(きゅうけい)する: to take a break — neutral and general; the word used at work or school
  • 一息(ひといき)つく: to catch one's breath — emphasizes relief after finishing something demanding
