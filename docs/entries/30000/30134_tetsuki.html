手(て)つき

てつき
noun
way one uses one's hands; hand movements
1. way one uses one's hands; hand movements
The visible manner in which someone moves their hands while doing something. Almost always used with a modifier that judges the movement as skilled, clumsy, gentle, and so on.
慣(な)れた手(て)つきで魚(さかな)をさばく。
To fillet a fish with practiced hands.
危(あぶ)なっかしい手(て)つきで包丁(ほうちょう)を使(つか)っている。
He is using the kitchen knife with an alarmingly clumsy grip.
彼女(かのじょ)は優(やさ)しい手(て)つきで赤(あか)ちゃんを抱(だ)き上(あ)げ、そっと布団(ふとん)に寝(ね)かせた。
She picked the baby up with gentle hands and laid it softly on the futon.

Nearly always appears as 「modifier}な{手(て)つきで」 describing how an action is performed. It is about the look of the movement, not about the hands themselves.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 慣(な)れた手(て)つき: practiced, assured hand movements
  • 危(あぶ)なっかしい手(て)つき: clumsy, unsafe-looking handling
  • 優(やさ)しい手(て)つき: gentle hands
  • 鮮(あざ)やかな手(て)つき: deft, impressive handling

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 手(て)さばき: handling, dexterity — emphasizes technique and speed, typically praising skill
  • 仕草(しぐさ): gesture, mannerism — any bodily movement or habit, not limited to the hands
