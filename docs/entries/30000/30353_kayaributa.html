蚊遣(かや)り豚(ぶた)

かやりぶた
noun
pig-shaped mosquito coil holder
1. pig-shaped ceramic mosquito coil holder
A hollow ceramic pig with an opening in its side, used to hold a burning mosquito coil. A familiar emblem of Japanese summer, often seen on verandas and in seasonal advertising.
縁側(えんがわ)に蚊遣(かや)り豚(ぶた)が置(お)いてある。
A mosquito coil pig is sitting on the veranda.
蚊遣(かや)り豚(ぶた)は夏(なつ)の風物詩(ふうぶつし)だ。
The mosquito coil pig is one of the sights of summer.
祖母(そぼ)の家(いえ)では、今(いま)でも蚊遣(かや)り豚(ぶた)に蚊取(かと)り線香(せんこう)を入(い)れて使(つか)っている。
At my grandmother's house they still put mosquito coils in the ceramic pig and use it.

From 蚊遣(かや)り (driving off mosquitoes) plus 豚(ぶた) (pig). Also written 蚊取(かと)り豚(ぶた).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 蚊遣(かや)り豚(ぶた)を置(お)く: to set out a mosquito coil pig
  • 夏(なつ)の風物詩(ふうぶつし): a seasonal sight of summer
  • 蚊取(かと)り線香(せんこう)を入(い)れる: to put a coil inside it

CULTURAL NOTE:
The shape is said to derive from Edo-period pots used to burn smoky material against mosquitoes. Today it is as much a nostalgic ornament as a practical device, and it appears constantly in summer illustrations alongside wind chimes and watermelon.
