肖像権(しょうぞうけん)

しょうぞうけん
noun
portrait rights; right to one's own image
1. portrait rights; right to one's own image
The right not to have one's face or figure photographed, published, or used commercially without consent. In Japan it is recognized by case law rather than by a single statute.
肖像権(しょうぞうけん)に注意(ちゅうい)してください。
Please be mindful of portrait rights.
許可(きょか)なく写真(しゃしん)を使(つか)うと肖像権(しょうぞうけん)の侵害(しんがい)になる。
Using a photograph without permission constitutes an infringement of portrait rights.
学校(がっこう)の行事(ぎょうじ)の写真(しゃしん)をSNSに載(の)せる前(まえ)に、肖像権(しょうぞうけん)について保護者(ほごしゃ)の同意(どうい)を取(と)った。
Before posting photos of the school event on social media, we obtained the guardians' consent regarding portrait rights.

Composed of 肖像(しょうぞう) (portrait, likeness) + 権(けん) (right).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 肖像権(しょうぞうけん)の侵害(しんがい): infringement of portrait rights
  • 肖像権(しょうぞうけん)を侵(おか)す: to violate someone's portrait rights
  • 肖像権(しょうぞうけん)に配慮(はいりょ)する: to take portrait rights into consideration
  • 肖像権(しょうぞうけん)フリー(ふりー): free of portrait-rights restrictions

USAGE:
Everyday encounters with the word are usually notices — event programs, school newsletters, and photography rules at venues. Public figures are also protected by a commercial aspect of the right, sometimes called パブリシティ権(ぱぶりしてぃけん).
