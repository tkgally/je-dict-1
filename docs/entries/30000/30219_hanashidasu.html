1. to start talking; to begin to speak
To begin speaking, often after a silence or unexpectedly. The focus is on the moment speech starts, not on what is said.
急(きゅう)に話(はな)し出(だ)した。
He suddenly started talking.
黙(だま)っていた彼女(かのじょ)がゆっくりと話(はな)し出(だ)した。
She had been silent, and then slowly began to speak.
お酒(さけ)が入(はい)ると、父(ちち)は昔(むかし)の話(はなし)を話(はな)し出(だ)す。
Once he's had a drink, my father starts talking about the old days.
会議(かいぎ)が静(しず)まり返(かえ)ったところで、一番(いちばん)若(わか)い社員(しゃいん)が手(て)を挙(あ)げて話(はな)し出(だ)した。
When the meeting had fallen completely silent, the youngest employee raised a hand and started to speak.

ASPECT: 〜出(だ)す marks the onset of an action, so 話(はな)し出(だ)す normally appears in the past (話(はな)し出(だ)した) or in て-form leading into what followed. For speech already under way, use 話(はな)している.

FORMATION:
Compound verb: 話(はな)す (to talk) + 出(だ)す (to begin). The 〜出(だ)す pattern often carries a sense of suddenness or of something no longer held back.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 話(はな)し始(はじ)める: to begin talking — neutral, without the nuance of abruptness
  • 切(き)り出(だ)す: to broach a subject — used when raising a difficult topic
  • 口(くち)を開(ひら)く: to open one's mouth, to speak up — literary, often after a long silence
