1. to be hurried; to feel rushed; to be impatient
For a person's mind or breathing to be driven forward by impatience. Intransitive, and most often used of 気(き) (one's feelings) or 息(いき) (one's breath).
気(き)が急(せ)いて落(お)ち着(つ)かない。
I feel rushed and can't settle down.
階段(かいだん)を上(のぼ)ったら息(いき)が急(せ)いた。
My breath came short after I climbed the stairs.
締(し)め切(き)りが近(ちか)づくにつれて気(き)が急(せ)き、細(こま)かい確認(かくにん)を忘(わす)れてしまった。
As the deadline drew near I grew impatient and forgot to check the details.

An older, somewhat literary verb that survives mainly in set phrases. It describes the internal feeling of being hurried, not the act of hurrying.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 気(き)が急(せ)く: to feel rushed, to be impatient
  • 息(いき)が急(せ)く: for one's breathing to become short
  • 急(せ)いては事(こと)を仕損(しそん)じる: haste makes waste (proverb)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 急(いそ)ぐ: to hurry — the ordinary word, describing the action of moving fast
  • 焦(あせ)る: to be flustered — stronger, with a sense of anxiety about falling behind

RELATED FORMS:
せっかち (impatient, hasty) derives from this verb.
