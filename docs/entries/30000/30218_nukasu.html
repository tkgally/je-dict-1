1. to skip; to leave out; to omit
To pass over something that should have been included — a line while reading, a step in a procedure, or a meal.
一行(いちぎょう)抜(ぬ)かして読(よ)んでしまった。
I ended up skipping a line as I read.
朝食(ちょうしょく)を抜(ぬ)かすと午前中(ごぜんちゅう)に力(ちから)が出(で)ない。
If I skip breakfast, I have no energy in the morning.
手順(てじゅん)を一(ひと)つ抜(ぬ)かしただけで、機械(きかい)が動(うご)かなくなってしまった。
Just leaving out one step in the procedure made the machine stop working.
2. to overtake; to pass ahead of
To get in front of someone who was ahead. Very often used in the passive when someone cuts in line or passes you unfairly.
順番(じゅんばん)を抜(ぬ)かされた。
Someone cut in front of me in line.
最後(さいご)の直線(ちょくせん)で二人(ふたり)抜(ぬ)かした。
I passed two runners on the final straight.
列(れつ)に並(なら)んでいたのに横(よこ)から抜(ぬ)かされて、さすがに腹(はら)が立(た)った。
I was waiting in line and someone cut in from the side, which honestly made me angry.

TRANSITIVITY: 抜(ぬ)かす is transitive and takes を. Its intransitive partner is 抜(ぬ)ける (to come out, to be missing): 名前(なまえ)が抜(ぬ)けている (the name is missing) vs 名前(なまえ)を抜(ぬ)かす (to leave the name out).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 食事(しょくじ)を抜(ぬ)かす: to skip a meal
  • 順番(じゅんばん)を抜(ぬ)かす: to cut in line
  • 腰(こし)を抜(ぬ)かす: to be so startled one's legs give way (idiom)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 抜(ぬ)く: to pull out; to overtake — the base verb, more common for passing in a race
  • 追(お)い抜(ぬ)く: to overtake — neutral and specific about passing someone ahead
  • 省(はぶ)く: to omit — deliberate and often sensible, whereas 抜(ぬ)かす is usually careless
