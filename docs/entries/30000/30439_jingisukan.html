ジンギスカン

じんぎすかん
noun
grilled mutton dish of Hokkaido
1. grilled mutton dish of Hokkaido; Genghis Khan barbecue
Slices of mutton or lamb grilled with vegetables on a dome-shaped iron skillet and dipped in a soy-based sauce. The best-known local dish of Hokkaido, eaten at restaurants, at beer gardens, and outdoors.
北海道(ほっかいどう)でジンギスカンを食(た)べた。
I ate jingisukan in Hokkaido.
ジンギスカン鍋(なべ)で羊肉(ようにく)と野菜(やさい)を焼(や)く。
You grill the mutton and vegetables on a jingisukan skillet.
札幌(さっぽろ)では花見(はなみ)の季節(きせつ)に公園(こうえん)でジンギスカンを楽(たの)しむ人(ひと)も多(おお)い。
In Sapporo, many people enjoy jingisukan in the parks during cherry-blossom season.

Named after ジンギスカン, the Japanese form of Genghis Khan; the link to the Mongol ruler is a marketing legend rather than history. The dish itself dates from early twentieth-century Hokkaido, where sheep were raised for wool.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ジンギスカン鍋(なべ): the dome-shaped skillet used for the dish (also the dish itself)
  • ジンギスカンを食(た)べに行(い)く: to go out for jingisukan
  • 生(なま)ラムのジンギスカン: jingisukan made with fresh (unfrozen) lamb

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
The curved skillet lets the fat run down to the vegetables at the rim while the meat cooks on top. In Hokkaido, outdoor jingisukan at 花見(はなみ) or beer gardens is as ordinary as a barbecue elsewhere in Japan.
