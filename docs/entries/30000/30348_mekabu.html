めかぶ

めかぶ
noun
mekabu; the frilly root part of wakame seaweed
1. mekabu; sporophyll of wakame
The thick, ruffled part at the base of a wakame frond, sold chopped and eaten for its slippery texture. Usually written in hiragana or katakana.
めかぶを食(た)べる。
I eat mekabu.
めかぶは体(からだ)にいいそうだ。
They say mekabu is good for you.
朝(あさ)ごはんに、めかぶと納豆(なっとう)を混(ま)ぜて食(た)べるのが習慣(しゅうかん)になっている。
Mixing mekabu with natto for breakfast has become a habit of mine.

The base of a wakame plant, just above the holdfast. When chopped it becomes very slippery, and that texture is the point of the dish.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 刻(きざ)みめかぶ: chopped mekabu (the usual supermarket form)
  • めかぶ納豆(なっとう): mekabu mixed with natto
  • めかぶの酢(す)の物(もの): mekabu in vinegar dressing
  • ねばねばする: to be sticky and slippery

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • わかめ: wakame — the leafy blade of the same plant, sold separately
  • もずく: mozuku — a different slippery seaweed, usually served in vinegar
