1. to be thorough; to be attentive; to be well looked after
Said of care, attention, or management that extends to every corner of something. Most often used in the ている form to describe a place, service, or piece of work as impeccably tended.
掃除(そうじ)が行(い)き届(とど)いている。
The cleaning has been done thoroughly.
この旅館(りょかん)はサービスが行(い)き届(とど)いている。
The service at this inn is attentive in every detail.
手入(てい)れの行(い)き届(とど)いた庭(にわ)を見(み)ると気持(きも)ちがいい。
It feels good to look at a well-tended garden.
生徒(せいと)の数(かず)が多(おお)すぎて、一人(ひとり)ひとりに指導(しどう)が行(い)き届(とど)かない。
There are too many students, so instruction cannot reach every one of them.

A compound of 行(い)く (to go) + 届(とど)く (to reach). The subject is the care or attention itself, marked with が, not the person providing it.

ASPECT:
The plain form is rare in the present tense. The normal affirmative use is 行(い)き届(とど)いている (is thorough, is well looked after), a resulting state, while the negative 行(い)き届(とど)かない points out where care is falling short.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 掃除(そうじ)が行(い)き届(とど)いている: is spotlessly clean
  • サービスが行(い)き届(とど)いている: the service is attentive
  • 手入(てい)れの行(い)き届(とど)いた: well-tended (as a modifier)
  • 目(め)が行(い)き届(とど)く: to keep an eye on everything

TRANSITIVITY:
Intransitive. It takes no direct object; the area covered is marked with に (すみずみ}に{行(い)き届(とど)く, to reach every corner).
