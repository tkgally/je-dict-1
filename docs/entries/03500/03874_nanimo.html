何(なに)も

なにも
adverb
nothing, (not) anything
1. nothing, not anything
Used with negative verb forms to mean 'nothing at all.'
何(なに)も知(し)らない。
I don't know anything.
何(なに)も食(た)べていない。
I haven't eaten anything.
何(なに)も見(み)えなかった。
I couldn't see anything.
何(なに)も言(い)わないでください。
Please don't say anything.
冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)には何(なに)もなかったので、買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)った。
There was nothing in the refrigerator, so I went shopping.
2. there's no need to
Used to indicate that something is unnecessary.
何(なに)も急(いそ)がなくてもいい。
There's no need to hurry.
何(なに)も泣(な)かなくてもいいよ。
There's no need to cry.
何(なに)も心配(しんぱい)することはない。
There's nothing to worry about.
何(なに)も謝(あやま)る必要(ひつよう)はない。
There's no need to apologize.
何(なに)もそこまで怒(おこ)らなくてもいいのに。
You don't need to get that angry.

USAGE: 何(なに)も is always used with negative verb forms to mean "nothing" or "not anything." It cannot be used in affirmative sentences.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 何(なに)も食(た)べない (to not eat anything)
  • 何(なに)も言(い)わない (to not say anything)
  • 何(なに)も知(し)らない (to not know anything)
  • 何(なに)もない (there's nothing)
  • 何(なに)も〜なくてもいい (there's no need to...)

CONTRAST:

  • 何(なに)か (something - affirmative)
  • 何(なに)も (nothing - negative)

POSITION IN SENTENCE:

  • 何(なに)も stands where the object would go, before the verb, and the negative must come later in the same clause.
  • Sense 2 puts it at the head of the clause, often with そこまで or そんなに-type phrases, and carries a reproachful "you didn't have to" tone.

SIMILAR ADVERBS:

  • 全然(ぜんぜん): not at all — negates the degree of an action, while 何(なに)も negates the object
  • 全(まった)く: entirely (not) — stronger and more formal
  • 誰(だれ)も: nobody — the same 〜も + negative pattern applied to people
  • 何(なに)にも is an emphatic variant of 何(なに)も heard in speech

Related Words

Contrast: 何(なに)か (something)
Related: 誰(だれ)も
