井戸(いど)

いど
noun
well (water source)
1. well
A deep hole dug into the ground to reach underground water. Traditional wells were common in Japan before modern plumbing.
井戸(いど)から水(みず)を汲(く)む。
To draw water from a well.
この村(むら)には古(ふる)い井戸(いど)がある。
There is an old well in this village.
井戸(いど)の水(みず)は冷(つめ)たくて美味(おい)しい。
Well water is cold and delicious.

USAGE: In modern Japan, wells are rare in urban areas but still found in rural regions and preserved as cultural heritage.

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 井戸(いど)を掘(ほ)る (to dig a well)
  • 井戸(いど)水(みず) (well water)
  • 井戸(いど)端(ばた)会議(かいぎ) (gossiping at the well - lit. 'well-side conference')

The expression 井戸(いど)端(ばた)会議(かいぎ) refers to casual gossip, historically shared by women gathering at the communal well.
