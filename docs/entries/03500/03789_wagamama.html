わがまま

わがまま
na-adjective, noun
selfish, willful
1. selfish, self-centered, willful
Behavior characterized by doing what one wants without considering others; selfishness or capriciousness.
わがままを言(い)わないで。
Don't be selfish. / Don't make selfish demands.
わがままを言(い)う = to make selfish demands
彼(かれ)はわがままな子(こ)だ。
He is a selfish child.
わがままを許(ゆる)してください。
Please forgive my selfishness.
Often used apologetically when making a personal request
あの人(ひと)はわがままで、いつも自分(じぶん)の思(おも)い通(どお)りにしたがる。
That person is selfish and always wants things to go their own way.
子供(こども)のわがままを何(なん)でも聞(き)いていると、大人(おとな)になってから困(こま)ることになる。
If you give in to every selfish demand a child makes, it will cause problems when they grow up.
わがままを聞(き)く = to give in to selfish demands

わがまま describes putting one's own wishes ahead of everyone else's — selfish, self-indulgent, wilful. It is used constantly of children who will not do as they are told, and of adults who behave the same way.

It is both a na-adjective and a noun. As a noun it names the selfish behaviour itself, which is why it takes を and a verb: わがままを言(い)う is literally 'to say a selfishness,' i.e. to make demands one has no right to make.

Common patterns:

  • わがままを言(い)う: to make selfish demands
  • わがままな人(ひと): a selfish person
  • わがままを通(とお)す: to get one's own way

Register: usually a criticism, but it is very often turned on oneself as a softener — わがままを言(い)ってすみません, 'sorry to be difficult,' is a normal way to preface an inconvenient request. Said of a child it is mild; said flatly of an adult it stings.

Similar words: 自分勝手(じぶんかって) is blunter and purely critical, with none of わがまま's affectionate or self-deprecating uses. The opposite quality is 素直(すなお), being frank and unresisting.

Orthography: usually written in hiragana; the kanji 我儘(わがまま) exists but is uncommon.

Related Words

Synonym: 自分勝手(じぶんかって) (blunter, more critical)
Related: 勝手(かって) (doing as one pleases)
Antonym: 素直(すなお) (obedient, unresisting)
