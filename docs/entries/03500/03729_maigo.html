迷子(まいご)

まいご
noun
lost child
1. lost child
A child who has become separated from their parents or guardians.
迷子(まいご)になった。
I got lost.
子供(こども)が迷子(まいご)になった。
The child got lost.
迷子(まいご)の子供(こども)を交番(こうばん)に連(つ)れて行(い)った。
I took the lost child to the police box.
お祭(まつ)りで人(ひと)が多(おお)くて、子供(こども)が迷子(まいご)になりやすい。
It's easy for children to get lost at festivals because of the crowds.
「迷子(まいご)のお知(し)らせです」と店内(てんない)のアナウンスが流(なが)れた。
A store announcement came on: 'We have a lost child announcement.'
Common announcement in Japanese stores
2. lost person, someone who is lost
By extension, anyone who has lost their way (often used playfully for adults).
駅(えき)で迷子(まいご)になった。
I got lost at the station.
初(はじ)めての町(まち)で迷子(まいご)になった。
I got lost in a town I was visiting for the first time.
大(おお)きなビルの中(なか)で迷子(まいご)になってしまった。
I got lost inside the large building.
地図(ちず)を見(み)ていたのに迷子(まいご)になるなんて、恥(は)ずかしい。
It's embarrassing to get lost even though I was looking at a map.
Playful adult usage
大人(おとな)になっても、知(し)らない場所(ばしょ)では迷子(まいご)になることがある。
Even as an adult, you can get lost in unfamiliar places.

迷子(まいご) is literally a lost child — the kanji are 迷(まい) 'to be lost, confused' and 子(ご) 'child.' It is the standard word in announcements and signage at stations, department stores, and festivals.

It is also used freely of adults, usually with a light, self-deprecating tone: an adult who says 迷子(まいご)になりました is admitting to being thoroughly lost, a little comically. For a serious case — a missing person whose whereabouts are unknown — the word is 行方不明(ゆくえふめい), not 迷子(まいご).

Note the grammar: Japanese treats being lost as a state you become, so the verb is なる, not ある or する.

Common collocations:

  • 迷子(まいご)になる: to get lost
  • 迷子(まいご)センター: lost-child centre
  • 迷子(まいご)のお知(し)らせ: a lost-child announcement
  • 迷子(まいご)札(ふだ): an identification tag worn by children at crowded events

Related expressions:

  • 道(みち)に迷(まよ)う: to lose one's way. This describes the act of going astray; 迷子(まいご) names the person in that state.
  • はぐれる: to get separated from the group you were with, which is often how one becomes a 迷子(まいご).
