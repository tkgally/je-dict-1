まあ

まあ
interjection / adverb
well; my!; so-so
1. well, now
Used when making a modest, hesitant, or transitional statement.
まあ、仕方(しかた)ないか。
Well, I guess it can't be helped.
まあ、いいでしょう。
Well, that should be fine.
まあ、そうですね。
Well, I suppose so.
まあ、大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)だろう。
Well, it'll probably be okay.
まあ、待(ま)ってください。
Now, please wait.
まあまあ、落(お)ち着(つ)いて。
Now, now, calm down.
2. my!, oh!
An exclamation expressing surprise (often used by women).
まあ、驚(おどろ)いた！
Oh my, what a surprise!
Often used by women
まあ、素敵(すてき)！
Oh my, how lovely!
まあ、大変(たいへん)！
Oh dear!
まあ、久(ひさ)しぶり！
Oh my, it's been so long!
まあ、こんなに大(おお)きくなって！前(まえ)に会(あ)ったときはまだ小(ちい)さかったのに。
Oh my, you've grown so much! You were still little the last time we met.
3. more or less, so-so
Indicating something is acceptable but not exceptional.
まあまあの結果(けっか)だった。
The results were so-so.
まあまあ美味(おい)しい。
It's fairly good.
まあまあだね。
It's so-so.
まあまあ元気(げんき)です。
I'm doing fairly well.
まあまあの成績(せいせき)だった。
The grades were passable.

まあ has several distinct uses that are told apart mainly by intonation and context. It is always written in kana.

HESITATION / SOFTENING (drawn-out, falling tone): softens a statement or turns firm agreement into a mild concession — "well…", "I guess". It typically opens a sentence, often before いい, そう, or a guess like だろう, making the whole remark tentative rather than definite.

SURPRISE (short, rising tone): a standalone exclamation of mild surprise or admiration — "oh my!", "oh dear!". This use is traditionally feminine and now sounds somewhat old-fashioned. It comes before the thing noticed (まあ、素敵(すてき)！).

MODERATE DEGREE (reduplicated まあまあ): means "so-so, passable" — neither good nor bad. Used on its own, before an adjective (まあまあ美味(おい)しい), or as まあまあの + noun ("passable results").

CALMING (まあまあ、 said soothingly): the same reduplicated form used to calm someone — "there, there", "now, now" (まあまあ、落(お)ち着(つ)いて).
