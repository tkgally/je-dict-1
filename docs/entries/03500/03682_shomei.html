1. signature, autograph
One's name written by oneself, especially on documents for authentication or approval.
ここに署名(しょめい)してください。
Please sign here.
署名(しょめい)欄(らん)に名前(なまえ)を書(か)いた。
I wrote my name in the signature field.
署名(しょめい)欄(らん) = signature field/column
契約書(けいやくしょ)に署名(しょめい)する前(まえ)に、よく読(よ)んでください。
Please read it carefully before signing the contract.

Without the person's signature, this document is invalid.

If you use an electronic signature, you don't need to send paper documents.

署名(しょめい) is the formal word for writing one's own name on a document to authenticate or endorse it: contracts, official forms, petitions, and treaties. As a noun it is the signature itself; with する it is the act of signing.

GRAMMAR:

  • 署名(しょめい)する is 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive): the document signed is not a direct object.
  • What is signed takes に, not を: 契約書(けいやくしょ)に署名(しょめい)する (sign the contract). 署名(しょめい)を appears when the signature itself is the object, as in 署名(しょめい)を集(あつ)める (collect signatures).
  • 署名(しょめい)している describes the resulting state (has signed), not an action in progress.
  • It combines freely as the first half of compounds: 署名(しょめい)欄(らん), 署名(しょめい)運動(うんどう), 電子(でんし)署名(しょめい).

CULTURAL NOTE:
On Japanese official paperwork a personal seal (はんこ / 印鑑(いんかん)) has traditionally carried the weight a handwritten signature carries elsewhere, and forms often ask for 署名(しょめい)捺印(なついん) (signature and seal) together. Digital procedures have made 電子(でんし)署名(しょめい) increasingly common.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 署名(しょめい)する (to sign)
  • 署名(しょめい)運動(うんどう) (petition drive, signature campaign)
  • 署名(しょめい)活動(かつどう) (signature-gathering activity)
  • 署名(しょめい)欄(らん) (signature field/column)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • サイン is more casual and used for autographs from celebrities or informal signatures
  • 署名(しょめい) is more formal, used for documents and petitions
  • 記名(きめい) is simply having a name written in — it may be printed, typed, or written by someone else — whereas 署名(しょめい) must be in the person's own hand
