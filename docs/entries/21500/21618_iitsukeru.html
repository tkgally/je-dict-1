1. to tell on someone; to report someone's misbehavior
To go to a parent, teacher, or authority figure and report what someone has done wrong. Commonly used by or about children.
先生(せんせい)に言(い)いつけるよ。
I'm going to tell the teacher on you.
弟(おとうと)がお母(かあ)さんに言(い)いつけた。
My little brother told on me to Mom.
「言(い)いつけてやる」と子(こ)どもが泣(な)きながら走(はし)って行(い)った。
"I'm going to tell on you!" the child said, running off in tears.
2. to order; to command; to instruct
To give an order or instruction to someone, typically from a position of authority.
上司(じょうし)に言(い)いつけられた仕事(しごと)。
Work ordered by a superior.
父(ちち)に言(い)いつけられた通(とお)りにした。
I did as my father instructed.
社長(しゃちょう)が秘書(ひしょ)に買(か)い物(もの)を言(い)いつけた。
The president ordered the secretary to go shopping.

This verb has two distinct usages that depend on context. The "tell on" sense is very common in children's language, while the "order" sense appears in broader contexts.

SENSE 1 — TATTLING:
Very common in childhood contexts. The pattern is: XにYを言(い)いつける (to report Y's behavior to X). Often used as a threat: 「言(い)いつけるよ！」

SENSE 2 — ORDERING:
Implies giving a directive from a position of authority. Can sound somewhat old-fashioned or stern compared to modern alternatives.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 先生(せんせい)に言(い)いつける: to tell the teacher
  • 親(おや)に言(い)いつける: to tell one's parents
  • 用事(ようじ)を言(い)いつける: to assign an errand

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 告(つ)げ口(ぐち)する: to tattle — more specifically about informing/snitching
  • 命(めい)じる: to order — more formal, official
  • 指示(しじ)する: to instruct — neutral, workplace standard
