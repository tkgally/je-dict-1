突(つ)き出(だ)し

つきだし
noun
appetizer (served at a bar or izakaya); pushing out
1. appetizer; small dish served automatically at an izakaya or bar
A small dish of food that is served automatically when you sit down at an izakaya or bar, similar to a cover charge. The customer does not order it.
突(つ)き出(だ)しは枝豆(えだまめ)だった。
The appetizer was edamame.
この店(みせ)の突(つ)き出(だ)しはいつも美味(おい)しい。
The appetizer at this restaurant is always delicious.
居酒屋(いざかや)に入(はい)ると、注文(ちゅうもん)しなくても突(つ)き出(だ)しが出(で)てくる。
When you enter an izakaya, an appetizer is served even without ordering.
2. pushing out; thrusting out
The act of pushing or thrusting something outward. Also used in sumo wrestling for a technique of pushing the opponent out of the ring.
力士(りきし)が相手(あいて)を突(つ)き出(だ)しで破(やぶ)った。
The sumo wrestler defeated his opponent with a thrust-out.
棚(たな)の突(つ)き出(だ)しに頭(あたま)をぶつけた。
I bumped my head on the shelf's protrusion.
横綱(よこづな)は突(つ)き出(だ)しで一気(いっき)に勝負(しょうぶ)を決(き)めた。
The yokozuna settled the match at once with a thrust-out.

In sense 1, 突(つ)き出(だ)し refers to the small appetizer dish automatically served at Japanese izakaya and bars. It functions as a cover charge — the cost is added to the bill regardless of whether the customer wants it. This practice surprises some visitors to Japan.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 突(つ)き出(だ)しが出(で)る: an appetizer is served
  • 突(つ)き出(だ)し代(だい): appetizer charge
  • 突(つ)き出(だ)しで勝(か)つ: to win by thrust-out (sumo)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • お通(とお)し: appetizer — same meaning as sense 1, more common in eastern Japan; 突(つ)き出(だ)し is more common in western Japan
  • 前菜(ぜんさい): appetizer — a general term for starters at a restaurant, usually ordered by the customer

CULTURAL NOTE:
The 突(つ)き出(だ)し system is unique to Japanese dining culture. It typically costs 300-500 yen and serves as both a snack and a seat charge. Some tourists find it confusing since it appears on the bill without being ordered.
