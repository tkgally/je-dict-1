1. to challenge; to take on a rival or opponent
To confront or compete against someone or something, especially a strong opponent or a difficult adversary.
王者(おうじゃ)に挑戦(ちょうせん)する。
To challenge the champion.
彼(かれ)は世界(せかい)チャンピオンに挑戦(ちょうせん)した。
He challenged the world champion.
その若(わか)い選手(せんしゅ)は恐(おそ)れずに強豪(きょうごう)チームに挑戦(ちょうせん)した。
The young player fearlessly took on the powerhouse team.
2. to attempt; to try something difficult
To take on a difficult task, try a new experience, or push oneself beyond one's comfort zone. Implies a positive, proactive attitude.
新(あたら)しい料理(りょうり)に挑戦(ちょうせん)する。
To try a new dish.
マラソンに挑戦(ちょうせん)してみたい。
I'd like to try running a marathon.
今年(ことし)こそ資格(しかく)試験(しけん)に挑戦(ちょうせん)しようと決(き)めた。
I decided that this year I would take on the certification exam.

A very common and positive word emphasizing courage and proactive effort. In sense 1, the person challenging is typically the underdog or the one initiating the contest. In sense 2, it implies a spirit of adventure and willingness to push boundaries.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 挑戦(ちょうせん)状(じょう): a letter of challenge
  • 挑戦(ちょうせん)者(しゃ): challenger
  • 挑戦(ちょうせん)的(てき)な: challenging, provocative
  • 新(あたら)しいことに挑戦(ちょうせん)する: to try something new
  • 難題(なんだい)に挑戦(ちょうせん)する: to tackle a difficult problem

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 試(ため)す: to try/test — more neutral, less implication of difficulty
  • 立(た)ち向(む)かう: to stand up to, to confront — emphasizes opposition more than personal growth
  • トライする: to try — more casual, often used for sports

USAGE:
Frequently used in motivational contexts, sports commentary, and self-improvement talk. The noun form 挑戦(ちょうせん) is also extremely common on its own.
