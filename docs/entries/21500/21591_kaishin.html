1. reform; renewal; making things new
The act of fundamentally changing and renewing systems, institutions, or practices. Most famously associated with the Taika Reform (大化(たいか)の改新(かいしん)) of 645 CE, one of the most important events in early Japanese history.
制度(せいど)の改新(かいしん)が必要(ひつよう)だ。
Reform of the system is necessary.
大化(たいか)の改新(かいしん)は日本(にほん)の歴史(れきし)の転換点(てんかんてん)だった。
The Taika Reform was a turning point in Japanese history.
組織(そしき)を改新(かいしん)して、時代(じだい)に合(あ)った形(かたち)にする必要(ひつよう)がある。
We need to reform the organization and bring it into a form suited to the times.

Compound of 改(かい) (change, reform) and 新(しん) (new). Most strongly associated with 大化(たいか)の改新(かいしん) (Taika Reform, 645 CE), which centralized political power under the emperor and is a key topic in Japanese history education.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 大化(たいか)の改新(かいしん): the Taika Reform (645 CE)
  • 制度(せいど)の改新(かいしん): institutional reform
  • 改新(かいしん)を行(おこな)う: to carry out reform

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 改革(かいかく): reform — the most common modern word for reform, used broadly
  • 刷新(さっしん): renovation, overhaul — implies sweeping, dramatic renewal
  • 革新(かくしん): innovation, reform — emphasizes radical or progressive change
  • 維新(いしん): restoration, revolution — used historically (明治(めいじ)維新(いしん), Meiji Restoration)
