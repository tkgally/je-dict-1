1. to enjoy (as a pastime); to have refined taste in; to dabble in
To enjoy or practice something as a hobby or cultivated pursuit, often implying modest skill. Frequently used when humbly describing one's own hobbies.
酒(さけ)をたしなむ。
To enjoy drinking (in moderation).
若(わか)い頃(ころ)、茶道(さどう)を少(すこ)したしなんでいた。
When I was young, I dabbled a little in tea ceremony.
父(ちち)は囲碁(いご)をたしなむ人(ひと)で、週末(しゅうまつ)はいつも碁(ご)を打(う)っている。
My father is someone who enjoys go, and he always plays on weekends.
2. to be well-mannered; to exercise self-restraint; to be proper
To behave in a restrained, dignified, and socially appropriate manner. This sense often appears in the form たしなみ (noun).
身(み)だしなみに気(き)をつける。
To pay attention to one's grooming.
社会人(しゃかいじん)としてのたしなみを持(も)つ。
To have the proper decorum expected of a working adult.
大人(おとな)のたしなみとして、最低限(さいていげん)のマナーは身(み)につけておきたい。
As a matter of adult decorum, I want to have at least basic manners.

An elegant, somewhat literary verb with two related but distinct senses. In sense 1, it conveys modest enjoyment of a cultivated pursuit — using たしなむ to describe one's hobbies is more humble than saying 趣味(しゅみ) (hobby). In sense 2, it relates to propriety and self-discipline.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 酒(さけ)をたしなむ: to drink (moderately/with refinement)
  • 茶道(さどう)をたしなむ: to practice tea ceremony (as a hobby)
  • 俳句(はいく)をたしなむ: to enjoy composing haiku
  • 身(み)だしなみ: personal grooming/appearance
  • 大人(おとな)のたしなみ: adult decorum/refinement

KANJI:
Can be written as 嗜(たしな)む, but the kanji is uncommon and hiragana is more typical.

REGISTER:
Slightly literary and refined. Using this verb to describe one's hobbies implies modesty and cultivation.
