1. to begin to move; to start moving
To start physical movement after being still. Can refer to machines, vehicles, people, or animals beginning motion.
電車(でんしゃ)がゆっくりと動(うご)き始(はじ)めた。
The train slowly began to move.
赤(あか)ちゃんが自分(じぶん)で動(うご)き始(はじ)める時期(じき)だ。
It's the age when babies start moving on their own.
修理(しゅうり)が終(お)わって、やっとエンジンが動(うご)き始(はじ)めた。
After the repair was finished, the engine finally started running.
2. to start to take action; to get underway
Figuratively, for a project, plan, organization, or situation to begin progressing or developing.
プロジェクトが動(うご)き始(はじ)めた。
The project started to get underway.
世論(せろん)が動(うご)き始(はじ)めている。
Public opinion is starting to shift.
新(あたら)しい法律(ほうりつ)の施行(しこう)に向(む)けて、行政(ぎょうせい)が動(うご)き始(はじ)めた。
The government has started taking action toward implementing the new law.

A compound verb formed from 動(うご)く (to move) and 始(はじ)める (to begin). The ~始(はじ)める auxiliary pattern is very productive in Japanese, attaching to the stem of other verbs to express the beginning of an action.

TRANSITIVITY: Intransitive

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ゆっくりと動(うご)き始(はじ)める: to slowly begin to move
  • やっと動(うご)き始(はじ)める: to finally start moving
  • 本格的(ほんかくてき)に動(うご)き始(はじ)める: to start moving in earnest

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 動(うご)き出(だ)す: to start moving — similar meaning, more sudden or spontaneous nuance
  • 始動(しどう)する: to start up — more formal, often for machines or systems
