1. to tell a lie; to lie; to say something untrue
The standard expression for telling a deliberate falsehood. Uses the verb つく in the sense of uttering or producing.
嘘(うそ)をついてはいけない。
You must not tell lies.
彼(かれ)は嘘(うそ)をついているに違(ちが)いない。
He must be lying.
子(こ)どもの頃(ころ)、よく嘘(うそ)をついて叱(しか)られた。
When I was a child, I often got scolded for telling lies.
嘘(うそ)をつくのは簡単(かんたん)だが、信頼(しんらい)を取(と)り戻(もど)すのは難(むずか)しい。
Telling a lie is easy, but regaining trust is difficult.

The most basic and common way to say "to lie" in Japanese. The verb つく here is a godan verb meaning "to utter" (different from 付(つ)く "to attach" or 着(つ)く "to arrive"). The expression is a fixed collocation — 嘘(うそ) always takes を and つく in this meaning.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 嘘(うそ)をつく: to tell a lie
  • 嘘(うそ)をつき通(とお)す: to keep lying, to maintain a lie
  • 大(おお)嘘(うそ)をつく: to tell a big lie
  • 嘘(うそ)つき: liar (noun)

RELATED EXPRESSIONS:

  • 嘘(うそ)も方便(ほうべん): the end justifies the means (proverb — even lies can be expedient)
  • 嘘(うそ)みたい: unbelievable, too good to be true
  • 嘘(うそ)でしょ: you're kidding, no way
