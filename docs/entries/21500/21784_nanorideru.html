1. to come forward; to volunteer; to step forward
To present oneself voluntarily to take on a task, responsibility, or role.
ボランティアに名乗(なの)り出(で)た。
I volunteered.
リーダーに名乗(なの)り出(で)る人(ひと)がいなかった。
There was no one who stepped forward to be the leader.
危険(きけん)な任務(にんむ)だと分(わ)かっていたが、彼(かれ)は自(みずか)ら名乗(なの)り出(で)た。
He knew it was a dangerous mission, but he came forward of his own accord.
2. to identify oneself; to reveal one's identity
To announce who one is, especially when sought or when identity was previously unknown.
犯人(はんにん)が名乗(なの)り出(で)た。
The culprit turned himself in.
目撃者(もくげきしゃ)が警察(けいさつ)に名乗(なの)り出(で)た。
A witness came forward to the police.
匿名(とくめい)で寄付(きふ)をしていた人物(じんぶつ)が、ついに名乗(なの)り出(で)て正体(しょうたい)を明(あ)かした。
The person who had been donating anonymously finally came forward and revealed their identity.

A compound verb combining 名乗(なの)る (to give one's name, to call oneself) and 出(で)る (to come out). The 出(で)る component adds the sense of stepping forward from a group or emerging publicly.

USAGE:

  • Often used with に to indicate the role or purpose: ～に名乗(なの)り出(で)る (to volunteer for ~)
  • 自(みずか)ら名乗(なの)り出(で)る emphasizes doing so of one's own initiative
  • In sense 2, often involves revealing oneself to authorities or the public

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ボランティアに名乗(なの)り出(で)る: to volunteer
  • 自(みずか)ら名乗(なの)り出(で)る: to come forward of one's own accord
  • 警察(けいさつ)に名乗(なの)り出(で)る: to turn oneself in to the police
  • 候補(こうほ)に名乗(なの)り出(で)る: to put oneself forward as a candidate

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 志願(しがん)する: to volunteer, apply — more formal; often used for military or official contexts
  • 立候補(りっこうほ)する: to run as a candidate — specifically for elections
  • 申(もう)し出(で)る: to offer, propose — broader; can be used for requests and suggestions
  • 自首(じしゅ)する: to turn oneself in — specifically to police; stronger than 名乗(なの)り出(で)る in legal contexts
