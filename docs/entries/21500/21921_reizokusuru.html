1. to be subservient to; to be enslaved; to be in servitude
to be in a position of subjugation or servitude; to be subordinate to someone or something in a way that implies lack of freedom or autonomy
権力(けんりょく)に隷属(れいぞく)する。
To be subservient to power.
植民地(しょくみんち)の人々(ひとびと)は長(なが)い間(あいだ)隷属(れいぞく)状態(じょうたい)に置(お)かれた。
The people of the colony were kept in a state of servitude for a long time.
誰(だれ)にも隷属(れいぞく)せず、自由(じゆう)に生(い)きたい。
I want to live freely, subservient to no one.
技術(ぎじゅつ)に隷属(れいぞく)するのではなく、技術(ぎじゅつ)を使(つか)いこなすべきだ。
Rather than being enslaved by technology, we should master it.

A strong, formal word describing a state of subjugation, servitude, or slavery. Always carries a strongly negative connotation, implying the loss of freedom and dignity. Used in historical, political, philosophical, and social commentary contexts. Can also be used metaphorically about being enslaved by abstract things like money, technology, or power.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ～に隷属(れいぞく)する: to be subservient to ~
  • 隷属(れいぞく)状態(じょうたい): state of servitude
  • 隷属(れいぞく)関係(かんけい): relationship of subservience
  • 隷属(れいぞく)から解放(かいほう)される: to be liberated from servitude
  • 隷属(れいぞく)を強(し)いる: to impose servitude

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 従属(じゅうぞく)する: to be subordinate — less harsh; describes a hierarchical relationship without implying oppression
  • 服従(ふくじゅう)する: to obey, to submit — focuses on the act of following orders
  • 支配(しはい)される: to be ruled, to be dominated — describes being under someone's control

ETYMOLOGY:
Composed of 隷(れい) (slave, servant) + 属(ぞく) (belong to). The first character originally referred to a class of servants or slaves.
