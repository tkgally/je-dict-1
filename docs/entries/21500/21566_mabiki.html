1. thinning out; culling; removing some to give others room to grow
In agriculture and gardening, the process of removing some seedlings or plants so the remaining ones have space to grow properly.
苗(なえ)を間引(まび)きする。
To thin out seedlings.
大根(だいこん)の芽(め)が出(で)たら、間引(まび)きをして間隔(かんかく)をあける。
When radish sprouts appear, thin them out to create spacing.
間引(まび)きした芽(め)はサラダにして食(た)べられる。
The thinned sprouts can be eaten in a salad.
2. reducing frequency; cutting service; operating at reduced capacity
Reducing the number or frequency of something, such as train services or production runs.
台風(たいふう)の影響(えいきょう)で間引(まび)き運転(うんてん)になった。
Service was reduced due to the typhoon.
朝(あさ)のラッシュ時(じ)に間引(まび)き運転(うんてん)が行(おこな)われた。
Reduced service was operated during the morning rush.
人手不足(ひとでぶそく)のため、バスの便数(びんすう)が間引(まび)きされている。
Due to labor shortages, the number of bus services has been reduced.

Compound of 間(ま) (space, interval) and 引(ひ)く (to pull). Literally "pulling out to create space."

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 間引(まび)き運転(うんてん): reduced service (trains, buses)
  • 間引(まび)きする: to thin out
  • 間引(まび)き菜(な): thinned seedlings (used in cooking)
  • 苗(なえ)を間引(まび)く: to thin out seedlings

USAGE:
The verb form is 間引(まび)く (godan verb). In the agriculture sense, it's a normal and positive gardening practice. In the transportation sense, 間引(まび)き運転(うんてん) is a common phrase heard in announcements during weather disruptions or labor shortages.

HISTORICAL NOTE:
Historically, 間引(まび)き also referred to the tragic practice of infanticide in times of famine in pre-modern Japan, literally "thinning" a family to survive. This usage is now found only in historical contexts.
