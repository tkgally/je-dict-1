1. to get caught up in; to be involved in; to be dragged into
Describes being involuntarily drawn into a situation, conflict, or incident. The passive form of 巻(ま)き込(こ)む (to involve, to drag in), emphasizing the lack of choice on the subject's part.
事故(じこ)に巻(ま)き込(こ)まれた。
I got caught up in an accident.
友人(ゆうじん)のけんかに巻(ま)き込(こ)まれてしまった。
I got dragged into my friend's quarrel.
無関係(むかんけい)の市民(しみん)が紛争(ふんそう)に巻(ま)き込(こ)まれることがあってはならない。
Uninvolved civilians must never be caught up in a conflict.

The passive form of 巻(ま)き込(こ)む (to involve, to drag in, to engulf). While 巻(ま)き込(こ)む focuses on the agent doing the involving, 巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる emphasizes the victim's perspective — being pulled into something against one's will.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 事件(じけん)に巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる: to get caught up in an incident
  • 事故(じこ)に巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる: to be involved in an accident
  • 争(あらそ)いに巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる: to get dragged into a dispute
  • 犯罪(はんざい)に巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる: to get mixed up in a crime
  • トラブルに巻(ま)き込(こ)まれる: to get caught up in trouble

USAGE:
Almost always used with the particle に to mark the situation one is dragged into. Carries a strong implication that the involvement was involuntary and usually unwelcome.

ACTIVE FORM:

巻(ま)き込(こ)む means "to involve" or "to drag in" from the agent's perspective:

  • 周(まわ)りを巻(ま)き込(こ)む: to involve those around you
  • 他人(たにん)を巻(ま)き込(こ)むな: don't drag others into this
