1. forcing someone to do something; coercion; compulsion
The act of pressuring or compelling someone to do something against their will.
無理強(むりじ)いはしないでください。
Please don't force me.
お酒(さけ)の無理強(むりじ)いはよくない。
Forcing someone to drink is not good.
子(こ)どもに勉強(べんきょう)を無理強(むりじ)いしても逆効果(ぎゃくこうか)になることがある。
Forcing a child to study can sometimes have the opposite effect.

Composed of 無理(むり) (unreasonable, impossible) + 強(し)い (forcing). Describes the act of pressuring someone into doing something they don't want to do.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 無理強(むりじ)いする: to force; to coerce
  • 無理強(むりじ)いはしない: to not force (someone)
  • お酒(さけ)の無理強(むりじ)い: forcing someone to drink alcohol
  • 無理強(むりじ)いされる: to be forced/coerced

USAGE:
Often appears in contexts about social pressure, such as drinking culture, eating, or work demands. The する form (無理強(むりじ)いする) is commonly used as a verb meaning 'to force.' The word carries a clearly negative connotation—it always implies that the pressure is unwelcome.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 強制(きょうせい): compulsion — more formal and official; used for legal or institutional contexts
  • 強要(きょうよう): coercion, demand — stronger and more aggressive than 無理強(むりじ)い
  • 押(お)し付(つ)ける: to force upon — more casual; focuses on imposing things (opinions, tasks) on others
