1. to dry up; to be parched
For a body of water, land, or surface to completely dry out due to heat or lack of rain.
池(いけ)が干上(ひあ)がった。
The pond dried up.
日照(ひで)りが続(つづ)いて田(た)んぼが干上(ひあ)がってしまった。
The rice paddies dried up after the continued drought.
猛暑(もうしょ)で川(かわ)の水(みず)がすっかり干上(ひあ)がり、魚(さかな)が死(し)んでしまった。
The river water completely dried up in the intense heat, and the fish died.
2. to be left high and dry; to lose one's livelihood
Figuratively, to lose one's source of income or means of living. Often used about businesses or individuals running out of resources.
仕事(しごと)がなくなって干上(ひあ)がる。
To be left high and dry after losing work.
客(きゃく)が来(こ)なくなったら、この店(みせ)は干上(ひあ)がってしまう。
If customers stop coming, this shop will be ruined.
スポンサーが撤退(てったい)したら、あのチームは干上(ひあ)がってしまうだろう。
If the sponsors pull out, that team will probably be left high and dry.

USAGE:
Literally describes water evaporating completely, leaving a surface dry. Figuratively, describes losing one's livelihood or source of income. The figurative sense is common in everyday speech.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 池(いけ)が干上(ひあ)がる (the pond dries up)
  • 川(かわ)が干上(ひあ)がる (the river dries up)
  • 干上(ひあ)がってしまう (to end up dried out / ruined)
