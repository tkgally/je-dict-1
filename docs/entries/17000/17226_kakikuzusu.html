1. to write in cursive; to write loosely or roughly
To write characters in a simplified, flowing style rather than in their standard printed form. Can refer to intentional calligraphic cursive or to careless, hard-to-read handwriting.
漢字(かんじ)を書(か)き崩(くず)して書(か)く。
To write kanji in cursive style.
書(か)き崩(くず)した字(じ)は読(よ)みにくい。
Loosely written characters are hard to read.
祖父(そふ)の手紙(てがみ)は書(か)き崩(くず)してあって、何(なに)が書(か)いてあるか分(わ)からなかった。
My grandfather's letter was written in such a loose hand that I couldn't tell what it said.

WORD FORMATION:
書(か)く (to write) + 崩(くず)す (to break down, to simplify). Literally 'to break down the writing,' meaning to simplify character strokes.

USAGE:
In calligraphy, 書(か)き崩(くず)す can be a positive term describing artistic cursive (草書(そうしょ) or 行書(ぎょうしょ) style). In everyday contexts, it often describes handwriting that is too loose or messy to read easily.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 字(じ)を書(か)き崩(くず)す (to write characters loosely)
  • 書(か)き崩(くず)した文字(もじ) (loosely written characters)
  • 達筆(たっぴつ)で書(か)き崩(くず)してある (written in skilled cursive)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 崩(くず)し字(じ): cursive characters — the noun form for characters written in simplified style
