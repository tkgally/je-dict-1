1. deep-frying without batter or coating
A cooking method where ingredients are deep-fried directly in oil without any flour, breadcrumbs, or batter. Used as a suru verb.
野菜(やさい)を素揚(すあ)げにする。
To deep-fry the vegetables without batter.
茄子(なす)の素揚(すあ)げはカレーによく合(あ)う。
Deep-fried eggplant without batter goes well with curry.
素揚(すあ)げにすると素材(そざい)の味(あじ)がそのまま楽(たの)しめる。
Deep-frying without batter lets you enjoy the natural flavor of the ingredients.

USAGE:
A cooking term commonly found in recipes. The 素(す) prefix means "plain" or "without additions," contrasting with 天(てん)ぷら (tempura, battered and fried) or 唐揚(からあ)げ (coated with starch or flour). Commonly used for vegetables like 茄子(なす) (eggplant), 蓮根(れんこん) (lotus root), and 南瓜(かぼちゃ) (pumpkin).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 素揚(すあ)げにする (to deep-fry without coating)
  • 素揚(すあ)げした野菜(やさい) (vegetables fried without batter)
  • 素揚(すあ)げにして塩(しお)で食(た)べる (to deep-fry plain and eat with salt)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 天(てん)ぷら: tempura — battered and deep-fried
  • 唐揚(からあ)げ: deep-fried with starch coating — typically for chicken
