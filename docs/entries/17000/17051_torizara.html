取(と)り皿(ざら)

とりざら
noun
small individual plate; serving plate
1. small individual plate; serving plate
A small plate used to take portions from shared dishes when eating with others. Essential in Japanese dining culture where food is often served communally.
取(と)り皿(ざら)をもう一(いち)枚(まい)ください。
Could I have one more small plate, please?
大皿(おおざら)の料理(りょうり)を取(と)り皿(ざら)に取(と)り分(わ)けた。
I portioned the food from the large platter onto individual plates.
居酒屋(いざかや)では料理(りょうり)をみんなで分(わ)けるので、取(と)り皿(ざら)が何枚(なんまい)も必要(ひつよう)になる。
At izakaya, everyone shares dishes, so you need many individual plates.

CULTURE:
Reflects the Japanese communal dining style where dishes are placed in the center of the table and diners take portions onto their own small plates. At restaurants, it is perfectly normal to ask the staff for extra 取(と)り皿(ざら). Different from a personal plate (自分(じぶん)の皿(さら)) in Western-style individual plating.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 取(と)り皿(ざら)を用意(ようい)する (to prepare individual plates)
  • 取(と)り皿(ざら)に取(と)る (to put food on one's plate)
  • 取(と)り皿(ざら)を下(さ)げる (to clear the individual plates)
