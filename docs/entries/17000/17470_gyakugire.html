1. getting angry at the person who has a right to be upset; lashing out when one is at fault
When someone who is in the wrong — the one who should be apologizing — instead gets angry at the person who confronts or criticizes them. A reversal of expected emotional roles.
注意(ちゅうい)したら逆(ぎゃく)ギレされた。
When I warned them, they got angry at me instead.
遅刻(ちこく)した本人(ほんにん)が逆(ぎゃく)ギレするなんて信(しん)じられない。
I can't believe the person who was late is the one getting angry.
ミスを指摘(してき)しただけなのに逆(ぎゃく)ギレされて、こちらが悪(わる)いみたいな空気(くうき)になった。
I only pointed out a mistake, but they lashed out at me, and the atmosphere made it seem like I was the one in the wrong.

USAGE:
A colloquial word that became widely used in the 2000s. Written with a mix of kanji and katakana: 逆(ぎゃく)ギレ. The ギレ comes from キレる (to snap, to lose one's temper). Always describes an unreasonable emotional reaction — the person who should be apologizing instead attacks.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 逆(ぎゃく)ギレする (to lash out when at fault)
  • 逆(ぎゃく)ギレされる (to be on the receiving end)
  • 逆(ぎゃく)ギレして怒鳴(どな)る (to lash out and yell)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • キレる: to snap, to lose one's temper — general term for getting angry suddenly
  • 八(や)つ当(あ)たり: taking out one's anger on someone — misdirecting anger at an innocent party
