命令形(めいれいけい)

めいれいけい
noun
imperative form; command form
1. imperative form; command form
In grammar, the verb form used to give direct commands or orders. In Japanese, formed by changing the verb ending (e.g., 食(た)べろ, 行(い)け).
命令形(めいれいけい)は強(つよ)い口調(くちょう)になる。
The imperative form sounds forceful.
「行(い)く」の命令形(めいれいけい)は「行(い)け」です。
The imperative form of 'iku' is 'ike.'
日本語(にほんご)の命令形(めいれいけい)は男性(だんせい)が使(つか)うことが多(おお)く、女性(じょせい)は丁寧(ていねい)な表現(ひょうげん)を好(この)む。
The Japanese imperative form is more commonly used by men, while women tend to prefer polite expressions.

A grammar term composed of 命令(めいれい) (command, order) and 形(けい) (form). Refers to the conjugated form of Japanese verbs used for direct commands.

IMPERATIVE FORM BY VERB TYPE:

  • 一段(いちだん) verbs: stem + ろ (食(た)べる → 食(た)べろ)
  • 五段(ごだん) verbs: え-row ending (行(い)く → 行(い)け, 読(よ)む → 読(よ)め)
  • する → しろ
  • 来(く)る → 来(こ)い

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 命令形(めいれいけい)に変(か)える: to change into the imperative form
  • 命令形(めいれいけい)で言(い)う: to say in the imperative form
  • 命令形(めいれいけい)の活用(かつよう): conjugation of the imperative form

USAGE:
The bare imperative is considered rough and masculine in Japanese. It appears in sports cheering, emergency situations, and casual male speech. In formal or polite contexts, ～てください or ～なさい is used instead.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 肯定形(こうていけい): affirmative form
  • 否定形(ひていけい): negative form
  • 活用形(かつようけい): conjugated form — the general term for all verb forms
  • 命令(めいれい): command, order — the base noun
