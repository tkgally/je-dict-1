1. stirring; slight movement of the body; fidgeting
A small, often unconscious movement of the body, such as shifting one's weight, squirming, or fidgeting. Most commonly used in the negative — 身じろぎもしない or 身じろぎもせず — to describe someone remaining perfectly still.
身(み)じろぎもしない。
Not moving a muscle.
猫(ねこ)は身(み)じろぎもせずに獲物(えもの)を見(み)つめていた。
The cat watched its prey without stirring at all.
緊張(きんちょう)のあまり、身(み)じろぎ一(ひと)つできなかった。
I was so nervous that I couldn't move a muscle.

A literary and descriptive word almost always encountered in negative constructions. The pattern 身(み)じろぎもしない (not stirring at all) is a common literary device to convey tension, concentration, or fear.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 身(み)じろぎもしない: not moving at all; perfectly still
  • 身(み)じろぎもせず: without stirring (connective form)
  • 身(み)じろぎ一(ひと)つしない: not making the slightest movement

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 微動(びどう)だにしない: not budging an inch — very similar in meaning and usage
  • じっとする: to stay still; to keep motionless — more everyday
  • 動(うご)かない: not moving — plain, neutral description

ETYMOLOGY:
From 身(み) (body) and じろぐ (to stir, to shift), an older verb meaning to move slightly. The verb じろぐ itself is rarely used independently in modern Japanese.
