喉(のど)が渇(かわ)く

のどがかわく
expression
to be thirsty; to get thirsty
1. to be thirsty; to get thirsty; to have a dry throat
The standard expression for feeling thirsty. Literally means 'the throat dries out.' This is the natural way to express thirst in Japanese, rather than using a single adjective.
喉(のど)が渇(かわ)いた。
I'm thirsty.
運動(うんどう)した後(あと)は喉(のど)が渇(かわ)く。
I get thirsty after exercising.
喉(のど)が渇(かわ)いたので、コンビニで冷(つめ)たいお茶(ちゃ)を買(か)った。
I was thirsty, so I bought a cold tea at the convenience store.

The standard Japanese expression for being thirsty. Literally '喉(のど) (throat) が (subject marker) 渇(かわ)く (to dry out).' Japanese expresses thirst through this phrase rather than a single adjective. The past form 喉(のど)が渇(かわ)いた is very common in conversation to say 'I'm thirsty.'

USAGE:
The kanji 渇(かわ)く means to become parched or dry from lack of moisture. Note the difference from 乾(かわ)く (to dry, to become dry), which is used for objects like laundry.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 喉(のど)が渇(かわ)いた: I'm thirsty (past/resultative)
  • 喉(のど)がカラカラに渇(かわ)く: to be extremely thirsty (throat bone-dry)
  • 喉(のど)の渇(かわ)きを潤(うるお)す: to quench one's thirst

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • お腹(なか)が空(す)く: to be hungry — the parallel expression for hunger
  • 喉(のど)が乾燥(かんそう)する: for the throat to be dry — medical/physical dryness, not thirst
