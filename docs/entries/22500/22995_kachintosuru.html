1. to get irritated; to feel a flash of anger; to be offended
To feel a sudden, sharp pang of irritation or anger in response to something someone said or did. The reaction is typically immediate and involuntary.
その一言(ひとこと)にカチンときた。
That one remark really got to me.
上司(じょうし)の態度(たいど)にカチンとしたが、我慢(がまん)した。
I got annoyed at my boss's attitude, but held it in.
「まだそんなこともできないの」と言(い)われて、カチンときて言(い)い返(かえ)してしまった。
When they said 'You still can't do something like that?', I got angry and snapped back.

An onomatopoeic expression evoking the sharp 'click' sound of something hard being struck. Describes a sudden spike of irritation, not a slow build-up. The feeling is typically triggered by a specific remark or behavior.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • カチンとくる: to get ticked off (very common variant using くる instead of する)
  • カチンときた: got irritated (past tense of カチンとくる)
  • 一言(ひとこと)にカチンとする: to be set off by a single remark

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • むかつく: to feel pissed off — stronger, more vulgar; describes sustained irritation or nausea
  • いらっとする: to feel a flash of irritation — very similar but slightly milder
  • かちんとくる: same meaning, alternative verb pattern (くる instead of する); more common in casual speech

REGISTER:
Casual and conversational. Not used in formal writing. The variant カチンとくる is more natural in everyday speech than カチンとする.
