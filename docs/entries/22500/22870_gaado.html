1. guard; defense; protection
The act of defending or protecting, especially in sports like boxing or basketball. Also used figuratively for being emotionally guarded.
ガードを固(かた)める。
To strengthen one's guard.
彼(かれ)はガードが堅(かた)くて隙(すき)がない。
His guard is solid with no openings.
相手(あいて)のパンチをガードする。
To guard against the opponent's punch.
2. railway overpass; elevated railway bridge over a road
A bridge or viaduct where railway tracks pass over a road. Unique to Japanese usage of the English word.
ガード下(した)の居酒屋(いざかや)に入(はい)った。
We went into an izakaya under the railway overpass.
線路(せんろ)のガードをくぐって向(む)こう側(がわ)に出(で)た。
We passed under the railway overpass and came out on the other side.
このガード下(した)は背(せ)の高(たか)いトラックは通(とお)れない。
Tall trucks can't pass under this railway overpass.

From English "guard." Has developed a meaning unique to Japanese: a railway overpass or elevated track bridge over a road.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ガードが堅(かた)い: to have a tight guard; to be guarded (also figuratively: hard to approach emotionally)
  • ガードを固(かた)める: to strengthen one's guard
  • ガードする: to guard; to defend
  • ガード下(した): under the railway overpass (a common location for small shops and bars)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 防御(ぼうぎょ): defense — the native Japanese word for defense or protection
  • 高架(こうか): elevated structure — a more formal term for an overpass or viaduct
  • 陸橋(りっきょう): overpass, footbridge — can refer to pedestrian overpasses as well
