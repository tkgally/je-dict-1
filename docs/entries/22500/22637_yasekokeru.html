1. to become gaunt; to be emaciated; to be skin and bones
To become extremely and unhealthily thin, with bones and hollow features visibly apparent. Carries a strongly negative connotation, implying illness, neglect, or severe hardship.
病気(びょうき)で痩(や)せこけてしまった。
He became emaciated from illness.
痩(や)せこけた猫(ねこ)が道端(みちばた)にいた。
There was a gaunt cat on the roadside.
久(ひさ)しぶりに会(あ)った祖父(そふ)は痩(や)せこけていて驚(おどろ)いた。
I was shocked to see my grandfather looking so gaunt when I met him after a long time.

A compound verb formed from 痩(や)せる (to lose weight, to become thin) and こける (to become hollow, to be sunken). Describes an extreme degree of thinness — not just slim but unhealthily thin, with visible bones and hollow features. Carries a strongly negative connotation.

USAGE:
Most often used in the past tense or ている form to describe someone's current emaciated state. The た-form (痩(や)せこけた) frequently modifies nouns as an adjective.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 痩(や)せこけた体(からだ): an emaciated body
  • 痩(や)せこけた顔(かお): a gaunt face
  • 痩(や)せこけた犬(いぬ): a scrawny dog
  • 病気(びょうき)で痩(や)せこける: to waste away from illness

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 痩(や)せる: to lose weight, to become thin — neutral, not necessarily negative
  • 痩(や)せ細(ほそ)る: to become thin and frail — similar meaning but slightly less extreme
  • 衰弱(すいじゃく)する: to weaken, to become debilitated — focuses on loss of strength rather than appearance
