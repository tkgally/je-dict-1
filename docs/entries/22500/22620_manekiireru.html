1. to invite in; to usher in; to welcome inside
To welcome or guide someone into an enclosed space such as a room, home, or building, often as a hospitable or formal gesture.
客(きゃく)を部屋(へや)に招(まね)き入(い)れた。
I invited the guest into the room.
彼(かれ)は笑顔(えがお)で私(わたし)たちを家(いえ)に招(まね)き入(い)れた。
He welcomed us into his home with a smile.
社長(しゃちょう)は来客(らいきゃく)を応接室(おうせつしつ)に招(まね)き入(い)れた。
The company president ushered the visitors into the reception room.
2. to bring about (something undesirable); to invite (trouble, disaster)
Used figuratively to describe causing or attracting an unwanted outcome, such as failure, confusion, or criticism, through one's actions or negligence.
油断(ゆだん)が失敗(しっぱい)を招(まね)き入(い)れた。
Carelessness invited failure.
その決定(けってい)は大(おお)きな混乱(こんらん)を招(まね)き入(い)れることになった。
That decision ended up bringing about great confusion.
不用意(ふようい)な発言(はつげん)が批判(ひはん)を招(まね)き入(い)れた。
A careless remark invited criticism.

A compound verb formed from 招(まね)く (to invite, to beckon) and 入(い)れる (to put in, to let in). Sense 1 is the literal meaning of welcoming someone into a space. Sense 2 is figurative, meaning to bring about or invite an undesirable outcome.

USAGE:

  • Sense 1: Person を Place に招(まね)き入(い)れる
  • Sense 2: Consequence を招(まね)き入(い)れる (the figurative sense often drops the に phrase)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 客(きゃく)を招(まね)き入(い)れる: to invite a guest in
  • 家(いえ)に招(まね)き入(い)れる: to welcome into one's home
  • 災(わざわ)いを招(まね)き入(い)れる: to invite disaster

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 迎(むか)え入(い)れる: to receive, to welcome in — more formal and warmer in tone
  • 招(まね)く: to invite — broader meaning; does not necessarily imply entering a space
  • 通(とお)す: to show in, to let through — focuses on guiding someone inside
