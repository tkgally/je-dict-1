1. to reserve; to book; to make a reservation
To arrange in advance to secure a place, service, or item, such as a hotel room, restaurant table, or appointment.
ホテルを予約(よやく)した。
I booked a hotel.
来週(らいしゅう)の金曜日(きんようび)に歯医者(はいしゃ)を予約(よやく)してある。
I have a dentist appointment booked for next Friday.
人気(にんき)のレストランなので、一(いっ)か月(げつ)前(まえ)から予約(よやく)しないと入(はい)れない。
It's such a popular restaurant that you can't get in unless you reserve a month in advance.

An essential everyday word for making reservations and appointments. Used for hotels, restaurants, doctor visits, tickets, and any service that requires advance booking.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 予約(よやく)を取(と)る: to make a reservation
  • 予約(よやく)を入(い)れる: to put in a reservation
  • 予約(よやく)を取(と)り消(け)す: to cancel a reservation
  • 予約(よやく)済(ず)み: reserved; already booked
  • 予約(よやく)制(せい): reservation-only (system)
  • 完全(かんぜん)予約(よやく)制(せい): by reservation only

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 申(もう)し込(こ)む: to apply, sign up — used when registering for events, memberships, or services rather than reserving a spot
  • 取(と)り置(お)く: to set aside, hold — used when asking a store to hold an item for you

USAGE:
Both 予約(よやく)する and 予約(よやく)を取(と)る are natural. The noun 予約(よやく) is also used alone, as in 予約(よやく)なしで (without a reservation}). Online booking is often called ネット{予約(よやく).
