1. to be angular; to be square-shaped
To have sharp corners or edges rather than being rounded.
角張(かくば)った顔(かお)をしている。
He has an angular face.
この箱(はこ)は角張(かくば)っていて持(も)ちにくい。
This box is angular and hard to carry.
角張(かくば)ったデザインよりも丸(まる)みのある形(かたち)の方(ほう)が人気(にんき)がある。
Rounded shapes are more popular than angular designs.
2. to be stiff; to be overly formal
To behave in a rigid, formal manner; to be uptight.
そんなに角張(かくば)らなくていいよ。
You don't need to be so stiff about it.
角張(かくば)った態度(たいど)をやめてリラックスしよう。
Let's stop being so formal and relax.
初対面(しょたいめん)だからといって角張(かくば)る必要(ひつよう)はないよ。
Just because it's our first meeting doesn't mean you need to be so stiff.

Derived from 角(かく) (corner, angle). In the physical sense, it describes objects or facial features with sharp, square edges. In the figurative sense, it describes a person whose behavior or manner is rigid and overly formal, as if they have 'sharp edges' in their personality. Most commonly encountered in the た-form as a modifier: 角張(かくば)った.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 角張(かくば)った顔(かお): an angular face
  • 角張(かくば)った形(かたち): a square shape
  • 角張(かくば)った態度(たいど): a stiff attitude
  • 角張(かくば)った字(じ): angular handwriting

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 四角(しかく)い: square; rectangular — a straightforward adjective for square shapes without the figurative meaning
  • 堅苦(かたくる)しい: stiff; formal — an adjective specifically for overly formal behavior or atmosphere
  • 丸(まる)い: round — the physical opposite; also figuratively means 'mellow' or 'easygoing'
