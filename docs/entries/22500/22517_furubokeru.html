1. to look old and worn; to become shabby with age
For objects or buildings to show visible signs of age and deterioration.
古(ふる)ぼけた本(ほん)が並(なら)んでいる。
Old, worn books are lined up.
この建物(たてもの)はすっかり古(ふる)ぼけてしまった。
This building has become completely run-down.
古(ふる)ぼけた写真(しゃしん)の中(なか)に、若(わか)い頃(ころ)の祖母(そぼ)の姿(すがた)が写(うつ)っていた。
In the faded old photograph, my grandmother's figure from her younger days was captured.

A descriptive verb that conveys the appearance of age and wear on physical objects. Composed of 古(ふる)い (old) and ぼける (to become blurred/faded). Most often appears in the past participle form 古(ふる)ぼけた as a modifier before nouns. It can carry a nostalgic or atmospheric tone, or simply describe decay.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 古(ふる)ぼけた家(いえ): a run-down house
  • 古(ふる)ぼけた写真(しゃしん): a faded old photograph
  • 古(ふる)ぼけた本(ほん): a worn old book
  • 古(ふる)ぼけた看板(かんばん): a weathered old sign

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 古(ふる)びる: to grow old; to show age — similar meaning but slightly more neutral, less emphasis on shabbiness
  • 老朽化(ろうきゅうか)する: to deteriorate with age — a formal term used for infrastructure, buildings, and equipment
  • くたびれる: to be worn out — focuses on being tired or used up, applies to clothes and people
