1. to read too much into; to overinterpret; to overthink the meaning of
To interpret something more deeply than intended, finding hidden meanings or implications that may not actually be there.
深読(ふかよ)みしすぎだよ。
You're reading too much into it.
彼(かれ)のメールを深読(ふかよ)みして、不安(ふあん)になった。
I overinterpreted his email and got anxious.
相手(あいて)の言葉(ことば)を深読(ふかよ)みせずに、素直(すなお)に受(う)け止(と)めたほうがいい。
It's better to take the other person's words at face value rather than reading too much into them.

Describes the tendency to overanalyze words, actions, or situations and find meanings that were not intended. Very common in everyday conversation, especially about interpersonal communication.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 深読(ふかよ)みしすぎる: to read way too much into something
  • 深読(ふかよ)みしすぎ: overthinking it (casual)
  • 言葉(ことば)を深読(ふかよ)みする: to overinterpret someone's words
  • 深読(ふかよ)みは禁物(きんもつ): overthinking is counterproductive

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 考(かんが)えすぎる: to overthink — broader; covers worrying or analyzing too much about anything, not just interpreting meaning
  • 勘(かん)ぐる: to be suspicious, read motives into — implies suspicion about someone's intentions
  • 邪推(じゃすい)する: to be cynically suspicious — to assume the worst about someone's motives

NOTE:
Often used self-referentially when someone realizes they have been overthinking (深読(ふかよ)みしてたかも — 'Maybe I was reading too much into it').
