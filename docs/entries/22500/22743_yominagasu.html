1. to skim over; to read without paying close attention; to read and let pass
To read text superficially without engaging deeply with the content, or to read something and deliberately not react to it.
記事(きじ)を読(よ)み流(なが)す。
To skim through an article.
長(なが)いメールだったので読(よ)み流(なが)してしまった。
It was a long email, so I just skimmed through it.
大事(だいじ)な内容(ないよう)だから読(よ)み流(なが)さないでちゃんと読(よ)んでほしい。
It's important content, so I'd like you to read it properly and not just skim over it.

A compound verb of 読(よ)む ('to read') + 流(なが)す ('to let flow, to let pass'). The 流(なが)す component conveys the sense of letting the content 'flow by' without absorbing it. This can be intentional (deliberately not engaging) or unintentional (failing to pay attention).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 記事(きじ)を読(よ)み流(なが)す: to skim an article
  • メールを読(よ)み流(なが)す: to skim an email
  • さらっと読(よ)み流(なが)す: to casually skim through

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 斜(なな)め読(よ)み: skimming, speed reading — a noun describing the act of reading diagonally/quickly
  • 読(よ)み飛(と)ばす: to skip over (parts of text) — implies actively skipping sections entirely
  • 流(なが)し読(よ)み: skimming — a noun with the same meaning, with reversed word order
