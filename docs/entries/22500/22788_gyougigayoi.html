行儀(ぎょうぎ)が良(よ)い

ぎょうぎがよい
expression
well-mannered; well-behaved
1. well-mannered; well-behaved
Describes someone whose conduct and manners are proper and refined.
あの子(こ)は行儀(ぎょうぎ)がいい。
That child is well-behaved.
行儀(ぎょうぎ)よく座(すわ)りなさい。
Sit properly.
食事中(しょくじちゅう)は行儀(ぎょうぎ)よくするように親(おや)に言(い)われて育(そだ)った。
I was raised being told by my parents to behave properly during meals.

行儀(ぎょうぎ) means manners or conduct, particularly at the table or in social situations. The full expression 行儀(ぎょうぎ)が良(よ)い describes someone whose behavior meets social standards. Often used about children. The adverbial form 行儀(ぎょうぎ)よく (properly, with good manners) is very common.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 行儀(ぎょうぎ)よく食(た)べる: to eat with good manners
  • 行儀(ぎょうぎ)よく座(すわ)る: to sit properly
  • 行儀(ぎょうぎ)が悪(わる)い: ill-mannered, badly behaved
  • お行儀(ぎょうぎ): manners (with polite prefix, often used with children)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 礼儀正(れいぎただ)しい: polite, courteous — focuses on formal politeness and social etiquette
  • 品(ひん)がいい: elegant, refined — emphasizes class and sophistication
  • 躾(しつけ)がいい: well-disciplined — focuses on upbringing and training
  • マナーがいい: to have good manners — loanword equivalent, often used in public/social contexts
