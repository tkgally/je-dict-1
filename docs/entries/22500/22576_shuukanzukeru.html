1. to make a habit of; to habituate; to train (someone/oneself) to do something regularly
早起(はやお)きを習慣付(しゅうかんづ)ける。
To make a habit of waking up early.
子(こ)どもに手洗(てあら)いを習慣付(しゅうかんづ)けた。
I trained my child to make hand washing a habit.
毎日(まいにち)少(すこ)しずつでも運動(うんどう)を習慣付(しゅうかんづ)けることが大切(たいせつ)だ。
It is important to make a habit of exercising a little every day.

A compound verb from 習慣(しゅうかん) (habit, custom) and 付(づ)ける (to attach, to apply). Means to establish something as a regular habit, either for oneself or for others. Often used in contexts of child-rearing, health, and self-improvement.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 早起(はやお)きを習慣付(しゅうかんづ)ける: to make early rising a habit
  • 運動(うんどう)を習慣付(しゅうかんづ)ける: to make exercise a habit
  • 読書(どくしょ)を習慣付(しゅうかんづ)ける: to make reading a habit
  • 子(こ)どもに習慣付(しゅうかんづ)ける: to instill a habit in children

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 身(み)に付(つ)ける: to acquire (a skill, habit) — broader; not limited to habits
  • 慣(な)れる: to get used to — passive process of becoming accustomed rather than deliberate habit formation
  • 癖(くせ)にする: to make into a habit — more colloquial; can be positive or negative
