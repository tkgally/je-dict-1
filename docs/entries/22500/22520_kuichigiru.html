1. to bite off; to tear off with one's teeth
To use one's teeth to rip or tear a piece from something.
犬(いぬ)がロープを食(く)いちぎった。
The dog bit through the rope.
肉(にく)を食(く)いちぎるように食(た)べた。
He ate by tearing off chunks of meat with his teeth.
野生(やせい)の動物(どうぶつ)が獲物(えもの)の肉(にく)を食(く)いちぎって食(た)べている場面(ばめん)は迫力(はくりょく)がある。
The scene of a wild animal tearing off and eating its prey's flesh is powerful.

A vivid, somewhat violent compound verb formed from 食(く)う (to eat, rough/masculine form) and ちぎる (to tear off). It conveys forceful biting that separates a piece. Often used in descriptions of animals feeding, or figuratively to emphasize fierce or aggressive eating.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ロープを食(く)いちぎる: to bite through a rope
  • 肉(にく)を食(く)いちぎる: to tear off meat
  • 糸(いと)を食(く)いちぎる: to bite through thread

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 噛(か)みちぎる: to bite off — very similar meaning; uses the standard verb for biting (噛(か)む) rather than the rougher 食(く)う
  • 噛(か)み切(き)る: to bite through — emphasizes cutting cleanly through something
  • ちぎる: to tear off — tearing with hands, not teeth
