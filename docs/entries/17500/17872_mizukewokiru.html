水気(みずけ)を切(き)る

みずけをきる
expression
to drain off moisture; to pat dry
1. to drain off moisture; to pat dry; to wring out water
To remove excess water or moisture from food, laundry, or other items. A common cooking and household term.
豆腐(とうふ)の水気(みずけ)を切(き)る。
To drain the moisture from tofu.
野菜(やさい)を洗(あら)ったら、しっかり水気(みずけ)を切(き)ってください。
After washing the vegetables, please drain them thoroughly.
水気(みずけ)を切(き)らないまま揚(あ)げると、油(あぶら)がはねて危険(きけん)だ。
If you fry without draining the moisture, the oil will splatter and it's dangerous.

USAGE:
Primarily a cooking term. 水気(みずけ) (moisture, water content) + を切(き)る (to cut/remove). Essential in Japanese cooking where excess moisture affects texture and oil temperature.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 水気(みずけ)をよく切(き)る (to drain well)
  • 水気(みずけ)をしっかり切(き)る (to drain thoroughly)
  • キッチンペーパーで水気(みずけ)を取(と)る (to remove moisture with a paper towel)

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 水(みず)を切(き)る: to drain water — similar meaning, slightly more general
  • 水分(すいぶん)を飛(と)ばす: to evaporate moisture — using heat rather than draining
