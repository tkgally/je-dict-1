1. to subside, to be suppressed, to calm down
For disturbances, pain, emotions, or unrest to settle down and return to a calm state. Implies that something turbulent or agitated has been brought under control or has naturally subsided.
痛(いた)みがようやく鎮(しず)まった。
The pain finally subsided.
騒(さわ)ぎが鎮(しず)まるまで待(ま)った。
I waited until the commotion died down.
怒(いか)りが鎮(しず)まってから、冷静(れいせい)に話(はな)し合(あ)おう。
Let's talk calmly after the anger has subsided.

KANJI DISTINCTION:

  • 鎮(しず)まる: for disturbances, pain, emotions, or unrest subsiding — emphasizes suppression
  • 静(しず)まる: for noise or activity becoming quiet — emphasizes silence/stillness

The transitive counterpart is 鎮(しず)める (to suppress, to calm, to quell).

GRAMMAR:
Godan verb (intransitive). The subject is the thing that subsides (pain, anger, unrest).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 痛(いた)みが鎮(しず)まる: the pain subsides
  • 怒(いか)りが鎮(しず)まる: the anger dies down
  • 騒(さわ)ぎが鎮(しず)まる: the commotion settles

Related Words

Contrast: 静(しず)まる (to become quiet, to fall silent)
