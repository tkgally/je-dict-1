1. oral tradition; oral transmission
The passing down of stories, knowledge, customs, or history by word of mouth rather than through written records. Also used as a suru verb meaning to hand down orally.
この物語(ものがたり)は口承(こうしょう)で伝(つた)えられてきた。
This story has been passed down through oral tradition.
口承(こうしょう)文学(ぶんがく)は世界中(せかいじゅう)にある。
Oral literature exists all over the world.
文字(もじ)を持(も)たない民族(みんぞく)の歴史(れきし)は口承(こうしょう)によって受(う)け継(つ)がれてきた。
The history of peoples without a writing system has been carried on through oral tradition.

USAGE:
A compound of 口(こう) (mouth) + 承(しょう) (to receive, to carry on). Refers to the cultural practice of transmitting stories, songs, legends, and knowledge by word of mouth across generations. Common in anthropology, folklore studies, and literature.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 口承(こうしょう)文学(ぶんがく) (oral literature)
  • 口承(こうしょう)文化(ぶんか) (oral culture)
  • 口承(こうしょう)で伝(つた)える (to pass down orally)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 口伝(くでん): oral instruction — emphasizes teaching or transmitting skills by word of mouth
  • 言(い)い伝(つた)え: legend, tradition — a specific story handed down, rather than the practice of oral transmission itself
