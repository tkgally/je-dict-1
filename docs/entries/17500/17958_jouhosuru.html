1. to concede; to make a concession; to compromise
To yield or give ground on a point during a negotiation, argument, or discussion. Implies voluntarily stepping back from one's position to reach an agreement.
お互(たが)いに譲歩(じょうほ)する必要(ひつよう)がある。
We need to make concessions to each other.
相手(あいて)が一歩(いっぽ)も譲歩(じょうほ)しなかった。
The other side didn't concede a single step.
交渉(こうしょう)が長引(ながび)いたが、最終的(さいしゅうてき)に双方(そうほう)が譲歩(じょうほ)して合意(ごうい)に達(たっ)した。
The negotiations dragged on, but ultimately both sides made concessions and reached an agreement.

USAGE:
Used in negotiations, disputes, and relationship contexts. Often implies a deliberate, reasoned decision to yield rather than being forced to give in.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 譲歩(じょうほ)を求(もと)める (demand concessions)
  • 大幅(おおはば)な譲歩(じょうほ) (major concession)
  • 一歩(いっぽ)も譲歩(じょうほ)しない (not yield an inch)
  • 相互(そうご)譲歩(じょうほ) (mutual compromise)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 妥協(だきょう)する: to compromise — implies settling for a less-than-ideal outcome, sometimes with negative nuance
  • 折(お)れる: to give in — more colloquial; implies yielding under pressure
  • 歩(あゆ)み寄(よ)る: to meet halfway — implies both sides making an effort to close the gap
