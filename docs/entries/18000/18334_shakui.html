爵位(しゃくい)

しゃくい
noun
peerage; title of nobility
1. peerage; title of nobility; rank of nobility
A hereditary or granted rank within a system of nobility. In Japan, the peerage system (華族制度) existed from 1869 to 1947.
爵位(しゃくい)を授(さず)ける。
To confer a title of nobility.
彼(かれ)は功績(こうせき)により爵位(しゃくい)を与(あた)えられた。
He was granted a peerage for his achievements.
戦後(せんご)の改革(かいかく)で日本(にほん)の爵位(しゃくい)制度(せいど)は廃止(はいし)された。
Japan's peerage system was abolished in the postwar reforms.

Japan had a five-rank peerage system modeled on European nobility: 公爵(こうしゃく) (duke/prince), 侯爵(こうしゃく) (marquis), 伯爵(はくしゃく) (count/earl), 子爵(ししゃく) (viscount), and 男爵(だんしゃく) (baron). This system was abolished with the 1947 constitution. The word now appears mainly in historical contexts and translations of Western literature. Common collocations: 爵位(しゃくい)を授(さず)ける (confer a peerage), 爵位(しゃくい)を剥奪(はくだつ)する (strip of a peerage).
