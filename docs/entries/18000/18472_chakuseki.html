1. taking one's seat; sitting down
The formal act of sitting down in one's designated or assigned seat. Used in formal settings such as ceremonies, classrooms, meetings, and performances. The opposite of 起立 (standing up).
全員(ぜんいん)、着席(ちゃくせき)してください。
Everyone, please take your seats.
開演(かいえん)前(まえ)にお着席(ちゃくせき)ください。
Please be seated before the performance begins.
先生(せんせい)が教室(きょうしつ)に入(はい)ると、生徒(せいと)たちはすぐに着席(ちゃくせき)した。
When the teacher entered the classroom, the students immediately sat down.

着席(ちゃくせき) is a formal word for the act of sitting down in one's seat. Most commonly used as 着席(ちゃくせき)する.

REGISTER:
Formal. Used in announcements, instructions, and ceremonies. In everyday conversation, 座(すわ)る is the natural choice.

COMMON CONTEXTS:

  • Classrooms: 先生(せんせい)が来(く)る前(まえ)に着席(ちゃくせき)してください
  • Ceremonies and performances: お着席(ちゃくせき)ください (polite form with お)
  • Meetings: 全員(ぜんいん)着席(ちゃくせき)したら始(はじ)めます

PAIRED ANTONYM:
起立(きりつ) (standing up). The pair 起立(きりつ)・着席(ちゃくせき) is a set phrase in Japanese schools, used as commands at the start and end of class.

CONTRAST:

  • 着席(ちゃくせき): formal, implies a designated seat
  • 座(すわ)る: neutral, any act of sitting down
