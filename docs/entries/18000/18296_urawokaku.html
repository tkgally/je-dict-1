1. to outwit; to outsmart; to catch off guard; to do the unexpected
To act in a way that the other party does not expect, thereby gaining an advantage. Often used in strategy, competition, and negotiations.
相手(あいて)の裏(うら)をかく作戦(さくせん)だ。
It's a strategy to outwit the opponent.
敵(てき)の裏(うら)をかいて勝利(しょうり)を収(おさ)めた。
We outsmarted the enemy and achieved victory.
予想(よそう)の裏(うら)をかくような展開(てんかい)に、観客(かんきゃく)は驚(おどろ)きの声(こえ)を上(あ)げた。
The audience gasped at the unexpected turn of events that defied all predictions.

USAGE:
The verb かく here means to go beyond or surpass (not "to write" or "to scratch"). The 裏(うら) refers to the hidden or unexpected side. The expression implies cleverness and is often used positively for strategic thinking, though it can also mean being deceptive.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 相手(あいて)の裏(うら)をかく (to outwit the opponent)
  • 予想(よそう)の裏(うら)をかく (to defy expectations)
  • 裏(うら)をかかれる (to be outwitted — passive form)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 出(で)し抜(ぬ)く: to get the jump on, to steal a march — implies taking action before the other
  • 騙(だま)す: to deceive — more negatively connoted, implies dishonesty
