供(そな)え物(もの)

そなえもの
noun
offering; oblation
1. offering; oblation
Food, flowers, or other items placed before a deity, Buddha, or the spirits of the deceased as a religious or commemorative offering.
仏壇(ぶつだん)に供(そな)え物(もの)を置(お)いた。
I placed an offering on the Buddhist altar.
供(そな)え物(もの)の果物(くだもの)を下(さ)げてから食(た)べた。
I took down the fruit offering and then ate it.
お盆(ぼん)には先祖(せんぞ)の霊(れい)のために供(そな)え物(もの)を用意(ようい)するのが日本(にほん)の習慣(しゅうかん)だ。
It is a Japanese custom to prepare offerings for the spirits of one's ancestors during Obon.

USAGE:
Typical offerings include fruit, rice, sweets, flowers, and incense. After being offered, food items are often taken down and eaten by the family — this is called 供(そな)え物(もの)を下(さ)げる. The word is used for both Buddhist altars (仏壇(ぶつだん)) and Shinto shrines (神棚(かみだな)).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 供(そな)え物(もの)を供(そな)える (to make an offering)
  • 供(そな)え物(もの)を下(さ)げる (to take down an offering)
  • 神(かみ)への供(そな)え物(もの) (an offering to the gods)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • お{供(そな)え}: the same word with the polite prefix お, commonly used in everyday speech
  • 奉納(ほうのう): dedication — more formal, refers to dedicating items to a shrine or temple
