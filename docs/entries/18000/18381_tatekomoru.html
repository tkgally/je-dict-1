1. to barricade oneself in; to hole up; to shut oneself in
To take refuge inside a building or enclosed space and refuse to come out, often in a standoff situation. Commonly used in news reports about hostage situations or sieges. Can also be used more casually for shutting oneself in a room.
犯人(はんにん)がビルに立(た)てこもった。
The suspect barricaded himself in a building.
人質(ひとじち)を取(と)って銀行(ぎんこう)に立(た)てこもる事件(じけん)が起(お)きた。
An incident occurred in which someone took hostages and barricaded themselves in a bank.
試験(しけん)前(まえ)は部屋(へや)に立(た)てこもって朝(あさ)から晩(ばん)まで勉強(べんきょう)していた。
Before exams, I'd shut myself in my room and study from morning till night.

CONJUGATION:
Godan verb (ら row): 立(た)てこもる・立(た)てこもります
て-form: 立(た)てこもって
た-form: 立(た)てこもった

Also written as 立(た)て籠(こ)もる with the kanji 籠(こ)もる.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ビルに立(た)てこもる (to barricade oneself in a building)
  • 人質(ひとじち)を取(と)って立(た)てこもる (to take hostages and barricade oneself)
  • 部屋(へや)に立(た)てこもる (to shut oneself in a room)
  • 立(た)てこもり事件(じけん) (a barricade/siege incident)

USAGE:
Frequently appears in news reports about criminal incidents. In casual usage, it can describe someone who shuts themselves in a room to concentrate on something, though 引(ひ)きこもる is more common for that.
