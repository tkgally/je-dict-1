1. to flutter down; to fall gently; to drift down
Describes something light falling slowly and gracefully through the air, as if dancing. Used for things like leaves, petals, snowflakes, and feathers. A poetic, evocative word.
桜(さくら)の花(はな)びらが舞(ま)い落(お)ちてきた。
Cherry blossom petals came fluttering down.
雪(ゆき)が静(しず)かに舞(ま)い落(お)ちる夜(よる)だった。
It was a night when snow was quietly drifting down.
風(かぜ)に吹(ふ)かれた落(お)ち葉(ば)が舞(ま)い落(お)ちて、地面(じめん)を赤(あか)や黄色(きいろ)に染(そ)めていった。
Fallen leaves blown by the wind fluttered down, coloring the ground red and yellow.

USAGE:
An ichidan (一段(いちだん)) compound verb combining 舞(ま)う (to dance) and 落(お)ちる (to fall). The verb evokes a graceful, floating motion. More literary and poetic than simply 落(お)ちる. Commonly used in descriptions of nature and seasonal imagery.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 花(はな)びらが舞(ま)い落(お)ちる (petals flutter down)
  • 雪(ゆき)が舞(ま)い落(お)ちる (snow drifts down)
  • 落(お)ち葉(ば)が舞(ま)い落(お)ちる (fallen leaves flutter down)
  • 羽(はね)が舞(ま)い落(お)ちる (a feather flutters down)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 散(ち)る: to scatter, to fall (of petals) — broader, less poetic
  • 降(ふ)り注(そそ)ぐ: to pour down — suggests heavier, more abundant falling
