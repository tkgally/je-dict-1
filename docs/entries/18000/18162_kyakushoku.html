脚色(きゃくしょく)

きゃくしょく
noun
dramatization; adaptation; embellishment
1. dramatization; adaptation
The process of adapting a novel, true story, or other source material into a screenplay, stage play, or other dramatic format.
この映画(えいが)は小説(しょうせつ)を脚色(きゃくしょく)したものだ。
This movie is an adaptation of a novel.
実話(じつわ)を脚色(きゃくしょく)してドラマにした。
They dramatized a true story and made it into a drama.
脚色(きゃくしょく)賞(しょう)を受賞(じゅしょう)した脚本家(きゃくほんか)がインタビューに答(こた)えていた。
The screenwriter who won the award for best adapted screenplay was giving an interview.
2. embellishment; exaggeration
Adding dramatic flair or exaggeration to a story or account, making it more interesting or impressive than the reality. Often implies the speaker is stretching the truth.
その話(はなし)はだいぶ脚色(きゃくしょく)されている。
That story has been considerably embellished.
彼(かれ)の武勇伝(ぶゆうでん)には脚色(きゃくしょく)が多(おお)い。
His tales of heroism contain a lot of embellishment.
脚色(きゃくしょく)なしで事実(じじつ)だけを話(はな)してほしい。
I want you to tell me just the facts, without any embellishment.

USAGE:
Sense 1 is the original and primary meaning, used in film, theater, and literary contexts. Sense 2 is a natural extension — when someone dramatizes their account of events, they are adding 脚色(きゃくしょく). Both senses can be used as a する verb.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 脚色(きゃくしょく)する (to dramatize; to adapt; to embellish)
  • 脚色(きゃくしょく)賞(しょう) (award for best adapted screenplay)
  • 脚色(きゃくしょく)を加(くわ)える (to add embellishment)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 脚本(きゃくほん): screenplay — the finished script itself, not the adaptation process
  • 改編(かいへん): adaptation — more neutral, often used for reorganizing or restructuring content
