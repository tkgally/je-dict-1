気(き)が進(すす)まない

きがすすまない
expression
reluctant; unwilling; not in the mood
1. reluctant; unwilling; not in the mood for
Expresses a lack of enthusiasm or willingness to do something. Literally 'one's spirit does not advance.' Used when one feels unenthusiastic about an activity without necessarily having a strong reason to refuse.
あまり気(き)が進(すす)まない。
I'm not really feeling up to it.
飲(の)み会(かい)に誘(さそ)われたが、気(き)が進(すす)まなかった。
I was invited to a drinking party, but I wasn't in the mood.
正直(しょうじき)に言(い)うと、その仕事(しごと)を引(ひ)き受(う)けるのは気(き)が進(すす)まないんです。
To be honest, I'm reluctant to take on that job.

One of many 気(き) expressions in Japanese. Always used in the negative — the positive form 気(き)が進(すす)む is very rare.

USAGE:
A softer, more polite way to express reluctance than directly refusing. Useful when you want to decline without giving a firm "no."

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • あまり気(き)が進(すす)まない (I'm not really feeling up to it)
  • 気(き)が進(すす)まないけど (I'm reluctant, but...)
  • 正直(しょうじき)気(き)が進(すす)まない (to be honest, I'm not keen)

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 気(き)が乗(の)らない: not in the mood (close synonym)
  • 乗(の)り気(き)じゃない: not keen on it (more casual)
  • やる気(き)がある: to be willing/motivated (positive counterpart)
