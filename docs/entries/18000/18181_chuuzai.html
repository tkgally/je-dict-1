1. stationing; being posted; residing for duty
Being assigned to live and work at a particular location, especially overseas, for work purposes. Commonly used for diplomats, company employees on overseas assignments, and police officers assigned to local posts.
父(ちち)は三年間(さんねんかん)アメリカに駐在(ちゅうざい)していた。
My father was posted in America for three years.
駐在(ちゅうざい)さんが毎朝(まいあさ)通学路(つうがくろ)に立(た)っている。
The local police officer stands along the school route every morning.
駐在さん is a colloquial way to refer to a resident police officer.
来月(らいげつ)からシンガポールに駐在(ちゅうざい)することになり、家族(かぞく)で引(ひ)っ越(こ)す準備(じゅんび)をしている。
I'll be posted to Singapore starting next month, so we're preparing for the family to move.

USAGE:
Very common in business contexts for overseas assignments. A 駐在員(ちゅうざいいん) (expatriate employee) is someone posted abroad by their company. In rural Japan, 駐在(ちゅうざい)さん refers to the local resident police officer stationed at a small police box (駐在所(ちゅうざいしょ)).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 駐在員(ちゅうざいいん) (expatriate employee; stationed worker)
  • 駐在所(ちゅうざいしょ) (residential police box)
  • 海外(かいがい)駐在(ちゅうざい) (overseas posting)
  • 駐在(ちゅうざい)妻(づま) (expat wife)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 赴任(ふにん): taking up a post — focuses on the act of going to a new assignment
  • 常駐(じょうちゅう): permanent stationing — being stationed full-time at a client site or location
