冷(ひ)え性(しょう)

ひえしょう
noun
sensitivity to cold; poor circulation
1. sensitivity to cold; poor circulation; tendency to feel cold
A constitutional tendency to feel cold easily, especially in the hands, feet, and lower body. Recognized in Japanese and East Asian medicine as a condition requiring treatment. Much more commonly discussed in Japan than in Western cultures.
私(わたし)は冷(ひ)え性(しょう)で、冬(ふゆ)がつらい。
I'm sensitive to cold, so winter is tough for me.
冷(ひ)え性(しょう)を改善(かいぜん)するために生姜(しょうが)を食(た)べるようにしている。
I try to eat ginger to improve my cold sensitivity.
女性(じょせい)は男性(だんせい)に比(くら)べて冷(ひ)え性(しょう)になりやすいと言(い)われている。
Women are said to be more prone to cold sensitivity than men.

CULTURE:
A concept deeply rooted in East Asian medicine. In Japan, 冷(ひ)え性(しょう) is widely recognized as a legitimate health concern, and many remedies are marketed for it — from warming foods like ginger to hot baths and special socks. The concept has no direct equivalent in Western medicine.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 冷(ひ)え性(しょう)を改善(かいぜん)する (to improve cold sensitivity)
  • 冷(ひ)え性(しょう)対策(たいさく) (measures against cold sensitivity)
  • 末端(まったん)冷(ひ)え性(しょう) (cold extremities — cold hands and feet)
  • 冷(ひ)え性(しょう)に効(き)く (effective for cold sensitivity)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 寒(さむ)がり: person who feels the cold — describes a person's temperament rather than a medical condition
