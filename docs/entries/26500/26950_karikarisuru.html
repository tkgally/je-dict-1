1. to be crispy; to be crunchy
Describes a hard, dry crunchiness when biting into food. The texture is harder and more brittle than サクサク, like toasted bread, crispy bacon, or burnt edges.
ベーコンをカリカリに焼(や)いた。
I fried the bacon until crispy.
カリカリした食感(しょっかん)のクッキーが好(す)きだ。
I like cookies with a crispy, crunchy texture.
外側(そとがわ)がカリカリで中(なか)がもちもちのピザが食(た)べたい。
I want to eat pizza that's crispy on the outside and chewy on the inside.
2. to be irritated; to be on edge; to be worked up
Describes a state of being tense, annoyed, or short-tempered. Often used when someone is stressed and easily angered by small things.
そんなにカリカリするなよ。
Don't get so worked up.
上司(じょうし)が朝(あさ)からカリカリしていて話(はな)しかけにくい。
My boss has been on edge since morning and is hard to approach.
試験(しけん)前(まえ)でみんなカリカリしているから、静(しず)かにしてあげよう。
Everyone's stressed because of the exam, so let's keep it quiet for them.

An onomatopoeic word with two distinct meanings. The food sense mimics the sound of biting into something hard and crunchy. The irritation sense may derive from the image of scratching or scraping — a grating, tense feeling.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • カリカリに焼(や)く: to bake/grill until crispy
  • カリカリに揚(あ)げる: to deep-fry until crunchy
  • カリカリした食感(しょっかん): crunchy texture
  • カリカリしないで: don't get so worked up

TEXTURE COMPARISON:

Japanese has many onomatopoeia for crunchiness, each evoking a different texture:

  • サクサク: light, flaky crispness (tempura, pastry)
  • カリカリ: hard, brittle crunchiness (toast, bacon)
  • パリパリ: thin, snapping crispness (chips, dried seaweed)
  • ザクザク: rough, chunky crunch (gravel, coarse-cut cabbage)
  • ポリポリ: quiet crunching (snacking on crackers)
  • バリバリ: aggressive crunching (chomping through hard food)
