1. to space out; to be dazed; to be absentminded
To be in a vague, unfocused mental state — either from tiredness, distraction, or simply relaxing without thinking about anything in particular.
ぼーっとしないで。
Don't space out.
疲(つか)れてぼーっとしている。
I'm so tired that I'm spacing out.
休日(きゅうじつ)は何(なに)もしないでぼーっとしていたい。
On my days off, I just want to zone out and do nothing.
2. to be hazy; to be blurry; to appear indistinct
Describes something appearing vague or indistinct visually, such as a shape seen through fog or a blurred outline.
霧(きり)でぼーっとしか見(み)えない。
It's only hazily visible through the fog.
遠(とお)くの山(やま)がぼーっと霞(かす)んでいた。
The distant mountains appeared hazily through the mist.
暗(くら)い部屋(へや)の中(なか)で、電灯(でんとう)の光(ひかり)がぼーっと浮(う)かんでいた。
In the dark room, the light from the lamp glowed faintly.

A mimetic (onomatopoeia-derived) expression describing a vague, unfocused state. The long vowel ー and small っ are essential to the word's feel — they suggest a drawn-out, vacant quality.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ぼーっと立(た)っている: standing there in a daze
  • ぼーっと見(み)つめる: staring blankly
  • ぼーっとした顔(かお): a dazed face, a vacant expression
  • ぼーっとして聞(き)いていなかった: was spacing out and wasn't listening

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ぼんやり(ぼんやり): absent-minded, vague — very close in meaning but slightly more literary; ぼーっと is more casual and spoken
  • うっかり: carelessly, by accident — implies an unintentional mistake rather than a general mental state
  • うとうとする: to doze off — specifically about sleepiness, not general absent-mindedness

USAGE:
Very common in everyday conversation. Often used by parents and teachers scolding children: ぼーっとしてないで ("Stop spacing out!"). Also used positively to describe deliberately relaxing: ぼーっとする時間(じかん)がほしい ("I want some time to just zone out").
