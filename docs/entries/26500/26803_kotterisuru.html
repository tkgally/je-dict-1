1. to be rich; to be heavy (in flavor or texture)
describes food or drink that has a rich, heavy, oily, or concentrated flavor
このラーメンはこってりしている。
This ramen is rich and heavy.
こってりした味(あじ)のスープが好(す)きだ。
I like rich-tasting soup.
夏(なつ)はこってりしたものより、さっぱりしたものが食(た)べたくなる。
In summer, I want to eat light food rather than heavy food.

Used almost exclusively to describe food and drink. Indicates a rich, oily, or thick quality that makes something feel heavy or indulgent. Often used in contrast with さっぱり (light, refreshing).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • こってりした味(あじ): rich flavor
  • こってりしたスープ: thick/rich soup
  • こってりした料理(りょうり): heavy/rich cooking
  • こってり系(けい): rich-style (e.g., ramen)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • あっさり: the opposite — light, simple, not heavy
  • さっぱり: also light/refreshing, but broader in usage beyond food
  • こくがある: has depth/richness of flavor, but without the negative heaviness connotation

USAGE:
Very commonly seen on restaurant menus and food reviews. Ramen shops often categorize their broth as こってり or あっさり. The こってり・あっさり pair is fundamental food vocabulary in Japanese.
