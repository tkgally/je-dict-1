1. to improve; to make better; to reform
To change something for the better, addressing problems or deficiencies. Used for both tangible things (processes, conditions) and abstract things (relationships, situations). Central to Japanese business philosophy.
生活(せいかつ)習慣(しゅうかん)を改善(かいぜん)する。
To improve one's lifestyle habits.
労働(ろうどう)環境(かんきょう)を改善(かいぜん)するために会議(かいぎ)を開(ひら)いた。
We held a meeting to improve the working environment.
食事(しょくじ)と運動(うんどう)で体調(たいちょう)が大(おお)きく改善(かいぜん)した。
My physical condition improved greatly through diet and exercise.
この問題(もんだい)を改善(かいぜん)しなければ、お客(きゃく)さんの満足度(まんぞくど)は上(あ)がらない。
If we don't improve this problem, customer satisfaction won't increase.

One of the most well-known Japanese business concepts internationally. The noun 改善(かいぜん) (kaizen) refers to continuous, incremental improvement and is widely used as a loanword in English business contexts, particularly in manufacturing and lean management.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 業務(ぎょうむ)改善(かいぜん): operational improvement
  • 体質(たいしつ)改善(かいぜん): constitutional improvement
  • 環境(かんきょう)改善(かいぜん): environmental improvement
  • 改善(かいぜん)策(さく): improvement measure
  • 改善(かいぜん)点(てん): point of improvement

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 直(なお)す: to fix — corrects something broken rather than making something better
  • 向上(こうじょう)する: to improve — emphasizes raising quality or level upward
  • 是正(ぜせい)する: to correct — fixing something wrong or unjust, more formal
