咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)

くわえたばこ
noun
cigarette dangling from the mouth
1. cigarette dangling from the mouth; smoking with a cigarette held in the lips
The act or state of holding a lit cigarette in one's mouth while doing something else with the hands. Often evokes a casual, rough, or nonchalant image.
咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)で新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)む。
Read the newspaper with a cigarette dangling from one's mouth.
彼(かれ)は咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)のまま作業(さぎょう)を続(つづ)けた。
He continued working with a cigarette in his mouth.
昭和(しょうわ)の映画(えいが)では、刑事(けいじ)が咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)で事件(じけん)現場(げんば)に現(あらわ)れるシーンがよくあった。
In Showa-era films, there were often scenes of detectives showing up at crime scenes with cigarettes dangling from their mouths.

A compound of 咥(くわ)える (to hold in the mouth) and 煙草(たばこ) (cigarette/tobacco). Also written as くわえタバコ or くわえたばこ in kana. The image evokes a certain roughness or nonchalance, often associated with old-fashioned masculinity or working-class culture.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)で歩(ある)く: walk with a cigarette in one's mouth
  • 咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)のまま: with a cigarette still in one's mouth
  • 咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ)禁止(きんし): no smoking while walking/working (with cigarette in mouth)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 歩(ある)き煙草(たばこ): smoking while walking
  • 喫煙(きつえん): smoking (formal/neutral term)

CULTURAL NOTE:
咥(くわ)え煙草(たばこ) carries a strong visual image in Japanese culture, often associated with hard-boiled detectives, laborers, and old film stars. In modern Japan, it is considered bad manners and is prohibited in many workplaces and public areas.
