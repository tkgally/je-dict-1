見得(みえ)

みえ
noun
dramatic pose (in kabuki); striking a pose
See also: 見栄(みえ) (vanity, showing off - same reading, different kanji)
1. dramatic pose (in kabuki); striking a pose
A stylized, frozen pose held by a kabuki actor at a climactic moment, often with crossed eyes and an exaggerated expression. By extension, any showy or boastful display of resolve.
役者(やくしゃ)が見得(みえ)を切(き)った。
The actor struck a dramatic pose.
歌舞伎(かぶき)の見得(みえ)は観客(かんきゃく)が一番(いちばん)盛(も)り上(あ)がる瞬間(しゅんかん)だ。
The mie pose is the moment in kabuki when the audience gets most excited.
「絶対(ぜったい)勝(か)ってみせる」と大(おお)きな見得(みえ)を切(き)ったが、結果(けっか)は惨敗(ざんぱい)だった。
He made a big show of declaring "I'll definitely win," but the result was a crushing defeat.

Almost always appears in the idiom 見得(みえ)を切(き)る (to strike a dramatic pose). In the figurative sense, it describes making a bold declaration or showy display of resolve — often with an implication that the speaker may not deliver.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 見得(みえ)を切(き)る: to strike a pose; to make a bold declaration
  • 大(おお)きな見得(みえ)を切(き)る: to make a grand show of resolve
  • 見得(みえ)を張(は)る: to put on airs (less common variant)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 見栄(みえ): vanity, showing off — same reading, related etymologically, but refers to outward appearance for social effect rather than a theatrical pose. 見栄(みえ)を張(は)る = to keep up appearances
  • ポーズ: pose — loanword, generic and modern
  • 大言壮語(たいげんそうご): big talk, grandiose words — emphasizes the boastful claim rather than the pose
