捨(す)て値(ね)

すてね
noun
throwaway price; rock-bottom price; dirt-cheap price
1. throwaway price; rock-bottom price; dirt-cheap price
A price so low it is as if the item were being thrown away — used for goods sold well below market value, often in clearance sales, distressed sales, or urgent liquidations.
捨(す)て値(ね)で売(う)る。
To sell at a throwaway price.
閉店(へいてん)セール(せーる)で捨(す)て値(ね)になっていた。
At the closing sale, prices had dropped to throwaway levels.
急(きゅう)に引(ひ)っ越(こ)すことになり、家具(かぐ)を捨(す)て値(ね)で処分(しょぶん)した。
Having to move suddenly, I got rid of the furniture at dirt-cheap prices.

Literally "abandonment price" — a price treated as if the seller is simply discarding the item.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 捨(す)て値(ね)で売(う)る: to sell at a throwaway price
  • 捨(す)て値(ね)で買(か)う: to buy at a rock-bottom price
  • 捨(す)て値(ね)になる: (prices) drop to throwaway levels
  • 捨(す)て値(ね)処分(しょぶん): disposal at rock-bottom prices

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 格安(かくやす): bargain price — implies good value but not necessarily desperate selling
  • 叩(たた)き売(う)り: fire sale — emphasizes aggressive, urgent selling
