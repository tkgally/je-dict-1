1. to stand one's ground; to stay put; to hold firm
To remain in place instead of retreating or giving up, often in a difficult or dangerous situation.
その場(ば)に踏(ふ)みとどまる。
He stands his ground on the spot.
逃(に)げ出(だ)したかったが、踏(ふ)みとどまった。
I wanted to run away, but I held my ground.
苦(くる)しい状況(じょうきょう)でも、チームは最後(さいご)まで踏(ふ)みとどまった。
Even in a tough situation, the team held firm to the very end.
2. to hold back; to restrain oneself; to stop short of doing something
To check an impulse and refrain from an action one was about to take, especially something rash or regrettable.
言(い)い返(かえ)そうとして、踏(ふ)みとどまった。
I was about to talk back, but I stopped myself.
衝動買(しょうどうが)いをしそうになったが、踏(ふ)みとどまった。
I almost made an impulse purchase, but I held myself back.
会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)めようかと思(おも)ったが、家族(かぞく)のことを考(かんが)えて踏(ふ)みとどまった。
I thought about quitting the company, but thinking of my family, I held back.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). Compound of 踏(ふ)む (to step) + とどまる (to stay).

USAGE:
Sense 1 is physical or figurative steadfastness — not retreating. Sense 2 is self-restraint — stopping just before doing something one would regret. Both often appear in the past tense to describe a moment of resolve.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ぎりぎりで踏(ふ)みとどまる: to just barely hold back
  • 思(おも)いとどまる: to think better of it (close synonym for sense 2)
  • 現地(げんち)に踏(ふ)みとどまる: to stay on at the site
