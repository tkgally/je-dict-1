1. to show signs of pain; to act as if in pain; to complain of pain
To outwardly express or behave as though feeling pain. Formed from the adjective 痛(いた)い plus the suffix ～がる, which describes another person's visible feelings rather than one's own.
子(こ)どもが足(あし)を痛(いた)がる。
The child is complaining that their leg hurts.
注射(ちゅうしゃ)を痛(いた)がって、泣(な)き出(だ)した。
Dreading the pain of the shot, the child started crying.
犬(いぬ)がしきりに耳(みみ)を痛(いた)がるので、動物病院(どうぶつびょういん)に連(つ)れて行(い)った。
Since the dog kept showing signs that its ear hurt, I took it to the vet.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). The painful body part or cause is marked with を: 足(あし)を痛(いた)がる, 注射(ちゅうしゃ)を痛(いた)がる.

USAGE:
The ～がる suffix describes a third person's outward signs of feeling, so 痛(いた)がる is used about others, not oneself. To say your own leg hurts, use 足(あし)が痛(いた)い, not 足(あし)を痛(いた)がる.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ひどく痛(いた)がる: to be in evident severe pain
  • 患部(かんぶ)を痛(いた)がる: to show pain in the affected area
  • 痛(いた)がって泣(な)く: to cry from pain
