1. to come in; to enter (moving toward the speaker or scene)
Compound of 入(はい)る (to enter) + くる (to come). Expresses the movement of something entering a space from outside, with the directional sense of movement toward the speaker or the focal scene. Used for people, air, light, information, or other things flowing in.
冷気(れいき)が窓(まど)から入(はい)ってくる。
Cold air comes in through the window.
誰(だれ)かが部屋(へや)に入(はい)ってきた。
Someone came into the room.
SNSを通(とお)じて海外(かいがい)からも情報(じょうほう)が入(はい)ってくるようになった。
Information has come to flow in from overseas through social media as well.

The ～てくる pattern attached to 入(はい)る conveys that the entering is directed toward the speaker or scene. Compare 入(はい)っていく (to go in, moving away) and 入(はい)る (neutral).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 部屋(へや)に入(はい)ってくる: to come into the room
  • 情報(じょうほう)が入(はい)ってくる: information comes in
  • 光(ひかり)が入(はい)ってくる: light comes in
  • 風(かぜ)が入(はい)ってくる: a breeze comes in

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 入(はい)る: to enter — neutral, no directional connotation
  • 入(はい)っていく: to go in — the movement is away from the speaker
