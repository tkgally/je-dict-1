1. to come out; to emerge (toward the speaker or from within)
To come out of an enclosed space, to emerge from somewhere, or to appear on the scene. Combines 出る (to exit) with くる (to come), indicating movement toward the speaker or present location.
猫(ねこ)が押入(おしい)れから出(で)てきた。
The cat came out of the closet.
玄関(げんかん)から誰(だれ)かが出(で)てくる音(おと)がした。
I heard someone coming out from the front door.
2. to appear; to come up (in conversation, text, or data)
To appear or turn up in a non-physical sense — such as a word appearing in a book, a name coming up in conversation, or a problem emerging.
新(あたら)しい単語(たんご)が教科書(きょうかしょ)に出(で)てきたら、すぐに調(しら)べるようにしている。
When new vocabulary appears in the textbook, I look it up right away.
話(はなし)の中(なか)で彼(かれ)の名前(なまえ)が出(で)てきた。
His name came up in the conversation.

A compound of 出(で)る + くる. The くる component indicates the movement is directed toward the present location or the speaker.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • ～から出(で)てくる: to come out from ~
  • 話(はなし)に出(で)てくる: to come up in conversation
  • 問題(もんだい)が出(で)てくる: a problem emerges

CONTRAST:

  • 出(で)ていく: to go out (away from speaker)
  • 出(で)る: to exit (neutral direction)
