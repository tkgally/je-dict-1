べし

べし
auxiliary
should; must; ought to (formal/archaic)
1. should; must; ought to
Classical auxiliary expressing strong obligation, appropriateness, or natural expectation. Attaches to the dictionary form of a verb. Found in formal maxims, notices, classical literature, and stiff written prose.
立(た)ち入(い)るべからず。
No entry. (Literally: One must not enter.)
べからず is the negative form of べし: "must not"
為政者(いせいしゃ)は民(たみ)の声(こえ)に耳(みみ)を傾(かたむ)けるべし。
Those who govern should listen to the voice of the people.
武士(ぶし)は名誉(めいよ)を重(おも)んずるべしと言(い)われていた。
It was said that a samurai should value honor.

Archaic/formal auxiliary. In modern Japanese, べし has largely been replaced by べき (attributive) and should/must expressions using なければならない or ～すべき.

INFLECTIONS:

  • べし (predicative): 行くべし (one should go)
  • べき (attributive): 行くべき道(みち) (the path one should take)
  • べく (conjunctive): 行くべく (in order to go; so as to go)
  • べからず (negative): 行くべからず (one must not go)

The negative べからず still appears commonly on formal notices and signs.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • べき: should; ought to (the attributive form, widely used in modern Japanese)
  • べく: in order to (conjunctive form, used in formal writing)
