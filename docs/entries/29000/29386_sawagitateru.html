1. to make a fuss; to raise a clamor; to sensationalize
To make a loud fuss or raise an outcry about something, often more than the matter deserves. Frequently used of the media or a crowd blowing an issue out of proportion. The topic is marked with を.
やじ馬(うま)が騒(さわ)ぎ立(た)てる。
The onlookers kick up a fuss.
メディアが事件(じけん)を騒(さわ)ぎ立(た)てた。
The media made a big fuss about the incident.
小(ちい)さなミスを、そんなに騒(さわ)ぎ立(た)てる必要(ひつよう)はない。
There's no need to make such a fuss over a small mistake.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). The topic made a fuss about is marked with を; it is also used intransitively of people clamoring. Compound of 騒(さわ)ぐ (to make noise) + 立(た)てる.

USAGE:
Usually negative — it implies overreaction or sensationalizing rather than legitimate concern.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 大(おお)げさに騒(さわ)ぎ立(た)てる: to make an exaggerated fuss
  • マスコミが騒(さわ)ぎ立(た)てる: the media sensationalize it
