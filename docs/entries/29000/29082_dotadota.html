どたどた

どたどた
adverb, onomatopoeia
with heavy clumsy footsteps; clomping; stomping
1. with loud, heavy footsteps; clomping around noisily
The sound or manner of heavy, clumsy footsteps — people running or walking noisily, as on a wooden floor or stairs. Often suggests a lack of grace or of consideration for the noise.
子(こ)どもがどたどたと走(はし)る。
The children run around noisily.
二階(にかい)でどたどたと足音(あしおと)がする。
There are heavy footsteps stomping around upstairs.
廊下(ろうか)をどたどたと走(はし)ると、下(した)の階(かい)の人(ひと)に迷惑(めいわく)がかかる。
Running down the hallway with heavy footsteps disturbs the people on the floor below.

Imitates loud, heavy, clumsy footsteps — typically of someone running or hurrying noisily indoors. Usually carries a slightly negative tone of clumsiness or inconsideration. Often used as どたどたと.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • どたどた(と)走(はし)る: to run with heavy footsteps
  • どたどたと歩(ある)く: to clomp around

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ばたばた: hurried, busy movement or flapping; emphasizes rushed busyness more than heavy footfalls
  • どすどす: even heavier, more thudding footsteps
