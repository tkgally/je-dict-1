口一杯(くちいっぱい)

くちいっぱい
noun, adverb
mouthful; with one's mouth full
1. mouthful; with one's mouth full; filling the mouth
A quantity of food that fills the mouth, or the state of having one's mouth packed full.
口一杯(くちいっぱい)に頬張(ほおば)る。
To stuff one's mouth full.
口一杯(くちいっぱい)にケーキを入(い)れて話(はな)そうとした。
He tried to talk with his mouth full of cake.
子供(こども)が口一杯(くちいっぱい)にご飯(はん)を詰(つ)め込(こ)んで、お母(かあ)さんに叱(しか)られた。
The child crammed rice into his mouth and was scolded by his mother.

USAGE:
Describes the quantity that fills the mouth or the act of eating with a very full mouth. Also written as 口(くち)いっぱい. Related to the expression 頬張(ほおば)る (to stuff one's cheeks with food).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 口一杯(くちいっぱい)に頬張(ほおば)る (to stuff one's mouth full)
  • 口一杯(くちいっぱい)に詰(つ)め込(こ)む (to cram into one's mouth)
  • 口一杯(くちいっぱい)の幸(しあわ)せ (a mouthful of happiness)
