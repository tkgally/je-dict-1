増水(ぞうすい)

ぞうすい
noun / verb (suru)
rising water; swelling of a river
1. rising water level; swelling of a river
An increase in the water level of a river, lake, or other body of water, typically due to heavy rain, snowmelt, or upstream conditions. Often discussed in the context of flood warnings.
大雨(おおあめ)で川(かわ)が増水(ぞうすい)した。
The river rose due to heavy rain.
増水(ぞうすい)した川(かわ)には絶対(ぜったい)に近(ちか)づかないでください。
Please absolutely do not go near the swollen river.
台風(たいふう)の影響(えいきょう)で上流(じょうりゅう)が増水(ぞうすい)しており、下流(かりゅう)でも氾濫(はんらん)の恐(おそ)れがあると警告(けいこく)された。
Due to the typhoon, the upstream area has swollen, and a warning was issued that flooding may also occur downstream.

USAGE:
Used as a noun or with する. Frequently heard in weather reports and disaster warnings during the rainy season (梅雨(つゆ)) and typhoon season. The opposite is 減水(げんすい) (receding water).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 川(かわ)が増水(ぞうすい)する (a river swells)
  • 増水(ぞうすい)注意報(ちゅういほう) (rising water advisory)
  • 急激(きゅうげき)な増水(ぞうすい) (sudden rise in water level)
  • 増水(ぞうすい)した川(かわ) (a swollen river)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 氾濫(はんらん): flooding — when water overflows its banks, a more severe condition than rising water
  • 洪水(こうずい): flood — a large-scale inundation
