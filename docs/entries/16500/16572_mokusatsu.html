黙殺(もくさつ)

もくさつ
noun / verb (suru)
ignoring; killing with silence
1. ignoring; dismissing without comment; killing with silence
Deliberately choosing not to respond to or acknowledge something, effectively treating it as if it does not exist. A deliberate, often strategic silence.
提案(ていあん)は黙殺(もくさつ)された。
The proposal was met with silence.
政府(せいふ)は批判(ひはん)を黙殺(もくさつ)し続(つづ)けている。
The government continues to ignore the criticism.
記者(きしゃ)の質問(しつもん)を黙殺(もくさつ)して、会見(かいけん)場(じょう)を去(さ)った。
He ignored the reporter's question and left the press conference.

USAGE:
A strong word that literally combines 黙(もく) (silence) and 殺(さつ) (to kill). The "killing" metaphor emphasizes that the silence is intentional and dismissive — not mere oversight. Often used in political, diplomatic, and media contexts.

HISTORICAL NOTE:
This word is historically notable: in July 1945, Japanese Prime Minister Suzuki used 黙殺(もくさつ) regarding the Potsdam Declaration. The word was translated as "reject" by Western media, though it may have been intended as "withhold comment." This mistranslation is sometimes cited as a factor in the decision to use atomic weapons.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 黙殺(もくさつ)する (to ignore, to dismiss with silence)
  • 黙殺(もくさつ)される (to be ignored)
  • 意見(いけん)を黙殺(もくさつ)する (to ignore an opinion)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 無視(むし): ignoring — more common and neutral; lacks the deliberate, strategic nuance
  • 黙認(もくにん): tacit approval — similar use of silence but implies acceptance rather than rejection
