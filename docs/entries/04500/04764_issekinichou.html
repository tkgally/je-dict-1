一石二鳥(いっせきにちょう)

いっせきにちょう
noun, expression
killing two birds with one stone
1. killing two birds with one stone
Achieving two goals with a single action. A four-character idiom (四字熟語).
運動(うんどう)しながら英語(えいご)を聞(き)くのは一石二鳥(いっせきにちょう)だ。
Listening to English while exercising is killing two birds with one stone.
自転車(じてんしゃ)通勤(つうきん)は健康(けんこう)にも環境(かんきょう)にもいい、一石二鳥(いっせきにちょう)だ。
Commuting by bicycle is good for both health and the environment—two birds with one stone.
一石二鳥(いっせきにちょう)の方法(ほうほう)を考(かんが)えよう。
Let's think of a way to kill two birds with one stone.

A well-known 四字熟語(よじじゅくご) (four-character idiom) borrowed from the English proverb. Literally: one stone, two birds. Used when describing efficient solutions or multitasking. Very commonly used in everyday speech.
