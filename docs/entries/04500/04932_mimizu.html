蚯蚓(みみず)

みみず
noun
earthworm
1. earthworm
A soft-bodied, segmented worm that lives in soil. Important for soil aeration and composting.
庭(にわ)を掘(ほ)ったら蚯蚓(みみず)が出(で)てきた。
Earthworms came out when I dug in the garden.
蚯蚓(みみず)は土(つち)を肥(こ)やします。
Earthworms enrich the soil.
釣(つ)りの餌(えさ)に蚯蚓(みみず)を使(つか)います。
I use earthworms as fishing bait.

蚯蚓(みみず) is the common word for earthworm. Usually written in hiragana (みみず) since the kanji {蚯蚓} is rarely used.

ECOLOGICAL ROLE:

  • Aerate soil and improve drainage
  • Break down organic matter into compost
  • Enrich soil nutrients through castings
  • Widely valued in gardening and agriculture

COMMON EXPRESSIONS:

  • 蚯蚓(みみず)腫(ば)れ: a welt or linear swelling on the skin (literally "earthworm swelling")
  • 蚯蚓(みみず)のような字(じ): illegibly scrawled handwriting (literally "letters like earthworms")

USAGE:

  • Counter: 匹(ひき)
  • Used as fishing bait (釣(つ)りの餌(えさ))
  • Children often encounter them when digging in soil

CULTURAL NOTE:
In traditional folk medicine, dried earthworm (地竜(じりゅう)) was used as a fever reducer. The expression 蚯蚓(みみず)に小便(しょうべん)をかけると腫(は)れる is a folk superstition warning children not to urinate on earthworms.
