河馬(かば)

かば
noun
hippopotamus
1. hippopotamus, hippo
A large semiaquatic mammal native to sub-Saharan Africa, known for spending much of its time in rivers and lakes.
河馬(かば)は水(みず)の中(なか)で過(す)ごすことが多(おお)いです。
Hippos spend much of their time in the water.
動物園(どうぶつえん)で河馬(かば)が大(おお)きな口(くち)を開(あ)けていました。
The hippo was opening its large mouth at the zoo.
河馬(かば)は見(み)た目(め)より危険(きけん)な動物(どうぶつ)です。
Hippos are more dangerous animals than they appear.
河馬(かば)は草(くさ)を食(た)べます。
Hippos eat grass.
河馬(かば)の赤(あか)ちゃんが動物園(どうぶつえん)で生(う)まれて、大(おお)きな話題(わだい)になった。
A baby hippo was born at the zoo and became a big topic of conversation.

WRITING:

  • The kanji 河馬(かば) is rare in everyday text. Animal names are normally written in katakana, so カバ is the usual form; かば also appears.
  • The name is literally "river horse" (河(かわ) = river, 馬(うま) = horse), matching the Greek origin of "hippopotamus."

COUNTING:

  • Counted with 頭(とう), the counter for large animals: 一頭(いっとう), 二頭(にとう).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 動物園(どうぶつえん)の河馬(かば) (the hippo at the zoo) — where learners most often meet the word
  • 河馬(かば)が大(おお)きな口(くち)を開(あ)ける (the hippo opens its huge mouth)

USAGE NOTE:

  • Despite its placid image, 河馬(かば) is often described in Japanese as 見(み)た目(め)より危険(きけん) (more dangerous than it looks), a phrase worth knowing in its own right.
