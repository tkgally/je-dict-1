苔(こけ)

こけ
noun
moss
1. moss
Small, soft green plants that grow in dense mats on rocks, trees, and damp ground.
石(いし)に苔(こけ)が生(は)えている。
Moss is growing on the stone.
苔(こけ)が生(は)えた庭(にわ)は美(うつく)しい。
A garden covered with moss is beautiful.
転(ころ)がる石(いし)には苔(こけ)が生(は)えない。
A rolling stone gathers no moss. (Proverb)
Japanese version of the English proverb
雨上(あめあ)がりの森(もり)では、苔(こけ)が美(うつく)しい緑色(みどりいろ)に輝(かがや)いている。
In the forest after rain, the moss shines in a beautiful green.
京都(きょうと)の西芳寺(さいほうじ)は苔寺(こけでら)とも呼(よ)ばれ、境内(けいだい)を覆(おお)う苔(こけ)の美(うつく)しさで知(し)られています。
Saihoji Temple in Kyoto is also called the Moss Temple and is known for the beauty of moss covering its grounds.

Moss carries strong aesthetic associations in Japan — age, stillness, and the beauty of things that take time (侘(わ)び寂(さ)び).

COLLOCATIONS:

  • 苔(こけ)が生(は)える - moss grows (on something)
  • 苔(こけ)に覆(おお)われる - to be covered in moss
  • 苔(こけ)むす - to become mossy with age (literary; the phrase in the national anthem, 「苔(こけ)のむすまで」, means 'until the stones grow moss')

TYPES:

  • 蘚類(せんるい) (true mosses)
  • 苔類(たいるい) (liverworts)
  • 地衣類(ちいるい) (lichens - often lumped in colloquially)

COMPOUNDS:

  • 苔玉(こけだま) (moss ball, a decorative planting)
  • 苔寺(こけでら) (moss temple, the nickname of 西芳寺(さいほうじ) in Kyoto)

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Moss is cultivated rather than removed in a 日本庭園(にほんていえん), where a 苔(こけ)-covered 石(いし) or 庭(にわ) signals a garden that has been tended for generations. Note that the English proverb 'a rolling stone gathers no moss' is used in Japanese with the same wording but often the opposite reading: for many speakers it praises the moss, not the motion.
