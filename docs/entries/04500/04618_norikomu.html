1. to board, to get into
To get on or into a vehicle, ship, or aircraft.
電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)り込(こ)んだ。
I got on the train.
子供(こども)たちが車(くるま)に乗(の)り込(こ)んだ。
The children got into the car.
満員(まんいん)のバスに無理(むり)やり乗(の)り込(こ)んだ。
I forced my way onto the crowded bus.
駅(えき)に着(つ)いたら、すぐに新幹線(しんかんせん)に乗(の)り込(こ)んでください。
When you arrive at the station, please board the bullet train right away.
荷物(にもつ)を持(も)って急(いそ)いでタクシーに乗(の)り込(こ)んだが、道(みち)が混(こ)んでいて間(ま)に合(あ)わなかった。
I grabbed my luggage and hurried into the taxi, but the roads were congested and I didn't make it in time.
2. to march into, to go into boldly
To enter a place with determination, often to confront someone or deal with a situation.
怒(おこ)った客(きゃく)が店(みせ)に乗(の)り込(こ)んできた。
An angry customer marched into the store.
一人(ひとり)で相手(あいて)の会社(かいしゃ)に乗(の)り込(こ)んだ。
I marched into the other party's company alone.
警察(けいさつ)が犯人(はんにん)のいる建物(たてもの)に乗(の)り込(こ)んだ。
The police stormed into the building where the suspect was hiding.
父(ちち)は文句(もんく)を言(い)うために学校(がっこう)に乗(の)り込(こ)んでいった。
My father went marching into the school to complain.
記者(きしゃ)たちが真実(しんじつ)を明(あき)らかにするために市役所(しやくしょ)に乗(の)り込(こ)んで、担当者(たんとうしゃ)に説明(せつめい)を求(もと)めた。
The reporters marched into city hall to uncover the truth and demanded an explanation from the person in charge.

乗(の)り込(こ)む is an intransitive verb (自動詞(じどうし)) meaning to board a vehicle or enter boldly.

COMPOUND STRUCTURE:

  • 乗(の)る (to ride) + 込(こ)む (into) → to get into (a vehicle)

ASPECT (ている):
乗(の)り込(こ)んでいる indicates a state of being aboard or having entered.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)り込(こ)む (board a train)
  • 車(くるま)に乗(の)り込(こ)む (get into a car)
  • 敵地(てきち)に乗(の)り込(こ)む (march into enemy territory)
  • ～に乗(の)り込(こ)んでくる (come marching into~)

NUANCE:
When used for entering a place (not a vehicle), implies determination, boldness, or sometimes aggression. Different from simple 入(はい)る.
