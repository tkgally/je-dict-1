1. to become moss-covered; to grow mossy
For stones, ground, buildings, or other surfaces to become covered with moss over time. Often used poetically to evoke age, tranquility, and the passage of time.
庭(にわ)の石(いし)が苔(こけ)むしている。
The stones in the garden are covered in moss.
古(ふる)い石段(いしだん)が苔(こけ)むして滑(すべ)りやすくなっている。
The old stone steps have become mossy and slippery.
何百年(なんびゃくねん)も経(た)った石垣(いしがき)が苔(こけ)むして、歴史(れきし)の重(おも)みを感(かん)じさせる。
The stone wall, hundreds of years old, is covered in moss, giving a sense of the weight of history.

A poetic and literary verb evoking the passage of time and natural beauty. Famous from the Japanese national anthem 君(きみ)が代(よ): "苔(こけ)のむすまで" (until the pebbles grow into moss-covered boulders).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 苔(こけ)むした石(いし): a moss-covered stone
  • 苔(こけ)むした屋根(やね): a mossy roof
  • 苔(こけ)むすまで: until moss grows (i.e., for an eternity)

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Moss holds special aesthetic value in Japanese culture, symbolizing age, tranquility, and wabi-sabi. 苔(こけ)寺(でら) (Saihoji temple in Kyoto) is famous for its moss garden.

GRAMMAR:
Godan verb (す row). Conjugates: 苔(こけ)むさない, 苔(こけ)むして, 苔(こけ)むします. The past participle 苔(こけ)むした is commonly used as a modifier.
