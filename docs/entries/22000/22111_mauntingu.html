1. one-upmanship; asserting social dominance; showing off superiority
The behavior of trying to establish oneself as superior to others in conversation or social situations, often through subtle boasting, name-dropping, or belittling others' achievements. A modern buzzword in Japanese social commentary.
それはマウンティングだよ。
That's just one-upmanship.
SNSでマウンティングする人(ひと)が多(おお)い。
There are a lot of people who try to one-up others on social media.
自分(じぶん)の子供(こども)の成績(せいせき)を自慢(じまん)するのは、ただのマウンティングにしか聞(き)こえない。
Bragging about your child's grades just sounds like one-upmanship.

Originally from animal behavior terminology (mounting to establish dominance), this loanword gained wide currency in Japanese from around 2015 as a buzzword describing competitive social behavior among humans. It is now commonly used in casual conversation, social media, and pop psychology.

The abbreviated form マウント is also very common, especially in the phrase マウントを取(と)る (to assert dominance).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • マウンティングする: to engage in one-upmanship
  • マウントを取(と)る: to assert dominance, to one-up someone
  • マウンティング女子(じょし): women who one-up each other
  • 無意識(むいしき)のマウンティング: unconscious one-upmanship

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 自慢(じまん): boasting — straightforward bragging, while マウンティング implies a competitive dynamic
  • マウント: mount — shortened form used in the same way
  • 見栄(みえ)を張(は)る: to put on airs — trying to look better than one is, but without the competitive element
