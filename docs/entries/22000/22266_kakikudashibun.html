A term from classical Japanese education. In kanbun study, the original Chinese text is first annotated with reading marks (返(かえ)り点(てん) and 送(おく)り仮名(がな)), then rewritten as 書(か)き下(くだ)し文(ぶん) — a version readable as Japanese.

KANBUN READING PROCESS:
1. 白文(はくぶん): the original Chinese text without annotations
2. 訓読(くんどく)文(ぶん): text with reading marks added
3. 書(か)き下(くだ)し文(ぶん): the full Japanese-order rendering
4. 現代語訳(げんだいごやく): modern Japanese translation

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 書(か)き下(くだ)し文(ぶん)にする: to render into Japanese reading order
  • 書(か)き下(くだ)し文(ぶん)に直(なお)す: to convert to Japanese reading order

USAGE:
This term is encountered mainly in 国語(こくご) (Japanese language) education at the high school level, where students study 漢文(かんぶん) (classical Chinese texts) as part of the curriculum.
