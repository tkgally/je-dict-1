1. warding off evil; exorcism; purification from bad luck
A ritual or act performed to drive away bad luck or evil spirits, typically at a shrine or temple. Commonly done during unlucky years (厄年) or after a series of misfortunes.
神社(じんじゃ)で厄払(やくばら)いをした。
I had a purification ritual done at a shrine.
今年(ことし)は厄年(やくどし)なので厄払(やくばら)いに行(い)く予定(よてい)だ。
This year is an unlucky year, so I plan to go for a purification ritual.
悪(わる)いことが続(つづ)いたので、家族(かぞく)みんなで厄払(やくばら)いの祈祷(きとう)を受(う)けることにした。
Since bad things kept happening, our whole family decided to receive a purification prayer.

A traditional Japanese practice of seeking spiritual purification, most commonly performed at Shinto shrines. It is especially common during 厄年(やくどし) (unlucky years), which are specific ages believed to bring misfortune (e.g., 25 and 42 for men, 19 and 33 for women).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 厄払(やくばら)いをする: to have a purification done
  • 厄払(やくばら)いの祈祷(きとう): purification prayer
  • 厄払(やくばら)いに行(い)く: to go for purification

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Many Japanese visit shrines at the start of the new year or around 節分(せつぶん) (early February) for 厄払(やくばら)い. The 豆(まめ)まき (bean-throwing) ceremony at 節分(せつぶん) is itself a form of 厄払(やくばら)い.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • お{祓(はら)い}: purification ritual — broader term, not limited to bad luck
  • 厄除(やくよ)け: protection against evil — focuses on prevention rather than removal
