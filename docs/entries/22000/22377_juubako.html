重箱(じゅうばこ)

じゅうばこ
noun
tiered box; stacking food box
1. tiered box; stacking food box; jubako
A set of square lacquered boxes that stack on top of each other, traditionally used for serving special occasion foods such as osechi (New Year dishes). Typically comes in sets of two to five tiers.
重箱(じゅうばこ)に料理(りょうり)を詰(つ)める。
To pack food into a tiered box.
三段(さんだん)の重箱(じゅうばこ)を用意(ようい)した。
I prepared a three-tiered box.
正月(しょうがつ)には家族(かぞく)みんなで重箱(じゅうばこ)に詰(つ)めたおせち料理(りょうり)を囲(かこ)む。
At New Year's, the whole family gathers around osechi dishes packed in tiered boxes.

A traditional Japanese container consisting of stackable square boxes, usually lacquered. Most strongly associated with おせち料理(りょうり) (New Year dishes) but also used for picnic foods and other special occasions.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 重箱(じゅうばこ)に詰(つ)める: to pack into a tiered box
  • 三段(さんだん)重(じゅう): three-tier box (abbreviated form)
  • 重箱(じゅうばこ)読(よ)み: jubako reading — a kanji compound read with on'yomi + kun'yomi (a linguistics term named after this word)
  • 漆(うるし)塗(ぬ)りの重箱(じゅうばこ): lacquered tiered box

CULTURAL NOTE:
The traditional number of tiers is five (or four, avoiding the unlucky number), though three-tier sets are most common today. Each tier of an おせち重箱(じゅうばこ) traditionally holds specific types of dishes. The word also appears in the linguistic term 重箱(じゅうばこ)読(よ)み, describing kanji compounds where the first character uses on'yomi and the second uses kun'yomi — just like 重(じゅう)(on) + 箱(ばこ)(kun).

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 弁当箱(べんとうばこ): lunch box — a single-compartment everyday container, unlike the stacking 重箱(じゅうばこ)
  • お重(じゅう): abbreviation of 重箱(じゅうばこ), very common in spoken Japanese
