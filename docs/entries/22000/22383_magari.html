1. renting a room (in someone's house); subletting; lodging
Renting a room within someone else's home or existing space, rather than renting an entire apartment or house. Can also refer to borrowing part of a commercial space.
間借(まが)りで暮(く)らしている。
I'm living in a rented room.
知人(ちじん)の家(いえ)に間借(まが)りする。
To rent a room at an acquaintance's house.
学生(がくせい)の頃(ころ)は大家(おおや)さんの家(いえ)の二階(にかい)を間借(まが)りしていた。
When I was a student, I rented the second floor of the landlord's house.

Describes renting part of a space, not the whole unit. Traditionally refers to renting a room in someone's home, a common arrangement in older Japan and still seen today. Recently also used for businesses sharing commercial space (e.g., a pop-up restaurant operating inside another restaurant's space).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 間借(まが)りする: to rent a room / to sublet
  • 間借(まが)り人(にん): lodger / tenant
  • 間借(まが)り生活(せいかつ): life as a lodger
  • 間借(まが)りカレー: pop-up curry restaurant (operating inside another space)
  • 間借(まが)り営業(えいぎょう): operating out of a shared/borrowed space

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 下宿(げしゅく): boarding house / lodging — a house specifically set up to take in boarders, with meals often included
  • 賃貸(ちんたい): rental — renting an entire apartment or house
  • 同居(どうきょ): living together — sharing a home, but implies a closer relationship

MODERN USAGE:
The term 間借(まが)り has gained new life in the food industry, where 間借(まが)り営業(えいぎょう) refers to a restaurant operating inside another establishment's space during off-hours, a popular low-cost way to start a food business.
