霊感(れいかん)

れいかん
noun
spiritual sensitivity; psychic ability; inspiration
1. spiritual sensitivity; psychic ability; sixth sense for the supernatural
The ability to sense or perceive spirits, ghosts, or supernatural phenomena.
彼女(かのじょ)は霊感(れいかん)が強(つよ)い。
She has a strong spiritual sensitivity.
霊感(れいかん)のある人(ひと)には幽霊(ゆうれい)が見(み)えるらしい。
Apparently, people with psychic ability can see ghosts.
あの古(ふる)い屋敷(やしき)に行(い)ったら、霊感(れいかん)がなくても何(なに)か感(かん)じるだろう。
If you go to that old mansion, you'd probably feel something even without psychic sensitivity.
2. inspiration; divine inspiration; creative flash
A sudden flash of insight or creative inspiration, as if coming from a higher source.
芸術家(げいじゅつか)にとって霊感(れいかん)は大切(たいせつ)だ。
Inspiration is important for artists.
散歩(さんぽ)をしているときに霊感(れいかん)を得(え)た。
I received inspiration while taking a walk.
ベートーヴェンは自然(しぜん)の中(なか)から音楽(おんがく)の霊感(れいかん)を受(う)けたと言(い)われている。
It is said that Beethoven received musical inspiration from nature.

Composed of 霊(れい) (spirit, soul) + 感(かん) (feeling, sense). The two senses — supernatural sensitivity and creative inspiration — both carry the idea of perceiving something beyond ordinary awareness.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 霊感(れいかん)が強(つよ)い — to have strong spiritual sensitivity
  • 霊感(れいかん)がある — to have psychic ability
  • 霊感(れいかん)を受(う)ける — to receive inspiration
  • 霊感(れいかん)を得(え)る — to gain inspiration
  • 霊感(れいかん)商法(しょうほう) — spiritualist fraud (a scam exploiting belief in spirits)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • インスピレーション — inspiration (loanword; neutral, no supernatural connotation)
  • 直感(ちょっかん) — intuition (rational/instinctive, not supernatural)
  • 第六感(だいろっかん) — sixth sense (can be supernatural or just strong intuition)
  • 超能力(ちょうのうりょく) — supernatural powers (broader; includes telekinesis, telepathy, etc.)

CULTURAL NOTE:
Belief in 霊感(れいかん) is common in Japanese popular culture. TV shows about haunted places and people with spiritual abilities are popular. 霊感(れいかん)商法(しょうほう) (spiritual fraud) — where scammers claim psychic powers to sell expensive items — is a recognized social problem.
