跳(と)び箱(ばこ)

とびばこ
noun
vaulting box; vaulting horse
1. vaulting box; vaulting horse
A piece of gymnastics equipment consisting of stacked rectangular sections that athletes run up to and vault over. A staple of Japanese school physical education.
跳(と)び箱(ばこ)を跳(と)ぶ。
To vault over the box.
八(はち)段(だん)の跳(と)び箱(ばこ)が跳(と)べるようになった。
I became able to vault over an eight-level box.
小学校(しょうがっこう)の体育(たいいく)で跳(と)び箱(ばこ)が苦手(にがて)だった思(おも)い出(で)がある。
I have memories of being bad at the vaulting box in elementary school gym class.

Composed of 跳(と)び (jumping) + 箱(ばこ) (box). A quintessential piece of Japanese school gym equipment. The box consists of stacked trapezoidal sections (typically 5-8 levels), and students run up to it, push off a springboard, and vault over it. The height is adjusted by adding or removing sections.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 跳(と)び箱(ばこ)を跳(と)ぶ: to vault over the box
  • 跳(と)び箱(ばこ)八(はち)段(だん): eight-level vaulting box
  • 跳(と)び箱(ばこ)が得意(とくい): to be good at vaulting
  • 跳(と)び箱(ばこ)が苦手(にがて): to be bad at vaulting

TECHNIQUES:

  • 開脚(かいきゃく)跳(と)び: straddle vault (legs apart)
  • 閉脚(へいきゃく)跳(と)び: squat vault (legs together)
  • 台上(だいじょう)前転(ぜんてん): forward roll on top of the box

CULTURAL NOTE:
The 跳(と)び箱(ばこ) is a nearly universal experience in Japanese physical education. Being able to clear a high number of levels is a source of pride for children.
