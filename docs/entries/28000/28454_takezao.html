竹(たけ)ざお

たけざお
noun
bamboo pole
1. bamboo pole
A long, straight section of bamboo used as a pole — for laundry, fishing, vaulting, or supporting plants. Often written 竹竿(たけざお) with kanji.
竹(たけ)ざおで洗濯物(せんたくもの)を干(ほ)す。
To hang laundry on a bamboo pole.
長(なが)い竹(たけ)ざおの先(さき)に釣(つ)り糸(いと)をつけた。
I attached fishing line to the end of a long bamboo pole.
昔(むかし)は、庭(にわ)に立(た)てた竹(たけ)ざおに布団(ふとん)をかけて日(ひ)に当(あ)てたものだ。
In the old days, people would drape futons over bamboo poles in the yard to air them in the sun.

Compound of 竹(たけ) (bamboo) + 竿(さお) (pole, rod). Note the rendaku: さお → ざお. Often written with the kanji 竹竿(たけざお).

USAGE:
A traditional household and outdoor tool. Familiar Japanese sights: laundry hung on a 竹(たけ)ざお stretched between two posts, the long pole used by the 竹(たけ)ざお売(う)り (itinerant bamboo-pole seller, whose distinctive call was once common in residential neighborhoods).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 竹(たけ)ざおを立(た)てる: to set up a bamboo pole
  • 竹(たけ)ざおにかける: to hang on a bamboo pole
  • 釣(つ)り用(よう)の竹(たけ)ざお: a bamboo fishing pole
