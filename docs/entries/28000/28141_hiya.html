冷(ひ)や

ひや
noun
cold water; room-temperature sake
1. cold water; room-temperature (unwarmed) sake
A short word for something served cold or unheated. Refers to cold water (especially お冷(ひ)や at a restaurant) or to sake served at room temperature rather than warmed.
お冷(ひ)やをください。
Could I have some cold water, please?
今日(きょう)は酒(さけ)を冷(ひ)やで飲(の)む。
Today I'll drink the sake cold.
熱燗(あつかん)より冷(ひ)やのほうが好(す)きだ。
I prefer sake at room temperature over hot sake.

A noun from the verb 冷(ひ)やす (to cool). It refers to something served cold or unwarmed, and has two everyday uses.

MAIN USES:

  • お冷(ひ)や: a glass of cold water, especially the free water served at a restaurant
  • 冷(ひ)や: sake drunk at room temperature (or chilled), as opposed to heated sake

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 熱燗(あつかん): hot, warmed sake — the opposite way of serving sake
  • 冷(つめ)たい水(みず): cold water — the plain descriptive phrase
