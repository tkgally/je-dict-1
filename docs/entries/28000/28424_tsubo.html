ツボ

つぼ
noun
pressure point; the right spot; the key point
1. pressure point (in acupressure or massage)
A specific point on the body used in acupuncture, acupressure, and massage to relieve tension or pain. Often written in katakana.
肩(かた)のツボを押(お)す。
Press the pressure point on the shoulder.
足(あし)の裏(うら)にはたくさんのツボがある。
There are many pressure points on the soles of the feet.
疲(つか)れたときは首(くび)のツボをやさしく押(お)すと楽(らく)になる。
When you're tired, gently pressing the pressure points on the neck makes you feel better.
2. the right spot; the key point; what makes (someone) tick
Figuratively, the precise point that hits the mark — what triggers laughter, what works, or the essential point of a topic. Common in the idiom ツボに嵌(は)まる.
彼(かれ)のジョークがツボにはまった。
His joke really hit the spot (cracked me up).
この先生(せんせい)は試験(しけん)のツボをよく知(し)っている。
This teacher knows the key points of the exam very well.
プレゼンの成功(せいこう)は、聞(き)き手(て)のツボを押(お)さえているかどうかで決(き)まる。
The success of a presentation depends on whether you've grasped the key points for the audience.

Usually written in katakana (ツボ) in both senses, though the underlying kanji is 壺(つぼ). The acupuncture sense (sense 1) is the original; the figurative use (sense 2) extends from the idea of a precise spot that produces a result.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ツボを押(お)す: press a pressure point
  • ツボにはまる: hit the spot; crack (someone) up
  • ツボを押(お)さえる: grasp the key points
  • 笑(わら)いのツボ: what makes someone laugh; one's sense of humor

NOTE:
Not to be confused with 坪(つぼ) (a unit of area, ~3.3 m²) or 壺(つぼ) (a pot or jar).
