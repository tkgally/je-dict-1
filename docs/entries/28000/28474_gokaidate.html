五階建(ごかいだ)て

ごかいだて
noun
five-story building
1. five-story building; five-floor building
A building constructed with five stories. A standard example of the 〜階(かい)建(だ)て counter pattern, which describes how many floors a building has.
五階建(ごかいだ)てのアパートだ。
It's a five-story apartment building.
駅前(えきまえ)に新(あたら)しい五階建(ごかいだ)てのビルが建(た)った。
A new five-story building went up in front of the station.
古(ふる)い五階建(ごかいだ)ての団地(だんち)にはエレベーターがないことが多(おお)い。
Old five-story housing complexes often don't have elevators.

Compound of 五(ご) (five) + 階(かい) (floor counter) + 建(だ)て (constructed). One member of the productive 〜階(かい)建(だ)て pattern.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 五階建(ごかいだ)てのアパート: five-story apartment building
  • 五階建(ごかいだ)てのビル: five-story building
  • 五階建(ごかいだ)ての団地(だんち): five-story public housing

RELATED PATTERNS:

  • 二(に)階(かい)建(だ)て: two-story
  • 三(さん)階(かい)建(だ)て: three-story
  • 高層(こうそう)階(かい)建(だ)て / 高層(こうそう)ビル: high-rise building
  • 平屋(ひらや)建(だ)て: single-story (irregular — no 階(かい))

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Many older Japanese 団地(だんち) (public housing complexes) built in the 1960s-70s are five-story walk-ups, since buildings up to five floors were not required by law to have elevators.
