三猿(さんざる)

さんざる
noun
the three wise monkeys
1. the three wise monkeys (see-no-evil, hear-no-evil, speak-no-evil)
The famous trio of monkeys covering their eyes, ears, and mouth, representing the maxim 'see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil'. Most strongly associated with the carving at Nikko's Tosho-gu shrine.
日光(にっこう)で三猿(さんざる)の彫刻(ちょうこく)を見(み)た。
I saw the three wise monkeys carving in Nikko.
三猿(さんざる)の置(お)き物(もの)をお土産(みやげ)に買(か)った。
I bought a three-wise-monkeys figurine as a souvenir.
三猿(さんざる)は「見(み)ざる、聞(き)かざる、言(い)わざる」という教(おし)えを表(あらわ)している。
The three wise monkeys represent the teaching 'see not, hear not, speak not'.

Also called 三(さん)匹(びき)の猿(さる). The reading ざる} is a play on words: it sounds both like {猿(さる) (monkey, voiced as ざる in compounds) and like the archaic negative ending ~ざる ('not doing'), as in 見(み)ざる ('not seeing').

THE THREE PRINCIPLES:

  • 見(み)ざる: see no evil
  • 聞(き)かざる: hear no evil
  • 言(い)わざる: speak no evil

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
The most famous depiction is the 17th-century wood carving at 東照宮(とうしょうぐう) in 日光(にっこう). The motif has Buddhist origins and spread internationally, becoming a globally recognized symbol. Often used today to comment ironically on willful ignorance.
