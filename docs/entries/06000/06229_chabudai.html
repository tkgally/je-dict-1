ちゃぶ台(だい)

ちゃぶだい
noun
low dining table

Definitions

1. low dining table, chabudai
A short-legged Japanese table traditionally used for meals, around which family members would sit on the floor. Symbol of Showa-era family dining.

Examples

ちゃぶ台(だい)を囲(かこ)んで家族(かぞく)で食事(しょくじ)をした。
The family gathered around the chabudai for a meal.
祖父(そふ)の家(いえ)にはまだちゃぶ台(だい)がある。
There's still a chabudai at my grandfather's house.
昭和(しょうわ)の家庭(かてい)ではちゃぶ台(だい)が一般的(いっぱんてき)だった。
The chabudai was common in Showa-era households.

Notes

CHARACTERISTICS:

  • Short legs (typically 30-40 cm high)
  • Usually round or square
  • Can be folded and stored when not in use
  • Used while sitting on the floor (畳(たたみ) or cushions)

CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The ちゃぶ台(だい) became popular in the Meiji and Taisho periods and symbolizes 昭和(しょうわ)-era family life. It represents the image of the family gathered together for meals.

EXPRESSION: ちゃぶ台(だい)返(がえ)し (chabudai-gaeshi)
"Flipping the chabudai" - refers to dramatically overturning the table in anger, associated with the stereotypical strict father figure (頑固親父(がんこおやじ)) in Showa dramas. Now used metaphorically for sudden, dramatic rejection or reversal.

MODERN CONTEXT: Most Japanese households now use Western-style dining tables, but ちゃぶ台(だい) are still found in traditional settings and remain popular in the form of low 座卓(ざたく) (floor tables) for 鍋(なべ) (hot pot) gatherings.
