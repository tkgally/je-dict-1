1. to trample, to step on hard
To step on something forcefully, pressing it down with one's foot.
虫(むし)を踏(ふ)みつけてしまった。
I accidentally stepped on a bug.
落(お)ち葉(ば)を踏(ふ)みつけながら歩(ある)く。
Walking while treading on fallen leaves.
怒(おこ)った子供(こども)が地面(じめん)を足(あし)で踏(ふ)みつけていた。
The angry child was stomping on the ground with their feet.
2. to trample on (figurative)
To violate, disregard, or treat with contempt someone's rights, feelings, or dignity.
人(ひと)の尊厳(そんげん)を踏(ふ)みつけてはいけない。
You must not trample on people's dignity.
弱(よわ)い者(もの)の権利(けんり)を踏(ふ)みつけるような社会(しゃかい)であってはならない。
We must not be a society that tramples on the rights of the weak.
相手(あいて)の気持(きも)ちを踏(ふ)みつけるような発言(はつげん)は許(ゆる)されない。
Remarks that trample on the other person's feelings are unacceptable.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). No pair verb.

COMPOUND STRUCTURE: 踏(ふ)む (to step on) + つける (to attach/press). The compound emphasizes pressing down firmly.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 虫(むし)を踏(ふ)みつける (step on a bug)
  • 足(あし)で踏(ふ)みつける (trample with one's foot)
  • 権利(けんり)を踏(ふ)みつける (trample on rights)
  • 尊厳(そんげん)を踏(ふ)みつける (trample on dignity)

Usage note: The figurative sense is common in formal writing and speeches about human rights or ethics.
