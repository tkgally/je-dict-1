1. to bite into, to dig into
To physically cut or sink into a surface, like a rope digging into skin or teeth biting into food.
ベルトが腰(こし)に食(く)い込(こ)む。
The belt digs into my waist.
ロープが肩(かた)に食(く)い込(こ)んで痛(いた)い。
The rope is digging into my shoulder and it hurts.
重(おも)い荷物(にもつ)のひもが指(ゆび)に食(く)い込(こ)んで跡(あと)がついた。
The strap of the heavy bag dug into my fingers and left marks.
2. to cut into, to eat into
To extend into or encroach upon something, especially time, budget, or territory.
残業(ざんぎょう)が私生活(しせいかつ)に食(く)い込(こ)んでいる。
Overtime work is eating into my personal life.
工事(こうじ)が予算(よさん)に食(く)い込(こ)んできた。
The construction has started to eat into the budget.
会議(かいぎ)が長引(ながび)いて昼休(ひるやす)みの時間(じかん)に食(く)い込(こ)んでしまった。
The meeting ran long and ate into the lunch break.
3. to make inroads into, to gain a foothold in
To penetrate or establish a presence in a market, industry, or area of influence.
新規(しんき)市場(しじょう)に食(く)い込(こ)みたい。
We want to make inroads into a new market.
この会社(かいしゃ)は海外(かいがい)市場(しじょう)に食(く)い込(こ)むことに成功(せいこう)した。
This company succeeded in making inroads into overseas markets.
中小(ちゅうしょう)メーカーが大手(おおて)の牙城(がじょう)に食(く)い込(こ)むのは簡単(かんたん)ではない。
It's not easy for small manufacturers to make inroads into the territory of major companies.

USAGE: 食(く)い込(こ)む is a compound verb combining 食(く)う (to bite, to eat) and 込(こ)む (into). It expresses penetrating or cutting into something, either physically or figuratively.

TRANSITIVITY: Intransitive (自動詞(じどうし)). Takes に for what is being penetrated.

ASPECT: 食(く)い込(こ)んでいる indicates an ongoing state of something cutting or biting into something else.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 肉(にく)に食(く)い込(こ)む (to dig into flesh)
  • 予算(よさん)に食(く)い込(こ)む (to cut into the budget)
  • 市場(しじょう)に食(く)い込(こ)む (to make inroads into a market)
  • 睡眠時間(すいみんじかん)に食(く)い込(こ)む (to eat into sleep time)

REGISTER: Neutral. Common in both everyday and business contexts.
