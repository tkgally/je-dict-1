1. to take the plunge, to make a decisive move
To make a firm decision to do something significant, especially after hesitation.
ついに転職(てんしょく)に踏(ふ)み切(き)った。
I finally took the plunge and changed jobs.
起業(きぎょう)に踏(ふ)み切(き)るには勇気(ゆうき)がいる。
It takes courage to take the plunge into starting a business.
長年(ながねん)悩(なや)んだ末(すえ)、海外(かいがい)移住(いじゅう)に踏(ふ)み切(き)ることにした。
After years of deliberation, I decided to take the plunge and move abroad.
2. to take off, to jump
To push off the ground with one's foot to jump or leap, as in athletics.
左足(ひだりあし)で踏(ふ)み切(き)って跳(と)んだ。
I pushed off with my left foot and jumped.
選手(せんしゅ)は力強(ちからづよ)く踏(ふ)み切(き)って跳(と)んだ。
The athlete pushed off powerfully and jumped.
踏(ふ)み切(き)る位置(いち)がずれて、ファウルになってしまった。
The takeoff position was off, and it ended up being a foul.

USAGE: 踏(ふ)み切(き)る is a compound verb combining 踏(ふ)む (to step on) and 切(き)る (to cut, completion). The figurative sense (sense 1) of making a decisive commitment is more common in everyday use.

TRANSITIVITY: Intransitive (自動詞(じどうし)). Takes に for the action one decides to take.

ASPECT: 踏(ふ)み切(き)った emphasizes completion of the decision. 踏(ふ)み切(き)れない indicates inability to make the decision.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 決断(けつだん)に踏(ふ)み切(き)る (to make a firm decision)
  • 購入(こうにゅう)に踏(ふ)み切(き)る (to take the plunge to purchase)
  • 独立(どくりつ)に踏(ふ)み切(き)る (to decide to become independent)

RELATED: 踏切(ふみきり) (railroad crossing) uses the same kanji but is a different word.

NOTE: Often used with ついに or 思(おも)い切(き)って to emphasize the decisiveness.
