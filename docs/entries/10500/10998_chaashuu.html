チャーシュー

ちゃーしゅー
noun
char siu, roast pork
1. char siu, braised/roast pork
Seasoned pork that is braised or roasted, commonly served as a ramen topping or in rice bowls. Adapted from Chinese char siu.
チャーシューをトッピングする。
To add char siu as a topping.
チャーシュー麺(めん)の大盛(おおも)りをください。
I'll have a large char siu ramen, please.
この店(みせ)のチャーシューは低温(ていおん)で時間(じかん)をかけて調理(ちょうり)しているのでとても柔(やわ)らかい。
This restaurant's char siu is very tender because it's slow-cooked at a low temperature.

ORIGIN:
From Chinese 叉焼(チャーシャオ) (chāshāo). The Japanese version is typically braised in soy sauce and mirin rather than roasted with a sweet glaze as in Chinese cooking.

USAGE:
In Japan, チャーシュー most commonly refers to pork slices served on ramen. Many ramen shops offer extra チャーシュー as a topping. It is also served over rice as チャーシュー丼(どん).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • チャーシュー麺(めん): char siu ramen (with extra pork)
  • チャーシュー丼(どん): char siu rice bowl
  • 自家製(じかせい)チャーシュー: house-made char siu
  • 炙(あぶ)りチャーシュー: seared/torched char siu
