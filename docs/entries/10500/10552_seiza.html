正座(せいざ)

せいざ
noun
formal Japanese sitting (kneeling)
1. formal Japanese sitting, kneeling position
The traditional Japanese sitting posture of kneeling with legs folded underneath, sitting on one's heels. Considered the most formal way to sit on tatami.
正座(せいざ)して待(ま)っていた。
I was waiting in formal sitting position.
長(なが)い時間(じかん)正座(せいざ)すると足(あし)が痺(しび)れる。
Your legs go numb if you sit in seiza for a long time.
茶道(さどう)では正座(せいざ)が基本(きほん)の姿勢(しせい)とされている。
In the tea ceremony, seiza is considered the standard posture.

KANJI:
正(せい) (correct/proper) + 座(ざ) (sitting). Literally "correct sitting."

USAGE:
正座(せいざ)する is the verb form (to sit in seiza). This posture is expected in formal settings like 茶道(さどう) (tea ceremony), 書道(しょどう) (calligraphy), and 武道(ぶどう) (martial arts), as well as at funerals and formal gatherings on 畳(たたみ).

Many modern Japanese people find 正座(せいざ) uncomfortable for extended periods, and it is common for legs to fall asleep (足(あし)が痺(しび)れる).

NOTE ON HOMOPHONES:
Do not confuse with 星座(せいざ) (constellation), which has the same reading.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 正座(せいざ)する: to sit in seiza
  • 正座(せいざ)を崩(くず)す: to shift out of seiza into a more relaxed position
  • 足(あし)が痺(しび)れる: legs go numb (from seiza)
