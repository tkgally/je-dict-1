取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)

とりこしぐろう
noun
unnecessary worry, needless anxiety
1. unnecessary worry, needless anxiety
Worrying about something that hasn't happened yet and may never happen. Borrowing trouble.
それは取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)だよ。
You're worrying for nothing.
取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)をしても仕方(しかた)がない。
There's no point in worrying about things that haven't happened.
心配(しんぱい)していたけど、結局(けっきょく)取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)だった。
I was worried, but in the end it turned out to be needless anxiety.

USAGE:
Commonly used to reassure someone who is worrying unnecessarily, or to reflect on one's own past worries that turned out to be unfounded. Often appears as 取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)をする (to worry needlessly) or 取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)だった (it was needless worry).

ETYMOLOGY:
From 取(と)り越(こ)す (to bring forward, to anticipate) + 苦労(くろう) (hardship, worry). Literally "suffering brought forward" — worrying about something before it happens.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)をする: to worry needlessly
  • 取(と)り越(こ)し苦労(くろう)に終(お)わる: to end up being needless worry
