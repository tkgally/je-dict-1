回(まわ)り道(みち)

まわりみち
noun
detour; roundabout way
1. detour; roundabout route
A longer, indirect route taken instead of the most direct path, whether by necessity (road closure) or by choice.
工事(こうじ)中(ちゅう)なので回(まわ)り道(みち)をした。
There was construction, so I took a detour.
景色(けしき)のいい回(まわ)り道(みち)を楽(たの)しんだ。
I enjoyed a scenic detour.
急(いそ)いでいるのに回(まわ)り道(みち)をさせられて困(こま)った。
I was in a hurry and was frustrated at being forced to take a detour.
2. roundabout approach; indirect way of doing something
A figurative detour — taking a longer or less efficient approach to achieving a goal. Can imply either inefficiency or a valuable learning experience.
人生(じんせい)に回(まわ)り道(みち)はつきものだ。
Detours are a natural part of life.
回(まわ)り道(みち)のように見(み)えても、その経験(けいけん)はきっと役(やく)に立(た)つ。
Even if it seems like a detour, that experience will surely prove useful.
効率(こうりつ)を求(もと)めすぎると、かえって回(まわ)り道(みち)になることもある。
Seeking too much efficiency can actually end up being a roundabout approach.

USAGE:
回(まわ)り道(みち) can be used as a noun or as a suru-verb (回(まわ)り道(みち)する — to take a detour). The figurative sense is very common, especially when reflecting on life choices or career paths.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 回(まわ)り道(みち)をする (take a detour)
  • 回(まわ)り道(みち)になる (turn out to be a detour)
  • 人生(じんせい)の回(まわ)り道(みち) (life's detours)
  • 少(すこ)し回(まわ)り道(みち)する (take a slight detour)

SIMILAR WORDS:
迂回(うかい) means "detour" in a more formal or technical sense, often used for traffic or military contexts. 遠回(とおまわ)り means "going the long way around" and is close in meaning but emphasizes distance.
