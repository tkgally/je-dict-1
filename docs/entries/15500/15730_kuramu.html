1. to feel dizzy; to have one's head spin
To experience a sensation of dizziness or lightheadedness, often from looking down from a height or standing up suddenly.
高(たか)い所(ところ)から下(した)を見(み)て目(め)が眩(くら)んだ。
I looked down from a high place and felt dizzy.
立(た)ち上(あ)がったら目(め)が眩(くら)んだ。
I felt dizzy when I stood up.
急(きゅう)に立(た)ち上(あ)がると目(め)が眩(くら)むことがあるので気(き)をつけてください。
Please be careful, as standing up suddenly can cause dizziness.
2. to be dazzled; to be blinded; to lose one's judgment
To have one's vision or judgment impaired, either by bright light or figuratively by greed, desire, or temptation.
太陽(たいよう)の光(ひかり)に目(め)が眩(くら)んだ。
I was blinded by the sunlight.
金(かね)に目(め)が眩(くら)んで悪事(あくじ)に手(て)を染(そ)めた。
Blinded by money, he turned to crime.
欲(よく)に目(め)が眩(くら)んで大切(たいせつ)なものを見失(みうしな)ってはいけない。
You must not be blinded by greed and lose sight of what's important.

眩(くら)む is almost always used in the pattern 目(め)が眩(くら)む, meaning to feel dizzy or to be dazzled/blinded.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive)
  • Pattern: 目(め)が眩(くら)む

ASPECT (ている):
目(め)が眩(くら)んでいる indicates an ongoing state of dizziness or being dazzled.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 目(め)が眩(くら)む: to feel dizzy; to be dazzled
  • 金(かね)に目(め)が眩(くら)む: to be blinded by money
  • 欲(よく)に目(め)が眩(くら)む: to be blinded by greed
  • 高(たか)い所(ところ)で目(め)が眩(くら)む: to feel dizzy at a high place

USAGE BY SENSE:
Sense 1 (physical dizziness): triggered by heights, standing up suddenly, bright light.
Sense 2 (figurative): losing good judgment due to greed or temptation. Very common idiom.

Related Words

Related: まぶしい (dazzling, blinding)
