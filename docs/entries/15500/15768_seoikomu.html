背負(せお)い込(こ)む

せおいこむ
verb
to take on; to shoulder; to burden oneself with
1. to take on; to shoulder; to burden oneself with
To take on a heavy responsibility, burden, or problem — often more than one should or can handle. A compound verb formed from 背負う (to carry on one's back) + 込む (into/deeply). Almost always used figuratively for emotional, financial, or social burdens.
一人(ひとり)で全部(ぜんぶ)背負(せお)い込(こ)まないで。
Don't take everything on by yourself.
彼(かれ)は多額(たがく)の借金(しゃっきん)を背負(せお)い込(こ)んでしまった。
He ended up saddled with a large debt.
責任感(せきにんかん)が強(つよ)い人(ひと)ほど、問題(もんだい)を背負(せお)い込(こ)みやすい。
The stronger one's sense of responsibility, the more likely they are to burden themselves with problems.

USAGE:
背負(せお)い込(こ)む implies an excessive or overwhelming burden, often self-imposed. It is frequently used with 一人(ひとり)で (by oneself) to describe someone who tries to handle everything alone.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 一人(ひとり)で背負(せお)い込(こ)む (take it all on alone)
  • 借金(しゃっきん)を背負(せお)い込(こ)む (be saddled with debt)
  • 責任(せきにん)を背負(せお)い込(こ)む (shoulder responsibility)
  • 悩(なや)みを背負(せお)い込(こ)む (burden oneself with worries)

SIMILAR WORDS:
背負(せお)う means "to carry on one's back" and can be either literal or figurative. 背負(せお)い込(こ)む adds the nuance of taking on too much or getting deeply burdened. 抱(かか)え込(こ)む also means "to take on" problems but implies holding them close rather than carrying them on one's back.
