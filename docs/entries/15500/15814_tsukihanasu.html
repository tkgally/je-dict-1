突(つ)き放(はな)す

つきはなす
verb
to push away; to reject coldly
1. to push away; to shove off
To physically push someone or something away from oneself with force.
相手(あいて)を突(つ)き放(はな)した。
I pushed the other person away.
彼(かれ)はしがみつく子供(こども)を突(つ)き放(はな)して逃(に)げた。
He shoved away the child clinging to him and ran.
犯人(はんにん)は警備員(けいびいん)を突(つ)き放(はな)して走(はし)り去(さ)った。
The culprit shoved the security guard away and ran off.
2. to reject coldly; to cast off; to refuse to help
To emotionally distance oneself from someone by refusing their pleas, requests, or dependence. Used figuratively to describe a cold or detached attitude.
冷(つめ)たく突(つ)き放(はな)すような言(い)い方(かた)をした。
He spoke in a cold, dismissive way.
助(たす)けを求(もと)めたが、突(つ)き放(はな)されてしまった。
I asked for help, but was coldly rejected.
子供(こども)の自立(じりつ)のために、時(とき)には突(つ)き放(はな)すことも必要(ひつよう)だ。
For a child's independence, it's sometimes necessary to let go and not help.

USAGE:
突(つ)き放(はな)す is a godan verb. It is a compound of 突(つ)く (to thrust) + 放(はな)す (to release). The physical sense (sense 1) involves forceful pushing, while the figurative sense (sense 2) describes emotional coldness or deliberate distancing.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 冷(つめ)たく突(つ)き放(はな)す (coldly reject)
  • 突(つ)き放(はな)すような態度(たいど) (dismissive attitude)
  • 突(つ)き放(はな)すような言(い)い方(かた) (cold way of speaking)

SIMILAR WORDS:
押(お)しのける means to push aside, focusing on clearing a path. 突(つ)き放(はな)す emphasizes the force and finality of the action. In the figurative sense, 見捨(みす)てる (to abandon) is stronger and more permanent, while 突(つ)き放(はな)す can be a temporary or intentional act of tough love.
