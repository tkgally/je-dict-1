立(た)ち回(まわ)り

たちまわり
noun
maneuvering; fight scene; conduct
1. maneuvering; managing; conduct in social situations
The way a person handles themselves, navigates situations, or manages interactions — especially in business or politics. Implies skillful or strategic behavior.
彼(かれ)は立(た)ち回(まわ)りがうまい。
He's good at maneuvering in social situations.
会議(かいぎ)での立(た)ち回(まわ)りが上手(じょうず)な人(ひと)は出世(しゅっせ)しやすい。
People who are good at handling themselves in meetings tend to advance quickly.
関係者(かんけいしゃ)の間(あいだ)をうまく立(た)ち回(まわ)りして、プロジェクトを成功(せいこう)させた。
By skillfully maneuvering among the stakeholders, they made the project a success.
2. fight scene; sword-fighting scene; stage combat
A choreographed fight scene in kabuki, film, or theater. Originally from kabuki terminology for on-stage combat.
この映画(えいが)の立(た)ち回(まわ)りシーンは迫力(はくりょく)がある。
The fight scenes in this movie are powerful.
歌舞伎(かぶき)の立(た)ち回(まわ)りは型(かた)が決(き)まっていて美(うつく)しい。
The fight scenes in kabuki follow set forms and are beautiful.
俳優(はいゆう)たちは立(た)ち回(まわ)りの練習(れんしゅう)に何(なん)ヶ月(げつ)もかけたそうだ。
Apparently the actors spent months practicing the fight choreography.

USAGE:
Sense 1 is used with verbs like うまい or 上手(じょうず) to describe someone skilled at navigating social or professional situations. The verb form 立(た)ち回(まわ)る means "to maneuver" or "to get around."

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 立(た)ち回(まわ)りがうまい (good at maneuvering)
  • 立(た)ち回(まわ)りが下手(へた) (clumsy at handling situations)
  • 派手(はで)な立(た)ち回(まわ)り (showy fight scene)

SIMILAR WORDS:
根回(ねまわ)し refers specifically to behind-the-scenes groundwork to build consensus before a meeting. 立(た)ち回(まわ)り is broader and includes visible maneuvering during interactions themselves.
