1. to let die out; to extinguish; to discontinue
To allow something to come to an end or cease to exist, often through neglect or inaction. Used for traditions, lineages, fires, and other things that can be maintained or lost.
伝統(でんとう)を絶(た)やしてはいけない。
We must not let the tradition die out.
火(ひ)を絶(た)やさないように薪(まき)をくべた。
I added firewood so the fire wouldn't go out.
先祖(せんぞ)から続(つづ)く家系(かけい)を絶(た)やさないように、跡継(あとつ)ぎを探(さが)している。
They are looking for a successor so as not to let the family line that has continued from their ancestors die out.
2. to run out of; to use up the supply of
To use up or fail to maintain a stock of something. Often used in the negative form to mean keeping a constant supply.
笑顔(えがお)を絶(た)やさない。
She never stops smiling.
花(はな)を絶(た)やさず飾(かざ)っている。
She always keeps flowers on display without letting them run out.
あの店(みせ)はいつも客(きゃく)が絶(た)えないので、予約(よやく)なしでは入(はい)れない。
That restaurant always has a steady stream of customers, so you can't get in without a reservation.
Uses the intransitive form 絶(た)えない.

TRANSITIVITY: 絶(た)やす is transitive. The intransitive counterpart is 絶(た)える (to cease, to die out).

ASPECT (ている): 絶(た)やしている indicates an ongoing state of having let something lapse.

NEGATIVE FORM: Very frequently used in the negative (絶(た)やさない) to express maintaining something continuously: 笑顔(えがお)を絶(た)やさない (to always keep smiling).

PARTICLE PATTERNS:

  • [thing]を絶(た)やす (to let something die out)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 笑顔(えがお)を絶(た)やさない: to always keep smiling
  • 火(ひ)を絶(た)やさない: to keep the fire going
  • 伝統(でんとう)を絶(た)やす: to let a tradition die out
  • 花(はな)を絶(た)やさない: to always have flowers (on display)
