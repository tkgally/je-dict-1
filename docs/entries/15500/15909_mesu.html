召(め)す

めす
verb, godan
to eat; to drink; to wear (honorific)
1. to eat; to drink (honorific)
An honorific verb used to describe someone of high status eating or drinking. More elevated than 食(た)べる or 飲(の)む.
どうぞお召(め)し上(あ)がりください。
Please help yourself. (honorific: eat/drink)
お客様(きゃくさま)は何(なに)を召(め)し上(あ)がりますか。
What would you like to eat, sir/madam?
社長(しゃちょう)はもう昼食(ちゅうしょく)を召(め)し上(あ)がりましたか。
Has the president already had lunch?
2. to wear; to put on (honorific)
An honorific verb for wearing clothes or accessories. Often heard in retail settings as お召(め)しになる.
素敵(すてき)なドレスをお召(め)しですね。
What a lovely dress you're wearing.
こちらのコートをお召(め)しになりませんか。
Would you like to try on this coat?
寒(さむ)いので、どうぞ上着(うわぎ)をお召(め)しになってください。
It's cold, so please put on a jacket.
3. to summon; to call for (honorific)
An honorific verb meaning to summon or call someone. Used in formal or historical contexts.
王(おう)が家臣(かしん)を召(め)した。
The king summoned his retainer.
殿様(とのさま)がお医者(いしゃ)を召(め)した。
The lord summoned a doctor.
天皇(てんのう)が臣下(しんか)を御前(ごぜん)に召(め)して意見(いけん)を求(もと)めたと伝(つた)えられている。
It is said that the emperor summoned his subjects before him to seek their opinions.

USAGE: 召(め)す is an honorific (sonkeigo) verb with several meanings depending on context. The most common modern uses are お召(め)し上(あ)がる (to eat/drink, honorific) and お召(め)しになる (to wear, honorific).

The "summon" meaning is mostly used in historical or formal contexts.

ASPECT (ている): お召(め)しになっている describes the state of wearing something.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • お召(め)し上(あ)がる: to eat/drink (honorific compound)
  • お召(め)しになる: to wear (honorific form)
  • お召(め)し物(もの): clothing (honorific)

PARTICLE PATTERNS:

  • [food/drink]を召(め)し上(あ)がる (to eat/drink something)
  • [clothing]をお召(め)しになる (to wear something)
  • [person]を召(め)す (to summon someone)
