雨(あま)だれ

あまだれ
noun
raindrops (falling from eaves); dripping rain
1. raindrops falling from eaves or rooftops; dripping rain
Water dripping from the edge of a roof or eaves during or after rain. Can also refer to the sound or traces left by such dripping. Written as 雨垂れ in full kanji. A word with poetic and seasonal associations.
軒先(のきさき)から雨(あま)だれが落(お)ちている。
Raindrops are dripping from the eaves.
雨(あま)だれの音(おと)を聞(き)きながら眠(ねむ)りについた。
I fell asleep listening to the sound of dripping rain.
雨(あま)だれ石(いし)を穿(うが)つ、という諺(ことわざ)がある。
There is a proverb that says 'raindrops wear through stone.'

USAGE:
雨(あま)だれ has a gentle, atmospheric quality and often appears in literary descriptions of rainy days. The proverb 雨(あま)だれ石(いし)を穿(うが)つ (raindrops wear through stone) is the Japanese equivalent of 'constant dripping wears away the stone,' meaning persistence overcomes any obstacle.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 雨(あま)だれの音(おと) (the sound of dripping rain)
  • 雨(あま)だれが落(お)ちる (raindrops fall/drip)
  • 雨(あま)だれ石(いし)を穿(うが)つ (constant effort brings results — proverb)

SIMILAR WORDS:
雨粒(あまつぶ) (raindrop) refers to individual drops of rain falling from the sky, while 雨(あま)だれ specifically means water dripping from eaves or a roof edge.
