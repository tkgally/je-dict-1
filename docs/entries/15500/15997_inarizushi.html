稲荷寿司(いなりずし)

いなりずし
noun
inari sushi; sushi rice in fried tofu pouches
1. inari sushi; sushi rice stuffed in sweetened fried tofu pouches
A type of sushi consisting of vinegared rice packed inside pouches of deep-fried tofu (abura-age) that have been simmered in a sweet soy sauce mixture. A popular, affordable everyday sushi often found at convenience stores and bento shops.
お弁当(べんとう)に稲荷寿司(いなりずし)を入(い)れた。
I put inari sushi in the lunch box.
祖母(そぼ)の作(つく)る稲荷寿司(いなりずし)は甘(あま)くて美味(おい)しい。
The inari sushi my grandmother makes is sweet and delicious.
お花見(はなみ)のとき、手作(てづく)りの稲荷寿司(いなりずし)を持(も)って行(い)くのが我(わ)が家(や)の習慣(しゅうかん)だ。
It's our family tradition to bring homemade inari sushi when we go cherry blossom viewing.

USAGE:
Named after the Shinto deity 稲荷(いなり), whose fox messengers are said to be fond of fried tofu. Often shortened to おいなりさん in casual speech, with the honorific さん reflecting affection for the food. It is one of the most common types of sushi eaten at home and is a staple of bento culture.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • おいなりさん (casual/affectionate name for inari sushi)
  • 稲荷寿司(いなりずし)を作(つく)る (to make inari sushi)
  • 油揚(あぶらあ)げ (fried tofu pouch — the main wrapping ingredient)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 手巻(てま)き寿司(ずし): hand-rolled sushi — another casual, home-style sushi
  • 巻(ま)き寿司(ずし): rolled sushi — nori-wrapped cylindrical sushi
