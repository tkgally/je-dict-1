1. settlement; colonization
The act of moving to and settling in a new or undeveloped area, especially for farming or development purposes.
北海道(ほっかいどう)に入植(にゅうしょく)した。
They settled in Hokkaido.
明治(めいじ)時代(じだい)に多(おお)くの人々(ひとびと)が北海道(ほっかいどう)に入植(にゅうしょく)した。
Many people settled in Hokkaido during the Meiji era.
戦後(せんご)、政府(せいふ)の政策(せいさく)により満州(まんしゅう)からの引揚者(ひきあげしゃ)が各地(かくち)の開拓地(かいたくち)に入植(にゅうしょく)した。
After the war, returnees from Manchuria settled in pioneer lands across the country under government policy.

A compound of 入(にゅう) (to enter) and 植(しょく) (to plant, colonize). Refers to the act of settling in a new area, typically for agricultural development.

Historically important in Japan, especially regarding the settlement of Hokkaido during the Meiji era, Japanese emigration to Manchuria and other territories in the early 20th century, and postwar agricultural resettlement programs.

Also used in international contexts to describe colonization and settlement of territories.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 入植(にゅうしょく)者(しゃ): settler, colonist
  • 入植(にゅうしょく)地(ち): settlement, colony
  • 入植(にゅうしょく)事業(じぎょう): colonization project
  • 入植(にゅうしょく)を開始(かいし)する: to begin settlement

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 開拓(かいたく): pioneering, reclamation — emphasizes the act of developing undeveloped land
  • 植民(しょくみん): colonization — stronger political and imperial connotations
  • 移住(いじゅう): migration, relocation — broader term for moving to a new place to live
