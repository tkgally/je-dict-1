1. whistleblowing; internal disclosure of wrongdoing
The act of an insider—typically an employee or member of an organization—reporting illegal activities, fraud, safety violations, or other misconduct to external authorities or the public.
内部告発(ないぶこくはつ)で不正(ふせい)が発覚(はっかく)した。
The fraud was uncovered through whistleblowing.
社員(しゃいん)が会社(かいしゃ)の違法行為(いほうこうい)を内部告発(ないぶこくはつ)した。
An employee blew the whistle on the company's illegal activities.
内部告発者(ないぶこくはつしゃ)を保護(ほご)する法律(ほうりつ)が整備(せいび)されつつある。
Laws to protect whistleblowers are gradually being put in place.

Refers specifically to disclosure by someone inside an organization, distinguishing it from external investigation or journalism. The word carries a serious tone and is used in legal, corporate, and media contexts.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 内部告発(ないぶこくはつ)する: to blow the whistle
  • 内部告発(ないぶこくはつ)を行(おこな)う: to carry out whistleblowing
  • 内部告発者(ないぶこくはつしゃ): whistleblower
  • 内部告発(ないぶこくはつ)制度(せいど): whistleblower system
  • 匿名(とくめい)の内部告発(ないぶこくはつ): anonymous whistleblowing

CULTURAL NOTE:
Japan enacted the Whistleblower Protection Act (公益通報者保護法(こうえきつうほうしゃほごほう)) in 2004 to shield whistleblowers from retaliation. Despite the law, cultural emphasis on group loyalty and harmony means whistleblowers often face social pressure.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 告発(こくはつ): accusation, indictment — broader term for any formal accusation, not limited to insiders
  • 通報(つうほう): reporting, notification — more neutral, used for reporting incidents to police or authorities
  • 密告(みっこく): secret tip-off, informing — carries a more negative connotation, suggesting betrayal
