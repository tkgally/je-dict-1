たばこ屋(や)

たばこや
noun
tobacco shop; cigarette shop
1. tobacco shop; cigarette shop
A small shop specializing in selling cigarettes, tobacco products, and often stamps and prepaid cards. A traditional feature of Japanese neighborhoods, though declining in number.
角(かど)にたばこ屋(や)がある。
There's a tobacco shop on the corner.
たばこ屋(や)で切手(きって)も売(う)っている。
They also sell stamps at the tobacco shop.
昔(むかし)はどの商店街(しょうてんがい)にもたばこ屋(や)があったが、今(いま)ではコンビニに取(と)って代(か)わられている。
In the past every shopping street had a tobacco shop, but now they've been replaced by convenience stores.

A compound of たばこ (tobacco/cigarettes) + 屋(や) (shop). Traditional たばこ屋(や) are small, often family-run shops typically located at street corners. They were once ubiquitous in Japan and served as neighborhood landmarks and informal gathering spots.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • たばこ屋(や)のおばあちゃん(おばあちゃん): the old lady at the tobacco shop
  • 町(まち)のたばこ屋(や): the neighborhood tobacco shop
  • たばこ屋(や)で買(か)う: to buy at the tobacco shop

CULTURAL NOTE:
Traditional たばこ屋(や) also sell 収入印紙(しゅうにゅういんし) (revenue stamps), 切手(きって) (postage stamps), and 宝(たから)くじ (lottery tickets). With the spread of convenience stores and vending machines, standalone tobacco shops have become increasingly rare, especially in urban areas.
