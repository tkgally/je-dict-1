1. bisque firing; unglazed pottery; earthenware
Pottery that has been fired without glaze, or the first firing of clay before glazing. The resulting ware is porous and has a rough, natural texture.
素焼(すや)きの植木鉢(うえきばち)は通気性(つうきせい)がよい。
Unglazed flower pots have good breathability.
釉薬(ゆうやく)をかける前(まえ)に素焼(すや)きをする。
You do a bisque firing before applying the glaze.
この土器(どき)は縄文時代(じょうもんじだい)の素焼(すや)きで、装飾(そうしょく)が施(ほどこ)されている。
This earthenware is an unglazed piece from the Jomon period, decorated with ornamentation.
2. plain grilling; grilling without seasoning
Cooking food, especially seafood or vegetables, by grilling without added sauce, seasoning, or oil, to bring out the ingredient's natural flavor.
海老(えび)の素焼(すや)きが美味(おい)しい。
Plain-grilled shrimp is delicious.
魚(さかな)は素焼(すや)きにして塩(しお)で食(た)べるのが一番(いちばん)だ。
Fish is best plain-grilled and eaten with salt.
野菜(やさい)を素焼(すや)きにすると、素材(そざい)の甘(あま)みが引(ひ)き出(だ)される。
When you plain-grill vegetables, the natural sweetness of the ingredients comes out.

Composed of 素(す) (plain, bare, unadorned) and 焼(や)き (firing, grilling). The 素(す) prefix indicates that nothing extra has been added — no glaze in ceramics, no seasoning in cooking.

USAGE:
In ceramics, 素焼(すや)き refers to both the process and the product. It is the essential first step in making glazed pottery. In cooking, 素焼(すや)き emphasizes simplicity and respect for the ingredient's natural taste — a core value in Japanese cuisine.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 素焼(すや)きの器(うつわ): unglazed vessel
  • 素焼(すや)きの植木鉢(うえきばち): unglazed flowerpot
  • 素焼(すや)きにする: to bisque-fire; to plain-grill
  • 野菜(やさい)の素焼(すや)き: plain-grilled vegetables
  • 魚(さかな)の素焼(すや)き: plain-grilled fish

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 焼成(しょうせい): firing (kiln) — the technical term for kiln firing in general; 素焼(すや)き specifically means firing without glaze
  • 塩焼(しおや)き: salt-grilled — grilling with salt, one step up from the completely unseasoned 素焼(すや)き
  • 釉薬(ゆうやく): glaze — the coating applied after the 素焼(すや)き stage in pottery
