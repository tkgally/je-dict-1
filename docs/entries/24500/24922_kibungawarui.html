気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)い

きぶんがわるい
expression
to feel sick; to feel unwell; to feel bad
1. to feel sick; to feel nauseous; to feel unwell
To experience physical discomfort such as nausea, dizziness, or general malaise.
気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)いので休(やす)みます。
I feel sick, so I'm going to rest.
電車(でんしゃ)の中(なか)で急(きゅう)に気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)くなった。
I suddenly felt sick on the train.
気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)い方(かた)は係員(かかりいん)にお声(こえ)がけください。
If you are feeling unwell, please speak to a staff member.
2. to feel bad; to feel unpleasant; to be in a bad mood
To experience an unpleasant emotional state, whether caused by someone's behavior, a situation, or one's own mood.
あの態度(たいど)は気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)い。
That attitude is unpleasant.
嘘(うそ)をつかれて気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)い。
Being lied to makes me feel bad.
朝(あさ)から気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)くて、何(なに)もやる気(き)が起(お)きない。
I've been in a bad mood since morning and don't feel like doing anything.

An essential everyday expression combining 気分(きぶん) (mood, feeling) with 悪(わる)い (bad). This is one of the most important phrases for learners to know when they need to communicate physical discomfort in Japanese.

The physical sense (sense 1) is especially useful in practical situations: on public transportation, at work, or in medical settings. The phrase 気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)くなった (I started feeling sick) is commonly heard on trains and buses in Japan.

USAGE:

  • The past form 気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)かった describes a past state
  • The change-of-state form 気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)くなる means "to start feeling sick/bad"
  • In polite speech: 気分(きぶん)が悪(わる)いです or 気分(きぶん)が優(すぐ)れません (more formal)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 具合(ぐあい)が悪(わる)い: to feel unwell — very similar but focuses more on physical condition; slightly more objective
  • 体調(たいちょう)が悪(わる)い: to be in poor physical condition — emphasizes ongoing health rather than momentary feeling
  • 気持(きも)ちが悪(わる)い: to feel disgusted, to feel creepy — stronger emotional reaction, often to something repulsive
  • 吐(は)き気(け)がする: to feel nauseous — specifically about the urge to vomit
