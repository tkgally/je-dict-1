1. to short-circuit; to short out
For an electrical circuit to malfunction due to an unintended connection between conductors, often causing sparks or equipment failure.
コンセントがショートした。
The outlet short-circuited.
古(ふる)い配線(はいせん)がショートして火花(ひばな)が出(で)た。
The old wiring short-circuited and sparks flew.
雨(あめ)で基盤(きばん)がショートして機械(きかい)が動(うご)かなくなった。
The circuit board shorted out from rain and the machine stopped working.
2. to be unable to process; to overload mentally
Used figuratively to describe someone's brain freezing or becoming overwhelmed by too much information.
情報(じょうほう)が多(おお)すぎて頭(あたま)がショートした。
There was too much information and my brain short-circuited.
難(むずか)しい質問(しつもん)をされて頭(あたま)がショートしそうだ。
I was asked such a difficult question that my brain is about to short out.
同時(どうじ)にいろいろ頼(たの)まれて完全(かんぜん)にショートしている。
I've been asked to do so many things at once that I'm completely fried.

A suru verb derived from the English word "short" (as in short circuit). The literal sense describes electrical faults, while the figurative sense — describing mental overload — is very common in casual speech, especially among younger speakers.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • コンセントがショートする: the outlet shorts out
  • 配線(はいせん)がショートする: the wiring short-circuits
  • 頭(あたま)がショートする: one's brain short-circuits (figurative)
  • 基板(きばん)がショートする: the circuit board shorts out

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 漏電(ろうでん): electrical leakage — a related but distinct fault where current escapes from the intended path
  • パンクする: to go flat; to be overwhelmed — another loanword-based verb used figuratively for being overloaded, but implies bursting rather than electrical failure
