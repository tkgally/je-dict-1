1. firing; baking; kiln-firing
The process of heating clay, ceramics, bricks, or similar materials at high temperatures in a kiln to harden and strengthen them. A fundamental step in pottery and ceramic production.
この器(うつわ)は高温(こうおん)で焼成(しょうせい)されている。
This vessel was fired at high temperature.
焼成(しょうせい)温度(おんど)によって釉薬(ゆうやく)の色(いろ)が変(か)わる。
The color of the glaze changes depending on the firing temperature.
窯(かま)に入(い)れて約(やく)千二百度(せんにひゃくど)で焼成(しょうせい)すると、土(つち)が堅(かた)くなり水(みず)を通(とお)さなくなる。
When placed in the kiln and fired at about 1200 degrees, the clay hardens and becomes waterproof.

Composed of 焼(しょう) (to burn, to fire) and 成(せい) (to form, to make). Literally 'forming by fire.' A technical term central to ceramics and pottery, one of Japan's most celebrated traditional crafts.

USAGE:
Used as both a noun and as a verb with する. Found in ceramics workshops, museum descriptions of pottery, and industrial contexts involving bricks and tiles. Understanding this word is helpful when visiting pottery towns like 有田(ありた), 瀬戸(せと), or 益子(ましこ).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 焼成(しょうせい)する: to fire (in a kiln)
  • 焼成(しょうせい)温度(おんど): firing temperature
  • 焼成(しょうせい)時間(じかん): firing time
  • 本(ほん)焼成(しょうせい): main firing (second firing at high temperature)
  • 素(す)焼(や)き焼成(しょうせい): bisque firing (first firing before glazing)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 焼(や)く: to burn; to bake; to fire — the everyday verb, used broadly; 焼成(しょうせい) is the technical noun for the kiln process
  • 窯焼(かまや)き: kiln-fired — describes the method of firing, emphasizing the use of a kiln
  • 素焼(すや)き: bisque; unglazed firing — the first, lower-temperature firing before glazing, as opposed to the full 焼成(しょうせい)
