1. absent-mindedly; spaced out; in a daze
Describes being mentally unfocused, zoning out, or not paying attention.
ぼーっとしている。
I'm spacing out.
疲(つか)れてぼーっとする。
I'm so tired I can't focus.
授業(じゅぎょう)中(ちゅう)にぼーっとしていた。
I was spaced out during class.
ぼーっと窓(まど)の外(そと)を見(み)ていた。
I was staring absently out the window.
風邪(かぜ)をひいて頭(あたま)がぼーっとする。
I have a cold and my head feels foggy.
2. hazy; blurry; vague
Describes something appearing unclear or indistinct.
遠(とお)くの景色(けしき)がぼーっと見(み)える。
The distant scenery looks hazy.
霧(きり)でぼーっとしか見(み)えない。
I can only see hazily because of the fog.
明(あ)かりがぼーっと光(ひか)っている。
The light is glowing dimly.
暗(くら)い中(なか)にぼーっと人影(ひとかげ)が見(み)えた。
I could vaguely see a figure in the darkness.
眼鏡(めがね)を外(はず)すと全部(ぜんぶ)ぼーっと見(み)える。
When I take off my glasses, everything looks blurry.

ぼーっと is an onomatopoeic word describing mental or visual haziness. The two senses are related: both convey a lack of sharpness or focus.

Sense 1 (mental): Describes being spaced out, absent-minded, or unable to concentrate. ぼーっとする is extremely common in daily conversation.

Sense 2 (visual): Describes something appearing blurry, hazy, or indistinct — like shapes in fog or dim light.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • ぼーっとする: to space out, zone out
  • ぼーっと見(み)る: to stare blankly
  • ぼーっと過(す)ごす: to spend time idly
  • 頭(あたま)がぼーっとする: head feels foggy

TYPICAL CAUSES:

  • 疲(つか)れ (tiredness)
  • 寝不足(ねぶそく) (lack of sleep)
  • 風邪(かぜ) (cold/illness)
  • 考(かんが)え事(ごと) (lost in thought)

Note: ぼーっとするな is a common scolding expression meaning "Pay attention!" or "Stop daydreaming!"
