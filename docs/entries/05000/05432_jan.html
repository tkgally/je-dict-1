じゃん
particle
isn't it; right (casual sentence-ending)
1. isn't it; right
A casual sentence-ending particle that seeks the listener's agreement, or points out something the speaker takes to be obvious.
いいじゃん。
That's fine, isn't it?
知(し)ってるじゃん。
You know it, right?
できるじゃん！
You can do it! (See!)
Expressing pleasant surprise
それ、高(たか)いじゃん。買(か)えないよ。
That's expensive, isn't it? I can't buy it.
もう時間(じかん)ないじゃん。早(はや)く行(い)こうよ。
We don't have time anymore, right? Let's go quickly.

じゃん is a casual sentence-final particle. It is a contraction of じゃないか, and it keeps that phrase's rhetorical-question force: the speaker states something and invites the listener to see it the same way.

FUNCTIONS:

  • Seeking agreement: invites confirmation of what was just said, like English "right?" or "isn't it?"
  • Pointing out the evident: presents something as plain to see, often with mild triumph ("see, you can do it") or mild reproach ("you did say that")

PATTERNS:

じゃん attaches to the plain form of the preceding word, and だ is dropped before it:

  • Noun: 学生(がくせい)じゃん (you're a student, right?)
  • Na-adjective stem: 便利(べんり)じゃん (that's handy, isn't it?)
  • I-adjective: 安(やす)いじゃん (that's cheap!)
  • Verb, plain form: 言(い)ったじゃん (you did say that)

CONTRASTS:

  • ね seeks agreement gently and suits any register; じゃん is blunter and strictly casual
  • よね checks a belief the speaker already holds; じゃん pushes the listener to accept the point
  • じゃんか is the emphatic version, common when protesting
  • やん is the Kansai counterpart

COMMON MISTAKES:

  • Do not keep だ: not ×学生(がくせい)だじゃん, but 学生(がくせい)じゃん
  • Never use it with a superior or a customer. It marks the speaker as on familiar terms with the listener, so it can sound rude even when the content is polite.

REGISTER:
Historically a Tokai regional form, じゃん spread through Yokohama into Kanto speech and is now general casual Japanese, especially among younger speakers.

Related Words
