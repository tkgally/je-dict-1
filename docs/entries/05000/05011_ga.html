蛾(が)

が
noun
moth
1. moth
A nocturnal insect related to butterflies, typically with duller coloring and feathery antennae, attracted to light.
蛾(が)が電灯(でんとう)に集(あつ)まってくる。
Moths gather around the electric light.
飛(と)んで火(ひ)に入(い)る夏(なつ)の虫(むし)。
A summer insect flying into the fire. (Idiom: Bringing disaster upon oneself)
Often refers to moths' attraction to flames
蛾(が)は夜行性(やこうせい)の昆虫(こんちゅう)です。
Moths are nocturnal insects.

Differences from butterflies (蝶(ちょう)):

  • Active at night (夜行性(やこうせい))
  • Feathery antennae (触角(しょっかく)が羽毛状(うもうじょう))
  • Usually duller colors
  • Rest with wings flat

Common types in Japan:

  • ヤママユガ (Japanese silk moth)
  • オオミズアオ (luna moth)
  • カイコガ (silkworm moth)
  • スズメガ (hawk moths)

The proverb 「飛(と)んで火(ひ)に入(い)る夏(なつ)の虫(むし)」 describes someone recklessly rushing into danger, like a moth to a flame.

Related:

  • 蚕(かいこ) (silkworm) - the larva of カイコガ, historically important for silk production
  • 毛虫(けむし) (caterpillar) - moth/butterfly larva
