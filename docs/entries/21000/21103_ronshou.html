1. proof; demonstration; argumentation
The process of proving or demonstrating something through logical reasoning and evidence. Refers to both the act of proving and the logical argument itself.
論理的(ろんりてき)に論証(ろんしょう)する。
To prove something logically.
この仮説(かせつ)はまだ十分(じゅうぶん)に論証(ろんしょう)されていない。
This hypothesis has not yet been sufficiently demonstrated.
彼(かれ)は豊富(ほうふ)なデータを用(もち)いて、自説(じせつ)の論証(ろんしょう)を試(こころ)みた。
He attempted to prove his theory using abundant data.

An academic and intellectual word referring to the process of proving or demonstrating a point through logic and evidence. Used in philosophy, academia, debate, and formal writing.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 論証(ろんしょう)する: to prove, to demonstrate
  • 論証(ろんしょう)を試(こころ)みる: to attempt to prove
  • 論証(ろんしょう)過程(かてい): the process of argumentation
  • 論証(ろんしょう)責任(せきにん): burden of proof

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 証明(しょうめい): proof, demonstration — more general and everyday; can be used for mathematical proofs, ID verification, etc.
  • 立証(りっしょう): substantiation, proof — emphasizes establishing evidence, especially in legal contexts
  • 実証(じっしょう): empirical proof — proving through experiment or evidence rather than logic alone

ETYMOLOGY:
論(ろん) (argue, discuss) + 証(しょう) (evidence, proof) — "argue with proof."
