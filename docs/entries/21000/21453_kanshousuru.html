1. to interfere, to meddle, to intervene
To involve oneself in someone else's affairs or another country's internal matters without being asked. Carries a negative connotation of unwanted involvement.
他人(たにん)のことに干渉(かんしょう)するな。
Don't meddle in other people's business.
親(おや)があまり子供(こども)に干渉(かんしょう)しすぎるのはよくない。
It's not good for parents to interfere too much with their children.
その国(くに)は隣国(りんごく)の内政(ないせい)に干渉(かんしょう)したとして国際的(こくさいてき)に非難(ひなん)された。
That country was internationally condemned for interfering in a neighboring country's internal affairs.

A compound of 干(かん) (to concern, to relate to) and 渉(しょう) (to cross over, to be involved). The word carries a strongly negative nuance — it implies unwelcome involvement in matters that are not one's own.

USAGE:
The thing being interfered with takes the particle に. The pattern 〜に干渉(かんしょう)する is the standard construction.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 内政(ないせい)干渉(かんしょう): interference in internal affairs
  • 過度(かど)な干渉(かんしょう): excessive interference
  • 干渉(かんしょう)しすぎる: to meddle too much
  • 相互(そうご)不干渉(ふかんしょう): mutual non-interference

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 口出(くちだ)しする: to butt in, to stick one's nose in — more colloquial and everyday
  • 介入(かいにゅう)する: to intervene — more neutral, can be positive (e.g., mediating a dispute)
  • おせっかいを焼(や)く: to be nosy, to be a busybody — casual, describes someone who meddles out of misplaced helpfulness
