1. to pull out; to extract; to remove
To physically remove something by pulling it out of where it is embedded or contained.
雑草(ざっそう)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る。
To pull out weeds.
古(ふる)いクギを抜(ぬ)き取(と)った。
I pulled out the old nails.
医者(いしゃ)が傷口(きずぐち)から破片(はへん)を慎重(しんちょう)に抜(ぬ)き取(と)った。
The doctor carefully extracted the fragment from the wound.
2. to steal; to pilfer; to skim off
To secretly take something from a larger quantity without being noticed.
売上金(うりあげきん)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る。
To skim off from the sales proceeds.
在庫(ざいこ)から商品(しょうひん)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る社員(しゃいん)がいた。
There was an employee pilfering goods from the inventory.
検査(けんさ)のために製品(せいひん)をいくつか抜(ぬ)き取(と)った。
I pulled out a few products for inspection.

A compound verb from 抜(ぬ)く (to pull out) + 取(と)る (to take). The 取(と)る component adds the nuance of taking possession of what is removed.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 雑草(ざっそう)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る: to pull out weeds
  • 釘(くぎ)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る: to pull out nails
  • 栓(せん)を抜(ぬ)き取(と)る: to pull out a plug/cork
  • 抜(ぬ)き取(と)り検査(けんさ): spot check, random sampling

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 抜(ぬ)く: to pull out — simpler verb without the taking nuance; broader in meaning
  • 引(ひ)き抜(ぬ)く: to pull out, uproot — emphasizes the pulling force needed
  • 取(と)り除(のぞ)く: to remove, eliminate — focuses on getting rid of something rather than the physical pulling motion

RELATED EXPRESSION:
抜(ぬ)き取(と)り検査(けんさ) (spot check, random inspection) is a very common compound noun in quality control and manufacturing contexts.
