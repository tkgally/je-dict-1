1. intoxication; drunkenness; inebriation
The state of being drunk from alcohol, especially to a significant degree. A formal or literary term for drunkenness.
酩酊(めいてい)状態(じょうたい)で運転(うんてん)する。
To drive while intoxicated.
酩酊(めいてい)した客(きゃく)がトラブルを起(お)こした。
An intoxicated customer caused trouble.
酩酊(めいてい)して記憶(きおく)がないと言(い)っても、責任(せきにん)を免(まぬが)れることはできない。
Even if you say you were too drunk to remember, you cannot escape responsibility.

A formal, literary, or legal term for drunkenness. Both kanji contain the 酉(とり) radical (alcohol vessel), emphasizing the connection to alcohol. Used as a する verb (酩酊(めいてい)する = to become intoxicated).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 酩酊(めいてい)状態(じょうたい): state of intoxication
  • 酩酊(めいてい)運転(うんてん): drunk driving
  • 泥酔(でいすい)酩酊(めいてい): dead drunk, completely intoxicated

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 酔(よ)う: to get drunk — the everyday verb for becoming intoxicated
  • 泥酔(でいすい): dead drunk — emphasizes extreme intoxication, also formal
  • 酔(よ)っ払(ぱら)い: a drunk person — colloquial noun for someone who is drunk

REGISTER:
More formal and literary than everyday words for drunkenness. Commonly appears in legal contexts (酩酊(めいてい)運転(うんてん) in DUI laws), news reports, and literary prose. In casual conversation, people would say 酔(よ)っている or 酔(よ)っ払(ぱら)っている instead.
