1. stall (aeronautics); loss of lift
In aviation, the condition where an aircraft loses lift due to exceeding the critical angle of attack.
機体(きたい)が失速(しっそく)した。
The aircraft stalled.
失速(しっそく)を防(ふせ)ぐために速度(そくど)を上(あ)げた。
They increased speed to prevent a stall.
パイロットは失速(しっそく)警報(けいほう)が鳴(な)ったとき、すぐに操縦桿(そうじゅうかん)を押(お)した。
When the stall warning sounded, the pilot immediately pushed the control stick forward.
2. losing momentum; stalling out; slowdown
Figuratively, a sudden loss of momentum or progress in business, the economy, a career, or other endeavors. Very common in news and business contexts.
景気(けいき)が失速(しっそく)している。
The economy is losing momentum.
新商品(しんしょうひん)の売(う)り上(あ)げが失速(しっそく)した。
Sales of the new product stalled.
好調(こうちょう)だったチームが後半(こうはん)に入(はい)って失速(しっそく)し、優勝(ゆうしょう)を逃(のが)した。
The team that had been doing well lost momentum in the second half and missed the championship.

Originally an aviation term for an aerodynamic stall, now very commonly used figuratively to describe any sudden loss of momentum — particularly in business, sports, and politics.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 景気(けいき)が失速(しっそく)する: the economy loses momentum
  • 売(う)り上(あ)げが失速(しっそく)する: sales stall
  • 失速(しっそく)気味(ぎみ): showing signs of slowing down
  • 失速(しっそく)を防(ふせ)ぐ: to prevent a stall/slowdown

USAGE:
Used as a noun or with する as a suru-verb. The figurative sense (sense 2) is far more common in everyday Japanese than the aviation sense.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 減速(げんそく): deceleration — a gradual slowing, less dramatic than 失速(しっそく)
  • 低迷(ていめい): slump — a prolonged period of poor performance, while 失速(しっそく) implies a more sudden drop
