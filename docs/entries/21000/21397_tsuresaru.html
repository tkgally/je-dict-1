1. to take away (forcibly); to carry off; to abduct
To take someone away from a place, often implying force, coercion, or against the person's will. Used in contexts of kidnapping, abduction, or forcibly removing someone.
子供(こども)が連(つ)れ去(さ)られた。
The child was taken away.
犯人(はんにん)は被害者(ひがいしゃ)を車(くるま)で連(つ)れ去(さ)った。
The criminal took the victim away by car.
離婚(りこん)後(ご)に元(もと)配偶者(はいぐうしゃ)が子供(こども)を連(つ)れ去(さ)るケースが社会(しゃかい)問題(もんだい)になっている。
Cases of former spouses taking children away after divorce have become a social issue.

A compound verb combining 連(つ)れる (to take along) and 去(さ)る (to leave/go away). Strongly implies that the person being taken does not go willingly. Frequently appears in news reports about kidnapping, abduction, and custody disputes.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 子供(こども)を連(つ)れ去(さ)る: to take a child away
  • 無理(むり)やり連(つ)れ去(さ)る: to take away by force
  • 連(つ)れ去(さ)り事件(じけん): an abduction case

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 誘拐(ゆうかい)する: to kidnap — a legal/criminal term specifically for kidnapping
  • 拉致(らち)する: to abduct — often used for political abductions; strong connotation of force
  • 連(つ)れて行(い)く: to take someone along — neutral, no implication of force
