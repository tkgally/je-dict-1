勲章(くんしょう)

くんしょう
noun
decoration; medal; order of merit
1. decoration; medal; order of merit
An official award given by the government in recognition of distinguished service or achievement. Also used figuratively for something one is proud of, like a badge of honor.
勲章(くんしょう)を授(さず)かった。
I was awarded a decoration.
祖父(そふ)は戦争(せんそう)で勲章(くんしょう)をもらった。
My grandfather received a medal in the war.
その傷(きず)は勇気(ゆうき)の勲章(くんしょう)だと言(い)われた。
I was told that the scar was a badge of courage.
長年(ながねん)の功績(こうせき)が認(みと)められ、文化(ぶんか)勲章(くんしょう)が贈(おく)られた。
In recognition of years of achievement, the Order of Culture was bestowed.

Refers to official government decorations awarded for distinguished service, as well as the figurative sense of a "badge of honor" — something one wears proudly as proof of effort or experience.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 勲章(くんしょう)を授(さず)かる: to receive a decoration
  • 勲章(くんしょう)を贈(おく)る: to bestow a decoration
  • 文化(ぶんか)勲章(くんしょう): Order of Culture (Japan's highest cultural award)
  • 勲章(くんしょう)を胸(むね)につける: to wear a medal on one's chest

JAPAN'S DECORATION SYSTEM:

Japan awards decorations through the 叙勲(じょくん) system, typically to those who have contributed to society. Major categories include:

  • 旭日章(きょくじつしょう): Order of the Rising Sun
  • 瑞宝章(ずいほうしょう): Order of the Sacred Treasure
  • 文化勲章(ぶんかくんしょう): Order of Culture

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • メダル: medal — used for sports and competitions
  • 褒章(ほうしょう): medal of honor — awarded for specific acts of public service or moral courage
