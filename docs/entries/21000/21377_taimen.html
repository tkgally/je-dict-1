1. to meet face-to-face; to meet in person
To meet someone directly, in person rather than remotely. Often implies a significant or awaited meeting.
久(ひさ)しぶりに対面(たいめん)した。
We met face-to-face for the first time in a while.
オンラインではなく対面(たいめん)で話(はな)したい。
I want to talk in person, not online.
初(はじ)めて赤(あか)ちゃんと対面(たいめん)した瞬間(しゅんかん)、涙(なみだ)が出(で)た。
The moment I met the baby for the first time, tears came to my eyes.
2. to confront; to come face-to-face with
To face something directly, including difficult situations, challenges, or uncomfortable realities.
現実(げんじつ)に対面(たいめん)する。
To confront reality.
困難(こんなん)に対面(たいめん)しても諦(あきら)めない。
I won't give up even when confronted with difficulties.
長年(ながねん)避(さ)けてきた問題(もんだい)に対面(たいめん)する時(とき)が来(き)た。
The time has come to confront the problem I've been avoiding for years.

Has become especially common since the COVID-19 pandemic, where 対面(たいめん) (in-person) is contrasted with オンライン (online) or リモート (remote) in work and education contexts.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 対面(たいめん)授業(じゅぎょう): in-person classes
  • 対面(たいめん)販売(はんばい): face-to-face sales
  • 対面(たいめん)で会(あ)う: to meet in person
  • 対面(たいめん)式(しき): face-to-face format

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 面会(めんかい)する: to visit/meet — used for visiting someone in hospital, prison, etc.
  • 直接(ちょくせつ)会(あ)う: to meet directly — more casual alternative
  • 対峙(たいじ)する: to confront/stand against — stronger, implies opposition or standoff
