1. to quarrel; to argue with each other
To engage in a verbal dispute where both parties exchange heated words back and forth.
兄弟(きょうだい)でよく言(い)い合(あ)う。
The siblings often argue with each other.
くだらないことで言(い)い合(あ)うのはやめよう。
Let's stop arguing over trivial things.
二人(ふたり)は些細(ささい)なことで言(い)い合(あ)いになり、周(まわ)りの人(ひと)たちが心配(しんぱい)した。
The two got into an argument over something trivial, and the people around them grew worried.
2. to say to each other; to exchange words
To mutually express the same thing to one another, such as greetings, well-wishes, or shared sentiments.
「おめでとう」と言(い)い合(あ)った。
They said 'Congratulations' to each other.
友人(ゆうじん)と冗談(じょうだん)を言(い)い合(あ)って笑(わら)った。
My friend and I exchanged jokes and laughed.
卒業式(そつぎょうしき)の日(ひ)、クラスメートたちと「また会(あ)おうね」と言(い)い合(あ)って別(わか)れた。
On graduation day, my classmates and I parted ways saying 'Let's meet again' to each other.

A compound verb formed from 言(い)う (to say) + 合(あ)う (to do together/mutually). The 合(あ)う suffix adds reciprocity — both parties are speaking. In practice, sense 1 (arguing) is more common than sense 2 (exchanging words).

USAGE:

  • Sense 1 often appears as the noun form 言(い)い合(あ)い (an argument, a quarrel)
  • Sense 2 is typically used with a quoted phrase or specific content being exchanged
  • The subject is usually plural or implies two or more people

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 言(い)い合(あ)いになる: to get into an argument
  • 言(い)い合(あ)いをする: to have an argument
  • 冗談(じょうだん)を言(い)い合(あ)う: to exchange jokes
  • 悪口(わるくち)を言(い)い合(あ)う: to trade insults

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 口論(こうろん)する: a more formal word for arguing verbally
  • 喧嘩(けんか)する: to fight or quarrel; broader than 言(い)い合(あ)う, which is limited to verbal exchange
  • 話(はな)し合(あ)う: to discuss; implies a calmer, more productive exchange
