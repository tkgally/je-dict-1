1. water bath; double boiling; bain-marie
A cooking technique where food is heated indirectly by placing its container in a larger pot of hot water. Used for gentle, even heating — common for melting chocolate, heating retort pouches, and making delicate sauces.
チョコレートを湯煎(ゆせん)で溶(と)かす。
To melt chocolate in a water bath.
レトルトカレーを湯煎(ゆせん)で温(あたた)めた。
I heated the retort curry in a water bath.
バターは直火(じかび)ではなく湯煎(ゆせん)でゆっくり溶(と)かした方(ほう)がいい。
It's better to melt butter slowly in a water bath rather than over direct heat.

Compound of 湯(ゆ) (hot water) + 煎(せん) (roast, boil). A fundamental cooking technique found on food packaging instructions and in recipes throughout Japan. Retort food packages commonly instruct users to heat by 湯煎(ゆせん).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 湯煎(ゆせん)で溶(と)かす: to melt in a water bath
  • 湯煎(ゆせん)で温(あたた)める: to warm in a water bath
  • 湯煎(ゆせん)にかける: to put in a water bath
  • 湯煎(ゆせん)可能(かのう): can be heated by water bath (packaging label)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 直火(じかび): direct heat (cooking directly over flame)
  • 蒸(む)す: to steam (using steam to cook, different from water bath)
  • 電子(でんし)レンジ: microwave (modern alternative for reheating)
