1. to bring someone along into; to take someone into a place
To lead or take a person (or animal) into a specific place. Often implies the action is somewhat secretive, inappropriate, or against rules.
犬(いぬ)を店(みせ)に連(つ)れ込(こ)んだ。
I brought the dog into the store.
部外者(ぶがいしゃ)を社内(しゃない)に連(つ)れ込(こ)まないでください。
Please don't bring outsiders into the office.
知(し)らない人(ひと)を家(いえ)に連(つ)れ込(こ)むのは危険(きけん)だと親(おや)に言(い)われた。
My parents told me it's dangerous to bring strangers into the house.

Compound of 連(つ)れる (to take along) and 込(こ)む (into). The 込(こ)む component adds a sense of going inward, into an enclosed space. Often carries a negative or cautionary nuance — bringing someone where they perhaps shouldn't be, or doing so secretly.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 家(いえ)に連(つ)れ込(こ)む: to bring someone home
  • 部屋(へや)に連(つ)れ込(こ)む: to take someone into a room
  • ペットを連(つ)れ込(こ)む: to bring a pet in
  • 連(つ)れ込(こ)み禁止(きんし): no bringing others in

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 連(つ)れて行(い)く: to take someone along — neutral, no negative nuance
  • 引(ひ)き込(こ)む: to pull in; to draw into — can be used for dragging someone into a situation
  • 招(まね)き入(い)れる: to invite in — positive, welcoming nuance
