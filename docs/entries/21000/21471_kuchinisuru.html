1. to say; to mention; to put into words
To verbally express or bring up a topic, especially one that might otherwise remain unspoken.
彼(かれ)は不満(ふまん)を口(くち)にした。
He voiced his dissatisfaction.
誰(だれ)もその話題(わだい)を口(くち)にしなかった。
Nobody mentioned that topic.
彼女(かのじょ)は別(わか)れたあの日(ひ)のことを二度(にど)と口(くち)にしなかった。
She never again spoke of that day they parted.
2. to eat; to drink; to put in one's mouth
To consume food or drink, with a slightly refined nuance of literally placing something in one's mouth.
朝(あさ)から何(なに)も口(くち)にしていない。
I haven't eaten anything since morning.
体調(たいちょう)が悪(わる)くて食(た)べ物(もの)を口(くち)にできなかった。
I felt so unwell that I couldn't eat anything.
久(ひさ)しぶりに地元(じもと)の料理(りょうり)を口(くち)にして、懐(なつ)かしさがこみ上(あ)げてきた。
Tasting the local cuisine after so long, a wave of nostalgia welled up inside me.

An idiomatic expression using 口(くち) (mouth) + にする that has two distinct meanings depending on context. In sense 1, it means to verbalize or express something — to "put something into words." In sense 2, it means to eat or drink — to literally put something in one's mouth.

USAGE:

  • Sense 1 is often used in negative contexts (口(くち)にしない = to not mention, to keep silent about)
  • Sense 2 is slightly more formal or literary than 食(た)べる or 飲(の)む

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 不満(ふまん)を口(くち)にする: to voice dissatisfaction
  • 弱音(よわね)を口(くち)にする: to voice weakness or complaints
  • 本音(ほんね)を口(くち)にする: to speak one's true feelings
  • 何(なに)も口(くち)にしない: to not eat or drink anything

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 言(い)う: the most general word for speaking; 口(くち)にする implies deliberately putting thoughts into words
  • 述(の)べる: more formal, used for expressing opinions or facts
  • 食(た)べる: the standard word for eating; 口(くち)にする (sense 2) has a more refined or restrained nuance
