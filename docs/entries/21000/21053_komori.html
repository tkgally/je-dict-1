1. babysitting; looking after children
The act of taking care of young children, especially watching over them while parents are away.
今日(きょう)は子守(こも)りをしている。
I'm babysitting today.
妻(つま)が出(で)かけている間(あいだ)、子守(こも)りを頼(たの)まれた。
I was asked to look after the kids while my wife was out.
週末(しゅうまつ)は孫(まご)の子守(こも)りで一日(いちにち)があっという間(ま)に過(す)ぎる。
On weekends, the day flies by while I'm looking after my grandchildren.
2. babysitter; nursemaid
A person who looks after children. In historical contexts, often refers to a young girl hired to care for children.
子守(こも)りを雇(やと)った。
We hired a babysitter.
祖母(そぼ)が子守(こも)り代(が)わりだった。
My grandmother served as a babysitter.
昔(むかし)は近所(きんじょ)の年上(としうえ)の子(こ)が子守(こも)り役(やく)を務(つと)めることが多(おお)かった。
In the past, older neighborhood children often served as babysitters.

Refers to both the act of watching children and the person who does it. Also written as 子守(こもり) without the り in some compounds.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 子守(こも)りをする: to babysit
  • 子守(こも)り役(やく): the role of babysitter
  • 子守(こも)り歌(うた): lullaby
  • 子守(こも)り代(が)わり: acting as a babysitter

CULTURAL NOTE:
子守(こも)り歌(うた) (lullaby) is a well-known compound. Many traditional Japanese lullabies describe the hard life of young girls who worked as nursemaids.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ベビーシッター: babysitter — the modern, professional equivalent
  • 育児(いくじ): childcare/child-rearing — broader, refers to the ongoing process of raising children, not just watching them
