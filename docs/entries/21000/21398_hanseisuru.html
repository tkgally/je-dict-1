1. to reflect on one's actions; to feel remorse; to reconsider
To look back on one's own behavior or mistakes and recognize what went wrong, often with a sense of regret and a commitment to do better.
自分(じぶん)の行動(こうどう)を反省(はんせい)した。
I reflected on my actions.
彼(かれ)は全(まった)く反省(はんせい)していない様子(ようす)だった。
He didn't seem to feel remorseful at all.
失敗(しっぱい)を反省(はんせい)して、次(つぎ)は同(おな)じ間違(まちが)いを繰(く)り返(かえ)さないようにしたい。
I want to reflect on my failure and make sure I don't repeat the same mistake next time.

A culturally significant concept in Japan. 反省(はんせい) goes beyond simple reflection — it implies acknowledging mistakes, feeling genuine regret, and resolving to improve. It is frequently expected in social and professional contexts when someone has done something wrong.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 深(ふか)く反省(はんせい)する: to deeply reflect/feel remorse
  • 反省(はんせい)の色(いろ)がない: to show no sign of remorse
  • 反省(はんせい)点(てん): points for reflection/improvement
  • 反省(はんせい)会(かい): review meeting, post-mortem
  • 反省(はんせい)文(ぶん): written apology/reflection

CULTURAL NOTE:
In Japanese schools and workplaces, 反省(はんせい) is a routine practice. Students may be asked to write 反省(はんせい)文(ぶん) (reflection essays) after misbehavior, and teams hold 反省(はんせい)会(かい) (review sessions) after events or projects.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 後悔(こうかい)する: to regret — focuses on the feeling of wishing one hadn't done something, without the constructive element
  • 振(ふ)り返(かえ)る: to look back — neutral reflection without the implication of having done something wrong
