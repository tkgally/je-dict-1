1. to see; to come across; to catch sight of
To happen to see something or someone, often implying an unplanned or incidental encounter rather than deliberate looking.
珍(めずら)しい光景(こうけい)を目(め)にした。
I came across a rare sight.
そのニュースを目(め)にして驚(おどろ)いた。
I was surprised when I saw that news.
駅前(えきまえ)で偶然(ぐうぜん)昔(むかし)の友人(ゆうじん)を目(め)にして、思(おも)わず声(こえ)をかけた。
I happened to spot an old friend in front of the station and called out to them without thinking.

An idiomatic expression meaning to see or catch sight of something, often implying that the encounter was unplanned or incidental. It carries a slightly more literary or formal tone than 見(み)る.

USAGE:

  • Often used when encountering something unexpectedly or for the first time
  • Common in written Japanese and news reporting
  • Frequently appears in the past tense (目(め)にした)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 光景(こうけい)を目(め)にする: to witness a scene
  • 記事(きじ)を目(め)にする: to come across an article
  • 現実(げんじつ)を目(め)にする: to see the reality
  • 初(はじ)めて目(め)にする: to see for the first time

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 見(み)る: the basic word for seeing; neutral and general
  • 見(み)かける: to happen to see someone; implies spotting a person
  • 目撃(もくげき)する: to witness (more dramatic, often used for accidents or crimes)
