結界(けっかい)

けっかい
noun
barrier (spiritual or magical)
1. sacred boundary, spiritual barrier
In Buddhism, a ritually defined area that marks the boundary of a sacred space, keeping impurity out and sanctity in. Used at temples and during religious ceremonies.
寺(てら)の結界(けっかい)を越(こ)えてはならない。
You must not cross the temple's sacred boundary.
僧侶(そうりょ)が結界(けっかい)を張(は)って聖域(せいいき)を守(まも)った。
The monk put up a barrier to protect the sacred ground.
この場所(ばしょ)には古(ふる)くから結界(けっかい)が設(もう)けられていると言(い)われている。
It is said that a sacred boundary has been set at this place since ancient times.
2. magical barrier, force field
In anime, manga, and games, a supernatural protective barrier that blocks enemies or contains power. This is the more widely known meaning among younger speakers.
結界(けっかい)を張(は)れ！
Put up a barrier!
敵(てき)の攻撃(こうげき)を結界(けっかい)で防(ふせ)いだ。
I blocked the enemy's attack with a barrier.
魔法使(まほうつか)いが強力(きょうりょく)な結界(けっかい)を張(は)って町(まち)を守(まも)った。
The wizard put up a powerful barrier to protect the town.

USAGE:
The original Buddhist sense refers to a ritually purified boundary. In modern pop culture, the word has become extremely common in anime and manga to mean a magical force field or protective barrier. The key collocation 結界(けっかい)を張(は)る (to put up a barrier) is used in both senses.

ETYMOLOGY:
From Buddhist terminology. 結(けつ) means "to bind" and 界(かい) means "boundary" — a bound or established boundary separating sacred from profane space.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 結界(けっかい)を張(は)る: put up a barrier
  • 結界(けっかい)を破(やぶ)る: break through a barrier
  • 結界(けっかい)を解(と)く: release a barrier
  • 結界(けっかい)の中(なか): inside the barrier
