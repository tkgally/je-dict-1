1. to open
To cause something closed to become open. A transitive verb - you open something. The intransitive pair is 開(あ)く (aku).
窓(まど)を開(あ)けてください。
Please open the window.
ドアを開(あ)けましょうか？
Shall I open the door?
プレゼントの箱(はこ)を開(あ)けてみてください。中(なか)に何(なに)が入(はい)っているでしょうか。
Please try opening the gift box. What do you think is inside?
てみる = try doing
暑(あつ)いので、窓(まど)を開(あ)けてもいいですか？エアコンがないので、風(かぜ)を入(い)れたいです。
It's hot, so may I open the window? There's no air conditioner, so I want to let in the breeze.
鍵(かぎ)を使(つか)って引(ひ)き出(だ)しを開(あ)けました。中(なか)には古(ふる)い写真(しゃしん)が入(はい)っていました。
I used a key to open the drawer. There were old photographs inside.

TRANSITIVITY: 他動詞(たどうし) (transitive). Pair: 開(あ)く (intransitive, to open by itself). Pattern: Xを開(あ)ける.

ASPECT: ている indicates resulting state. 開(あ)けている usually means 'have opened (and it's still open)' rather than 'is opening.'

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 窓(まど)を開(あ)ける (open the window)
  • ドアを開(あ)ける (open the door)
  • 口(くち)を開(あ)ける (open one's mouth)
  • 穴(あな)を開(あ)ける (make a hole)

Opposite: 閉(し)める (to close something).

CONFUSABLE: 開(あ)ける vs 開(ひら)く, which are written with the same kanji but are not interchangeable. Use 開(あ)ける for things that swing, slide, or come apart to leave a gap: 窓(まど), ドア, lids, and containers. Use 開(ひら)く for things that unfold or spread out, and for holding an event: 本(ほん)を開(ひら)く (open a book) is standard, while 本(ほん)を開(あ)ける is not said.

Related Words

Antonym: 閉(し)める (to close)
Contrast: 開(ひら)く (to open (unfold, spread); to hold (an event))
