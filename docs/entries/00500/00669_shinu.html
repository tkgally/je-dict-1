1. to die, to pass away
The basic verb for dying, used of people and animals. Plants wither rather than die: 枯れる is the normal verb for them. When speaking about people, 亡くなる is usually preferred as the gentler, more respectful word.
その犬(いぬ)は去年(きょねん)死(し)にました。
That dog died last year.
虫(むし)が死(し)んでいる。
The insect is dead.
祖父(そふ)は十(じゅう)年(ねん)前(まえ)に死(し)にました。
My grandfather died ten years ago.
In more polite speech, 亡くなりました would be used
魚(さかな)に水(みず)をあげないと死(し)んでしまいますよ。
If you don't give water to the fish, it will die.
私(わたし)の飼(か)っていた猫(ねこ)が先月(せんげつ)死(し)んで、とても悲(かな)しいです。
The cat I was keeping died last month, and I'm very sad.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). No pair verb. Pattern: Xが死(し)ぬ (X dies).

ASPECT: ている indicates resulting state. 死(し)んでいる = 'is dead' (state, not process of dying).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 病気(びょうき)で死(し)ぬ (die of illness)
  • 事故(じこ)で死(し)ぬ (die in an accident)
  • 死(し)にそう (about to die - also used hyperbolically)
  • 死(し)んでしまった (has died - regret)
  • 死(し)ぬほど〜 (〜 to death: 死(し)ぬほど疲(つか)れた, dead tired)

CONJUGATION:
死(し)ぬ is the only ぬ-ending godan verb in everyday use. Its て/た forms take ん: 死(し)んで, 死(し)んだ (not 死(し)にて/死(し)にた).

WHICH VERB FOR WHAT DIES:

  • 死(し)ぬ: people and animals
  • 枯(か)れる: plants (wither, dry up) — 花(はな)が死(し)ぬ sounds wrong
  • 亡(な)くなる: people only, and the polite default when talking about someone's death

REGISTER:
Said of a person, 死(し)ぬ is blunt and factual. Use it for statistics, news, and one's own hypotheticals; switch to 亡(な)くなる when speaking of someone's family or in any careful conversation.

Related Words

Contrast: 亡(な)くなる (to pass away (polite))
Contrast: 枯(か)れる (to wither, to die (of plants))
Related: 命(いのち) (life)
