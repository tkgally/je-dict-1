一(いち)

いち
number
one
1. one
The number one. This is the most basic counting number in Japanese. When counting objects, いち is often combined with counters like 一(いっ)つ (hitotsu), 一(いっ)個(こ) (ikko), or 一(いち)人(にん) (ichinin), though the pronunciation may change depending on the counter.
一(いち)から十(じゅう)まで数(かぞ)えてください。
Please count from one to ten.
ペンを一(いち)本(ぽん)ください。
Please give me one pen.
いち becomes いっ before ぽん
一(いち)番(ばん)好(す)きな色(いろ)は何(なん)ですか。
What is your favorite (number one) color?
一番 means 'number one' or 'most'
一(いち)日(にち)に三(さん)回(かい)ごはんを食(た)べます。
I eat three meals a day.
一(いち)日(にち) means 'one day' or 'per day'
一(いち)年(ねん)に一(いち)回(かい)、家族(かぞく)みんなで旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きます。
We go on a trip with the whole family once a year.

The reading changes in many compound words and with different counters. For example: 一(ひと)つ (hitotsu, one thing), 一(いっ)個(こ) (ikko, one piece), 一(いっ)本(ぽん) (ippon, one long object). When used alone or in mathematical contexts, いち is the standard reading.
