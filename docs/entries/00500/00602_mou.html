もう

もう
adverb
already, anymore
1. already
Before the expected time; by now.
もう食(た)べましたか？
Have you already eaten?
もう十(じゅう)時(じ)ですよ。
It's already 10 o'clock.
宿題(しゅくだい)はもう終(お)わりました。
I've already finished my homework.
駅(えき)に着(つ)いた時(とき)、電車(でんしゃ)はもう出(で)ていました。
When I arrived at the station, the train had already left.
もう春(はる)なのに、今日(きょう)はとても寒(さむ)くて、外(そと)に出(で)たくないです。
Even though it's already spring, it's very cold today, and I don't want to go outside.
2. anymore (with negatives)
No longer; not from this point forward.
もう行(い)きません。
I won't go anymore.
もう会(あ)いたくない。
I don't want to see you anymore.
あの店(みせ)はもう開(あ)いていません。
That shop isn't open anymore.
甘(あま)い物(もの)はもう食(た)べないと決(き)めました。
I decided not to eat sweets anymore.
あの映画(えいが)は古(ふる)いので、もうどこでも見(み)ることができないと思(おも)います。
That movie is old, so I don't think you can watch it anywhere anymore.
3. more, another
An additional amount.
もう一(いっ)回(かい)お願(ねが)いします。
One more time, please.
もう一(ひと)つください。
Please give me one more.
もう少(すこ)し待(ま)ってください。
Please wait a little longer.
もう少(すこ)し大(おお)きい声(こえ)で話(はな)してください。
Please speak a little louder.
日本語(にほんご)がもう少(すこ)し上手(じょうず)になったら、日本(にほん)で働(はたら)きたいと思(おも)っています。
When my Japanese gets a little better, I want to work in Japan.

もう is a versatile adverb with several related meanings.

MEANINGS:
1. Already: An action/state completed earlier than expected
2. Anymore (with negatives): No longer, not from now on
3. More/another: An additional amount

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • もう〜した: already did ~
  • もう〜ない: not ~ anymore
  • もう一(いっ)回(かい): one more time
  • もう少(すこ)し: a little more
  • もう一(ひと)つ: one more (thing)

CONTRAST:

  • もう vs. まだ: "already" vs. "still/not yet"
  • もう食(た)べた (already ate) vs. まだ食(た)べていない (haven't eaten yet)

HOW TO TELL THE SENSES APART:
The surrounding grammar decides which meaning もう has. With a past or completed form it means "already"; with a negative it means "anymore"; directly before a number or quantity word it means "more."

WORD ORDER (sense 3): もう comes immediately before the amount, never after it — もう少(すこ)し, もう一(ひと)つ, もう一(いっ)回(かい). The reverse order is ungrammatical.

SPOKEN USE:
On its own, drawn out as もう〜, it works as an exclamation of exasperation, roughly "oh, come on" or "geez." This is casual speech only.
