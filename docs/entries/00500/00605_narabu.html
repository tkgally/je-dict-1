1. to line up, to queue
To form a line, or to sit or stand in a row. Intransitive - the people or things themselves are what line up, marked with が. The transitive pair is 並(なら)べる (naraberu), used when someone arranges the things.
ここに並(なら)んでください。
Please line up here.
人(ひと)がたくさん並(なら)んでいます。
Many people are lined up.
本(ほん)が棚(たな)に並(なら)んでいます。
Books are lined up on the shelf.
店(みせ)の前(まえ)に長(なが)い列(れつ)ができていて、みんな並(なら)んで待(ま)っていました。
There was a long line in front of the shop, and everyone was waiting in line.
子供(こども)たちは先生(せんせい)の言(い)うとおりに二(ふた)つの列(れつ)に並(なら)んで、静(しず)かに歩(ある)き始(はじ)めました。
The children lined up in two rows as the teacher instructed, and quietly started walking.

TRANSITIVITY: 自動詞(じどうし) (intransitive). Pair: 並(なら)べる (transitive). Pattern: Xが並(なら)ぶ (X lines up).

ASPECT: ている indicates resulting state. 並(なら)んでいる = 'is lined up' (in a line currently).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 列(れつ)に並(なら)ぶ (stand in line)
  • 人(ひと)が並(なら)んでいる (people are lined up)
  • 順番(じゅんばん)に並(なら)ぶ (line up in order)
  • 並(なら)んで歩(ある)く (walk side by side)

CONTRAST WITH THE PAIR: the subject of 並(なら)ぶ is the thing in the line, while the subject of 並(なら)べる is the person doing the arranging. 本(ほん)が並(なら)んでいる = the books are in a row; 本(ほん)を並(なら)べる = someone lines the books up.

USAGE: queuing is a strong social norm in Japan, so 並(なら)ぶ turns up constantly in everyday instructions and signs - at station platforms, shop entrances, and ticket counters.
