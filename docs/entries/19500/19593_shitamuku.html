1. to look down; to face downward
To direct one's face or gaze downward, often conveying shyness, sadness, shame, or deep thought.
彼女(かのじょ)は下向(したむ)いた。
She looked down.
恥(は)ずかしくて下向(したむ)いてしまった。
I was so embarrassed that I looked down.
叱(しか)られた子供(こども)は下向(したむ)いたまま黙(だま)っていた。
The scolded child remained silent, looking down.

A compound godan verb (く row) from 下(した) (below) + 向(む)く (to face). Describes the physical action of looking downward, which often carries emotional connotations such as embarrassment, sadness, or contemplation.

USAGE:
The て form 下向(したむ)いて and the たまま form 下向(したむ)いたまま are especially common, describing someone maintaining a downward gaze.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 下向(したむ)いたまま — while looking down
  • 下向(したむ)いて歩(ある)く — to walk looking down
  • 下向(したむ)いて黙(だま)る — to look down and stay silent

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 俯(うつむ)く — to look down, to cast one's eyes down; largely synonymous and slightly more literary
  • 下(した)を向(む)く — the same meaning expressed as a phrase (particle を inserted); interchangeable in most contexts

Related Words

Antonym: 上向(うわむ)く (to face upward; to improve)
