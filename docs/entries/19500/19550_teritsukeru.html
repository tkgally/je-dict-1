1. to shine down intensely; to beat down; to blaze down
Describes the sun shining fiercely and relentlessly, creating intense heat. Conveys the oppressive, punishing quality of strong sunlight.
太陽(たいよう)が照(て)りつけている。
The sun is beating down.
真夏(まなつ)の日差(ひざ)しが容赦(ようしゃ)なく照(て)りつけていた。
The midsummer sun was shining down mercilessly.
炎天下(えんてんか)で太陽(たいよう)に照(て)りつけられながら、工事(こうじ)現場(げんば)で働(はたら)いている人(ひと)たちがいる。
There are people working at construction sites under the blazing sun.

A vivid weather verb that specifically describes the sun bearing down with intense, oppressive heat. Almost exclusively used with 太陽(たいよう) or 日差(ひざ)し as the subject. Strongly associated with summer heat and often appears in descriptions of 猛暑(もうしょ) (extreme heat).

USAGE:
The progressive form 照(て)りつけている is the most common, describing ongoing fierce sunlight. The passive 照(て)りつけられる emphasizes the discomfort experienced by the person under the sun.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 太陽(たいよう)が照(て)りつける — the sun beats down
  • 日差(ひざ)しが照(て)りつける — the sunlight blazes down
  • 照(て)りつける暑(あつ)さ — scorching heat

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 照(て)りつく — similar meaning but less common; same nuance of relentless sunlight
  • 降(ふ)り注(そそ)ぐ — to pour down; used for both sunlight and rain, more neutral
  • 照(て)る — to shine; basic verb without the intensity or oppressive nuance
