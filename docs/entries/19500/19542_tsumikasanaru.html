1. to pile up; to accumulate; to build up
For things to stack up or accumulate over time, either physically (objects) or abstractly (experience, stress, effort).
疲(つか)れが積(つ)み重(かさ)なっている。
Fatigue has been building up.
小(ちい)さな努力(どりょく)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なって、大(おお)きな成果(せいか)になる。
Small efforts accumulate and become great results.
長年(ながねん)の不満(ふまん)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なり、ついに爆発(ばくはつ)した。
Years of frustration had piled up and finally exploded.

A compound verb combining 積(つ)む (to pile up) and 重(かさ)なる (to overlap, to stack). The intransitive form — things accumulate on their own or over time. Commonly used both for physical stacking and metaphorical accumulation of experiences, effort, stress, or grievances.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 経験(けいけん)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なる — experience accumulates
  • 努力(どりょく)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なる — efforts build up
  • 疲労(ひろう)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なる — fatigue piles up
  • 不満(ふまん)が積(つ)み重(かさ)なる — frustration builds up

TRANSITIVE PAIR:

  • 積(つ)み重(かさ)ねる — to pile up, to accumulate (transitive); used when someone deliberately builds up something

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • たまる — to accumulate, to build up; more casual and general
  • 蓄積(ちくせき)する — to accumulate; more formal/technical
  • 重(かさ)なる — to overlap, to pile up; one of the component verbs, less emphasis on gradual accumulation
