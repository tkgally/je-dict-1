引(ひ)き締(し)め

ひきしめ
noun
tightening; toning; austerity
1. tightening; firming up; toning
The act of making something tighter, firmer, or more disciplined. Used for physical toning (body), financial austerity (budgets), and figurative tightening (discipline, security).
金融(きんゆう)引(ひ)き締(し)めが続(つづ)いている。
Monetary tightening continues.
夏(なつ)に向(む)けて体(からだ)の引(ひ)き締(し)めを始(はじ)めた。
I started toning my body for summer.
不祥事(ふしょうじ)を受(う)けて、社内(しゃない)の規律(きりつ)引(ひ)き締(し)めが図(はか)られた。
Following the scandal, efforts were made to tighten discipline within the company.

The noun form of 引(ひ)き締(し)める (to tighten, to firm up). Used across a wide range of contexts — from economics and policy to fitness and personal discipline.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 金融(きんゆう)引(ひ)き締(し)め — monetary tightening
  • 財政(ざいせい)引(ひ)き締(し)め — fiscal austerity
  • 体(からだ)の引(ひ)き締(し)め — body toning, firming up
  • 規律(きりつ)の引(ひ)き締(し)め — tightening of discipline
  • 引(ひ)き締(し)め策(さく) — austerity measures

USAGE:
In economic news, 引(ひ)き締(し)め typically refers to central bank policies that restrict money supply (raising interest rates, reducing lending). In fitness and beauty contexts, it refers to toning muscles or firming skin. In organizational contexts, it means enforcing stricter discipline.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 引(ひ)き締(し)める — to tighten (the verb form)
  • 緩和(かんわ) — easing, relaxation; the antonym in economic policy
  • 締(し)め付(つ)け — crackdown, restriction; harsher connotation
