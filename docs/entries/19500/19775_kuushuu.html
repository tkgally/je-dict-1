1. air raid; aerial attack; air strike
An attack from aircraft, especially the bombing of cities or military targets. In Japanese, most strongly associated with the Allied bombing campaigns against Japan in World War II.
空襲(くうしゅう)警報(けいほう)が鳴(な)った。
The air raid siren sounded.
祖母(そぼ)は空襲(くうしゅう)を経験(けいけん)した世代(せだい)だ。
My grandmother is of the generation that experienced air raids.
東京(とうきょう)大空襲(だいくうしゅう)では、一夜(いちや)で10万人(まんにん)以上(いじょう)が亡(な)くなったと言(い)われている。
It is said that over 100,000 people died in a single night during the Great Tokyo Air Raid.

Composed of 空(くう) (sky, air) and 襲(しゅう) (attack, raid). In Japanese historical context, 空襲(くうしゅう) most commonly refers to the American bombing raids on Japanese cities during World War II, particularly the firebombing campaigns of 1945.

HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
The 東京(とうきょう)大空襲(だいくうしゅう) (Great Tokyo Air Raid) of March 10, 1945, is the most devastating single air raid in history. Many other major Japanese cities were also subjected to 空襲(くうしゅう). The word carries heavy historical weight and appears frequently in war memoirs, textbooks, and peace education.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 空襲(くうしゅう)警報(けいほう) — air raid warning/siren
  • 空襲(くうしゅう)を受(う)ける — to be hit by an air raid
  • 大空襲(だいくうしゅう) — great air raid, massive bombing
  • 空襲(くうしゅう)被害(ひがい) — air raid damage

RELATED TERMS:

  • 爆撃(ばくげき) — bombing; the act of dropping bombs
  • 空爆(くうばく) — aerial bombing; a modern synonym
  • 防空壕(ぼうくうごう) — air raid shelter
