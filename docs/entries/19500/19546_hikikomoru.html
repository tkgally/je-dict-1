1. to withdraw; to shut oneself in; to stay indoors
To seclude oneself at home or in a room, avoiding social contact and the outside world. Often used to describe both temporary withdrawal and the longer-term social phenomenon known as hikikomori.
雨(あめ)の日(ひ)は家(いえ)に引(ひ)きこもる。
I stay indoors on rainy days.
彼(かれ)は部屋(へや)に引(ひ)きこもって勉強(べんきょう)している。
He has shut himself in his room to study.
学校(がっこう)でいじめに遭(あ)ってから、息子(むすこ)は半年(はんとし)も家(いえ)に引(ひ)きこもっている。
Since being bullied at school, my son has been shut in at home for half a year.

Describes the act of withdrawing from society and staying indoors. While it can refer to simply staying home for a day, it is strongly associated with the social phenomenon of 引(ひ)きこもり, where individuals — often young people — withdraw from social life for extended periods.

USAGE:
The て-form 引(ひ)きこもって is very common, often followed by いる to describe an ongoing state. The noun form 引(ひ)きこもり refers both to the phenomenon and to the person experiencing it.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 家(いえ)に引(ひ)きこもる — to shut oneself in at home
  • 部屋(へや)に引(ひ)きこもる — to shut oneself in one's room
  • 引(ひ)きこもりがちになる — to tend to stay indoors

CULTURAL NOTE:
引(ひ)きこもり is recognized as a significant social issue in Japan. The Japanese government defines it as a state of withdrawal from social participation lasting six months or more. Estimates suggest over one million people are affected.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 閉(と)じこもる — to shut oneself in; emphasizes being enclosed, slightly more literary
  • 籠(こ)もる — to seclude oneself; broader meaning including retreating to concentrate
