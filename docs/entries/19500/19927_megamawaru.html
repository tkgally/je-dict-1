1. to feel dizzy; to have one's head spin
A physical sensation of dizziness or vertigo, as if things are spinning around.
急(きゅう)に立(た)ったら目(め)が回(まわ)った。
I felt dizzy when I stood up suddenly.
高(たか)いところから下(した)を見(み)ると目(め)が回(まわ)る。
Looking down from a high place makes me dizzy.
乗(の)り物酔(ものよ)いで目(め)が回(まわ)って、歩(ある)けなかった。
I was so dizzy from motion sickness that I couldn't walk.
2. to be extremely busy; to be swamped
Figuratively describes being so overwhelmed with work or tasks that one's head spins.
年末(ねんまつ)は目(め)が回(まわ)るほど忙(いそが)しい。
The end of the year is dizzyingly busy.
注文(ちゅうもん)が殺到(さっとう)して目(め)が回(まわ)る。
Orders are flooding in and I'm swamped.
引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの準備(じゅんび)やら手続(てつづ)きやらで、目(め)が回(まわ)るような毎日(まいにち)だ。
Between moving preparations and paperwork, every day is a whirlwind.

A common idiomatic expression using the body part 目(め) (eye). The literal image of eyes spinning works for both physical dizziness and figurative overwhelm. Sense 2 often appears in the pattern 目(め)が回(まわ)るほど忙(いそが)しい (so busy one's head spins).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 目(め)が回(まわ)るほど: to the point of dizziness; extremely (busy, etc.)
  • 目(め)が回(まわ)るような忙(いそが)しさ: dizzying busyness

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • めまいがする: to feel dizzy — more clinical/medical
  • 頭(あたま)がくらくらする: head is spinning — more casual
  • 猫(ねこ)の手(て)も借(か)りたい: so busy one would borrow a cat's paws — idiomatic for extreme busyness
