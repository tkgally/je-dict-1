1. to confess, to admit, to disclose
To reveal something that has been hidden, such as a secret, wrongdoing, or personal truth.
罪(つみ)を告白(こくはく)する。
To confess one's sins.
彼(かれ)は秘密(ひみつ)を告白(こくはく)した。
He confessed his secret.
長年(ながねん)隠(かく)していた過去(かこ)を友人(ゆうじん)に告白(こくはく)した。
I confessed to a friend the past I had hidden for many years.
2. to confess one's love, to ask someone out
To tell someone you have romantic feelings for them. This is a central concept in Japanese dating culture, where a formal confession typically marks the beginning of a relationship.
好(す)きな人(ひと)に告白(こくはく)する。
To confess one's feelings to the person one likes.
彼女(かのじょ)に告白(こくはく)したが、振(ふ)られた。
I confessed my feelings to her, but was turned down.
卒業式(そつぎょうしき)の日(ひ)に勇気(ゆうき)を出(だ)して告白(こくはく)した。
I mustered up my courage and confessed my feelings on graduation day.

Has two distinct uses: a general meaning of confessing/admitting something, and a culturally important meaning of declaring romantic feelings.

CULTURAL NOTE:
In Japanese dating culture, 告白(こくはく) is the act of formally telling someone you like them and asking them to date you. This confession is considered the official start of a relationship. Without a 告白(こくはく), two people are not considered to be dating, no matter how much time they spend together.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 愛(あい)を告白(こくはく)する: to confess one's love
  • 罪(つみ)を告白(こくはく)する: to confess one's sins
  • 告白(こくはく)される: to receive a confession of love
  • 告白(こくはく)タイム: confession time (in reality TV shows)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 白状(はくじょう)する: to confess — used for crimes or wrongdoing, often under pressure
  • 打(う)ち明(あ)ける: to confide, to reveal — more intimate, sharing personal feelings or secrets with someone close
