1. distant view; looking far away; gazing into the distance
The act of looking at something far away, or the view itself seen from a distance. A literary and descriptive word often used for scenic landscapes.
山(やま)の上(うえ)から海(うみ)を遠望(えんぼう)する。
To gaze at the sea from the mountaintop.
遠望(えんぼう)すると、富士山(ふじさん)の姿(すがた)が見(み)えた。
Looking into the distance, I could see the shape of Mt. Fuji.
展望台(てんぼうだい)からは、晴(は)れた日(ひ)には遠(とお)くの島々(しまじま)まで遠望(えんぼう)できる。
From the observation deck, you can see distant islands on clear days.

Composed of 遠(えん) (far, distant) and 望(ぼう) (to gaze, to hope). A somewhat literary word used in descriptive writing, travel guides, and scenic descriptions. Also functions as a する verb: 遠望(えんぼう)する (to gaze into the distance).

USAGE:
Often appears in travel writing, photography descriptions, and literary prose. More evocative and formal than simply 遠(とお)くを見(み)る (to look far away).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 遠望(えんぼう)する — to gaze into the distance
  • 遠望(えんぼう)できる — can be seen from afar
  • 山(やま)を遠望(えんぼう)する — to view a mountain from the distance

RELATED TERMS:

  • 展望(てんぼう) — panoramic view; broader, often from an elevated vantage point
  • 眺望(ちょうぼう) — view, prospect; similar but emphasizes the scenery being viewed
  • 望遠(ぼうえん) — telephoto, far-seeing; same kanji reversed, used in 望遠(ぼうえん)レンズ (telephoto lens)
