若葉(わかば)

わかば
noun
young leaves; fresh green leaves
1. young leaves; fresh green leaves; new foliage
Newly sprouted leaves in spring or early summer, typically bright green and tender.
若葉(わかば)が芽吹(めぶ)いた。
Young leaves have sprouted.
若葉(わかば)の緑(みどり)がまぶしい。
The green of the young leaves is dazzling.
五月(ごがつ)になると、山(やま)は若葉(わかば)で美(うつく)しく彩(いろど)られる。
In May, the mountains are beautifully colored with fresh green leaves.

A compound of 若(わか)い (young) and 葉(は) (leaf). A seasonal word strongly associated with late spring and early summer.

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
若葉(わかば) is a 季語(きご) (seasonal word) for early summer in haiku. The sight of fresh green leaves is celebrated in Japanese culture as a symbol of vitality and renewal. 若葉(わかば)マーク is also used informally to mean a beginner's mark — the green and yellow V-shaped sticker that new drivers must display.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 若葉(わかば)が茂(しげ)る — young leaves grow thick
  • 若葉(わかば)の季節(きせつ) — the season of fresh green leaves
  • 若葉(わかば)マーク — beginner's mark (for new drivers)

RELATED TERMS:

  • 新緑(しんりょく) — fresh greenery; the broader concept of spring/early summer foliage
  • 青葉(あおば) — green leaves; mature green foliage, slightly later in season
  • 紅葉(こうよう) — autumn leaves; the seasonal counterpart
