何(なん)でもない

なんでもない
expression, adjective
nothing special; no big deal
1. it's nothing, no problem, no big deal
Used to dismiss concern, brush off a question, or indicate that something is not worth worrying about.
「どうしたの？」「何(なん)でもない。」
"What's wrong?" "It's nothing."
大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)、何(なん)でもないから心配(しんぱい)しないで。
I'm fine, it's nothing, so don't worry.
あんな小(ちい)さなミスは何(なん)でもないことだ。
A small mistake like that is nothing to worry about.
2. trivial, insignificant, nothing special
Describes something as ordinary or unremarkable, not worthy of attention.
何(なん)でもない日常(にちじょう)が幸(しあわ)せだ。
An ordinary, unremarkable daily life is happiness.
何(なん)でもない風景(ふうけい)が懐(なつ)かしく感(かん)じられた。
An ordinary landscape felt nostalgic.
何(なん)でもない一言(ひとこと)が彼(かれ)の心(こころ)を深(ふか)く傷(きず)つけた。
An unremarkable comment deeply hurt his feelings.

An extremely common expression in daily conversation. In sense 1, it is typically used as a standalone response or predicate. In sense 2, it modifies a noun as an attributive adjective (何(なん)でもない + noun).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • 何(なん)でもないよ: it's nothing (casual, reassuring)
  • 何(なん)でもないことだ: it's no big deal
  • 何(なん)でもない顔(かお)をする: to act as if nothing happened
  • 何(なん)でもないように見(み)える: to look like nothing is wrong

USAGE:
Often used when someone notices that something might be wrong and the speaker wants to deflect attention. It can sound dismissive, so context and tone matter.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 大(たい)したことない: not a big deal — similar dismissive nuance but emphasizes lack of importance
  • 別(べつ)に: not particularly — more casual and sometimes curt
