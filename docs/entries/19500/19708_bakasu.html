1. to trick; to bewitch; to deceive through transformation
Originally describes the supernatural act of foxes or tanuki deceiving humans by shapeshifting. By extension, used for any clever trick or deception that fools someone.
狐(きつね)に化(ば)かされた。
I was tricked by a fox.
狸(たぬき)が人間(にんげん)を化(ば)かす話(はなし)は日本(にほん)の昔話(むかしばなし)に多(おお)い。
Stories of tanuki tricking humans are common in Japanese folktales.
まるで狐(きつね)に化(ば)かされたかのように、さっきまであった道(みち)が消(き)えていた。
As if bewitched by a fox, the road that had been there moments ago had vanished.

A verb rooted in Japanese folklore, where foxes (狐(きつね)) and tanuki (狸(たぬき)) are believed to have the ability to shapeshift and deceive humans. The passive form 化(ば)かされる ('to be tricked/bewitched') is especially common.

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive. The trickster is the subject; the person deceived is marked with を.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 狐(きつね)に化(ば)かされる — to be tricked by a fox
  • 狸(たぬき)に化(ば)かされる — to be tricked by a tanuki
  • 化(ば)かし合(あ)い — mutual deception, a battle of wits

CULTURAL NOTE:
The expression 狐(きつね)に化(ば)かされたよう ('as if tricked by a fox') is used when something inexplicable or bewildering happens — such as losing something that was just in your hand.

RELATED WORDS:

  • 化(ば)ける — to transform, to take the form of; the intransitive counterpart
  • 騙(だま)す — to deceive, to cheat; general-purpose word for deception
  • 惑(まど)わす — to bewilder, to lead astray; more literary
