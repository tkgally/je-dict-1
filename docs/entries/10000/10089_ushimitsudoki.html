丑三(うしみ)つ時(どき)

うしみつどき
noun
witching hour, dead of night
1. witching hour, dead of night
The hour of the ox (approximately 2-2:30 AM), traditionally believed to be when supernatural beings and ghosts are most active.
丑三(うしみ)つ時(どき)に目(め)が覚(さ)めた。
I woke up at the witching hour.
丑三(うしみ)つ時(どき)には外出(がいしゅつ)しないほうがいい。
It's best not to go out during the witching hour.
怪談(かいだん)では丑三(うしみ)つ時(どき)に幽霊(ゆうれい)が現(あらわ)れるとされている。
In ghost stories, spirits are said to appear during the witching hour.

ETYMOLOGY:
From the traditional Japanese time system: 丑(うし) (ox) + 三(み)つ (third division) + 時(どき) (time). In the old time system, the day was divided into 12 periods named after the Chinese zodiac animals. The 'hour of the ox' (丑(うし)の刻(こく)) was roughly 1-3 AM, and the third division would be approximately 2-2:30 AM.

CULTURAL NOTE:
丑三(うしみ)つ時(どき) is strongly associated with horror, ghosts, and the supernatural in Japanese folklore. It's the setting for many ghost stories and the traditional time for 丑(うし)の刻参(こくまい)り (shrine visits to curse someone).
