きょうび

きょうび
adverb
these days; nowadays
1. these days, nowadays
Used to comment on the current state of things, often with a nuance of complaint or surprise at how times have changed. More colloquial than さいきん or ちかごろ.
きょうび、そんなことする人(ひと)はいない。
Nowadays, nobody does that kind of thing.
きょうびの若(わか)い人(ひと)は新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)まないらしい。
Young people these days apparently don't read newspapers.
きょうび、現金(げんきん)しか使(つか)えない店(みせ)は珍(めずら)しくなった。
These days, shops that only accept cash have become rare.

Sometimes written in kanji as 今日(きょう)び.

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • きょうび〜ない: these days ... not
  • きょうびの〜: today's ..., ... of these days

USAGE: More conversational and slightly old-fashioned compared to 最近(さいきん) or 近頃(ちかごろ). Often carries a tone of mild complaint or commentary about changing times. Commonly used by middle-aged and older speakers.
