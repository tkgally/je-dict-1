〜など

など
particle
and so on, et cetera, such as
1. and so on, et cetera
Particle used after nouns to indicate that the list is not exhaustive, similar to 'etc.' or 'and so forth' in English.
りんごやみかんなどを買(か)いました。
I bought apples, oranges, and so on.
Sense 1: non-exhaustive list
本(ほん)やノートなどを持(も)ってきてください。
Please bring books, notebooks, and so on.
Sense 1: etc.
野菜(やさい)や果物(くだもの)などを食(た)べています。
I eat vegetables, fruits, and so on.
Sense 1: etc.
サッカー(さっかー)や野球(やきゅう)などのスポーツが好(す)きです。
I like sports such as soccer and baseball.
Sense 1: and so on
お茶(ちゃ)やコーヒーなどはいかがですか。
Would you like tea, coffee, or something like that?
Sense 1: and so on
2. such as, like
Used to give examples from a larger category, meaning 'things like' or 'such as.'
東京(とうきょう)や大阪(おおさか)などの大(おお)きな都市(とし)
Large cities such as Tokyo and Osaka
Sense 2: examples
英語(えいご)など外国語(がいこくご)を勉強(べんきょう)したい。
I want to study foreign languages such as English.
Sense 2: such as
京都(きょうと)などの古(ふる)い町(まち)が好(す)きです。
I like old towns such as Kyoto.
Sense 2: such as
ピアノなどの楽器(がっき)を習(なら)いたい。
I want to learn instruments such as piano.
Sense 2: such as
寿司(すし)など和食(わしょく)が食(た)べたいです。
I want to eat Japanese food such as sushi.
Sense 2: such as
3. (something) like (humble/dismissive)
Used to humble oneself or dismiss something as unimportant.
私(わたし)などにはできません。
Someone like me cannot do it.
Sense 3: humble/self-deprecating
こんなものなど要(い)りません。
I don't need something like this.
Sense 3: dismissive
私(わたし)などがお役(やく)に立(た)てるでしょうか。
Can someone like me be of help?
Sense 3: humble
そんなことなど気(き)にしないでください。
Please don't worry about something like that.
Sense 3: dismissive
私(わたし)などまだまだです。
Someone like me still has a long way to go.
Sense 3: humble

USAGE PATTERNS:

  • A や B など: A, B, and so on
  • A など: things like A
  • Noun + などの + Noun: nouns such as ~

VARIATIONS:

  • なんか (casual, spoken form of など)
  • なんて (casual, often with surprise or dismissive nuance)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 〜などと言(い)う (to say things like ~)
  • 〜などを含(ふく)む (to include things like ~)

NUANCE: When used about oneself (私(わたし)など), it conveys humility. When used dismissively about something else, it can sound condescending.
