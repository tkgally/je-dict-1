ボーイフレンド

ぼーいふれんど
noun
boyfriend
1. boyfriend; male romantic partner
A man one is dating in a romantic relationship. Loanword from English. In modern Japanese, 彼氏(かれし) is more commonly used in everyday conversation; ボーイフレンド sometimes implies a less serious or more casual male friend.
彼女(かのじょ)にはボーイフレンドがいる。
She has a boyfriend.
今(いま)、ボーイフレンドはいません。
I don't have a boyfriend right now.
新(あたら)しいボーイフレンドを両親(りょうしん)に紹介(しょうかい)した。
I introduced my new boyfriend to my parents.
大学(だいがく)の頃(ころ)のボーイフレンドとは今(いま)でも連絡(れんらく)を取(と)っている。
I'm still in touch with a boyfriend from my university days.

ボーイフレンド is a loanword from English "boyfriend." While it directly translates the English meaning, in everyday modern Japanese 彼氏(かれし) is far more common when referring to a romantic partner.

NUANCE COMPARED TO 彼氏(かれし):

  • 彼氏(かれし): standard, neutral term — "my boyfriend" in the romantic sense
  • ボーイフレンド: can suggest a casual relationship, a male friend who is close but not necessarily a serious partner, or carries a slightly dated/romanticized feel
  • In some contexts, ボーイフレンド is used by older speakers or in literary/translated contexts

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ボーイフレンドができる: to get a boyfriend
  • ボーイフレンドと別(わか)れる: to break up with one's boyfriend
  • ボーイフレンドと付(つ)き合(あ)う: to date a boyfriend

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 彼氏(かれし): boyfriend (most common everyday term)
  • 恋人(こいびと): romantic partner (gender-neutral, slightly more romantic)
  • パートナー: partner (modern, gender-neutral, often used for long-term relationships)

RELATED TERMS:

  • ガールフレンド: girlfriend (female counterpart, similar register issues)
  • 彼女(かのじょ): girlfriend (most common everyday term)
