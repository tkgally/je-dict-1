お姉(ねえ)ちゃん

おねえちゃん
noun
older sister (informal); big sis; young woman (familiar)
1. older sister (informal); big sis
An informal, affectionate way to refer to or address one's older sister. Warmer and more casual than お姉(ねえ)さん.
お姉(ねえ)ちゃん、一緒(いっしょ)に遊(あそ)ぼう。
Big sis, let's play together.
お姉(ねえ)ちゃんは大学(だいがく)で医学(いがく)を勉強(べんきょう)している。
My big sister is studying medicine at university.
小(ちい)さい頃(ころ)はいつもお姉(ねえ)ちゃんの後(あと)をついて回(まわ)っていた。
When I was little, I always followed my big sister around.
2. young woman (familiar address); miss
Used to address or refer to a young woman who is not one's sister, typically by children or in a friendly, informal manner.
お姉(ねえ)ちゃん、風船(ふうせん)ちょうだい。
Miss, can I have a balloon?
Said by a child to a young woman
隣(となり)のお姉(ねえ)ちゃんがお菓子(かし)をくれた。
The young woman next door gave me sweets.
あのお姉(ねえ)ちゃん、優(やさ)しそうだね。
That young lady seems kind, doesn't she?
Said by a child

The informal equivalent of お姉(ねえ)さん, using the diminutive suffix ちゃん instead of さん. Primarily used by younger siblings, children, or in casual family settings.

FORMALITY SCALE FOR "OLDER SISTER":

  • 姉(あね): humble/plain — used when referring to one's own sister to outsiders
  • お姉(ねえ)ちゃん: informal — used within the family, especially by children
  • お姉(ねえ)さん: polite — standard polite form, also used for young women in general
  • 姉上(あねうえ): archaic/literary — seen in period dramas and historical fiction

USAGE:
In Japanese families, younger siblings typically address their older sister as お姉(ねえ)ちゃん rather than by name. Adults may continue using this form well into adulthood.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • お姉(ねえ)さん: older sister (polite); young woman — more polite and versatile
  • 姉(あね): older sister (humble/plain) — used about one's own sister in formal speech
  • 姉貴(あねき): older sister (rough/masculine) — used by males in casual or tough speech
