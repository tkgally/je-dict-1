1. putting one's affairs in order; tidying up personal matters
The act of organizing, sorting, and settling one's personal belongings and affairs, typically in preparation for a major life change such as moving, retirement, or in anticipation of death.
引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの前(まえ)に身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)をする。
To put one's affairs in order before moving.
定年(ていねん)退職(たいしょく)を前(まえ)に、身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)を始(はじ)めた。
Ahead of retirement, I started putting my affairs in order.
祖父(そふ)は病気(びょうき)がわかってから、静(しず)かに身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)を進(すす)めていたらしい。
After learning of his illness, my grandfather apparently went about quietly putting his affairs in order.

A four-character compound meaning to organize one's personal surroundings and affairs. Composed of 身辺(しんぺん) (one's person/surroundings) + 整理(せいり) (organizing/sorting). Carries a sense of finality or preparation for a significant transition.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)をする: to put one's affairs in order
  • 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)を始(はじ)める: to start putting one's affairs in order
  • 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり)を進(すす)める: to proceed with putting one's affairs in order

USAGE:
Often implies a serious life transition — retirement, terminal illness, divorce, or simply aging. When someone says they are doing 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり), it can carry a somber tone, suggesting they are preparing for the end of a chapter in life. However, it can also be used more lightly for decluttering before a move.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 終活(しゅうかつ): end-of-life preparation activities — a modern term for activities like writing a will, organizing belongings, and planning one's funeral while still healthy
  • 断捨離(だんしゃり): decluttering — focuses on letting go of unnecessary possessions as a lifestyle philosophy. Less emotionally heavy than 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり).
  • 片付(かたづ)ける: to tidy up — a general term for cleaning and organizing. 身辺整理(しんぺんせいり) implies a more thorough and purposeful settling of affairs.
