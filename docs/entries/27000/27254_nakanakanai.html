なかなかない

なかなかない
expression
rare; hard to find; not easily come by
1. rare; hard to find; not easily come by
Describes something that is uncommon, scarce, or difficult to encounter. The combination of なかなか (not easily) with ない (not exist/not have) creates an expression meaning something is genuinely rare or special.
こんなチャンスはなかなかない。
An opportunity like this is rare.
こんなに美味(おい)しい店(みせ)はなかなかない。
It's rare to find a restaurant this good.
彼(かれ)のように信頼(しんらい)できる人(ひと)はなかなかいない。
It's hard to find someone as trustworthy as him.
こんなに天気(てんき)のいい日(ひ)は、一年(いちねん)を通(とお)してなかなかない。
A day with weather this nice is rare throughout the year.

Composed of なかなか + ない. The adverb なかなか with a negative predicate means "not easily" or "hardly." When combined with ない (nonexistent) or いない (no person), the expression conveys rarity or scarcity, often with a positive nuance — the thing being described is special precisely because it is rare.

Distinguish from なかなか + other negative verbs, which simply mean "not easily doing something":

  • なかなか来(こ)ない: not coming (despite expectations)
  • なかなか決(き)まらない: hard to decide

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • なかなかない機会(きかい): a rare opportunity
  • なかなかない経験(けいけん): an uncommon experience
  • なかなかないことだ: it's something that rarely happens

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • めったにない: very rare; seldom — stronger emphasis on extreme rarity
  • 珍(めずら)しい: rare; unusual — an adjective describing something uncommon or curious
  • 滅多(めった)に〜ない: hardly ever — emphasizes infrequent occurrence of an action
