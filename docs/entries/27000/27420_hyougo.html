兵庫(ひょうご)

ひょうご
noun
Hyogo — a prefecture in the Kansai region on Honshu's southwest coast, whose capital is Kobe; also short for {兵庫県|ひょうごけん}
1. Hyogo Prefecture — a prefecture in the Kansai region of western Honshu, on the Seto Inland Sea coast; capital is Kobe; often referenced simply as {兵庫|ひょうご} when context is clear
兵庫(ひょうご) is a prefecture (県(けん)) in the 近畿(きんき) (Kansai) region of central-western Honshu. The full official name is 兵庫県(ひょうごけん), and the prefectural capital is 神戸(こうべ) (Kobe). The prefecture stretches from the Sea of Japan in the north to the Seto Inland Sea in the south, making it one of the few prefectures with coastlines on two seas. It is famous for the port city of Kobe, the hot-spring town of Arima, the historic castle town of Himeji (姫路(ひめじ), with the World Heritage castle 姫路城(ひめじじょう)), and high-end products like 神戸牛(こうべぎゅう) (Kobe beef). The 1995 Great Hanshin Earthquake (阪神淡路大震災(はんしんあわじだいしんさい)) struck the prefecture, devastating Kobe.

兵庫(ひょうご) is the short name for 兵庫県(ひょうごけん) (Hyogo Prefecture), one of the 47 prefectures of Japan. It is in the Kansai (近畿(きんき)) region of western Honshu and is famous nationally and internationally for the port city of Kobe.

GEOGRAPHIC FACTS:

  • Region: 近畿(きんき) (Kansai)
  • Location: central-western Honshu
  • Capital: 神戸市(こうべし) (Kobe)
  • One of only a few prefectures with coastlines on TWO seas — the 日本海(にほんかい) (Sea of Japan) to the north and the 瀬戸内海(せとないかい) (Seto Inland Sea) to the south
  • Includes 淡路島(あわじしま) (Awaji Island), the largest island in the Seto Inland Sea

MAJOR CITIES:

  • 神戸(こうべ): Kobe — the capital, a major port city
  • 姫路(ひめじ): Himeji — castle town, home of 姫路城(ひめじじょう)
  • 西宮(にしのみや): Nishinomiya — site of 甲子園(こうしえん) stadium and 西宮神社(にしのみやじんじゃ)
  • 尼崎(あまがさき): Amagasaki — industrial city near Osaka
  • 芦屋(あしや): Ashiya — known as an upscale residential area

FAMOUS FOR:

  • 神戸(こうべ)ビーフ} (or {神戸牛(こうべぎゅう)): Kobe beef — internationally renowned premium beef
  • 姫路城(ひめじじょう): Himeji Castle — UNESCO World Heritage site, the iconic "White Heron Castle"
  • 有馬温泉(ありまおんせん): Arima Onsen — one of Japan's three oldest hot-spring resorts
  • 甲子園(こうしえん): Koshien Stadium — home of high school baseball championships and the Hanshin Tigers
  • 灘(なだ)の酒(さけ): sake from the Nada district of Kobe
  • 1995 阪神淡路大震災(はんしんあわじだいしんさい): Great Hanshin (Kobe) Earthquake

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 兵庫県(ひょうごけん): Hyogo Prefecture (full name)
  • 兵庫(ひょうご)出身(しゅっしん): from / native to Hyogo
  • 兵庫(ひょうご)在住(ざいじゅう): residing in Hyogo
  • 兵庫県(ひょうごけん)知事(ちじ): governor of Hyogo Prefecture
  • 兵庫(ひょうご)県内(けんない): within Hyogo Prefecture

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 神戸(こうべ): Kobe (the capital city)
  • 大阪(おおさか): Osaka (the neighboring prefecture to the east)
  • 京都(きょうと): Kyoto (also in Kansai)
  • 奈良(なら): Nara (also in Kansai)
  • 阪神(はんしん): "Osaka-Kobe" (a portmanteau used for the Osaka-Kobe metropolitan area, e.g., 阪神(はんしん)タイガース}, {阪神(はんしん)電車(でんしゃ))
  • 近畿(きんき) / 関西(かんさい): Kinki / Kansai region (the larger geographic area)
  • 県(けん): prefecture (the administrative unit)

USAGE NOTES:

  • In addresses and formal contexts, the full 兵庫県(ひょうごけん) is used
  • In casual conversation, especially when the listener knows the topic is prefectures, just 兵庫(ひょうご) is fine
  • The reading ひょうご has a long ō (extended o) — sometimes mis-romanized as "Hyogo" (no macron) which is the standard English spelling
  • Distinct from the homophone 標語(ひょうご) ("slogan, motto"), but context disambiguates them
  • The name 兵庫(ひょうご) dates from the 兵庫津(ひょうごのつ) historical port that gave the prefecture its name; the kanji literally mean "weapons / military" + "storehouse" and originally referred to a military storehouse
