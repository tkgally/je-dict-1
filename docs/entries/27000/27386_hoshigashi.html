干(ほ)し菓子(がし)

ほしがし
noun
dried sweets; dry confections (traditional Japanese sweets with very low moisture content)
1. dried sweets; dry confections (a category of traditional Japanese sweets with low moisture content and long shelf life)
A category of traditional Japanese sweets (和菓子(わがし)) characterized by very low moisture content (typically under 10%), giving them a hard, dry texture and a long shelf life. Common examples include 落雁(らくがん) (pressed sugar-and-rice-flour cakes), 金平糖(こんぺいとう) (small star-shaped sugar candies), 煎餅(せんべい) (in the sweet variety), and various sugar-coated and roasted sweets. They are especially associated with the formal tea ceremony, where they are served with 濃茶(こいちゃ) (thick tea) and high-grade matcha.
干(ほ)し菓子(がし)を食(た)べる。
To eat dried sweets.
抹茶(まっちゃ)と一緒(いっしょ)に干(ほ)し菓子(がし)が出(だ)された。
Dried sweets were served together with the matcha.
この店(みせ)の干(ほ)し菓子(がし)は保存(ほぞん)が利(き)くので、お土産(みやげ)にぴったりだ。
This shop's dried sweets keep well, so they're perfect as a souvenir.
茶会(ちゃかい)では、濃茶(こいちゃ)の前(まえ)に季節(きせつ)の花(はな)をかたどった干(ほ)し菓子(がし)が丁寧(ていねい)に並(なら)べられていた。
At the tea ceremony, dried sweets shaped to resemble seasonal flowers were carefully arranged before the thick tea.

干(ほ)し菓子(がし) is the traditional Japanese category of sweets characterized by very low moisture content, in contrast to 生菓子(なまがし) ("fresh sweets") which are moist and perishable.

FORMATION:

  • 干(ほ)し ("dried") + 菓子(がし) ("sweet, confection") → 干(ほ)し菓子(がし)
  • The 菓子(かし) component voices to {がし} as the second element of a compound (rendaku).

DEFINING FEATURES:

  • Moisture content typically under about 10%
  • Hard, dry, or brittle texture
  • Long shelf life — often months at room temperature
  • Frequently molded or shaped into seasonal motifs (flowers, leaves, animals)

COMMON EXAMPLES:

  • 落雁(らくがん): pressed cakes of fine sugar and rice flour
  • 金平糖(こんぺいとう): small, star-shaped sugar candies (originally Portuguese)
  • 煎餅(せんべい): sweet rice crackers (the sweet sugar-coated kind)
  • 有平糖(あるへいとう): pulled and twisted sugar candy
  • 州浜(すはま): roasted soybean flour sweets

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 干(ほ)し菓子(がし)を出(だ)す: to serve dried sweets
  • 干(ほ)し菓子(がし)を詰(つ)め合(あ)わせる: to assemble an assortment of dried sweets
  • 季節(きせつ)の干(ほ)し菓子(がし): seasonal dried sweets
  • 茶会(ちゃかい)の干(ほ)し菓子(がし): dried sweets for a tea ceremony

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 生菓子(なまがし): "fresh sweets" — moist sweets with high water content (e.g., 大福(だいふく), 練(ね)り切(き)り); short shelf life
  • 半生菓子(はんなまがし): "semi-dry sweets" — intermediate moisture content (e.g., 最中(もなか))
  • 和菓子(わがし): Japanese-style sweets — the broader category that includes 干(ほ)し菓子(がし), 生菓子(なまがし), and 半生菓子(はんなまがし)
  • 落雁(らくがん): a specific kind of 干(ほ)し菓子(がし) made from sugar and rice flour

TEA-CEREMONY CONTEXT:

  • In a formal tea ceremony, 干(ほ)し菓子(がし) accompany 濃茶(こいちゃ) (thick tea) or are served at less formal points in the gathering.
  • The sweets are often arranged on a flat tray (干(ほ)し菓子(がし)盆(ぼん) or 菓子器(かしき)) and chosen to reflect the season.

USAGE NOTES:

  • The reading {ほしがし} (with rendaku) is standard; {ほしかし} without voicing is non-standard.
  • The same kanji 菓子(かし) appears in many compounds: 和菓子(わがし), 洋菓子(ようがし), 駄菓子(だがし), etc.
