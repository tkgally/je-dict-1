ラッシュガード

らっしゅがーど
noun
rash guard — a close-fitting swim shirt worn for sun and abrasion protection at the beach, pool, or while surfing
1. rash guard — a stretchy, close-fitting swim shirt worn for sun protection and to prevent skin irritation (originally from surfing)
A stretchy, close-fitting swim shirt — typically made of nylon-and-spandex or polyester — designed to be worn in the water. The garment originated as a surfer's accessory, protecting the chest and torso from board-rash; in Japan it has become standard beach and pool wear for sun protection (日焼(ひや)け防止(ぼうし)) and modesty, often worn over a bikini or with swim shorts. Many Japanese ラッシュガード have UPF sun-protection ratings and are sold for adults and children alike.
ラッシュガードを着(き)る。
To wear a rash guard.
日焼(ひや)け対策(たいさく)にラッシュガードを買(か)った。
I bought a rash guard for sun protection.
子(こ)どもにはラッシュガード付(つ)きの水着(みずぎ)を選(えら)ぶ親(おや)が増(ふ)えている。
More parents are choosing swimsuits with built-in rash guards for their children.
紫外線(しがいせん)が強(つよ)いビーチでは、ビキニの上(うえ)に長袖(ながそで)のラッシュガードを羽織(はお)ると安心(あんしん)して過(す)ごせる。
At a beach where UV rays are strong, you can relax by throwing a long-sleeved rash guard over your bikini.

ラッシュガード is a loanword from English "rash guard," referring to a stretchy swim shirt worn for sun protection and abrasion prevention. It is one of the most common pieces of beach and pool wear in modern Japan.

ORIGIN:

  • English "rash guard" originally referred to a thin lycra/nylon top that surfers wear under or instead of a wetsuit to prevent rash from the surfboard's wax and surface.
  • In Japan, the term has expanded to cover any swim-friendly tight shirt worn for sun protection or modesty, not just for surfing.

DESIGN AND VARIANTS:

  • Long-sleeved (長袖(ながそで)) versions are the most common in Japan, prized for sun protection.
  • Short-sleeved (半袖(はんそで)) and zip-front (フルジップ) styles are also widely sold.
  • Many are UPF-rated for UV protection ({UVカット}).
  • Often sold as a set with swim shorts or matching bikini bottoms.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ラッシュガードを着(き)る: to wear a rash guard
  • ラッシュガードを羽織(はお)る: to throw on a rash guard over swimwear
  • 長袖(ながそで)ラッシュガード: long-sleeved rash guard
  • 子(こ)ども用(よう)ラッシュガード: children's rash guard
  • 日焼(ひや)け対策(たいさく)にラッシュガード: a rash guard for sun protection
  • UVカットラッシュガード: UV-cut rash guard
  • ラッシュガード付(つ)きの水着(みずぎ): swimwear with a built-in rash guard

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 水着(みずぎ): swimsuit (general) — covers all swimwear; a ラッシュガード is typically worn with a 水着(みずぎ)
  • ビキニ: bikini — often worn under or beneath a ラッシュガード
  • ウェットスーツ: wetsuit — much thicker neoprene suit for cold-water sports
  • スイムウェア: swimwear (loanword) — broader category
  • 日焼(ひや)け止(ど)め: sunscreen — the most direct alternative for sun protection
  • パーカー: a hooded jacket; sometimes worn over swimwear, but not water-friendly like a ラッシュガード
  • アームカバー: arm covers — alternative sun protection for the arms; can be worn on land or in water

USAGE NOTES:

  • The Japanese pronunciation has a long vowel on ー and is usually written ラッシュガード (note the small ッ).
  • The garment is gender-neutral and worn by men, women, and children; it has become particularly popular as part of conservative-sun-protection beach culture.
  • ラッシュガード is closely associated with beaches, pools, water parks, and surfing/SUP/snorkeling activities.
