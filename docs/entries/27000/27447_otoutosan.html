弟(おとうと)さん

おとうとさん
noun
younger brother (polite — someone else's)
1. younger brother (polite — referring to someone else's)
Polite term for the listener's or a third party's younger brother. The honorific さん makes it appropriate for talking about others; one's own younger brother is 弟(おとうと) (without さん).
弟(おとうと)さんはおいくつですか。
How old is your younger brother?
弟(おとうと)さんはどんなお仕事(しごと)をされていますか。
What kind of work does your younger brother do?
先日(せんじつ)、田中(たなか)さんの弟(おとうと)さんと偶然(ぐうぜん)駅(えき)でお会(あ)いしました。
The other day I happened to run into Mr. Tanaka's younger brother at the station.

Polite reference form for someone else's younger brother — 弟(おとうと) + the honorific suffix さん. Do not use for one's own younger brother when speaking to outsiders.

USAGE:

  • Used when talking about the listener's or a third party's younger brother
  • For one's own younger brother (to outsiders): 弟(おとうと)
  • Within the family, older siblings call a younger brother by name, not by 弟(おとうと)さん

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 弟(おとうと)さんはおいくつですか: How old is your younger brother?
  • 弟(おとうと)さんによろしく: Say hi to your younger brother
  • 弟(おとうと)さんとそっくり: looks just like your younger brother

RELATED WORDS:

  • 弟(おとうと): one's own younger brother (humble; for outsiders)
  • お兄(にい)さん: older brother (polite)
  • 妹(いもうと)さん: someone else's younger sister (parallel form)
  • 弟(おとうと)くん: even more familiar, used by close friends or to address a child
