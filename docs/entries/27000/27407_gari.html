ガリ

がり
noun
gari — thinly sliced pickled ginger served as a palate cleanser with sushi (sushi-restaurant term)
1. gari — thinly sliced ginger pickled in sweet vinegar, served alongside sushi as a palate cleanser between pieces
Thinly sliced young ginger pickled in sweet vinegar (甘酢(あまず)), served alongside sushi to be eaten between different pieces as a palate cleanser. ガリ is a sushi-restaurant term (すし}{屋(や)の隠語(いんご)) that has become widely known to ordinary speakers; the more general culinary term is ガリ}{生姜(しょうが) or 甘酢(あまず)生姜(しょうが). The pickled slices typically have a pale pink or pale yellow color (the pink hue can come from naturally young ginger or from added coloring).
ガリを食(た)べる。
To eat gari (pickled ginger).
寿司(すし)と一緒(いっしょ)にガリが出(で)てきた。
Gari was served along with the sushi.
ガリは次(つぎ)のネタを食(た)べる前(まえ)に口(くち)直(なお)しとして少(すこ)し食(た)べる。
You eat a little gari as a palate cleanser before having the next piece of sushi.
回転(かいてん)寿司(ずし)では、ガリやお茶(ちゃ)が自由(じゆう)に取(と)れるように客席(きゃくせき)に置(お)かれていることが多(おお)い。
At conveyor-belt sushi restaurants, gari and tea are often placed at the customer's seat so that diners can freely help themselves.

ガリ is the sushi-restaurant term for thinly sliced sweet-vinegar-pickled ginger. The name is said to imitate the gari-gari ({ガリガリ}) crunching sound made when biting into the pickled ginger.

ORIGIN OF THE NAME:

  • One common explanation is onomatopoeic: from {ガリガリ} (the crunching/gnawing sound)
  • Another explanation traces it to the verb 齧(かじ)る ("to bite, gnaw") and the early sliced-by-hand style of preparation
  • It is sushi-restaurant slang (隠語(いんご)) that has fully entered the general vocabulary

CULINARY ROLE:

  • Palate cleanser between different sushi pieces (especially when switching from one fish to another)
  • Mild digestive aid and antibacterial accompaniment to raw fish (a traditional rationale)
  • Visual accent on the sushi plate — a small mound of pale pink or yellow shreds

COLOR:

  • Naturally young ginger can give a pink hue due to compounds in young ginger reacting with vinegar
  • Mass-produced ガリ often uses food coloring to standardize the pink color
  • Pure-white versions also exist and are common at higher-end sushi shops

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ガリを食(た)べる: to eat gari
  • ガリを摘(つま)む: to pick up gari (with one's fingers or chopsticks)
  • ガリのお代(かわ)り: a refill of gari
  • 自家製(じかせい)ガリ: house-made gari
  • ピンクのガリ: pink gari
  • ガリと寿司(すし): gari and sushi

SUSHI-RESTAURANT SLANG (OTHER EXAMPLES):

  • ガリ = pickled ginger
  • アガリ = green tea (sushi-restaurant slang for お茶(ちゃ))
  • ムラサキ = soy sauce ({しょうゆ})
  • シャリ = sushi rice ({すし}めし)
  • ネタ = the topping piece of fish or other ingredient
  • ナミダ = wasabi ("tears")
  • ギョク = tamago (sweet egg sushi)

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 生姜(しょうが): ginger (the general word for the spice)
  • 甘酢(あまず)生姜(しょうが): sweet-vinegar pickled ginger (the dish-name form)
  • 紅(べに)生姜(しょうが): "red ginger" — bright red pickled ginger; thicker slices, sourer; a different preparation served as a garnish on yakisoba, gyudon, and takoyaki rather than with sushi
  • 新(しん)生姜(しょうが): young ginger — the source ingredient for high-quality gari
  • 漬物(つけもの): pickles (general)
  • 寿司(すし): sushi — the dish gari accompanies

USAGE NOTES:

  • Although it began as restaurant insider vocabulary, ガリ has long since become standard everyday Japanese; supermarket packs are labeled ガリ.
  • Caution: ガリ is a polyseme. ガリ can also colloquially describe a very thin person (from {ガリガリ} "skinny"), or refer to certain crunching sounds and other gari-gari connotations. Context distinguishes.
  • Etiquette: ガリ is eaten between sushi pieces, not on top of the sushi.
