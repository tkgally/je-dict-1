欅(けやき)

けやき
noun
zelkova tree (Zelkova serrata); Japanese zelkova — a tall deciduous shade tree common as a street and shrine tree, and a prized hardwood
1. zelkova tree (Zelkova serrata); Japanese zelkova — a tall deciduous tree native to Japan, with a broad spreading crown and serrated leaves, used as a street tree, shrine tree, and source of valuable hardwood
A tall deciduous tree (Zelkova serrata) native to Japan, Korea, and parts of China, reaching 20–35 meters with a broad vase-shaped crown and finely serrated leaves. 欅(けやき) is a beloved tree in Japan: planted as a street tree in many cities, often growing as a 御神木(ごしんぼく) (sacred tree) on shrine grounds, and prized for its durable, beautifully-grained hardwood used in traditional architecture, drums, and high-end furniture. Its autumn foliage turns yellow, orange, and red, making it a popular ornamental tree.

欅(けやき)並木(なみき)が秋(あき)に色(いろ)づく。
The zelkova-lined avenue turns color in autumn.
神社(じんじゃ)の境内(けいだい)には樹齢(じゅれい)数百年(すうひゃくねん)の欅(けやき)がそびえ立(た)っていた。
In the precincts of the shrine, a zelkova tree several hundred years old stood towering.
欅(けやき)は硬(かた)く木目(もくめ)が美(うつく)しいため、古(ふる)くから社寺(しゃじ)建築(けんちく)や太鼓(たいこ)の胴(どう)、高級(こうきゅう)家具(かぐ)の材料(ざいりょう)として重宝(ちょうほう)されてきた。
Because zelkova wood is hard and has a beautiful grain, it has long been prized as a material for shrine and temple architecture, drum bodies, and high-end furniture.

欅(けやき) is the standard Japanese name for the Japanese zelkova tree (Zelkova serrata), one of the most iconic native deciduous trees in Japan.

KANJI:

  • {欅} is a single-character kanji not in the Joyo kanji list; the reading {けやき} is fixed.
  • In written Japanese, the name may also appear in hiragana (けやき) or katakana (ケヤキ) — especially in botanical writing and place names.

DISTINGUISHING FEATURES:

  • Height: typically 20–35 meters, occasionally over 40 meters
  • Crown: broad, vase-shaped — the silhouette is distinctive at a distance
  • Bark: smooth gray when young, peeling in plates as the tree matures
  • Leaves: oval with finely serrated edges; ~3–9 cm long
  • Autumn color: yellow, orange, and red

CULTURAL ROLES:

  • Street tree: One of the most common street trees in Japanese cities; the 欅 lined boulevards of Tokyo and other cities are well known
  • Shrine tree: Many shrines have ancient 欅(けやき) as their 御神木(ごしんぼく) (sacred tree)
  • Symbol tree: Designated as the prefectural tree (県木(けんぼく)) of several prefectures including Fukushima, Saitama, and Miyagi
  • 欅(けやき)坂46(ざかフォーティーシックス): the name of a former Japanese pop group, taking its name from the tree-lined avenue 欅(けやき)並木(なみき)

WOOD:

  • One of Japan's most valued native hardwoods
  • Hard, dense, durable, with a beautiful grain pattern; resists rot
  • Traditional uses: large beams in shrine and temple architecture, taiko-drum bodies, tansu (chests), high-end staircases, sword scabbards

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 欅(けやき)の木(き): a zelkova tree
  • 欅(けやき)並木(なみき): a row of zelkovas, a zelkova-lined avenue
  • 大(おお)欅(けやき): a great zelkova (often referring to a famous old tree)
  • 御神木(ごしんぼく)の欅(けやき): the sacred zelkova of (a shrine)
  • 欅(けやき)材(ざい): zelkova wood (as building/craft material)
  • 欅(けやき)の木目(もくめ): zelkova grain
  • 樹齢(じゅれい)〜年(ねん)の欅(けやき): a zelkova X years old

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 楡(にれ): elm — the zelkova is related to elms (same family, Ulmaceae); often confused at a distance
  • 椋(むく)(の}{木(き)): muku tree (Aphananthe aspera) — another large native Ulmaceae tree, sometimes confused with zelkova
  • 桜(さくら): cherry — another iconic Japanese tree; cherry blossoms in spring, zelkovas show off in autumn
  • 銀杏(いちょう): ginkgo — another famous street/shrine tree with brilliant yellow autumn color
  • 並木(なみき): a tree-lined avenue; 欅(けやき)並木(なみき) is a common compound
  • 広葉樹(こうようじゅ): broad-leaved (deciduous) tree — the general category

USAGE NOTES:

  • Pronunciation: {けやき} (three morae). Hiragana spelling is common in everyday writing because the kanji is outside the Joyo list.
  • The botanical Latin name Zelkova actually derives from a Caucasian language (Georgian), not Japanese.
  • The tree may be encountered as a place-name component (e.g., 欅(けやき)が丘(おか) "zelkova hill") and is a frequent presence in Japanese poetry, photography, and seasonal travel writing.
