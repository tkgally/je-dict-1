竹垣(たけがき)

たけがき
noun
bamboo fence — a traditional Japanese garden fence made from bamboo culms or split bamboo strips
1. a fence made of bamboo, especially the traditional decorative or functional bamboo fences used in Japanese gardens, temples, and around traditional houses
A fence constructed from bamboo culms (竹(たけ) "bamboo" + 垣(かき) "fence, hedge"). 竹垣(たけがき) is closely associated with traditional Japanese gardens, tea-house grounds, and temple compounds, where it serves both as a boundary marker and as an aesthetic element. There are many named styles distinguished by how the bamboo is cut, woven, or bound — for example 建仁寺垣(けんにんじがき) (vertical split-bamboo panels), 四(よ)つ目垣(めがき) (open lattice), and 御簾垣(みすがき) (horizontal slatted style). Modern uses include both authentic bamboo and synthetic look-alikes used in suburban gardens.
竹垣(たけがき)を作(つく)る。
To build a bamboo fence.
庭(にわ)に低(ひく)い竹垣(たけがき)を巡(めぐ)らせた。
I put a low bamboo fence around the garden.
古(ふる)いお寺(てら)の境内(けいだい)は、苔(こけ)むした竹垣(たけがき)で囲(かこ)まれていた。
The grounds of the old temple were surrounded by a moss-covered bamboo fence.
京都(きょうと)の町家(まちや)の庭(にわ)には、建仁寺垣(けんにんじがき)と呼(よ)ばれる竹垣(たけがき)がよく使(つか)われている。
In the gardens of Kyoto townhouses, a kind of bamboo fence called kenninji-gaki is often used.

竹垣(たけがき) is a fence made of bamboo, strongly associated with traditional Japanese gardens and architecture. The compound is 竹(たけ) (bamboo) + 垣(かき) (fence/hedge), with rendaku turning {かき} into がき.

CONSTRUCTION:

  • Vertical bamboo culms or split-bamboo strips bound to a horizontal frame
  • Often tied with black palm-fiber rope (棕櫚縄(しゅろなわ)) for visual contrast
  • May be solid (for screening) or open-lattice (for partial visibility)

COMMON STYLES (NAMED VARIETIES):

  • 建仁寺垣(けんにんじがき): solid panel of vertical split bamboo, named after Kennin-ji temple in Kyoto
  • 四(よ)つ目垣(めがき): open square-lattice style; common in tea gardens
  • 御簾垣(みすがき): horizontal slatted style resembling a bamboo blind
  • 大津垣(おおつがき): woven horizontal-and-vertical style
  • 鉄砲垣(てっぽうがき): alternating wide bamboo culms on opposite sides of a horizontal pole
  • 竜安寺垣(りょうあんじがき): low decorative style at Ryoan-ji temple

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 竹垣(たけがき)を作(つく)る: to build a bamboo fence
  • 竹垣(たけがき)を結(ゆ)う: to tie/construct a bamboo fence (using rope)
  • 竹垣(たけがき)を巡(めぐ)らせる: to put a bamboo fence around (a property)
  • 竹垣(たけがき)で囲(かこ)む: to surround with a bamboo fence
  • 竹垣(たけがき)越(ご)しに: across/over a bamboo fence
  • 人工(じんこう)竹垣(たけがき): artificial bamboo fence (synthetic resin)

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 生(い)け垣(がき): a hedge made of living plants — different material, similar function
  • 板垣(いたがき): a fence made of wooden boards
  • 柵(さく): fence in general, including wire and metal fences
  • 塀(へい): a wall or solid fence (often plaster, concrete, or brick)
  • 籬(まがき): a literary/poetic word for a low rustic fence (often bamboo or brushwood)
  • 竹林(ちくりん): a bamboo grove (the source of the material)

CULTURAL CONTEXT:

  • An iconic feature of Japanese garden (日本庭園(にほんていえん)) design
  • Different temples and tea houses developed distinctive styles, many of which now bear the temple's name
  • Maintenance is a traditional craft; bamboo fences weather and need replacement every several years
