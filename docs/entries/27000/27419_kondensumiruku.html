コンデンスミルク

こんでんすみるく
noun
condensed milk — sweetened condensed milk; thick, sweet milk concentrate sold in tubes or cans, used as a topping or sweetener (loanword)
1. condensed milk — thick, viscous, very sweet milk concentrate (usually sold in soft tubes or cans), commonly used in Japan as a topping for strawberries, on shaved ice, in coffee, and in baking
Loanword from English "condensed milk." In Japanese, コンデンスミルク almost always refers specifically to sweetened condensed milk — milk thickened by removing much of its water and then heavily sweetened with sugar. The product has a thick, syrupy texture and is sold most familiarly in soft squeeze tubes (typical brand: 雪印(ゆきじるし)メグミルクの「練乳(れんにゅう)」), as well as in tin cans for baking. Common everyday uses include drizzling over fresh strawberries (a classic Japanese pairing), topping shaved ice (かき氷(ごおり)), sweetening Vietnamese-style coffee, and as a baking ingredient. Note that the older domestic Japanese term 練乳(れんにゅう) (literally "kneaded milk") refers to the same product and is often used interchangeably.
コンデンスミルクをかける。
To pour on condensed milk.
イチゴにコンデンスミルクをたっぷりかけた。
I put plenty of condensed milk on the strawberries.
夏(なつ)の定番(ていばん)は、抹茶(まっちゃ)のかき氷(ごおり)にコンデンスミルクをかけて食(た)べることだ。
A summer staple for me is matcha shaved ice topped with condensed milk.
ベトナムコーヒーは、コンデンスミルクの甘(あま)さと濃(こ)いコーヒーの苦味(にがみ)が絶妙(ぜつみょう)にマッチした飲(の)み物(もの)だ。
Vietnamese coffee is a drink in which the sweetness of condensed milk matches the bitterness of strong coffee perfectly.

コンデンスミルク is the loanword for sweetened condensed milk. In Japanese culinary contexts, it usually defaults to the sweetened variety; the unsweetened concentrated form is normally called 無糖(むとう)練乳(れんにゅう) or エバミルク (evaporated milk).

KEY TERMINOLOGY:

  • コンデンスミルク = 練乳(れんにゅう) = sweetened condensed milk (the everyday default)
  • 加糖(かとう)練乳(れんにゅう): explicit "sugar-added condensed milk" (technical / nutrition labeling)
  • 無糖(むとう)練乳(れんにゅう): unsweetened condensed (= evaporated) milk; sometimes also called エバミルク
  • エバミルク: evaporated milk (loanword, less sweet, used in cooking)

PRODUCT FORM:

  • Sold in soft squeeze tubes (most common in households for table use)
  • Also sold in tin cans (often used for baking and dulce-de-leche style desserts)
  • The texture is thick, glossy, and pourable; the color is creamy white to pale yellow

TYPICAL JAPANESE USES:

  • {イチゴ}にかける: drizzle on strawberries (a classic Japanese pairing)
  • かき氷(ごおり)にかける: pour over shaved ice
  • コーヒーに入(い)れる: add to coffee (especially Vietnamese-style coffee)
  • {パン}にぬる: spread on toast
  • 手作(てづく)りスイーツの材料(ざいりょう): as a baking / dessert ingredient
  • ミルキー系(けい)のスイーツ: in milky-flavored desserts (e.g., 生(なま)キャラメル, {ミルクジャム})

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • コンデンスミルクをかける: to pour on / drizzle
  • コンデンスミルクをぬる: to spread
  • コンデンスミルクを加(くわ)える: to add (in a recipe)
  • コンデンスミルクを混(ま)ぜる: to mix in
  • コンデンスミルクが甘(あま)い: condensed milk is sweet
  • たっぷりのコンデンスミルク: plenty of condensed milk

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 練乳(れんにゅう): condensed milk (the standard Japanese name; fully interchangeable in most contexts)
  • 無糖(むとう)練乳(れんにゅう): unsweetened condensed milk
  • エバミルク: evaporated milk (loanword)
  • 生(なま)クリーム: heavy cream / fresh cream
  • ミルク: milk (loanword; everyday spoken word)
  • 牛乳(ぎゅうにゅう): cow's milk (the formal Japanese term)
  • {ミルクジャム}: milk jam (similar very-sweet milk product made with longer cooking)
  • ドゥルセ・デ・レチェ / キャラメルクリーム: dulce de leche (a related caramelized condensed-milk product)

USAGE NOTES:

  • Although the loanword and the native term 練乳(れんにゅう) are interchangeable, packaging tends to favor 練乳(れんにゅう), while menus and casual writing often prefer コンデンスミルク
  • The trademarked tube product 「練乳(れんにゅう)」 by 雪印(ゆきじるし)メグミルク (Snow Brand) is so iconic that 練乳(れんにゅう) by itself often evokes the soft tube
  • Pronunciation: コンデンス (4 morae) + ミルク (3 morae); some speakers casually clip to コンデンス・ミルク with a perceptible pause
  • Not used in technical dairy-industry / nutrition-labeling contexts, where 加糖(かとう)練乳(れんにゅう) or 無糖(むとう)練乳(れんにゅう) is standard
