バツニ

ばつに
noun
divorced twice (slang)
1. having been divorced twice (slang)
Colloquial term meaning that someone has been divorced two times. Formed from バツ (the X mark for "wrong") and 二(に) ("two"). The metaphor comes from the X marks once placed on a person's family register entry when they were removed after a divorce. Part of the series バツイチ (divorced once), バツニ (twice), バツサン (three times).
彼女(かのじょ)はバツニだ。
She has been divorced twice.
バツニでも気(き)にしない人(ひと)は多(おお)い。
Many people don't mind that someone has been divorced twice.
彼(かれ)はバツニだが、今(いま)の奥(おく)さんとは幸(しあわ)せに暮(く)らしている。
He's been divorced twice, but he's living happily with his current wife.
バツニになっても再(ふたた)び結婚(けっこん)しようと思(おも)う人(ひと)もいる。
Some people still want to marry again even after two divorces.

バツニ is informal slang and would not appear in formal speech, news writing, or legal documents. It is most often heard in casual conversation, on TV variety shows, in tabloids, and in dating contexts (matchmaking, dating apps).

ETYMOLOGY:
The バツ ("X") refers to the historical practice of striking out a name on the family register (戸籍(こせき)) after a divorce. 二(に) simply means "two," indicating two such marks.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • バツニになる: to become divorced for the second time
  • バツニの人(ひと): a twice-divorced person
  • バツニ同士(どうし): a couple where both have been divorced twice

RELATED TERMS:

  • バツイチ: divorced once (the most common term in this series)
  • バツサン: divorced three times (less commonly heard)
  • 独身(どくしん): single, unmarried
  • 離婚(りこん): divorce (the formal term)
  • 再婚(さいこん): remarriage

USAGE NOTES:

  • More polite alternatives include 二度(にど)離婚(りこん)した人(ひと) ("a person who has been divorced twice") or 再々婚(さいさいこん)予定(よてい) ("planning to marry for a third time").
  • Avoid using バツニ to describe someone in their presence unless you know they are comfortable with the term — though playful, it can sound dismissive.
