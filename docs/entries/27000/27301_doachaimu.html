ドアチャイム

どあちゃいむ
noun
door chime; doorbell (chime-type)
1. door chime; doorbell that plays a chime tone
A doorbell device that produces a melodic chime when pressed, typical of Japanese homes and apartments. The term is also used for the chime sound that plays when train or shop doors open.
ドアチャイムが鳴(な)った。
The door chime rang.
ドアチャイムを押(お)してください。
Please press the door chime.
宅配(たくはい)の人(ひと)がドアチャイムを鳴(な)らしたが、誰(だれ)も出(で)なかった。
The delivery person rang the door chime, but no one answered.
最近(さいきん)のマンションのドアチャイムには、カメラがついていて訪問者(ほうもんしゃ)を確認(かくにん)できるものが多(おお)い。
Many recent apartment door chimes come with cameras so you can check who is visiting.

ドアチャイム is a wasei-eigo compound ("door" + "chime") referring to a doorbell, typically one that plays a soft melodic chime rather than a harsh buzz.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ドアチャイムを押(お)す: to press the door chime
  • ドアチャイムを鳴(な)らす: to ring the door chime
  • ドアチャイムが鳴(な)る: the door chime rings
  • ドアチャイムに出(で)る: to answer the door (when the chime rings)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • インターホン: intercom — combines a chime with a two-way audio (and often video) communication system; this is what most modern Japanese apartments actually have, though many people still call it ドアチャイム casually
  • 呼(よ)び鈴(りん): doorbell (more old-fashioned native term)
  • ドアベル: door bell (alternative loanword, less common)
  • チャイム: chime (general — also used for school bells, train chimes, and the boarding chime in trains)

USAGE NOTES:

  • In conversation, ドアチャイムが鳴(な)った and インターホンが鳴(な)った are often used interchangeably, even though they technically refer to slightly different devices.
  • The ピンポーン(ぴんぽーん) sound that the chime makes is so iconic that ピンポーンが鳴(な)る is a common informal way to say "the doorbell rang."
