奈良(なら)

なら
noun
Nara — a prefecture in the Kansai region, and its capital city; Japan's first permanent imperial capital (710-794) and home of the famous Todai-ji temple and free-roaming deer in Nara Park
1. Nara — a prefecture in the Kansai region of central Japan, or its capital city of the same name; full prefectural name is {奈良県|ならけん}
奈良(なら) refers either to 奈良県(ならけん) (Nara Prefecture) or its capital, 奈良市(ならし) (Nara City), in the 近畿(きんき) (Kansai) region of central Honshu. It is one of the few prefectures with the same name as its capital city. The city of Nara was Japan's first permanent imperial capital from 710 to 794 (the 奈良時代(ならじだい), Nara Period), and is internationally famous for its concentration of ancient temples, including 東大寺(とうだいじ) (with its colossal Great Buddha) and 興福寺(こうふくじ), the imperial-era 平城宮跡(へいじょうきゅうせき) (Heijo Palace ruins), and 奈良公園(ならこうえん) (Nara Park) where free-roaming deer are tame enough to feed by hand. The prefecture is landlocked — one of only eight prefectures in Japan with no coastline.

Nara is an older capital than Kyoto, and is the place where Japan's first full ritsuryo-state capital was established.

奈良(なら) refers to either 奈良県(ならけん) (Nara Prefecture) or 奈良市(ならし) (Nara City) — context usually makes the scope clear. Nara has outsized historical and cultural importance because it was Japan's first permanent imperial capital from 710 to 794 (奈良時代(ならじだい), Nara Period).

GEOGRAPHIC FACTS:

  • Region: 近畿(きんき) (Kansai)
  • Capital city: 奈良市(ならし) (same name as the prefecture)
  • Landlocked — one of only eight prefectures in Japan with no coast
  • Borders: 京都(きょうと), 大阪(おおさか), 三重(みえ), 和歌山(わかやま)
  • Mountainous in the south (the 紀伊(きい) mountain range)

HISTORICAL IMPORTANCE:

  • Site of 平城京(へいじょうきょう), Japan's first permanent capital (710-794)
  • The 奈良時代(ならじだい) (Nara Period) is one of the major eras of Japanese history
  • A formative period for Buddhism, court bureaucracy, and the writing of 古事記(こじき) and 万葉集(まんようしゅう)
  • The capital was later moved to 平安京(へいあんきょう) (Kyoto) in 794, beginning the 平安時代(へいあんじだい)

FAMOUS SIGHTS / FEATURES:

  • 東大寺(とうだいじ): Todai-ji — temple with the bronze 大仏(だいぶつ) (Great Buddha), one of the world's largest bronze Buddhas
  • 興福寺(こうふくじ): Kofuku-ji — historic temple with the famous 阿修羅(あしゅら) statue
  • 奈良公園(ならこうえん): Nara Park — large park with the free-roaming wild deer (鹿(しか)) considered sacred
  • 春日大社(かすがたいしゃ): Kasuga Taisha shrine, with thousands of stone and bronze lanterns
  • 法隆寺(ほうりゅうじ): Horyu-ji — one of the world's oldest wooden buildings, World Heritage
  • 平城宮跡(へいじょうきゅうせき): ruins of the Heijo Palace (the imperial palace site)
  • 薬師寺(やくしじ), 唐招提寺(とうしょうだいじ): other major historic temples

FAMOUS FOR:

  • The deer (奈良(なら)の鹿(しか)) of Nara Park
  • Ancient temples and World Heritage sites
  • The Nara Great Buddha (奈良(なら)の大仏(だいぶつ))
  • 柿(かき)の葉(は)寿司(ずし): persimmon-leaf-wrapped sushi (a regional specialty)
  • 三輪(みわ)そうめん: thin somen noodles from the Miwa area
  • 奈良漬(ならづけ): pickles aged in sake lees

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 京都(きょうと): Kyoto — the next capital after Nara
  • 大阪(おおさか): Osaka — neighboring prefecture to the west
  • 兵庫(ひょうご): Hyogo — also in Kansai
  • 近畿(きんき) / 関西(かんさい): Kinki / Kansai region
  • 平城京(へいじょうきょう): the original Nara-period capital city plan
  • 奈良時代(ならじだい): the Nara Period (historical era)
  • 鹿(しか): deer (the iconic animal of Nara Park)

USAGE NOTES:

  • Distinct from the homophone なら (the conditional particle / hypothetical "if it's X then") — disambiguated easily by context, and the place name uses kanji 奈良(なら)
  • City and prefecture share the name; if precision is needed, 奈良市(ならし) (city) or 奈良県(ならけん) (prefecture) is used
  • The kanji 奈良(なら) have no straightforward meaning in modern Japanese; they were chosen for their sound, with 奈(な) ("how, what") and 良(ら) ("good") as historical phonetic borrowings
