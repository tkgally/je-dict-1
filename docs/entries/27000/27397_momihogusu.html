1. to knead (a stiff muscle, knot, or piece of dough) so that it loosens or softens; to massage out tension
To work something hard or tight with the hands so that it loosens or softens — most commonly stiff shoulders, knotted muscles, or tense areas of the body. The compound combines 揉(も)む ("to knead, rub, massage") with the suffix {ほぐす} ("to undo, loosen, unravel"), so the overall meaning is "to knead-and-loosen." Applied to materials, it can also describe loosening up a hard piece of dough or other substance by kneading.
肩(かた)を揉(も)みほぐす。
To massage out one's stiff shoulders.
凝(こ)り固(かた)まった筋肉(きんにく)をゆっくり揉(も)みほぐした。
I slowly kneaded out my stiffened muscles.
長時間(ちょうじかん)座(すわ)り続(つづ)けたあとは、脚(あし)や腰(こし)を丁寧(ていねい)に揉(も)みほぐすとよい。
After sitting for long periods, it's good to thoroughly knead out the muscles in your legs and back.
2. to put (someone) at ease; to relieve nervousness or rigid feelings (figurative)
Used figuratively for emotional or social tension: easing someone's nervousness, breaking a stiff atmosphere, or putting a tense person at ease. The image is of metaphorically kneading away mental knots. Common collocations include 緊張(きんちょう)を揉(も)みほぐす ("to ease tension") and 気持(きも)ちを揉(も)みほぐす ("to put one's feelings at ease").
緊張(きんちょう)を揉(も)みほぐす。
To ease (someone's) tension.
先輩(せんぱい)の冗談(じょうだん)が場(ば)の雰囲気(ふんいき)を揉(も)みほぐした。
The senior colleague's joke loosened up the atmosphere of the room.
監督(かんとく)は試合(しあい)前(まえ)に選手(せんしゅ)たちと気軽(きがる)に話(はな)し、緊張(きんちょう)した気持(きも)ちを揉(も)みほぐした。
Before the game, the coach chatted casually with the players, easing their nervous feelings.

揉(も)みほぐす is a transitive godan verb meaning "to knead and loosen." Used both literally for stiff muscles and figuratively for emotional or social tension.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Transitive. Takes を for the object that is being kneaded/loosened: 肩(かた)を}{揉(も)みほぐす ("to massage out {one's shoulders}").
  • For massage given to another person, often combined with あげる/もらう/くれる: 肩(かた)を揉(も)みほぐしてもらう ("to have someone massage out one's shoulders").

CONJUGATION:

  • godan -す verb
  • ます-form: 揉(も)みほぐします
  • past: 揉(も)みほぐした
  • te-form: 揉(も)みほぐして
  • negative: 揉(も)みほぐさない

FORMATION:

  • 揉(も)む ("to knead, rub, massage") + ほぐす} ("to undo, loosen, unravel") → {揉(も)みほぐす
  • ほぐす} is itself a productive verb meaning "to loosen, untangle, break apart" (e.g., {肉(にく)をほぐす "to shred meat," 糸(いと)をほぐす "to untangle thread").

LITERAL USES:

  • Massaging stiff shoulders, neck, back, legs, or any muscle area
  • Kneading dough or other firm material to soften it
  • Working a knot out of a muscle or tissue

FIGURATIVE USES (less frequent than literal):

  • Easing nervousness, anxiety, or stiffness in an atmosphere
  • Often paired with 緊張(きんちょう) ("tension"), 気持(きも)ち ("feelings"), 雰囲気(ふんいき) ("atmosphere")

COMMON COLLOCATIONS (を + 揉(も)みほぐす):

  • 肩(かた)を揉(も)みほぐす: to massage out the shoulders
  • 筋肉(きんにく)を揉(も)みほぐす: to massage out the muscles
  • 凝(こ)りを揉(も)みほぐす: to massage out a stiff spot / knot
  • 腰(こし)を揉(も)みほぐす: to massage out one's lower back
  • 緊張(きんちょう)を揉(も)みほぐす: to ease tension (figurative)
  • 気持(きも)ちを揉(も)みほぐす: to put feelings at ease (figurative)
  • 雰囲気(ふんいき)を揉(も)みほぐす: to loosen up the atmosphere (figurative)

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 揉(も)む: to knead, rub, massage — the base verb; covers the kneading action without specifically implying that the target loosens as a result
  • ほぐす: to loosen, untangle, break apart — the suffix portion as an independent verb; broader than 揉(も)みほぐす and not limited to kneading
  • マッサージする: to massage (loanword verb) — more general; doesn't imply the loosening result
  • 指圧(しあつ)する: to apply shiatsu (finger-pressure massage)
  • 解(ほど)く: to untie, undo — closely related to ほぐす but used for things tied up (knots, ribbons)

USAGE NOTES:

  • 揉(も)みほぐす is a vivid, concrete verb; in casual conversation about massage, speakers often use it for the sensation of "working something out" rather than just generic touching or rubbing.
  • The figurative sense is less common than the literal one and has a slightly literary feel; everyday speakers might prefer 緊張(きんちょう)を和(やわ)らげる ("to ease tension") for the same idea.
