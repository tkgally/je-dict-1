時報(じほう)

じほう
noun
time signal; hourly chime; time announcement
1. time signal; hourly chime; time announcement
A signal — typically a series of beeps, a chime, or a recorded voice — that announces the exact time, especially on the hour. Used by radio and TV broadcasts, telephone services, and clock towers.
時計(とけい)が時報(じほう)を鳴(な)らした。
The clock chimed the time signal.
ラジオの時報(じほう)に合(あ)わせて時計(とけい)を調(ちょう)整(せい)した。
I set my watch by the radio time signal.
毎日(まいにち)正午(しょうご)になると、町(まち)の放送(ほうそう)から時報(じほう)が流(なが)れる。
Every day at noon, a time signal plays from the town's loudspeakers.
昔(むかし)は電話(でんわ)で「117」にかけると、時報(じほう)を聞(き)くことができた。
In the past, you could hear the time signal by calling 117 on the phone.

時報(じほう) literally combines 時(じ) (time) and 報(ほう) (report, signal). It refers to any signal that announces the exact time — whether a chime, beep, or recorded announcement.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 時報(じほう)が鳴(な)る: a time signal sounds
  • 時報(じほう)を流(なが)す: to broadcast a time signal
  • 時報(じほう)に合(あ)わせる: to set (a clock) by the time signal
  • ラジオの時報(じほう): the radio time signal
  • 正午(しょうご)の時報(じほう): the noon time signal

CULTURAL NOTE:

  • In Japan, the noon 時報(じほう) broadcast through municipal loudspeakers (防災(ぼうさい)無線(むせん)) is a daily ritual in many cities and towns — a melody such as 夕焼(ゆうや)け小(こ)焼(や)け or a chime indicates noon and 5 PM.
  • The classic NHK radio 時報(じほう) consists of three short "pi" beeps followed by a longer "pō" tone, marking the exact start of the next hour.
  • The phone-based time service at 117, run by NTT, was widely used to set clocks before networked devices became common.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 時(じ): time, hour
  • ドアチャイム: door chime
  • 鐘(かね): bell
  • アラーム: alarm
