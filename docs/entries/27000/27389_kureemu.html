クレーム

くれーむ
noun
(customer) complaint; objection; demand for redress (loanword from English "claim," but with the meaning "complaint" in Japanese)
1. complaint (from a customer or other party); formal objection; demand for redress or correction
A loanword from English "claim," but in Japanese it has shifted to mean a customer complaint or formal objection — typically a strongly-worded grievance directed at a company, business, or service provider. The Japanese sense focuses on the act of complaining and demanding redress, not on "claiming" something positive (which would be 主張(しゅちょう) or 請求(せいきゅう)). It is one of the most common loanwords in Japanese business and service-industry vocabulary.
クレームをつける。
To lodge a complaint.
お客様(きゃくさま)からクレームが入(はい)った。
We received a complaint from a customer.
クレーム対応(たいおう)は企業(きぎょう)にとって重要(じゅうよう)な業務(ぎょうむ)の一(ひと)つだ。
Handling complaints is one of the important duties for a company.
店長(てんちょう)は怒(おこ)っているお客様(きゃくさま)のクレームに真摯(しんし)に耳(みみ)を傾(かたむ)け、丁寧(ていねい)に謝罪(しゃざい)した。
The store manager listened earnestly to the angry customer's complaint and offered a polite apology.

クレーム is one of the most common business and service-industry loanwords in Japanese, with a meaning narrower than the English source word.

MEANING SHIFT FROM ENGLISH:

  • English "claim" covers "to assert," "to demand a right," and "a statement" generally.
  • Japanese クレーム has narrowed almost entirely to "customer complaint" or "formal grievance."
  • For the English "claim" meanings of "assertion" or "right," Japanese uses 主張(しゅちょう) (assertion), 請求(せいきゅう) (demand for payment), or 申(もう)し立(た)て (formal claim/statement).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • クレームをつける: to make a complaint (the standard verb collocation)
  • クレームを入(い)れる: to lodge a complaint
  • クレームが入(はい)る: a complaint comes in (often used by staff)
  • クレーム対応(たいおう): complaint handling
  • クレーム処理(しょり): processing of complaints
  • クレーム客(きゃく): complaining customer
  • クレームに対応(たいおう)する: to deal with a complaint
  • 悪質(あくしつ)なクレーム: malicious/unreasonable complaint
  • クレーマー: someone who frequently complains, often unreasonably (loanword "claimer")

REGISTER AND TONE:

  • Neutral business vocabulary; standard in customer service (接客(せっきゃく)), retail, and corporate communications.
  • Has a slightly negative connotation — receiving a クレーム is something to handle carefully.
  • A クレーマー ("chronic complainer") is more pointedly negative.

SIMILAR / RELATED TERMS:

  • 苦情(くじょう): complaint, grievance — the native-Japanese equivalent, slightly more formal; often used interchangeably with クレーム in business contexts
  • 文句(もんく): complaint, gripe — more casual; complaining about anything from work to weather
  • 不満(ふまん): dissatisfaction — an inner state rather than a stated complaint
  • 主張(しゅちょう): assertion, claim (in the English sense of "to claim that…")
  • 請求(せいきゅう): a (financial) demand, claim, billing — for the "claim" sense involving payment or compensation
  • 申(もう)し立(た)て: formal statement or claim (legal context)
  • クレーマー: a (chronic) complainer — derived from クレーム by adding -er suffix in Japanese fashion

USAGE NOTES:

  • The verb form クレームする exists but is much less common than クレームをつける.
  • In writing, 苦情(くじょう) is often preferred for a slightly more formal feel; クレーム is more natural in spoken business Japanese and signage like 「クレーム受付(うけつけ)」 ("complaints accepted here").
  • Pronunciation: クレーム (three morae: ku-reee-mu, with a long vowel on レー).
