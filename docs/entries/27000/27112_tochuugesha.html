1. stopping off partway; getting off at an intermediate station (on a longer journey)
Commonly used in the context of train travel, where a passenger breaks the journey at an intermediate station before continuing to the final destination.
途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)はできますか。
Can I make a stopover?
この切符(きっぷ)では途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)できません。
You cannot make a stopover with this ticket.
京都(きょうと)で途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)して観光(かんこう)する予定(よてい)です。
I plan to stop off in Kyoto for some sightseeing.
長距離(ちょうきょり)きっぷなら途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)が可能(かのう)な場合(ばあい)がある。
With a long-distance ticket, stopovers may be possible.

A railway term meaning to get off the train at an intermediate station during a longer journey, typically with the intention of continuing later. On JR lines, stopovers are generally allowed on tickets for journeys over 100 km, but local and commuter tickets do not permit this.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)する: to make a stopover
  • 途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)が可能(かのう): stopovers possible
  • 途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)不可(ふか): no stopovers allowed
  • 途中下車(とちゅうげしゃ)前途(ぜんと)無効(むこう): ticket void beyond stopover point

USAGE:
Often seen on signs and announcements at train stations. The figurative sense of stopping midway through something is occasionally used in casual conversation.
