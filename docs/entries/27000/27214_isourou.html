1. freeloader; sponger; dependent lodger; person living off someone else
A person who stays at someone else's home without paying rent or contributing to expenses. Carries a mildly negative nuance of imposing on another's hospitality.
友人(ゆうじん)の家(いえ)に居候(いそうろう)している。
I'm freeloading at a friend's house.
居候(いそうろう)の身(み)で文句(もんく)は言(い)えない。
As a freeloader, I can't really complain.
弟(おとうと)が仕事(しごと)を辞(や)めてから半年(はんとし)も居候(いそうろう)を続(つづ)けているので、困(こま)っている。
My younger brother has been freeloading for half a year since quitting his job, and it's become a problem.

Can function as both a noun and a suru-verb (居候(いそうろう)する = to freeload). The word has a self-deprecating quality when used about oneself, and a mildly critical tone when used about others. Historically, 居候(いそうろう) were common in the Edo period, when impoverished samurai or students would lodge with relatives or patrons.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 居候(いそうろう)する: to freeload; to sponge off someone
  • 居候(いそうろう)の身(み): the status of being a freeloader
  • 居候(いそうろう)生活(せいかつ): life as a freeloader
  • 居候(いそうろう)を続(つづ)ける: to keep freeloading

SET EXPRESSION:

  • 居候(いそうろう)三杯目(さんばいめ)にはそっと出(だ)し: a freeloader should quietly present their bowl for a third serving — a humorous proverb about knowing one's place as a guest

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 厄介者(やっかいもの): burden; nuisance — someone who causes trouble; more negative than 居候(いそうろう)
  • 間借(まが)り: renting a room — a paying arrangement, unlike freeloading
  • 同居(どうきょ): cohabitation; living together — neutral term without the freeloading nuance
