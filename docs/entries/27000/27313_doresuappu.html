1. dressing up; putting on more formal or fancy clothes
Wearing more formal, glamorous, or special-occasion clothing than usual. Can be used as a noun or with する as a verb.
パーティーにドレスアップして出(で)かけた。
I dressed up and went out to the party.
今日(きょう)は特別(とくべつ)な日(ひ)だから、ドレスアップしよう。
Today is a special day, so let's dress up.
結婚式(けっこんしき)に出席(しゅっせき)するために、ドレスアップして会場(かいじょう)に向(む)かった。
I dressed up to attend the wedding and headed to the venue.
普段(ふだん)はカジュアルな服装(ふくそう)だが、大事(だいじ)な食事会(しょくじかい)にはきちんとドレスアップして臨(のぞ)みたい。
I usually dress casually, but for an important dinner I want to dress up properly.

ドレスアップ is a loanword from English "dress up," used both as a noun and as a する verb in Japanese. It refers specifically to dressing in more formal or glamorous clothing — not to costumes (for which 仮装(かそう) or コスプレ are used).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ドレスアップする: to dress up (suru verb)
  • ドレスアップして出(で)かける: to go out dressed up
  • 少(すこ)しドレスアップする: to dress up a little
  • ドレスアップした姿(すがた): appearance / look when dressed up
  • ドレスアップする機会(きかい): an occasion to dress up

SCOPE NOTES:

  • ドレスアップ in Japanese refers to elegant or formal attire — evening dresses, suits, etc. It does NOT cover the English sense of "dressing up" as in wearing a costume; for that, use 仮装(かそう) or コスプレ.
  • Used about both men and women, but it's more often used describing women's outfits.

RELATED TERMS:

  • 正装(せいそう): formal attire (more strictly defined)
  • 着飾(きかざ)る: to dress up, to put on finery (native verb, slightly more literary)
  • カジュアル: casual
  • ドレスダウン: dressing down (the opposite — using a slightly less formal outfit than the occasion would suggest)

VERB USAGE:
As a する verb: ドレスアップする / ドレスアップして / ドレスアップした.
The transitivity is implicitly intransitive (the subject is the person dressing up): 彼女(かのじょ)はドレスアップした (She dressed up).
