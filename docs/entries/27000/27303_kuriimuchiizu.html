クリームチーズ

くりーむちーず
noun
cream cheese
1. cream cheese
A soft, mild, spreadable fresh cheese made from cream and milk. Used in Japan most often for cheesecake, bagels, and as a spread on bread or crackers.
クリームチーズをパンに塗(ぬ)る。
I spread cream cheese on bread.
クリームチーズを使(つか)ってチーズケーキを作(つく)った。
I made cheesecake using cream cheese.
ベーグルにスモークサーモンとクリームチーズをのせて食(た)べるのが好(す)きだ。
I like eating bagels topped with smoked salmon and cream cheese.
常温(じょうおん)で少(すこ)し柔(やわ)らかくしたクリームチーズに、蜂蜜(はちみつ)と胡椒(こしょう)を混(ま)ぜると、ワインによく合(あ)う。
Cream cheese softened a bit at room temperature, mixed with honey and pepper, goes very well with wine.

クリームチーズ is a loanword referring to cream cheese, the soft, mild, spreadable fresh cheese best known internationally as the basis for American-style cheesecake.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • クリームチーズを塗(ぬ)る: to spread cream cheese (on bread, etc.)
  • クリームチーズを使(つか)う: to use cream cheese (in cooking)
  • クリームチーズを混(ま)ぜる: to mix cream cheese (with other ingredients)
  • クリームチーズの入(はい)ったケーキ: cake containing cream cheese

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ナチュラルチーズ: natural cheese (the broader category cream cheese belongs to)
  • カマンベール: Camembert (a different type of soft cheese)
  • マスカルポーネ: mascarpone (Italian fresh cheese, often used similarly in desserts)
  • バター: butter — sometimes used as a spread substitute, but very different in flavor and culinary role

USAGE NOTES:

  • The KIRI brand and Philadelphia brand are the most recognized cream cheeses in Japan, both available in supermarkets in small individual packs.
  • Beyond Western recipes, クリームチーズ is increasingly used in Japan in fusion dishes, such as inside onigiri or with smoked fish.
