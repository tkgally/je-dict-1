腰(こし)がある

こしがある
expression
chewy; firm; al dente
1. chewy; firm; having a good bite; al dente
Describes noodles, mochi, or other starchy foods that have a pleasantly firm, springy texture — not soft or mushy. A desirable quality in Japanese noodle culture.
このうどんは腰(こし)がある。
These udon noodles are nice and chewy.
腰(こし)のある麺(めん)が好(す)きです。
I like firm, chewy noodles.
讃岐(さぬき)うどんは腰(こし)が強(つよ)くて、歯(は)ごたえがいい。
Sanuki udon has a strong chewiness and great bite.

USAGE:
腰(こし) here refers to the "body" or "backbone" of the noodle — its resilience and springiness. The opposite is 腰(こし)がない (soft, lacking firmness). Variations include 腰(こし)が強(つよ)い (very firm) and 腰(こし)のある麺(めん) (firm noodles, using the attributive form).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 腰(こし)のある麺(めん) (firm noodles)
  • 腰(こし)が強(つよ)い (very firm/chewy)
  • 腰(こし)がない (soft, lacking firmness — negative)

CULTURAL NOTE:
Firmness (腰(こし)) is considered a key quality indicator for udon and soba. 讃岐(さぬき)うどん from Kagawa Prefecture is famous for its strong 腰(こし). The concept is similar to Italian "al dente" for pasta.
