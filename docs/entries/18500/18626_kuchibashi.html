くちばし

くちばし
noun
beak; bill (of a bird)
1. beak; bill
The hard, pointed mouth part of a bird. Can also be written as 嘴(くちばし) in kanji, though the kana form is more common.
鳥(とり)のくちばしが黄色(きいろ)い。
The bird's beak is yellow.
ペリカンは大(おお)きなくちばしを持(も)っている。
Pelicans have large beaks.
親鳥(おやどり)がくちばしでひなに餌(えさ)を運(はこ)んでいた。
The parent bird was carrying food to the chick in its beak.

くちばし comes from 口(くち) (mouth) + 端(はし) (edge/tip). Usually written in kana; the kanji 嘴(くちばし) is rarely used.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • くちばしが長(なが)い: has a long beak
  • くちばしで突(つ)く: to peck with its beak
  • くちばしで餌(えさ)を運(はこ)ぶ: to carry food in its beak

IDIOM:

くちばしを挟(はさ)む means to butt into someone else's conversation or business. Equivalent to the English "to stick one's nose in."

  • くちばしを挟(はさ)むな: Don't butt in!

NOTE:
The shape and size of a bird's beak is a key topic in nature documentaries and science education in Japan, so this word appears often in those contexts.
