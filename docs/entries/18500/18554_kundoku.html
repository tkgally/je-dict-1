1. kun reading; reading a kanji using its native Japanese meaning; reading classical Chinese texts in Japanese word order
Reading kanji with the native Japanese word that matches the character's meaning, as opposed to the on'yomi (Sino-Japanese reading). Also refers to the practice of reading classical Chinese texts with Japanese word order.
「山(やま)」の訓読(くんよ)みは「やま」だ。
The kun reading of 山 is 'yama.'
この漢字(かんじ)は訓読(くんよ)みと音読(おんよ)みで意味(いみ)が変(か)わる。
This kanji changes meaning depending on the kun or on reading.
漢文(かんぶん)を訓読(くんどく)する方法(ほうほう)は高校(こうこう)の古典(こてん)で習(なら)う。
The method of reading classical Chinese in Japanese word order is taught in high school classics class.

USAGE:
In everyday kanji study, 訓読(くんよ)み (kun'yomi) refers to the native Japanese reading of a kanji character, while 音読(おんよ)み (on'yomi) is the Sino-Japanese reading. In classical studies, 訓読(くんどく) also refers to the technique of rearranging classical Chinese (漢文(かんぶん)) into Japanese word order for reading.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 訓読(くんよ)みする (to read using kun reading)
  • 訓読(くんよ)み (kun reading — the more common everyday form)
  • 漢文(かんぶん)の訓読(くんどく) (reading classical Chinese in Japanese)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 音読(おんよ)み: on'yomi — the Sino-Japanese reading of a kanji character
  • 訓(くん): kun — shortened form often used in dictionaries for the native reading
