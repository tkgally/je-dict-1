地酒(じざけ)

じざけ
noun
local sake; locally brewed sake
1. local sake; locally brewed sake
Sake produced by a local brewery, typically associated with a particular region and reflecting its water, rice, and brewing traditions. Valued for regional character and artisanal quality.
この地酒(じざけ)はおいしい。
This local sake is delicious.
旅先(たびさき)で地酒(じざけ)を飲(の)むのが楽(たの)しみだ。
I look forward to drinking local sake when I travel.
この居酒屋(いざかや)は全国(ぜんこく)の地酒(じざけ)を豊富(ほうふ)に取(と)り揃(そろ)えている。
This izakaya has an extensive selection of local sake from across the country.

The prefix 地(じ)～ indicates something local or regional: 地元(じもと) (local area), 地ビール(じびーる) (craft/local beer). Japanese sake culture places great value on regional varieties, and 地酒(じざけ) is a common draw for tourists visiting rural areas. Common collocations: 地酒(じざけ)を味(あじ)わう (to savor local sake), 地酒(じざけ)の飲(の)み比(くら)べ (local sake tasting comparison). Related: 日本酒(にほんしゅ) (Japanese sake — general term), 銘酒(めいしゅ) (fine/renowned sake).
