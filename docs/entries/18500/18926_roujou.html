籠城(ろうじょう)

ろうじょう
noun, verb-suru
holing up; besieging oneself in a castle; staying indoors
1. besieging oneself in a castle; holding out in a fortification
The historical military strategy of shutting oneself inside a castle or fortress to defend against attackers. A key concept in Japanese feudal warfare.
城(しろ)に籠城(ろうじょう)する。
To hole up in the castle.
兵糧(ひょうろう)攻(ぜ)めで籠城(ろうじょう)は長(なが)く続(つづ)かなかった。
The siege did not last long due to a food blockade.
籠城戦(ろうじょうせん)で半年間(はんとしかん)持(も)ちこたえた。
They held out for half a year in a siege.
2. holing up; shutting oneself in; staying indoors for an extended period
Figuratively, staying inside a building or room for a long time without going out. Used for studying intensively, working on a project, or refusing to leave.
試験前(しけんまえ)は家(いえ)に籠城(ろうじょう)する。
Before exams, I hole up at home.
締(し)め切(き)りが近(ちか)いので週末(しゅうまつ)は籠城(ろうじょう)だ。
The deadline is approaching, so I'm holing up this weekend.
図書館(としょかん)に籠城(ろうじょう)して論文(ろんぶん)を書(か)き上(あ)げた。
I holed up in the library and finished writing my paper.

Composed of 籠(ろう) (to be confined, basket) + 城(じょう) (castle). Takes する: 籠城(ろうじょう)する. Historical compounds: 籠城戦(ろうじょうせん) (siege warfare), 籠城(ろうじょう)作戦(さくせん) (siege strategy). The figurative use (sense 2) is very common in modern Japanese, often humorous or self-deprecating. Also written as 篭城(ろうじょう) with the simplified kanji.
