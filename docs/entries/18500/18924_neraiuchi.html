1. sniping; picking off; sharpshooting
Carefully aiming at and shooting a specific target. The literal sense of taking a well-aimed shot.
敵(てき)を狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにする。
To pick off the enemy with a well-aimed shot.
遠(とお)くの的(まと)を狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちした。
I took a aimed shot at the distant target.
犯人(はんにん)はカメラの死角(しかく)を狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにした。
The perpetrator targeted the camera's blind spots.
2. targeting; singling out; zeroing in on
Figuratively, deliberately targeting a specific person, company, or group for criticism, regulation, taxation, or attack. Very common in news and business contexts.
大企業(だいきぎょう)が狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにされた。
Large corporations were singled out.
特定(とくてい)の業界(ぎょうかい)を狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにする規制(きせい)。
Regulations that single out a specific industry.
新(あたら)しい税制(ぜいせい)は高所得者(こうしょとくしゃ)を狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにしていると批判(ひはん)されている。
The new tax system is being criticized for targeting high-income earners.

Composed of 狙(ねら)い (aiming) + 撃(う)ち (shooting). Takes する or the pattern 〜にする: 狙(ねら)い撃(う)ちにする (to target, to single out). The figurative sense (sense 2) is very common in news: 狙(ねら)い撃(う)ち課税(かぜい) (targeted taxation), 狙(ねら)い撃(う)ち規制(きせい) (targeted regulation). Often carries a negative connotation of unfair targeting when used figuratively.
