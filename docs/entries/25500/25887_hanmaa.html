ハンマー

はんまー
noun
hammer
1. hammer (tool)
A hand tool with a heavy head on a handle, used for driving nails or breaking things. The standard loanword for hammer in everyday Japanese.
ハンマーで釘(くぎ)を打(う)つ。
To drive a nail with a hammer.
ハンマーを振(ふ)り下(お)ろして壁(かべ)を壊(こわ)した。
I swung the hammer down and broke the wall.
工具箱(こうぐばこ)からハンマーとドライバーを取(と)り出(だ)した。
I took a hammer and screwdriver out of the toolbox.
2. hammer (athletics)
The heavy metal ball attached to a wire and handle used in the hammer throw event in track and field.
ハンマー投(な)げの選手(せんしゅ)は体格(たいかく)がいい。
Hammer throw athletes have good builds.
彼(かれ)はハンマー投(な)げで日本記録(にほんきろく)を更新(こうしん)した。
He set a new Japanese record in the hammer throw.
ハンマーを回転(かいてん)させながら遠(とお)くへ投(な)げる技術(ぎじゅつ)は難(むずか)しい。
The technique of spinning and throwing the hammer far is difficult.

From English "hammer." The standard term for a hammer in modern Japanese, used more commonly than the native word 金槌(かなづち) in many contexts, especially in DIY, construction, and sports.

In athletics, ハンマー投(な)げ (hammer throw) is one of the field events in track and field. Japan has produced notable hammer throw athletes.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • ハンマーで打(う)つ: to hit with a hammer
  • ハンマーを振(ふ)る: to swing a hammer
  • ハンマー投(な)げ: hammer throw (athletics)
  • ハンマーで壊(こわ)す: to break with a hammer
  • ハンマーで叩(たた)く: to strike with a hammer

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 金槌(かなづち): hammer — the native Japanese word, refers specifically to a metalworking or carpentry hammer
  • 槌(つち): mallet; hammer — broader native term, includes wooden mallets
  • トンカチ: hammer — colloquial/onomatopoeic term (from the sound of hammering)
