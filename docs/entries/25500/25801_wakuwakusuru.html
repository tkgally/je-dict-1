1. to be excited; to feel thrilled or exhilarated
Describes a feeling of excited anticipation or joyful nervousness, often before something pleasant or new. The reduplicative ワクワク mimics the sensation of a heart beating fast with excitement.
遠足(えんそく)の前日(ぜんじつ)、ワクワクして眠(ねむ)れなかった。
The night before the field trip, I was so excited I couldn't sleep.
新(あたら)しいゲームを買(か)ったので、ワクワクしている。
I'm excited because I bought a new game.
初(はじ)めての海外旅行(かいがいりょこう)にワクワクする気持(きも)ちを抑(おさ)えられなかった。
I couldn't contain my excitement about my first trip abroad.
子供(こども)たちはプレゼントを開(あ)けるのをワクワクしながら待(ま)っていた。
The children waited excitedly to open their presents.

An onomatopoeic expression describing excited anticipation or joyful nervousness. The base form ワクワク is a gitaigo (mimetic word for emotion) that evokes the physical sensation of one's heart racing with excitement.

USAGE:

  • ワクワクする — to feel excited (verb)
  • ワクワクした — was exciting / felt excited
  • ワクワクしている — is excited (ongoing state)
  • ワクワクするような — exciting, thrilling (modifying a noun)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 胸(むね)がワクワクする — one's heart is racing with excitement
  • ワクワク感(かん) — sense of excitement
  • ワクワクしながら待(ま)つ — to wait excitedly
  • ワクワクドキドキする — to feel excited and nervous at the same time

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • ドキドキする — heart pounding, but more from anxiety or nervousness; ワクワク is predominantly positive
  • 楽(たの)しみにする — to look forward to; more deliberate and cognitive, while ワクワク describes the involuntary physical feeling
  • 興奮(こうふん)する — to get excited; stronger and can include agitation, while ワクワク is almost always positive and gentle

REGISTER:
Used in everyday conversation by all ages. The katakana form ワクワク is standard. Writing it in hiragana (わくわく) is also common and slightly softer in tone.
