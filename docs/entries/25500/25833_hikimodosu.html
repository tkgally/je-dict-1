1. to pull back physically; to drag back to the original position
To physically pull someone or something back to where they were.
子(こ)どもの手(て)を引(ひ)き戻(もど)した。
I pulled the child back by the hand.
道(みち)に飛(と)び出(だ)そうとした犬(いぬ)をリードで引(ひ)き戻(もど)す。
To pull back a dog that was about to run into the road with its leash.
危険(きけん)な場所(ばしょ)に近(ちか)づいた子(こ)どもを引(ひ)き戻(もど)して、二度(にど)と行(い)かないように言(い)い聞(き)かせた。
I pulled the child back from the dangerous area and told them never to go there again.
2. to bring back; to restore to a previous state
To return someone or something to a former condition, situation, or mindset. Often used figuratively.
彼女(かのじょ)の声(こえ)が私(わたし)を現実(げんじつ)に引(ひ)き戻(もど)した。
Her voice brought me back to reality.
辞(や)めようとした社員(しゃいん)を説得(せっとく)して引(ひ)き戻(もど)した。
I persuaded the employee who was about to quit and brought them back.
懐(なつ)かしい写真(しゃしん)が昔(むかし)の記憶(きおく)を引(ひ)き戻(もど)してくれた。
The nostalgic photos brought back old memories for me.

A compound verb formed from 引(ひ)く (to pull) + 戻(もど)す (to return). The literal meaning of physically pulling something back extends naturally to figurative uses like restoring a state or recalling someone.

TRANSITIVITY:
Transitive. The intransitive counterpart is 引(ひ)き戻(もど)る (to go back, to return).

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 現実(げんじつ)に引(ひ)き戻(もど)す: to bring back to reality
  • 記憶(きおく)を引(ひ)き戻(もど)す: to bring back memories
  • 原点(げんてん)に引(ひ)き戻(もど)す: to bring back to the starting point
  • 手(て)を引(ひ)き戻(もど)す: to pull one's hand back

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 引(ひ)き返(かえ)す: to turn back; to retrace one's steps — focuses on reversing direction, not pulling someone/something
  • 呼(よ)び戻(もど)す: to call back; to summon back — uses calling rather than physical pulling
  • 連(つ)れ戻(もど)す: to bring back (a person) — implies taking someone back, often a runaway or missing person
