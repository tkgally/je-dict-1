1. to dawdle; to dilly-dally; to waste time being slow or hesitant
Describes being annoyingly slow in acting, deciding, or getting ready. Often implies laziness, hesitation, or lack of urgency, and is typically used critically.
ぐずぐずしないで早(はや)くしなさい。
Stop dawdling and hurry up.
ぐずぐずしていたら電車(でんしゃ)に乗(の)り遅(おく)れた。
I was dawdling and missed the train.
ぐずぐずしている暇(ひま)はない。すぐに行動(こうどう)に移(うつ)そう。
There's no time to dilly-dally. Let's take action immediately.

An onomatopoeic (gitaigo) expression where ぐずぐず mimics the sluggish, foot-dragging quality of the behavior. Often used by parents scolding children for being slow, or in situations where urgency is needed.

ぐずぐず can also be used as an adverb without する: ぐずぐず言(い)う (to grumble, to whine).

COMMON PATTERNS:

  • ぐずぐずしないで: stop dawdling; don't dilly-dally
  • ぐずぐずしていると: if you keep dawdling
  • ぐずぐず言(い)う: to grumble; to whine; to complain

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • もたもたする: to be clumsy and slow — emphasizes physical clumsiness or inefficiency
  • のろのろする: to be slow; to move sluggishly — focuses on slow physical movement
  • まごまごする: to be flustered and hesitant — implies confusion about what to do
  • ぐずる: to be fussy; to whine (said of children) — related but focuses on complaining rather than slowness
