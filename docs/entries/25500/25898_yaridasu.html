1. to start doing; to begin; to set about
To begin an action or activity, often with a sense of suddenly or energetically getting started. More casual and vivid than 始(はじ)める.
急(きゅう)にやり出(だ)した。
He suddenly started doing it.
子(こ)どもたちが庭(にわ)でサッカーをやり出(だ)した。
The children started playing soccer in the yard.
彼(かれ)は一度(いちど)やり出(だ)すと止(と)まらない性格(せいかく)だ。
He has the kind of personality where once he starts something, he can't stop.

A compound verb combining やる (to do) + 出(だ)す (auxiliary verb indicating the start of an action). The ～出(だ)す pattern is productive in Japanese, attaching to many verbs to express the sudden or spontaneous beginning of an action.

More colloquial and energetic than 始(はじ)める. Often implies that the action began somewhat abruptly or without much deliberation.

GRAMMAR NOTE:
The ～出(だ)す auxiliary can attach to many verb stems: 走(はし)り出(だ)す (start running), 泣(な)き出(だ)す (burst into tears), 笑(わら)い出(だ)す (start laughing). やり出(だ)す is the most general form since やる covers doing anything.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 急(きゅう)にやり出(だ)す: to suddenly start doing
  • やり出(だ)したら止(と)まらない: once started, can't stop
  • 勝手(かって)にやり出(だ)す: to start doing on one's own

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 始(はじ)める: to begin — neutral and formal, works in all registers
  • 取(と)りかかる: to set about; to get started on — emphasizes tackling a task
  • し出(だ)す: to start doing — similar compound but with する instead of やる, slightly more formal
