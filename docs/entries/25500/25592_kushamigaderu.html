くしゃみが出(で)る

くしゃみがでる
expression
to sneeze
1. to sneeze
To involuntarily expel air from the nose and mouth; the standard way to express sneezing in Japanese.
くしゃみが出(で)た。
I sneezed.
花粉(かふん)の季節(きせつ)はくしゃみが止(と)まらない。
During pollen season, I can't stop sneezing.
急(きゅう)にくしゃみが出(で)そうになって、慌(あわ)てて口(くち)を手(て)で押(お)さえた。
A sneeze suddenly felt like it was coming, and I hurriedly covered my mouth with my hand.

The natural Japanese way to say "to sneeze." Unlike English where "sneeze" is a single verb, Japanese uses the expression くしゃみが出(で)る (literally "a sneeze comes out"), treating the sneeze as something that happens involuntarily.

There is a popular Japanese superstition that if someone sneezes, it means someone is talking about them. One sneeze means someone is praising you, two means someone is criticizing you.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • くしゃみが止(と)まらない: can't stop sneezing
  • くしゃみが出(で)そう: feel like sneezing
  • 大(おお)きなくしゃみ: a big sneeze
  • くしゃみを連発(れんぱつ)する: to sneeze repeatedly

RELATED EXPRESSIONS:

  • 鼻水(はなみず)が出(で)る: to have a runny nose — often occurs together with sneezing
  • 咳(せき)が出(で)る: to cough — follows the same ～が出(で)る pattern for involuntary body responses
  • 花粉症(かふんしょう): hay fever — the most common cause of sneezing in spring in Japan
