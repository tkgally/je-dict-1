1. to yawn
The act of yawning, typically from sleepiness, boredom, or fatigue. The noun あくび (yawn) takes the particle を with する.
授業中(じゅぎょうちゅう)にあくびをしてしまった。
I accidentally yawned during class.
退屈(たいくつ)な映画(えいが)を見(み)ていて、何度(なんど)もあくびをした。
I yawned many times while watching a boring movie.
隣(となり)の人(ひと)があくびをしたら、つられてあくびが出(で)てしまうことがある。
When the person next to you yawns, you sometimes end up yawning too.

The standard way to say "to yawn" in Japanese. The noun あくび can also be written in kanji as 欠伸(あくび), though the hiragana form is far more common in modern usage.

USAGE PATTERNS:

  • あくびをする — to yawn (basic form)
  • あくびが出(で)る — a yawn comes out (involuntary feeling)
  • あくびを噛(か)み殺(ころ)す — to stifle a yawn

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 大(おお)きなあくび — a big yawn
  • あくびが止(と)まらない — can't stop yawning
  • あくびを我慢(がまん)する — to hold back a yawn
  • あくびがうつる — yawning is contagious

CULTURAL NOTE:
Yawning in public is generally considered rude in Japan, especially in formal settings. It is common practice to cover one's mouth with a hand when yawning. In business meetings or school, visible yawning may be taken as a sign of disrespect or lack of interest.
