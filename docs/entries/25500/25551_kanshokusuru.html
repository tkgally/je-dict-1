1. to finish eating everything; to eat it all; to clean one's plate
To eat all of a dish or meal without leaving anything. Often used when the portion was large or challenging.
大盛(おおも)りを完食(かんしょく)した。
I finished the large serving.
子供(こども)たちが給食(きゅうしょく)を完食(かんしょく)して先生(せんせい)が喜(よろこ)んだ。
The teacher was happy that the children ate all of their school lunch.
量(りょう)が多(おお)すぎて完食(かんしょく)できなかった。
There was too much food and I couldn't finish it all.

完食(かんしょく)する is a relatively modern word that has become common through food blogs, restaurant reviews, and social media. It combines 完(かん) (complete) and 食(しょく) (eat). It often carries a sense of accomplishment, especially when the portion was large.

USAGE:

  • ～を完食(かんしょく)する: to eat all of ~
  • 完食(かんしょく)できる/できない: to be able to / unable to finish eating

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 大盛(おおも)りを完食(かんしょく)する: to finish a large portion
  • 完食(かんしょく)必須(ひっす): must eat everything (restaurant rule)
  • 完食(かんしょく){チャレンジ}: eating challenge (to finish a huge dish)

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 食(た)べきる: to eat up completely — native Japanese equivalent, more casual
  • 平(たい)らげる: to eat up; to polish off — implies eating with gusto, slightly informal
  • 残(のこ)さず食(た)べる: to eat without leaving anything — descriptive phrase rather than a single word
