狐(きつね)

きつね
noun
fox
1. fox
A wild canine with a bushy tail, pointed ears, and reddish-brown fur. Foxes have deep significance in Japanese folklore as shapeshifters and messengers of Inari.
森(もり)で狐(きつね)を見(み)かけた。
I spotted a fox in the forest.
稲荷神社(いなりじんじゃ)には狐(きつね)の像(ぞう)がある。
There are fox statues at Inari shrines.
狐(きつね)うどんを注文(ちゅうもん)した。
I ordered kitsune udon.
Udon with sweet fried tofu

狐(きつね) refers to foxes.

Common expressions:

  • 狐(きつね)うどん (udon with sweet fried tofu - tofu is said to be foxes' favorite food)
  • 狐(きつね)の嫁入(よめい)り (sun shower - "fox's wedding")
  • 狐(きつね)につままれる (to be bewildered, as if bewitched by a fox)
  • 狐(きつね)と狸(たぬき)の化(ば)かし合(あ)い (battle of wits; literally foxes and tanuki trying to outwit each other)

Counter: 匹(ひき)

Cultural note:
In Japanese folklore, 狐(きつね) are believed to be messengers of 稲荷(いなり) (the god of rice/prosperity). They can transform into humans and may be benevolent or mischievous. The red gates (鳥居(とりい)) at Inari shrines, like Fushimi Inari in Kyoto, are guarded by fox statues.
