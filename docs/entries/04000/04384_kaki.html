柿(かき)

かき
noun
persimmon
1. persimmon
An orange fruit native to East Asia, eaten fresh when ripe or dried. Persimmons are a symbol of autumn in Japan.
庭(にわ)の柿(かき)が色(いろ)づいてきた。
The persimmons in the garden are starting to ripen.
干(ほ)し柿(がき)は甘(あま)くて美味(おい)しい。
Dried persimmons are sweet and delicious.
渋柿(しぶがき)はそのまま食(た)べられない。
Astringent persimmons cannot be eaten as they are.

柿(かき) refers to persimmons.

Types:

  • 甘柿(あまがき) (sweet persimmon - can be eaten fresh)
  • 渋柿(しぶがき) (astringent persimmon - must be dried or processed)
  • 干(ほ)し柿(がき) (dried persimmon)

Common expressions:

  • 柿(かき)の種(たね) (persimmon seed; also a type of rice cracker)
  • 柿(かき)が赤(あか)くなると医者(いしゃ)が青(あお)くなる (when persimmons turn red, doctors turn pale - persimmons are healthy)

Counter: 個(こ)

Cultural note:
Persimmon trees are common in Japanese gardens and countryside. The orange persimmons hanging from bare branches are an iconic autumn scene. 干(ほ)し柿(がき) (dried persimmons) are made by peeling and hanging persimmons to dry, transforming astringent persimmons into a sweet treat. The famous haiku by Masaoka Shiki mentions persimmons: 「柿(かき)食(く)えば鐘(かね)が鳴(な)るなり法隆寺(ほうりゅうじ)」
