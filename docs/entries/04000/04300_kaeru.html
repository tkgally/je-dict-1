蛙(かえる)

かえる
noun
frog
1. frog
An amphibian that lives both in water and on land, known for its jumping ability and croaking sound.
田(た)んぼで蛙(かえる)が鳴(な)いている。
Frogs are croaking in the rice paddy.
蛙(かえる)が池(いけ)に飛(と)び込(こ)んだ。
A frog jumped into the pond.
This echoes Matsuo Bashō's famous haiku
子供(こども)の頃(ころ)、よく蛙(かえる)を捕(つか)まえて遊(あそ)んだ。
When I was a child, I often played by catching frogs.
夏(なつ)の夜(よる)、田(た)んぼの近(ちか)くを歩(ある)くと蛙(かえる)の合唱(がっしょう)が聞(き)こえる。
On summer nights, you can hear a chorus of frogs when walking near the rice paddies.
蛙(かえる)の合唱(がっしょう) = chorus of frogs
「蛙(かえる)の子(こ)は蛙(かえる)」ということわざは、子(こ)は親(おや)に似(に)るという意味(いみ)で使(つか)われる。
The proverb 'a frog's child is a frog' is used to mean that children take after their parents.

蛙(かえる) refers to frogs in general, sometimes also including toads.

Alternative kanji: 蛙(かわず) (literary/poetic reading)

Common expressions:

  • 蛙(かえる)の子(こ)は蛙(かえる) (like father, like son; literally "a frog's child is a frog")
  • 井(い)の中(なか)の蛙(かわず)大海(たいかい)を知(し)らず (a frog in a well knows nothing of the great ocean - narrow-minded person)
  • 蛙(かえる)の合唱(がっしょう) (chorus of frogs)

Counter: 匹(ひき)

Cultural note:
蛙(かえる) is a homophone of 帰(かえ)る (to return), so frog charms are popular as good luck items for safe returns. Matsuo Bashō's famous haiku about a frog jumping into a pond (古池(ふるいけ)や蛙(かわず)飛(と)び込(こ)む水(みず)の音(おと)) is one of the most well-known poems in Japanese literature.
