怨霊(おんりょう)

おんりょう
noun
vengeful spirit, grudge ghost
1. vengeful spirit, wrathful ghost
The spirit of a deceased person who harbors deep resentment and returns to the world of the living to cause harm, disaster, or misfortune. A fundamental concept in Japanese folk religion and a recurring motif in Japanese literature and horror.
怨霊(おんりょう)に取(と)り憑(つ)かれた。
I was possessed by a vengeful spirit.
天変地異(てんぺんちい)は怨霊(おんりょう)の祟(たた)りだと恐(おそ)れられた。
Natural disasters were feared to be the curse of vengeful spirits.
菅原道真(すがわらのみちざね)は日本(にほん)で最(もっと)も有名(ゆうめい)な怨霊(おんりょう)のひとりで、後(のち)に天神(てんじん)として祀(まつ)られた。
Sugawara no Michizane is one of Japan's most famous vengeful spirits, and was later enshrined as the deity Tenjin.

CULTURAL CONTEXT:
Belief in 怨霊(おんりょう) has been a powerful force in Japanese history and culture. When powerful or unjustly treated people died with grudges, their spirits were believed to cause plagues, natural disasters, and political upheaval. To appease them, they were often enshrined as deities (御霊(ごりょう)信仰(しんこう), goryo shinko). Famous historical 怨霊(おんりょう) include 菅原道真(すがわらのみちざね), 平将門(たいらのまさかど), and 崇徳天皇(すとくてんのう).

Do not confuse with the homophone 音量(おんりょう) (volume, loudness), which uses different kanji.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 怨霊(おんりょう)の祟(たた)り: curse of a vengeful spirit
  • 怨霊(おんりょう)を鎮(しず)める: to pacify a vengeful spirit
  • 怨霊(おんりょう)に取(と)り憑(つ)かれる: to be possessed by a vengeful spirit
