有無(うむ)

うむ
noun
presence or absence, existence
1. presence or absence, whether something exists
Whether something is present or not; the question of existence or non-existence. Used in formal and written contexts to ask about or indicate whether something applies.
経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ)は問(と)いません。
Experience is not required. (Lit: We do not ask about the presence or absence of experience.)
有無(うむ)を言(い)わさず連(つ)れていかれた。
I was taken away without being allowed to say a word.
有無(うむ)を言(い)わさず is a set expression meaning 'without allowing any objection'.
応募(おうぼ)の際(さい)に資格(しかく)の有無(うむ)を確認(かくにん)してください。
Please confirm whether you have the qualifications when applying.

USAGE:
Used in formal and written Japanese, especially on forms and in official contexts. The pattern ～の有無(うむ) asks whether something exists or applies. The set phrase 有無(うむ)を言(い)わさず means 'without allowing objection; giving no choice'.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ): whether one has experience
  • 資格(しかく)の有無(うむ): whether one has qualifications
  • 有無(うむ)を言(い)わさず: without allowing any objection
  • 有無(うむ)を確認(かくにん)する: to check presence or absence
