1. to pull down; to drag down from a high position
To physically pull or drag something or someone down from a higher place.
棚(たな)から箱(はこ)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろした。
I pulled the box down from the shelf.
馬(うま)から引(ひ)き下(お)ろされた。
He was dragged down from the horse.
重(おも)い荷物(にもつ)をトラックから引(ひ)き下(お)ろすのに苦労(くろう)した。
I had trouble pulling the heavy luggage down from the truck.
2. to force someone out of a position; to topple
To remove someone from a position of power or authority, often forcefully or against their will.
社長(しゃちょう)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろす動(うご)きがある。
There is a movement to oust the company president.
国民(こくみん)の怒(いか)りが独裁者(どくさいしゃ)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろした。
The anger of the people toppled the dictator.
チームの監督(かんとく)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろそうとする声(こえ)が高(たか)まっている。
Calls to remove the team's manager are growing louder.
3. to withdraw money from a bank account
To take money out of a savings or bank account. A common everyday usage.
ATMでお金(かね)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろした。
I withdrew money from the ATM.
毎月(まいつき)給料(きゅうりょう)の一部(いちぶ)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろして貯金(ちょきん)している。
Every month I withdraw part of my salary and save it.
旅行(りょこう)の前(まえ)に銀行(ぎんこう)から現金(げんきん)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろしておいた。
I withdrew cash from the bank before the trip.

A compound verb from 引(ひ)く (to pull) + 下(お)ろす (to lower, bring down). Transitive verb (takes を). The physical sense (pulling something down) extends naturally to the figurative sense of removing someone from power, and the financial sense of withdrawing money.

TRANSITIVITY: transitive (paired with no standard intransitive form)

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 棚(たな)から引(ひ)き下(お)ろす: to pull down from a shelf
  • 権力(けんりょく)の座(ざ)から引(ひ)き下(お)ろす: to drag down from a position of power
  • お金(かね)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろす: to withdraw money
  • 預金(よきん)を引(ひ)き下(お)ろす: to withdraw savings

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 引(ひ)き出(だ)す: to pull out, to withdraw -- more general; also used for money
  • 下(お)ろす: to lower, to take down -- simpler form without the pulling nuance
  • 引(ひ)きずり下(お)ろす: to drag down forcefully -- more emphatic and aggressive
