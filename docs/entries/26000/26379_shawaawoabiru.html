シャワーを浴(あ)びる

しゃわーをあびる
expression
to take a shower
1. to take a shower
The standard way to express taking a shower in Japanese. Uses the verb 浴(あ)びる (to bathe in; to be showered with) rather than a direct translation of 'take.'
シャワーを浴(あ)びてくる。
I'm going to take a shower.
朝(あさ)、シャワーを浴(あ)びてから出(で)かけた。
I took a shower in the morning before heading out.
運動(うんどう)のあとはシャワーを浴(あ)びて着替(きが)えたい。
After exercising, I want to take a shower and change clothes.
暑(あつ)い日(ひ)は一日(いちにち)に二回(にかい)シャワーを浴(あ)びることもある。
On hot days, I sometimes take a shower twice a day.

In Japanese, you cannot say シャワーをする or シャワーを取(と)る the way English says 'take a shower.' The correct verb is 浴(あ)びる, which means to be showered with or bathed in something (water, light, criticism, etc.). This is one of the most common collocations beginners need to learn.

In Japan, many people prefer 湯船(ゆぶね)に浸(つ)かる (soaking in a bathtub) over showering alone, especially in the evening. Showering alone is more common in the morning or after exercise.

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 冷(つめ)たいシャワーを浴(あ)びる: to take a cold shower
  • 軽(かる)くシャワーを浴(あ)びる: to take a quick shower
  • シャワーを浴(あ)びてさっぱりする: to feel refreshed after a shower

SIMILAR EXPRESSIONS:

  • 風呂(ふろ)に入(はい)る: to take a bath — the general expression for bathing
  • 湯船(ゆぶね)に浸(つ)かる: to soak in the bathtub — specifically about the soaking part
