ぷくぷく

ぷくぷく
adverb, onomatopoeia
plump; chubby; bubbling
1. plump; chubby; pudgy
Describes a round, soft, well-fed appearance, especially of babies, small children, or animals. Carries a positive, endearing nuance.
ぷくぷくした赤(あか)ちゃんの手(て)がかわいい。
The baby's plump little hands are cute.
うちの猫(ねこ)はぷくぷくに太(ふと)ってしまった。
Our cat has gotten chubby.
ぷくぷくの頬(ほお)を触(さわ)りたくなる。
I can't help wanting to touch those plump cheeks.
2. bubbling; gurgling
The sound or appearance of bubbles rising to the surface of a liquid.
鍋(なべ)の中(なか)でぷくぷくと泡(あわ)が出(で)ている。
Bubbles are coming up in the pot.
水(みず)がぷくぷくと湧(わ)き出(で)ていた。
Water was bubbling up from the ground.
魚(さかな)がぷくぷくと口(くち)から泡(あわ)を出(だ)していた。
The fish was blowing bubbles from its mouth.

A mimetic word with two distinct senses. The "plump" sense is affectionate and positive — it suggests healthy roundness, not obesity. The "bubbling" sense directly imitates the sound and appearance of small bubbles.

GRAMMATICAL FORMS:

  • ぷくぷくした — plump (modifier before nouns)
  • ぷくぷくに太(ふと)る — to get chubby
  • ぷくぷくと泡(あわ)が出(で)る — bubbles come up

SIMILAR ONOMATOPOEIA:

  • ぽっちゃり — plump, slightly chubby (for people; more neutral/adult usage)
  • ぶくぶく — swelling up; bloated (negative nuance, unlike ぷくぷく)
  • ぐつぐつ — boiling vigorously (stronger bubbling than ぷくぷく)
  • ぶくぶく — bubbling heavily (larger bubbles than ぷくぷく)

NUANCE:
The ぷ sound in Japanese often evokes softness and roundness. ぷくぷく is almost always used positively or neutrally. For a negative "getting fat," Japanese speakers would more likely use ぶくぶく instead.
