はかなくなる

はかなくなる
expression
to pass away; to die (euphemism)
1. to pass away; to die (euphemism)
A gentle, literary euphemism for dying. Literally means 'to become fleeting/ephemeral,' evoking the Buddhist sense of life's transience. Used when speaking respectfully or poetically about someone's death.
祖母(そぼ)がはかなくなった。
My grandmother passed away.
若(わか)くしてはかなくなった詩人(しじん)の作品(さくひん)を集(あつ)めている。
I collect the works of poets who passed away young.
長(なが)い闘病(とうびょう)生活(せいかつ)の末(すえ)、彼女(かのじょ)は静(しず)かにはかなくなり、家族(かぞく)に見守(みまも)られながら旅立(たびだ)った。
After a long battle with illness, she quietly passed away, departing while watched over by her family.

From the adjective はかない (fleeting, transient, ephemeral) in its ku-form + なる (to become). The expression encapsulates a deeply Japanese sensibility about mortality — life is fleeting, and death is a natural return to impermanence.

This is one of many Japanese euphemisms for death, reflecting the cultural tendency to speak about death indirectly. It is more literary and poetic than 亡(な)くなる, which is the standard polite word for dying.

REGISTER:
Literary and poetic. Used in written prose, obituaries, speeches, and formal settings. In everyday speech, 亡(な)くなる is far more common.

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 亡(な)くなる: to pass away — the standard polite euphemism for death
  • 死(し)ぬ: to die — direct and blunt; avoided when speaking about people respectfully
  • 逝(い)く: to pass on — literary, gentle euphemism
  • 他界(たかい)する: to depart this world — formal, used in obituaries
  • 永眠(えいみん)する: to enter eternal sleep — very formal, found in written notices
