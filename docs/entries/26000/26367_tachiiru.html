1. to enter; to trespass; to go into a restricted area
To physically enter a place, especially somewhere that is restricted, prohibited, or off-limits. Often carries the nuance that entry is unauthorized or unwelcome.
ここは立(た)ち入(い)れません。
You cannot enter here.
工事(こうじ)現場(げんば)には立(た)ち入(い)らないでください。
Please do not enter the construction site.
許可(きょか)なく敷地(しきち)に立(た)ち入(い)ると、法律(ほうりつ)に違反(いはん)する場合(ばあい)がある。
Entering the premises without permission may be a violation of the law.
2. to get involved in; to pry into; to delve into
To go deeply into someone's personal affairs, a sensitive topic, or the details of a matter. Suggests overstepping boundaries.
他人(たにん)の私生活(しせいかつ)に立(た)ち入(い)るな。
Don't pry into other people's private lives.
もう少(すこ)し立(た)ち入(い)った話(はなし)をしてもいいですか。
May I ask some more in-depth questions?
個人的(こじんてき)なことに立(た)ち入(い)るつもりはないが、事情(じじょう)を聞(き)かせてほしい。
I don't intend to pry into personal matters, but I'd like to hear the circumstances.

A compound verb from 立(た)つ (to stand) and 入(い)る (to enter). The physical sense (sense 1) implies going into a place where one should not be — it is rarely used for ordinary, welcome entry. For neutral entry, 入(い)る alone is natural.

Also written as 立入(たちい)る (without okurigana for 入), especially in signs and legal language.

TRANSITIVITY:

  • Type: intransitive (自動詞(じどうし))
  • Pattern: [place]に立(た)ち入(い)る

ASPECT:

  • ている meaning: 立(た)ち入(い)っている = is inside (a restricted area), or is involved in (a matter)

PARTICLE PATTERNS:

  • [place]に立(た)ち入(い)る: to enter [place]
  • [matter]に立(た)ち入(い)る: to get involved in [matter]

COMMON COLLOCATIONS:

  • 立(た)ち入(い)った質問(しつもん): probing question
  • 立(た)ち入(い)った話(はなし): in-depth/personal conversation
  • 敷地(しきち)に立(た)ち入(い)る: to enter the premises
  • 無断(むだん)で立(た)ち入(い)る: to enter without permission

SIMILAR WORDS:

  • 入(はい)る: to enter — neutral, everyday entry
  • 侵入(しんにゅう)する: to intrude, break in — stronger, implies criminal intent
  • 踏(ふ)み込(こ)む: to step into, to raid — more forceful, often used for police raids
