  example translations, but as "long onion" in a third, so it contradicted itself. "Green onion"
  in American English normally means a scallion (青ねぎ/小ねぎ), a different vegetable from the
  thick white-shafted 長ねぎ. Gloss is now "Japanese long onion; Welsh onion" (definition gloss
  adds "Japanese leek"), and the two example translations now say "long onion" like the third.
- **15147 昇降** — example 1 (昇降口はどこですか) was translated "Where is the entrance/exit (for
  boarding)?". The parenthetical narrows 昇降口 to vehicles, but its commonest sense for a learner
  is the school entrance where shoes are changed. Now simply "Where is the entrance/exit?".
