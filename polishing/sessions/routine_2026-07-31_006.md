## Homograph substitution — the homophone family has a written-form twin

All five `error`-severity gloss/translation flags in the block fell on two entries, and both were
the model substituting a **different word sharing the same characters**:

- **23166 {激高|げきたか}** drew four flags (1 gloss + 3 translation) insisting the entry means
  "rage; fury". That is 激高 read **げきこう**. The entry documents the informal 激〜 intensifier
  word げきたか, "sky-high expensive", and situates it correctly in a productive set the notes
  spell out (激安, 激辛, 激甘, 激混み, 激レア). Entry correct, model wrong. All four rejected.
- **23182 {悪感|あくかん}** drew five flags (2 gloss + 3 translation) insisting on "unpleasant
  sensation; chill; nausea". That is the **おかん** reading. あくかん is "bad feeling; ill will;
  antipathy", which is what the entry documents, with 悪感情 / 反感 / 嫌悪感 / 好感 correctly
  contrasted. All five rejected.

This extends 2026-07-31 session 001's homophone finding (講読/購読): the substitution is not
limited to shared *readings*. An entry documenting the minority reading of a homograph will draw
a full sweep of confident error-severity flags, and the flag text gives no sign the model is
looking at a different word.

**Both rejections still produced coverage value**: 激高/げきこう was genuinely absent and was added
as candidate **C22661**; 悪寒/おかん was checked and already exists at 12670.

The third content flag, **23218 末恐ろしい** ("overwhelmingly promising" for the entry's
"frighteningly promising"), is a stylistic rewording that also discards the 恐ろしい element the
notes exist to explain. Rejected.
